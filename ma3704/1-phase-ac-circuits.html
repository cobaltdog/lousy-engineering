    1-Phase AC Circuits

    #Phasor Rules

    Notations:

    • Phasor is a complex number represented by a bar at the top Vˉ
    • Magnitude of a phasor (or RMS value) is a scalar number represented by V

    Note that a scalar number may be considered as a special complex
    number with zero imaginary part or in other words, zero phase angle.

    Polar and rectangular forms of a phasor:

    • Polar form — in terms of magnitude (RMS value) and phase angle Vˉ=V∠θ
    • Rectangular form — in terms of real and imaginary parts Vˉ=Vr​±jVm​

    Complex conjugate of a phasor:

    Note that a conjugate is represented by the superscript ∗.

    • Polar form — change the sign of the phase angle
      • E.g. Vˉ=V∠θ→Vˉ∗=∠−θ
    • Rectangular form — change the sign of the imaginary part
      • E.g. Vˉ=Vr​+jVm​→Vˉ∗=Vr​−jVm​

    #Phasor Rule 1 (×/÷)

    Multiplying (or dividing) a phasor by a positive constant k is equivalent
    to multiplying (or dividing) its magnitude by the positive constant k.

    💡
    Vˉ=V∠θ     →     kVˉ=kV∠θ     or     kVˉ​=kV​∠θ

    #Phasor Rule 2 (+/−)

    Adding or subtracting two phasors is equivalent to adding or
    subtracting their real parts and imaginary parts respectively.

    💡
    Vˉ1​+Vˉ2​=(Vr1​​+Vr2​​)+j(Vm1​​+Vm2​​)
    💡
    Vˉ1​−Vˉ2​=(Vr1​​+Vr2​​)−j(Vm1​​+Vm2​​)

    #Phasor Rule 3

    Multiplication of two phasors:

    • Resultant magnitude is the product of the magnitudes
    • Resultant phase angle is the sum of the phase angles
    💡
    Xˉ1​⋅Xˉ2​=(X1​⋅X2​)∠(θ1​+θ2​)

    #Phasor Rule 4

    A phasor divided by another phasor:

    • Resultant magnitude is the ratio of magnitudes
    • Resultant phase angle is the difference between the phase angles
      (Angle of numerator phasor minus the angle of denominator phasor)
    💡
    Xˉ2​Xˉ1​​=X2​X1​​∠(θ1​−θ2​)

    #Basic Circuit Elements

    Impedance is a complex number defined as Zˉ=Z∠θ=(R+jX).

    • Re — resistance R
    • Im — reactance X
    • Unit of R, X, and Z is ohms Ω

    Admittance is the reciprocal of impedance defined as Yˉ=Zˉ1​=(G+jB).

    • Re — conductance G
    • Im — susceptance B
    • Unit of G, B, and Y is siemens S

    Active power P is associated with:

    • Resistance R
    • Conductance G

    Reactive power Q is associated with:

    • Reactance X
    • Susceptance B

    #Resistance R

    Impedance of resistance,

    ZˉR​=R+j0=R∠0° Ω

    Expressed in ohms.

    Current through resistance,

    Iˉ=ZˉR​Vˉ​=R∠0°V∠0°​=I∠0° A

    Expressed in amperes.

    Voltage across R and the current through R are in phase.
    Thus, the phase difference between Vˉ and Iˉ is φ=0.
    Resistance does not absorb reactive power.

    Active power absorbed by R,

    💡
    P=VIcosφ=VI=I2R

    Expressed in watts.

    Reactive power absorbed by R,

    💡
    Q=VIsinφ=0

    Expressed in volt-ampere-reactive (VAR).

    #Inductance L

    Impedance of inductance,

    ZˉL​=0+jXL​=XL​∠90° Ω

    Expressed in ohms.

    Reactance of inductance,

    XL​=ωL=2πfL Ω

    Expressed in ohms.

    Current through inductance,

    Iˉ=ZˉL​Vˉ​=XL​∠90°V∠0°​=I∠−90° A

    Expressed in amperes.

    Current lags voltage by 90° or voltage leads current by 90°.
    Thus, the phase difference between Vˉ and Iˉ is φ=90°.
    A pure inductor does not consume active power.

    Active power absorbed by L,

    💡
    P=VIcosφ=0

    Expressed in watts.

    Reactive power absorbed by L,

    💡
    Q=VIsinφ=XL​V2​=I2XL​

    Expressed in volt-ampere-reactive (VAR).

    #Capacitance C

    Impedance of capacitance,

    ZˉC​=0−jXC​=XC​∠−90° Ω

    Expressed in ohms.

    Reactance of capacitance,

    XC​=ωC1​=2πfC1​ Ω

    Expressed in ohms.

    Current through capacitance,

    Iˉ=ZˉC​Vˉ​=XC​∠−90°V∠0°​=I∠90° A

    Expressed in amperes.

    Current leads voltage by 90° or voltage lags current by 90°.
    Thus, the phase difference between Vˉ and Iˉ is φ=−90°.
    A pure capacitor does not consume active power.

    Active power absorbed by C,

    💡
    P=VIcosφ=0

    Expressed in watts.

    Reactive power absorbed by C,

    💡
    Q=VIsinφ=−XC​V2​=−I2XC​

    Expressed in volt-ampere-reactive (VAR).

    #Complex Power and Composite Circuits

    Complex power Sˉ is composed of active power P and reactive power Q.

    💡
    Sˉ=P+jQ

    Expressed in volt-amperes (VA).

    Complex power Sˉ can be expressed in terms of complex voltage and complex current.

    💡
    Sˉ=Vˉ×Iˉ∗=V∠θ1​×I∠−θ2​=VI∠(θ1​−θ2​)=S∠φ

    where φ is the phase difference between Vˉ and Iˉ (also known as power factor angle)

    Power factor is given by,

    💡
    PF=cosφ

    #Complex Power in a Series Circuit

    From Kirchhoff’s voltage law,

    Vˉ=Vˉ1​+Vˉ2​+Vˉ3​

    Iˉ=Iˉ1​=Iˉ2​=Iˉ3​

    Complex power of the source,

    Sˉ=Vˉ1​Iˉ∗+Vˉ2​Iˉ∗+Vˉ3​Iˉ∗

    Sˉ=Sˉ1​+Sˉ2​+Sˉ3​

    #Complex Power in a Parallel Circuit

    From Kirchhoff’s current law,

    Iˉ=Iˉ1​+Iˉ2​+Iˉ3​

    Vˉ=Vˉ1​=Vˉ2​=Vˉ3​

    Complex power of the source,

    Sˉ=VˉIˉ1​∗+VˉIˉ2​∗+VˉIˉ3​∗

    Sˉ=Sˉ1​+Sˉ2​+Sˉ3​

    #R-L-C Circuit

    Impedance of a R-L-C series circuit is,

    💡
    Zˉ=R+jωL−jωC1​

    Magnitude of impedance is,

    💡
    Z=R2+(XL​−XC​)2​

    Phase difference between Vˉ and Iˉ is,

    💡
    φ=tan−1(RXL​−XC​​)

    When XL​>XC​:

    • Circuit is inductive
    • Overall reactive power is positive
    • Lagging power factor (current lags voltage)

    When XL​<XC​:

    • Circuit is capacitive
    • Overall reactive power is negative
    • Leading power factor (current leads voltage)
