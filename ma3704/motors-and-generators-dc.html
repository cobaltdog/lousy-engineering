    Motors and Generators (DC)

    #Fleming’s Rule

    #Fleming’s Left Hand Rule (Motor)

    A current carrying conductor in a magnetic field
    produces an induced force on the conductor.

    💡
    F=Bli

    where i is the magnitude of the current,
    and     l is the length of the conductor,
    and     B is the magnetic flux density

    #Fleming’s Right Hand Rule (Generator)

    A conductor moving at a velocity v in a magnetic field produces an induced emf according to Faraday’s law.

    💡
    eind​=Blv

    #Principle of Operation

    Assumptions:

    • Constant width air-gap between stator and armature (rotor)
    • Constant flux density between the pole faces
    • No flux density in the gap between the poles
    • Air-gap flux density is radial

    #Voltage Induced in a Rotating Loop

    Voltage induced in a rotating loop:

    • Segment ab velocity is perpendicular to magnetic field such that eab​=Blv
    • Segment cd velocity is perpendicular to magnetic field such that ecd​=Blv
    • Segments bc and da velocities are parallel to the field such that ebc​=eda​=0

    Total induced voltage across the loop,

    💡
    eind​=2Blv

    Tangential velocity of the segments
    can be expressed as v=rωmech​.

    eind​=2Bl(rωmech​)

    eind​=π2B​(πrl)(ωmech​)

    eind​=π2​(BAp​)(ωmech​)

    💡
    eind​=π2​ϕωmech​=kϕωmech​

    where Ap​ is the rotor surface area under each pole, obtained by Ap​=21​(2πrl)

    #Induced Torque in a Rotating Loop

    Induced force acting on the loop:

    • Segment ab current is perpendicular to magnetic field such that Fab​=Bli
    • Segment cd current is perpendicular to magnetic field such that Fcd​=Bli
    • Segments bc and da currents are parallel to the field such that Fbc​=Fda​=0
    • Induced forces act about the axis of rotation of radius r which give rise to torque T

    Total induced torque acting on the loop,

    💡

    Tind​=2Blir

    Alternatively,

    Tind​=π2B​(πrl)i

    Tind​=π2​(BAp​)i

    💡

    Tind​=π2​ϕi=kϕi

    where k is a constant representing
    the construction of the machine

    #DC Motor

    DC motors are described by their speed regulation.

    💡

    SR=nmech,fl​nmech,nl​−nmech,fl​​

    Internal generated voltage of armature,

    💡

    EA​=kϕωmech​

    Induced/developed torque,

    💡

    Tind​=kϕIA​

    Types of motor:

    • Separately excited motor — field and armature circuits are supplied by separate sources
    • Shunt motor — field and armature circuits are supplied by the same source

    For both types of circuits, terminal voltage can be obtained from,

    💡

    VT​=EA​+IA​RA​

    Induced torque vs speed,

    VT​=EA​+IA​RA​

    VT​=kϕωmech​+kϕTind​​RA​

    ωmech​=kϕVT​​−(kϕ)2RA​​Tind​

    As the load on the motor shaft increases,
    it begins to slow down and the internal voltage of the armature decreases.
    Armature current increases, which
    increases the induced torque.

    #Power Relations

    Power-flow diagram:

    • Copper losses — due to field and armature resistance
    • Core losses — due hysteresis and eddy currents in the core
    • Mechanical losses — friction, windage loss due to air inside motor casing
    • Stray losses — any losses that cannot be categorized

    Copper losses of a motor,

    💡

    PCL​=IF​2RF​+IA​2RA​

    Converted/developed power of a motor,

    💡

    Pconv​=Pin​−PCL​=EA​IA​=Tdev​ωmech​

    where Pin​=VF​IF​+VT​IL​ for a separately excited motor,
    and     Pin​=VT​IL​ for a shunt motor

    Output power of a motor,

    💡

    Pout​=Pconv​−Prot​=Tload​ωmech​

    #Parameters of Equivalent Circuit

    #Locked-Rotor Test

    Locked-rotor test:

    • Armature is locked such that it does not rotate, ωmech​=0 and EA​=0
    • Separate DC power sources supply VF​ and VT​ to the respective circuits
    • Currents IF​ and IA​ are adjusted to corresponding rated currents
    • Field and armature resistance are then measured

    Field resistance,

    💡

    RF​=IF​VF​​

    Armature resistance,

    💡

    RA​=IA​VT​​

    #No Load Test

    No load test:

    • Motor is allowed to run freely with no mechanical load attached to it
    • Converted power is only used to overcome rotational losses

    Rotational losses,

    💡

    Prot​=Pconv,nl​=EA​IA​

    #Speed Control of a Shunt Motor

    Speed-torque equation is given by,

    💡

    ωmech​=kϕVT​​−(kϕ)2RA​​Tind​

    Speed control:

    • Adjusting field resistance RF​, and thus field flux ϕ
    • Adjusting terminal voltage VT​ applied to armature
    • Inserting a resistor in series with armature circuit

    #Increasing Field Resistance

    Effect of increasing resistance:

    • Field current IF​ decreases → field flux ϕ also decreases
    • Internal generated voltage EA​=kϕωmech​ decreases
    • Armature current IA​ increases since IA​=RA​VT​−EA​​
    • Induced torque Tind​ increases since Tind​=kϕIA​
    • Tind​>Tload​ → motor speed ωmech​ increases
    • Speed ωmech​ increases until Tind​=Tload​
    • Motor rotates at this higher new speed

    #Increasing Armature Terminal Voltage

    Effect of increasing voltage:

    • Armature current IA​ increases since IA​=RA​VT​−EA​​
    • Induced torque Tind​ increases since Tind​=kϕIA​
    • Tind​>Tload​ → motor speed ωmech​ increases
    • Speed ωmech​ increases until Tind​=Tload​
    • Motor rotates at this higher new speed

    #Inserting a Resistor in Series

    Effect of series resistor:

    • Armature current IA​ decreases since IA​=RA​VT​−EA​​
    • Induced torque Tind​ decreases since Tind​=kϕIA​
    • Tind​<Tload​ → motor speed ωmech​ decreases
    • Speed ωmech​ decreases until Tind​=Tload​
    • Motor rotates at this lower new speed

    #DC Generator

    DC generators are described by their voltage regulation.

    💡

    VR=Vfl​Vnl​−Vfl​​×100%

    Types of generator:

    • Separately excited generator — field flux is supplied by a separate power source
    • Shunt generator — field circuit is directly connected to the terminals of the source

    For both types of circuits, terminal voltage can be obtained from,

    💡

    VT​=EA​−IA​RA​

    Note that direction of current flow is opposite of that in a DC motor.

    Varying terminal voltage in a separately excited generator (EA​=kϕωmech​):

    • Increase in ωmech​ → increase in EA​ → increase in terminal voltage VT​
    • Decrease in RF​ → increase in IF​ → increase in ϕ → increase in EA​

    #Power Relations

    The power-flow diagram of a DC generator is the reverse of a DC motor.

    Input power of a separately excited generator,

    💡

    Pin​=Tin​ωmech​+VF​IF​

    where Tin​ is the torque of the prime mover

    Input power of a shunt generator,

    💡

    Pin​=Tin​ωmech​

    Converted/developed power of a generator,

    💡

    Pconv​=Pin​−Prot​=EA​IA​=Tdev​ωmech​

    Output power of a generator,

    💡

    Pout​=Pconv​−PCL​=VT​IL​
