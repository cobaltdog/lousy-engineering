    Induction Motors (AC)

    #Rotating Magnetic Field

    Principle of operation:

    • Stator produces a rotating magnetic field from 3-phase AC power input
    • Rotating magnetic field induces an alternating emf and current in the rotor
    • Induced current in the rotor generates its own magnetic field
    • Interaction of the magnetic fields results in an induced torque
    • Induced torque turns rotor to align it with stator magnetic field

    Synchronous speed (speed of the rotating flux) expressed in revolutions per minute,

    💡

    nsync​=p120fs​​

    where fs​ is the frequency of the 3-phase supply in Hz,
    and     p is the number of poles formed by stator winding

    #Rotor Slip

    Rotor slip:

    • Rotor speed can never be equal to synchronous speed
    • If rotor turns at the same speed, rotor windings experience no change in magnetic flux
    • No change in magnetic flux results in no induced voltage and current in the rotor
    • No torque would be acting on the rotor and it would slow down by friction

    Slip speed of the rotor,

    💡

    nslip​=nsync​−nmech​

    The various speeds are expressed in rev/min.

    Slip of the rotor,

    💡

    s=nsync​nslip​​=nsync​nsync​−nmech​​=ωsync​ωsync​−ωmech​​

    If s=0, rotor is turning at synchronous speed (no slip).
    If     s=1, rotor is stationary.

    #Electrical Frequency of Rotor

    Induction motor is sometimes called a rotating transformer; stator (primary) induces voltage
    and current in the rotor (secondary), but rotor frequency is not the same as stator frequency.

    💡

    fr​=sfs​=120p​(nsync​−nmech​)

    #Equivalent Circuit of Motor

    Induction of voltages and currents in the rotor is essentially a transformer operation.

    • Greater the relative motion between rotor and stator, greater the induced voltage
    • Largest relative motion occurs when the rotor is stationary (locked-rotor condition)
    • Smallest induced voltage occurs when the rotor is turning at synchronous speed

    Magnitude of induced rotor voltage at any slip,

    ER​=2​2πfr​Nr​ϕmax​​

    ER​=2​2π(sfs​)Nr​ϕmax​​

    💡

    ER​=s(4.44fs​Nr​ϕmax​)=sER0​

    where ER0​ is the magnitude of induced rotor voltage at locked-rotor condition

    Reactance XR​ of the rotor depends on the frequency of the current and voltage in the rotor.

    XR​=ωr​LR​

    XR​=2πfr​LR​

    XR​=2π(sfs​)LR​

    💡

    XR​=s(2πfs​LR​)=sXR0​

    where XR0​ is the rotor reactance at locked-rotor condition

    Rotor current can be determined from,

    IˉR​=RR​+jXR​EˉR​​

    IˉR​=RR​+j(sXR0​)EˉR​​

    IˉR​=RR​/s+jXR0​EˉR​/s​

    💡

    IˉR​=RR​/s+jXR0​EˉR0​​

    #Final Equivalent Circuit

    Final equivalent circuit is obtained by reflecting the rotor side to the stator side.

    Reflected induced rotor voltage,

    💡

    E1​=aeff​ER0​

    From transformers, VP​=aVs​.

    Reflected induced rotor current,

    💡

    I2​=aeff​IR​​

    From transformers, IP​=aIs​​.

    Reflected rotor resistance,

    💡

    R2​=aeff​2RR​

    From transformers, RS′​=a2RS​.

    Reflected rotor reactance,

    💡

    X2​=aeff​2XR0​

    From transformers, XS′​=a2XS​.

    Core losses due to RC​ are usually lumped together with friction, windage, and stray losses, which are collectively called the rotational losses Prot​. Unless otherwise specified, RC​ will
    not be included in the equivalent circuit of an induction motor.

    #Power and Torque of Motor

    Power-flow diagram:

    • Stator copper loss — power loss in the stator windings
    • Rotor copper loss — power loss in the rotor windings
    • Rotational losses — core losses, friction, windage, and stray load losses
    • Air-gap power — power transferred from stator to rotor across air-gap between them
    • Converted/developed power — power converted from electrical to mechanical power

    #Power Relations

    Copper losses,

    PSCL​=3I1​2R1​

    PRCL​=3I2​2R2​

    Air-gap power,

    PAG​=Pin​−PSCL​=3I2​2sR2​​

    Converted power,

    Pconv​=PAG​−PRCL​

    Pconv​=3I2​2sR2​​−3I2​2R2​

    Pconv​=3I2​2R2​(s1​−1)

    Pconv​=3I2​2R2​(s1−s​)

    Pconv​=(1−s)(3I2​2sR2​​)

    💡

    Pconv​=(1−s)PAG​

    As shown above,

    Pconv​=3I2​2R2​(s1−s​)

    💡

    Pconv​=3I2​2Rconv​

    #Torque-Speed Characteristics

    Torque developed by the conversion of electrical to mechanical power,

    💡

    Tdev​=ωmech​Pconv​​=(1−s)ωsync​(1−s)PAG​​=ωsync​PAG​​

    Rewriting the above expression,

    Tdev​=ωsync​1​(3I2​2sR2​​)

    By calculating the current I2​, we can determine the torque developed by the motor.

    Apply Thevenin’s theorem which states that any linear circuit that can be separated
    by two terminals from the rest of the system can be replaced by a single voltage
    source in series with an equivalent impedance.

    Thevenin’s voltage,

    VˉTH​=Iˉ(jXm​)

    VˉTH​=R1​+j(X1​+XM​)Vˉϕ​​⋅jXM​

    VˉTH​=R1​+j(X1​+XM​)jXM​​Vˉϕ​

    💡

    VTH​=R1​2+(X1​+XM​)2​XM​​Vϕ​

    Thevenin’s impedance,

    ZˉTH​1​=R1​+jX1​1​+jXM​1​

    ZˉTH​1​=jXM​(R1​+jX1​)R1​+j(X1​+XM​)​

    💡

    ZˉTH​=R1​+j(X1​+XM​)jXM​(R1​+jX1​)​

    Replacing the left side of the equivalent circuit with Thevenin values,
    we obtained a simplified equivalent circuit to determine current I2​.

    Iˉ2​=ZˉTH​+jX2​+R2​/sVˉTH​​

    Iˉ2​=(RTH​+R2​/s)+j(XTH​+X2​)VˉTH​​

    💡

    I2​=(RTH​+R2​/s)2+(XTH​+X2​)2​VTH​​

    Maximum (pullout) torque occurs when the air-gap power is maximum.

    Tdev,max​=ωsync​PAG,max​​=ωsync​3I2​2​(sR2​​)max​

    For power consumed by sR2​​ to be maximum,

    💡

    sR2​​=∣RTH​+j(XTH​+X2​)∣=RTH​2+(XTH​+X2​)2​

    #Parameters of Equivalent Circuit

    #DC Test

    DC test:

    • Determine stator resistance R1​
    • DC voltage is applied to stator windings of the motor
    • No induced voltage in the rotor as the current is DC
    • Reactance of motor is therefore zero at direct current

    Current flows through two of the windings, thus total resistance of the circuit is 2R1​.

    💡
    R1​=2IDC​VDC​​

    Note that an induction motor is a 3-phase machine, and has one winding per phase.
    In the DC test, only 2 of the 3 windings are connected to the DC power supply.

    #Locked-Rotor Test

    Locked/blocked rotor test:

    • Determine rotor resistance R2​, and stator and rotor impedances X1​ and X2​
    • Rotor is locked/blocked such that it cannot turn, thus rotor slip s=1
    • AC voltage is applied to stator and current adjusted to full-load value
    • jXM​≫R2​+jX2​ such that no current flows through jXM​

    Input power is only consumed by the stator and rotor resistances.

    💡
    Pin​=3I1​2(R1​+R2​)

    Note that stator resistance R1​ has been obtained from the DC test.

    Total impedance of the circuit under locked-rotor test,

    💡

    I1​Vϕ​​=ZLR​=(R1​+R2​)2+(X1​+X2​)2​

    Stator and rotor impedances are usually equal.

    #No Load Test

    No load test:

    • Determine the rotational losses of the motor
    • Motor is allowed to spin freely — input power only used to overcome losses
    • At no load, rotor speed nmech​≈nsync​ such that rotor slip s≈0
    • sR2​​ will be very high such that no current flows through rotor
    • Rotor copper losses is zero as rotor current I2​ is negligible

    Current only flows through the stator windings and the core.

    💡
    I1​Vϕ​​=Znl​=R1​2+(X1​+XM​)2​

    Note that stator impedance X1​ has been obtained from the locked-rotor test.

    Input power is only used to overcome stator copper loss and rotational losses.

    💡
    Prot​=Pin​−PSCL​
