    #Magnetic Circuits

    Magnetic flux (Weber),

    φ=BA

    where A is the cross-section area

    Magnetic flux density (Tesla),

    B=μ0​μr​H=μH

    where permeability of free space μ0​=4π×10−7,
    and     μr​ is the relative permeability of a material,
    and     H is the magnetic field intensity

    Reluctance of a medium,

    R=μAl​

    where l is the mean path length,
    and S.I. unit of reluctance is     H−1

    Ohm’s law for magnetic circuits,

    Ni=φR=Hl

    where N is the number of turns of the coil

    #Transformers (AC)

    Voltage induced in a coil,

    Erms​=2​2πfNϕmax​​≈4.44fNϕmax​

    where ϕ is the magnetic flux passing through the coil

    Turns ratio,

    a=NS​NP​​=VS​VP​​=IP​IS​​

    where NP​ is number of turns of the primary coil,
    and     NS​ is number of turns of the secondary coil

    Impedance transformation,

    ZP​=a2ZS​ (Secondary referred to primary side)

    ZS​=a2ZP​​ (Primary referred to secondary side)

    #Real Transformer

    Open-Circuit Test

    • High-voltage side is opened (no current flow)
    • Only current flow through the core RC​ and XM​
    • Values obtained correspond to the low-voltage side

    Core resistance,

    RC,LV​=POC​VOC​2​

    Magnetizing reactance,

    XM,LV​=IXM​​VOC​​

    where IOC​=IRC​​2+IXM​​2​

    Short-Circuit Test

    • Low-voltage side is short-circuit
    • Only current flow through the coils ZP​ and ZS​
    • Values obtained correspond to the high-voltage side

    Equivalent resistance of the coils,

    Req,HV​=ISC​2PSC​​

    Equivalent reactance of the coils,

    Xeq,HV​=Zeq​2−Req​2​

    #Voltage Regulation

    Voltage regulation,

    VR=VS,fl​VS,nl​−VS,fl​​

    where VˉS,nl​=aVˉP​​=VˉS​+IˉS​Zˉeq,S​

    Output power,

    Pout​=VS​IS​cosθ

    where power factor PF=cosθ

    Transformer efficiency,

    η=Pin​Pout​​=Pout​+PCu​+Pcore​Pout​​

    where PCu​ and Pcore​ are power losses

    Copper loss due to resistance of coils,

    PCu​=IS​2Req,S​

    Equivalent resistance of the coils when reflected (referred) to secondary side.

    Core loss due to electromagnetism,

    If     s=1, rotor is stationary.

    #Parameters of Equivalent Circuit

    DC Test

    • Determine stator resistance R1​
    • DC voltage is applied to stator windings of the motor
    • No induced voltage in the rotor as the current is DC
    • Reactance of motor is therefore zero at direct current

    Stator resistance,

    R1​=21​(IDC​VDC​​)

    Locked-Rotor Test

    • Determine rotor resistance R2​, and stator and rotor impedances X1​ and X2​
    • Rotor is locked/blocked such that it cannot turn, thus rotor slip s=1
    • jXM​≫R2​+jX2​ such that no current flows through jXM​

    Rotor resistance,

    Pin​=3I1​2(R1​+R2​)

    Stator and rotor impedances,

    I1​Vϕ​​=ZLR​=(R1​+R2​)2+(X1​+X2​)2​

    Note that stator and rotor reactances are equal.

    No Load Test

    • Motor is allowed to spin freely — input power only used to overcome losses
    • At no load, rotor speed nmech​≈nsync​ such that rotor slip s≈0
    • sR2​​ will be very high such that no current flows through rotor
    • Rotor copper losses is zero as rotor current I2​ is negligible

    Magnetizing reactance,

    I1​Vϕ​​=Znl​=R1​2+(X1​+XM​)2​

    Rotational losses,

    Prot​=Pin​−PSCL​

    Prot​=3Vϕ​I1​cosθ−3I1​2R1​

    Locked-Rotor Test

    • Armature is locked such that it does not rotate, ωmech​=0 and EA​=0
    • Separate DC power sources supply VF​ and VT​ to the respective circuits
    • Currents IF​ and IA​ are adjusted to corresponding rated currents
    • Field and armature resistance are then measured
