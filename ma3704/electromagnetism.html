    Electromagnetism

    #Magnetic Fields

    A flow of electric current in a conductor creates a magnetic field around the conductor.

    • Direction of magnetic field is determined by right-hand-rule
    • Described by magnetic field intensity H
    • S.I. unit of A/m or At/m

    From Ampere’s law,

    ∮c​ H dl=Ienc​

    💡

    Hl=I

    where l is the length of the path

    For a single conductor,

    💡

    H=lI​=2πrI​

    where r is the radius of the circular path

    For multiple conductors,

    💡

    H=lNI​=2πrNI​

    where N is the number of conductors

    #Magnetic Flux φ and Flux Density B

    Magnetic field intensity produces a magnetic flux (analogous to current) that
    flows through the circular path, and the flux per unit area is the flux density.

    Total flux flowing through a medium,

    💡

    φ=BA

    where A is the cross-section area of the medium,
    S.I. unit of weber     (Wb)

    Flux density in free space (air),

    💡

    B0​=μ0​H

    where permeability of free space μ0​=4π×10−7 H/m,
    S.I. unit of weber per square meter     (Wb/m2) or Tesla (T)

    Flux density through a medium,

    💡

    B=μ0​μr​H=μH

    where μr​ is the relative permeability of the medium,
    and     μ is the permeability of the medium

    #Magnetic Circuits

    Flow of magnetic flux, created by current flowing
    in a coil, may be analyzed as a flow of current in
    an electric circuit.

    Assumptions:

    • Flux is restricted to magnetic core (no leakage)
    • Flux density is uniform with the magnetic core
      (taken as flux density along the mean path)

    Flux in a magnetic circuit,

    B=μH=μ(lNI​)

    Flux density of a magnetic circuit,

    φ=BA=μ(lANI​)

    φ=BA=l/μANI​

    φ=BA=RF​

    Ohm’s law for a magnetic equivalent circuit,

    💡

    F=NI=φR

    where F is the magnetomotive force (mmf),
    and     R is the reluctance of the core material

    #Magnetic Equivalent Circuit

    Drawing of equivalent circuit:

    • Identify the source and direction of magnetic flux
    • Trace the paths of magnetic flux through different sections
    • Determine the reluctance R of the different sections using μAl​

    Note that although the same flux may flow through different sections, reluctance of each
    section may be different if the core material or cross-section area of the core is different.

    #Faraday’s Law (Induced emf)

    When flux φ passes through a coil with N turns, the flux is said to have linked the coil.

    Flux linkage is defined as,

    💡

    λ=Nφ

    Faraday’s law states that a coil with flux linkage λ
    will produce an induced voltage e across the coil.

    💡

    e=−dtdλ​=−Ndtdφ​

    Negative sign indicates that the direction of the induced voltage will be such that it will tend to
    oppose the voltage creating the flux.

    #Self Inductance

    Consider current i through a coil which will then produce magnetic flux φ.

    Inductance of the coil is defined as,

    L=didλ​

    L=iλ​ (for linear systems)

    L=iNφ​

    L=iN​⋅RNi​

    💡

    L=RN2​

    S.I. unit of inductance is henry (H).

    For a magnetic circuit excited by an AC source, the induced emf is,

    💡

    e=−dtdλ​=−didλ​⋅dtdi​=−Ldtdi​

    If the resistance of the coil is considered,

    💡

    v=ri+Ldtdi​

    Input voltage is equal to the sum of voltage drop across
    the resistance and the induced emf in the coil (inductor).

    If the resistance of the coil is negligible,

    💡

    v=Ldtdi​

    Input voltage is equal to the induced emf in the coil (inductor).

    #Magnetic Field Energy (Magnetic Stored Energy)

    Consider a lossless magnetic circuit.

    If the current input is i at a voltage of v, electrical input energy is,

    P=dtdWin​​=vi

    dWin​=vi dt

    dWin​=−ei dt (substitute the expression for e)

    dWin​=i dλ (substitute the expression for λ)

    dWin​=Ni dφ

    Integrating the above gives the total electrical energy input into a magnetic system.
    In a lossless system, since there is no output, the input energy will be stored as
    magnetic field energy Wf​ or Wm​.

    Energy input to establish flux φ in a coil with N turns,

    Wf​=∫Ni dφ (substitute the expression for Ohm’s law)

    Wf​=∫φR dφ

    Wf​=21​φ2R

    Substituting Ohm’s law F=Ni=φR,

    Wf​=21​(φR)φ=21​Niφ

    Substituting inductance L=iλ​=iNφ​,

    Wf​=21​(Nφ)i=21​λi=21​i2L

    Expressions for magnetic field energy in a lossless system are,

    💡

    Wf​=21​φ2R=21​Niφ=21​λi=21​i2L

    Note that these expressions are valid only for linear systems
    since reluctance     R is assumed constant during integration.

    #Magnetic Field Energy Density

    Since Ni=Hl and φ=BA,
    the energy density is written as,

    Wf​=∫Ni dφ

    Wf​=∫Hl⋅A dB

    Wf​=Al∫H dB

    AlWf​​=∫H dB

    💡

    wf​=∫H dB

    Note that the product of cross-section area and length gives volume of the material.

    Since B=μH, evaluating the integral,

    💡

    wf​=2μB2​=21​HB

    S.I. unit of energy density is J/m3.

    #Electromechanical Energy Conversion

    Electromechanical devices convert electrical energy into mechanical energy, and vice versa.

    Energy balance equation for such devices,

    💡

    Win​=Wout​+Wf​+Wloss​

    where Win​ is the input electrical energy and Wout​ is the output mechanical energy

    Assuming no losses, the equation simplifies to,

    Win​=Wout​+Wf​

    dWin​=dWout​+dWf​

    Consider a electromagnetic system with one fixed
    and one movable part separated by a gap x. Total reluctance of the system is R1​.

    If the system is operating at P1 with:

    • Input current i1​
    • Magnetic flux φ1​
    • Stored energy Wf​=21​Ni1​φ1​

    Let the force experienced by the movable part at
    a gap distance of x be Fm​. The energy output is,

    dWout​=Fm​ dx

    As the movable part moves, the air gap decreases.
    Reluctance of the air gap decreases, and thus the
    total reluctance of the system decreases.

    Recall that reluctance R=μ/Al​.

    Operating point P1 moves to a point on line 2. As
    a result, there will be changes in input energy and stored energy.

    Output energy is then,

    dWout​=dWin​−dWf​

    Fm​ dx=dWin​−dWf​

    An expression for force Fm​ can be derived by evaluating dWin​ and dWf​ corresponding to
    the new operating point on line 2.

    #Constant Flux Condition

    The flux φ is held constant as the movable part moves a distance dx under the force Fm​, the operating point shifts horizontally.

    φ2​=φ1​

    Change in input energy is,

    dWin​=Ni dφ

    dWin​=Ni(φ2​−φ1​)

    dWin​=0

    Force developed is,

    Fm​ dx=dWin​−dWf​

    Fm​ dx=0−dWf​

    💡

    Fm​=−dxdWf​​

    The force developed is proportional to the rate of decrease of stored energy.

    Since Wf​=21​φ2R, the equation becomes,

    💡

    Fm​=−21​φ2dxdR(x)​

    The force developed is proportional to the rate of decrease of total reluctance.

    #Constant Current Condition

    The input current i is held constant whereby
    the operating point shifts vertically such that
    the flux changes from φ1​ to φ2​.

    Change in input energy is,

    dWin​=Ni dφ

    dWin​=Ni(φ2​−φ1​)

    Change in stored energy is,

    dWf​=21​Ni dφ

    dWf​=21​Ni(φ2​−φ1​)

    dWf​=21​ dWin​

    dWin​=2 dWf​

    Force developed is,

    Fm​ dx=dWin​−dWf​

    Fm​ dx=2 dWf​−dWf​

    💡

    Fm​=dxdWf​​

    The force developed is proportional to the rate of increase of stored energy.

    Since Wf​=21​i2L, the equation becomes,

    💡

    Fm​=21​i2dxdL(x)​

    The force developed is proportional to the rate of increase of inductance.
