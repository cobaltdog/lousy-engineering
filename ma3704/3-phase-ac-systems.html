    3-Phase AC Systems

    #General 3-Phase System

    3-phase voltages and currents:

    • Voltages are equal in magnitude but differ in phase angle by 120°
    • Quantities of each phase is represented by subscript a, b, and c
    • Live wires deliver power to loads, neutral wires act as return

    3-phase 4-wire system:

    • Single neutral wire connects all loads
    • Neutral current Iˉn​=IˉA​+IˉB​+IˉC​

    3-phase 3-wire system:

    • If loads are identical then Iˉn​=0
    • Neutral line can then be eliminated

    #Balanced 3-Phase System

    A 3-phase system is balanced if sources, transmission systems, and loads are balanced.

    • 3 voltages of equal magnitude and phase shifts of exactly 120° apart
    • 3-phase load is balanced if impedances have equal magnitude and phase angle
    • 3-phase transmission system is balanced if impedances of the 3 wires are equal

    Phase sequence a-b-c:

    • Vˉa​ leads Vˉb​    →   Vˉa​=V∠0°
    • Vˉb​ leads Vˉc​    →   Vˉb​=V∠−120°
    • Vˉc​ leads Vˉa​    →   Vˉc​=V∠120°

    Phase sequence a-c-b:

    • Vˉa​ leads Vˉc​    →   Vˉa​=V∠0°
    • Vˉb​ leads Vˉa​    →   Vˉb​=V∠120°
    • Vˉc​ leads Vˉb​    →   Vˉc​=V∠−120°

    #Types of Connections

    Star or Y (wye) connection:

    • Each source (or load) is connected to a neutral point
    • In a balanced system, voltage at neutral point is zero

    Mesh or Δ (delta) connection:

    • Sources and loads are connected in a delta form
    • No neutral point in a delta connection (always 3-phase 3-wire)
    • 3-phase 4-wire load cannot be connected to a Δ-connection generator

    #Phase and Line Quantities

    For both Y and Δ connections:

    • Phase voltage — voltage of each source, or across each load
    • Phase current — current of each source, or through each load
    • Line voltage — voltage between any two lines (excluding neutral)
    • Line current — current through any line (excluding neutral)

    Phase quantities are inside the dotted box and may not be accessible.
    Line quantities are outside the dotted box and are readily available.

    For Y-connection:

    • Phase voltage = Line voltage
    • Phase current = Line current
    • IˉA​=Iˉa​     IˉB​=Iˉb​     IˉC​=Iˉc​

    For Δ-connection:

    • Phase voltage = Line voltage
    • Phase current = Line current
    • Vˉab​=Vˉa​     Vˉbc​=Vˉb​     Vˉca​=Vˉc​

    #Relation between Phase and Line Voltages (Y-Connection)

    Phase voltages for sequence a-b-c,

    Vˉa​=V∠0°

    Vˉb​=V∠−120°

    Vˉc​=V∠120°

    Line voltages are then,

    Vˉab​=Vˉa​−Vˉb​=V∠0°−V∠−120°=3​V∠30°

    Vˉbc​=Vˉb​−Vˉc​=V∠−120°−V∠120°=3​V∠−90°

    Vˉca​=Vˉc​−Vˉa​=V∠120°−V∠0°=3​V∠150°

    Observations:

    • VL​=3​VP​
    • Line voltage leads their respective phase voltage by 30° (for sequence a-b-c)
    • Line voltage lags their respective phase voltage by 30° (for sequence a-c-b)

    #Relation between Phase and Line Currents (Δ-Connection)

    Phase currents for sequence a-b-c,

    Iˉa​=I∠0°

    Iˉb​=I∠−120°

    Iˉc​=I∠120°

    Line currents are then,

    IˉA​=Iˉa​−Iˉc​=I∠0°−I∠120°=3​I∠−30°

    IˉB​=Iˉb​−Iˉa​=I∠−120°−I∠0°=3​I∠−150°

    IˉC​=Iˉc​−Iˉb​=I∠120°−I∠−120°=3​I∠90°

    Observations:

    • IL​=3​IP​
    • Line current lags their respective phase current by 30° (for sequence a-b-c)
    • Line current leads their respective phase current by 30° (for sequence a-c-b)

    #3-Phase Total Complex Power

    Total complex power of a 3-phase system is the sum of all three phases.

    💡
    SˉT​=Sˉa​+Sˉb​+Sˉc​

    Under balanced condition, all phases have the same complex power.

    SˉT​=3Sˉa​=3Vˉa​Iˉa​∗=3VP​IP​∠φ

    For Y-connection,

    3VP​IP​∠φ=3​(3​VP​)IP​∠φ

    3VP​IP​∠φ=3​VL​IL​∠φ

    For Δ-connection,

    3VP​IP​∠φ=3​VP​(3​IP​)∠φ

    3VP​IP​∠φ=3​VL​IL​∠φ

    As shown above, for both Y and Δ connections of a balanced system,

    💡
    SˉT​=3VP​IP​∠φ=3​VL​IL​∠φ

    Note that φ is the angle difference between phase voltage and phase current.

    #Analysis of Balanced Systems

    A 3-phase circuit can be analyzed from its 1-phase equivalent circuit.

    Unless otherwise specified:

    • Voltage of a 3-phase system is always specified as line voltage
    • Current of a 3-phase system is always specified as line current
    • Power of a 3-phase system is always specified as total power
    • Impedance of a 3-phase system is specified as phase impedance

    Standard procedure of solving a Δ-connection system is
    by transforming it to an equivalent Y-connection system.

    From Δ to Y,

    💡
    ZˉY​=3ZˉΔ​​

    From Y to Δ,

    💡
    ZˉΔ​=3ZˉY​

    #Power Factor Correction

    Active power is given by P=VIcosφ=Scosφ.

    • For a given active power and voltage, current is reduced when power factor increase
    • Lower current — lower active and reactive power losses in the transmission cable
    • Lower voltage drops across transmission cable — improve voltage regulation

    From the power triangle:

    • φ can be changed by changing reactive power Q
    • Q can be changed by adding inductor/capacitor
    • Inductor absorbs positive reactive power
    • Capacitor absorbs negative reactive power

    When inductor is switched on:

    • Reactive power increases
    • Power factor angle increases to φnew​
    • Power factor decreases, and current increases

    When capacitor is switch on:

    • Reactive power decreases
    • Power factor angle decreases to φnew​
    • Power factor increases, and current decreases

    In most cases, a capacitor is added to improve power factor or reduce current.

    #Power Measurement

    A wattmeter is used to measure average power or active power.

    • Voltage coil (VC) to measure voltage
    • Current coil (CC) to measure current
    • Ideal case — coils has no impedance

    Power measured by a wattmeter,

    💡

    P=∣V∣×∣I∣×∣cosφ∣Vˉ,Iˉ​

    Total power in a 3-phase system is the sum of each phase.

    💡

    PT​=Pa​+Pb​+Pc​=Va​Ia​cosφa​+Vb​Ib​cosφb​+Vc​Ic​cosφc​

    #Two Wattmeter Method

    Blondel’s theorem:

    • Minimum number of wattmeters is one less than the number of wires
    • 3-phase, 3-wire system: 2 needed
    • 3-phase, 4-wire system: 3 needed
    • Valid for both Y- and Δ-systems
    • Valid for unbalanced and balanced

    #Under Balanced Condition

    Consider a Y-connected system with phase sequence a-b-c.
    Phase voltage Vˉa​ as the reference, and a lagging power factor angle φ.

    Phase voltages are,

    Vˉa​=VP​∠0°

    Vˉb​=VP​∠−120°

    Vˉc​=VP​∠120°

    Line voltages are,

    Vˉab​=VL​∠30°

    Vˉbc​=VL​∠−90°

    Vˉca​=VL​∠150°

    Line voltages are,

    Vˉba​=VL​∠−150°

    Vˉcb​=VL​∠90°

    Vˉac​=VL​∠−30°

    Line currents are,

    Iˉa​=IP​∠−φ

    Iˉb​=IP​∠−φ−120°

    Iˉc​=IP​∠−φ+120°

    Wattmeter reading across line ac,

    P1​=∣Vac​∣×∣Ia​∣×∣cosθ∣

    P1​=VL​IL​cos[−30°−(−φ)]

    P1​=VL​IL​cos(−30°+φ)

    💡

    P1​=VL​IL​cos(30°−φ)

    Wattmeter reading across line bc,

    P2​=∣Vbc​∣×∣Ib​∣×∣cosθ∣

    P2​=VL​IL​cos[−90°−(−φ−120°)]

    💡

    P2​=VL​IL​cos(30°+φ)

    Total active power of the system,

    P1​+P2​=VL​IL​cos(30°−φ)+VL​IL​cos(30°+φ)

    P1​+P2​=VL​IL​(3​cosφ)

    💡

    PT​=3​VL​IL​cosφ

    Total reactive power of the system,

    P1​−P2​=VL​IL​cos(30°−φ)−VL​IL​cos(30°+φ)

    P1​−P2​=VL​IL​sinφ

    3​(P1​−P2​)=3​VL​IL​sinφ

    💡

    QT​=3​(P1​−P2​)

    Power factor angle of the system,

    P1​+P2​P1​−P2​​=3​VL​IL​cosφVL​IL​sinφ​

    P1​+P2​P1​−P2​​=3​tanφ​

    💡

    φ=tan−1[P1​+P2​3​(P1​−P2​)​]

    When ∣φ∣=60° or PF=cosφ=0.5:

    • One of the wattmeter readings will be zero
    • Reading of other wattmeter is the total average power

    When ∣φ∣>60° and PF=cosφ<0.5:

    • One of the wattmeter readings will be negative
    • Reverse the connection of voltage or current coil

    When ∣φ∣=0° and PF=cosφ=0:

    • Both wattmeters give the same reading P1​=P2​
