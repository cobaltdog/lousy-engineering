    Transformers (AC)

    #Ideal Transformer

    Ideal transformer:

    • No leakage fluxes
    • Core losses are neglected
    • Windings have no resistance
    • Relative permeability of the core is infinite

    Turn ratios of a transformer,

    💡

    a=VS​(t)VP​(t)​=NS​NP​​

    where subscript P denotes primary winding,
    and subscript     S denotes secondary winding

    Since there is no magnetic potential drop,

    💡

    iS​(t)iP​(t)​=NP​NS​​=a1​

    Voltage ratio in terms of phasor,

    💡

    VˉS​VˉP​​=a

    Current ratio in terms of phasor,

    💡

    IˉS​IˉP​​=a1​

    Turns ratio of an ideal transformer affects only the magnitudes of voltages and currents.
    Phase angle of voltages and currents remain unchanged for the primary and secondary.

    Dot convention (in the schematic at one end of each winding):

    • If VP​ is positive at the dotted end, then VS​ is also positive at the dotted end
    • If IP​ flows into the dotted end of primary, IS​ flows out the dotted end of secondary

    #Power in an Ideal Transformer

    Active power supplied to transformer is given by Pin​=VP​IP​cosθP​.
    Active power supplied by transformer is given by Pout​=VS​IS​cosθS​.

    Substituting the appropriate relations,

    Pout​=VS​IS​cosθS​

    Pout​=(aVP​​)(aIP​)cosθP​

    Pout​=VP​IP​cosθP​

    Pout​=Pin​

    Output power of an ideal transformer is equal to its input power.

    The same relation applies to reactive power Q and complex power S.

    #Impedance Transformation through a Transformer

    Impedance transformation:

    • Presence of a transformer can alter the impedance seen by the voltage source
    • Transformer changes the voltage and current levels in the secondary coil
    • Secondary coil has an apparent impedance as “seen” by the source
    • Replace secondary coil with an equivalent load ZL′​

    Actual load impedance of secondary coil,

    ZL​=IS​VS​​

    Apparent impedance of primary coil,

    ZL′​=IP​VP​​

    ZL′​=IS​/aaVS​​

    ZL′​=IS​a2VS​​

    💡

    ZL′​=a2ZL​

    The load impedance that is “reflected“ from the secondary to the primary coil.

    #Real Transformer

    Not all the flux produced in the primary coil passes through the secondary coil.
    Some of the flux lines leave the iron core and pass through the air instead.
    Portion of flux that goes through one coil but not the other is leakage flux.

    Total flux in the primary coil,

    💡

    ϕ​P​=ϕM​+ϕLP​

    where ϕ​P​ is the total average primary flux,
    and     ϕM​ is the mutual flux linking the coils,
    and     ϕLP​ is the leakage flux of the primary coil

    Total flux in the secondary coil,

    💡

    ϕ​S​=ϕM​+ϕLS​

    where ϕLS​ is the leakage flux of the secondary coil

    Faraday’s law for the primary coil,

    VP​(t)=NP​dtdϕ​P​​

    VP​(t)=NP​dtdϕM​​+NP​dtdϕLP​​

    VP​(t)=eP​(t)+eLP​(t)

    Faraday’s law for the secondary coil,

    VS​(t)=NS​dtdϕ​S​​

    VS​(t)=NS​dtdϕM​​+NS​dtdϕLS​​

    VS​(t)=eS​(t)+eLS​(t)

    Note that dtdϕM​​=NP​eP​(t)​=NS​eS​(t)​. Rearranging the equation we get eS​(t)eP​(t)​=NS​NP​​=a.
    The smaller the leakage fluxes, the closer the transformer approximates an ideal one.

    #Exact Equivalent Circuit

    The leakage flux path is through air, which has a much higher reluctance than the core.

    ϕLP​=R1​NP​IP​=PNP​IP​

    ϕLS​=R1​NS​IS​=PNS​IS​

    where P is permeance of the flux path

    Leakage flux of primary and secondary,

    eLP​(t)=NP​dtdϕLP​​=NP​dtd​(PNP​IP​)=NP​2PdtdIP​​=LP​dtdIP​​

    eLS​(t)=NS​dtdϕLS​​=NS​dtd​(PNS​IS​)=NS​2PdtdIS​​=LS​dtdIS​​

    where LP​ is the leakage inductance of the primary coil,
    and     LS​ is the leakage inductance of the secondary coil

    RP​ is resistance of the primary coil. XP​ is reactance of the primary coil due to LP​.

    RS​ is resistance of the secondary coil. XS​ is reactance of the secondary coil due to LS​.

    RC​ and XM​ are the resistance and reactance of losses in the core — excitation branch.

    #Approximate Equivalent Circuit

    The exact circuit is more complex to analyze due to the location of the excitation branch.

    Simplifying the exact circuit:

    • Excitation branch adds another node to the circuit (more complex analysis)
    • Excitation current is typically 2% to 3% of the full-load current
    • Move the excitation branch to the front of the transformer
    • Primary and secondary impedances are then in series
    • Excitation branch may be neglected entirely

    #Open-Circuit Test

    Open-circuit test:

    • High voltage side is set to open-circuit
    • No current flowing through a2RS​ and ja2XS​
    • Measurements are done on the low voltage side
    • RP​ and jXP​ are too small to cause a significant voltage drop
    • All the input current flows through the excitation branch of the transformer

    Total current flow through core resistance and impedance,

    IOC​=IRC​​2+IXM​​2​

    IXM​​=IOC​2−IRC​​2​

    Resistance of the core is given by,

    💡

    RC,LV​=POC​VOC​2​

    Reactance of the core is given by,

    💡

    XM,LV​=IXM​​VOC​​

    Note that the values obtained refer (reflect) to the low voltage side.

    #Short-Circuit Test

    Short-circuit test:

    • Low voltage side is set to short-circuit
    • Measurements are done on the high voltage side
    • Negligible current flows through the excitation branch
    • Voltage drop across the transformer attributed to the series elements

    Equivalent resistance is given by,

    💡

    Req,HV​=ISC​2PSC​​

    Equivalent reactance is given by,

    💡

    Xeq,HV​=Zeq​2−Req​2​

    Note that the values obtained refer (reflect) to the high voltage side.

    #Voltage Regulation

    A real transformer has impedances within it.
    Output voltage varies with the load even if
    input voltage remains constant.

    💡

    VR=Vs,nl​VS,nl​−VS,fl​​×100%

    For an ideal transformer, VR=0.
    No-load voltage     VS,nl​=aVP​​.

    #Transformer Efficiency

    Input and output power of a transformer,

    Pout​=VS​IS​cosθS​

    Pin​=PCu​+Pcore​+Pout​

    Losses in power due to resistance of copper windings, and core losses.

    Transformer efficiency,

    💡

    η=Pin​Pout​​=IS​2Req,S​+Pcore​+VS​IS​cosθS​VS​IS​cosθS​​

    #Autotransformer

    Autotransformer:

    • Windings are connected together electrically at one end
    • Electrical connection compensates for voltage drops that occur
    • Part of the energy is transferred from the primary to secondary by conduction

    Notations:

    • C represents common coil
    • SE represents series coil
    • L represents low-voltage side
    • H represents high-voltage side

    Consider a step-down autotransformer.
    Let the series coil be the primary coil.

    Turns ratio as a two-winding transformer,

    💡

    a=NC​NSE​​=VL​VH​−VL​​

    Turns ratio as an autotransformer,

    💡

    a′=VL​VH​​=VL​(VH​−VL​)+VL​​=a+1

    Apparent power as a two-winding transformer,

    STW​=(VH​−VL​)IH​=VL​(IL​−IH​)

    Apparent power as an autotransformer,

    Sauto​=VH​IH​=VL​IL​

    Sauto​=VH​(VH​−VL​STW​​)

    Sauto​=STW​(VH​VH​−VL​​)−1

    Sauto​=STW​(1−a′1​)−1

    Sauto​>STW​
