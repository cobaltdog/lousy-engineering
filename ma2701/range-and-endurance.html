    MA2701 · FLIGHT PERFORMANCE

    Range and Endurance

    Range is the ground distance an aircraft can travel with a given amount of fuel.
    Endurance is the time duration the aircraft can fly with a given amount of fuel.

    #Definitions

    #Range

    Range is affected by:

    • Engine performance (specific fuel consumption)
    • Aerodynamics of aircraft
    • Capacity of fuel tanks
    • Altitude

    Safe range:

    • Maximum distance between two airports which an aircraft can fly
      on a safe, reliable and regular service with a specified payload
    • Includes takeoff, climb, cruise, descent, landing, wind effects, etc.

    Still air range (SAR):

    • Takeoff with maximum fuel and climb to cruise altitude
    • Cruise until all the fuel has been consumed
    • Cruise distance covered is the still air range

    Gross still air range (GSAR):

    • Begin at selected altitude with full fuel
    • Cruise until all the fuel has been consumed
    • Takeoff and landing segments are ignored
    • Cruise distance covered is the gross still air range

    GSAR is the horizontal straight line ground distance when an aircraft flies under cruise
    conditions and consumes all of its fuel. It is useful in the preliminary design stages of
    an aircraft as it is easier to compute GSAR that safe range.

    #Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC)

    Engine performance of a propeller aircraft is defined by its specific fuel consumption c,
    which is defined as the weight of fuel burnt per second b per unit power produced P.

    💡
    c=Pb​

    SFC is expressed in N/W/s. SFC is also known as power specific fuel consumption.

    For propeller aircraft, SFC is related to TSFC by,

    💡
    c=Vct​ηpr​​

    where ηpr​ is the propeller efficiency

    #Thrust Specific Fuel Consumption (TSFC)

    Engine performance of a jet aircraft is defined by its thrust specific fuel consumption ct​,
    which is defined as the weight of fuel burnt per second b per unit thrust produced T.

    💡
    ct​=Tb​

    TSFC is expressed in N/N/s.

    #Endurance

    Endurance is the time duration the aircraft can fly with a given amount of fuel.
    In other words, the duration the aircraft can stay airborne under cruise flight
    conditions with the given amount of fuel.

    Note that maximum endurance flight does not necessarily equate to maximum range,
    and vice versa. Endurance is typically more beneficial for aircraft used in surveillance,
    surveys, recreational flying, etc.

    For commercial aircraft, endurance becomes critical when an aircraft is required to
    circle around (loiter) in a holding pattern until landing clearance is given by ATC.

    #General Expressions

    Weight definitions:

    • W0​ is the gross weight of the aircraft (full fuel, payload, and crew)
    • W1​ is the weight of the aircraft with empty fuel tanks
    • Wf​ is the instantaneous weight of fuel
    • W is the instantaneous weight of aircraft

    Note that W0​−W1​=Max. Weight of Fuel and W=W1​+Wf​.

    As fuel is consumed, Wf​ decreases, and thus, weight of the aircraft W decreases.

    dtdW​=dtdWf​​=−b

    dtdW​=−ct​T

    For steady, straight level flight, thrust required is equal to drag, and lift is equal to weight.

    dtdW​=−ct​LD​W

    dt=−ct​1​DL​WdW​

    Range is defined as the ground distance covered,

    dR=Vg​ dt=V dt

    R=∫t0​t1​​V dt

    R=∫W0​W1​​ −ct​V​DL​WdW​

    💡
    R=∫W1​W0​​ ct​V​DL​WdW​

    Assuming there is no wind, ground speed is equal to flight speed.

    Endurance is defined as the time duration,

    E=∫t0​t1​​dt

    E=∫W0​W1​​ −ct​1​DL​WdW​

    💡
    E=∫W1​W0​​ ct​1​DL​WdW​

    #Propeller Aircraft

    #Maximum Range

    For a propeller aircraft, it is more convenient to express range in terms of SFC,

    R=∫W1​W0​​ ct​V​DL​WdW​

    R=∫W1​W0​​ cηpr​​DL​WdW​

    R=cηpr​​DL​ lnW1​W0​​

    To maximize range of the aircraft:

    • Maximize propeller efficiency ηpr​
    • Minimize specific fuel consumption c
    • Maximize lift-to-drag ratio, which is achieved by flying at VDmin​​
    • Maximize fuel capacity, which is achieved by maximizing the ratio W1​W0​​

    💡
    VRmax​​=VDmin​​=ρS2W​CD0​​k​​​
    💡
    Rmax​=cηpr​​2kCD0​​​1​ lnW1​W0​​

    Maximum range can be achieved through constant speed cruising or constant altitude cruising. To maintain a constant VRmax​​, since W decreases continuously, the aircraft
    needs to gain altitude such that     ρ will also decrease.

    To maintain a constant altitude, and thus constant ρ, the aircraft’s maximum range
    speed will decrease as     W decreases continuously. However, this increases the flight
    time to the destination.

    #Maximum Endurance

    For a propeller aircraft, it is more convenient to express endurance in terms of SFC,

    E=∫W1​W0​​ ct​1​DL​WdW​

    E=∫W1​W0​​ cVηpr​​DL​WdW​

    E=∫W1​W0​​ cηpr​​2ρS​​CD​CL​3/2​W3/2dW​

    E=cηpr​​2ρS​CD​CL​3/2​(W1​​1​−W0​​1​)

    To maximize endurance of the aircraft:

    • Maximize propeller efficiency ηpr​
    • Minimize specific fuel consumption c
    • Maximize CD​CL​3/2​ ratio, which is achieved by flying at VPmin​​
    • Maximize air density ρ, which is achieved by flying at sea level
    • Maximize fuel capacity, which is achieved by maximizing W1​​1​−W0​​1​

    💡
    CD​CL​3/2​max​=41​(kCD0​​1/33​)43​
    💡
    VEmax​​=VPmin​​=ρS2W​3CD0​​k​​​
    💡
    Emax​=cηpr​​2ρS​ CD​CL​3/2​max​(W1​​1​−W0​​1​)

    For maximum endurance, a propeller aircraft has to fly at sea level at flight speed VEmax​​.

    #Graphical Method

    Range is maximum when the least fuel is consumed per unit distance.

    hrFuel Consumed​=ηpr​SFC​×PR​

    hr×VFuel Consumed​=ηpr​SFC​×VPR​​

    DistanceFuel Consumed​=ηpr​SFC​×VPR​​

    DistanceFuel Consumed​=ηpr​SFC​×TR​

    Thus, maximum range is given by minimum VPR​​, or the minimum drag Dmin​.
    The point that gives the maximum range can be found be drawing a tangent
    to the power required curve from the origin.

    Note that PR​=TR​V=DV.

    Endurance is maximum when the least fuel is consumed per unit time.

    hrFuel Consumed​=SFC×Engine HP

    hrFuel Consumed​=SFC×ηpr​Propeller HP​

    hrFuel Consumed​=SFC×ηpr​PR​​

    Thus, maximum endurance is given by minimum PR​.

    #Jet Aircraft

    #Maximum Range

    For a jet aircraft,

    R=∫W1​W0​​ ct​V​DL​WdW​

    R=∫W1​W0​​ ct​1​(DVL​)WdW​

    R=∫W1​W0​​ ct​1​ρS2W​​CD​CL​​​WdW​

    DVL​=VCD​CL​​

    DVL​=ρSCL​2L​​CD​CL​​

    DVL​=ρS2W​​CD​CL​​​

    Integrating the above equation,

    R=∫W1​W0​​ ct​1​ρS2​​CD​CL​​​W​dW​

    R=ct​2​ρS2​​CD​CL​​​(W0​​−W1​​)

    To maximize range of the aircraft:

    • Minimize thrust specific fuel consumption ct​
    • Minimize air density ρ, which is achieved by flying at highest possible altitude
    • Maximize CD​CL​​​ ratio, which is achieved by flying at speed that maximizes it
    • Maximize fuel capacity, which is achieved by maximizing W0​​−W1​​

    💡
    CD​CL​​​max​=43​(3kCD0​​31​)41​
    💡
    VRmax​​=VCL​​/CD​max​​=ρS2W​CD0​​3k​​​
    💡
    Rmax​=ct​2​ρS2​​CD​CL​​​max​(W0​​−W1​​)

    Maximum range can be achieved by flying at highest possible altitude — service ceiling,
    as permitted by the initial weight of the aircraft. However, to maintain maximum range,
    the aircraft needs to fly at a higher altitude, where air density     ρ is lower, to compensate
    for the decrease in     W. Thus, pilots often request for a change in altitude, climbing to
    higher altitudes in steps — “step climb”.

    #Maximum Endurance

    For a jet aircraft,

    E=∫W1​W0​​ ct​1​DL​WdW​

    E=ct​1​DL​ lnW1​W0​​

    To maximize endurance of the aircraft:

    • Minimize thrust specific fuel consumption ct​
    • Maximize lift-to-drag ratio, which is achieved by flying at VDmin​​
    • Maximize fuel capacity, which is achieved by maximizing the ratio W1​W0​​

    💡
    VEmax​​=VDmin​​=ρS2W​CD0​​k​​​
    💡
    Emax​=2ct​kCD0​​​1​ lnW1​W0​​

    For maximum endurance, a jet aircraft can fly at any altitude but at flight speed VEmax​​.

    #Graphical Method

    Range is maximum when the least fuel is consumed per unit distance.

    hrFuel Consumed​=TSFC×Drag

    hr×VFuel Consumed​=TSFC×VD​

    DistanceFuel Consumed​=TSFC×VD​

    Thus, maximum range is given by minimum VD​. The point that gives the maximum
    range can be found be drawing a tangent to the drag curve from the origin.

    Endurance is maximum when the least fuel is consumed per unit time.

    hrFuel Consumed​=TSFC×Engine Thrust

    hrFuel Consumed​=TSFC×Drag

    Thus, maximum endurance is given by minimum drag.

    #Effect of Wind

    Recall the definitions of range and endurance, R=∫t0​t1​​Vg​ dt and E=∫t0​t1​​dt.
    Since range is an integral of ground speed Vg​, it is affected by wind.
    On the other hand, endurance is not affected by wind.

    Rprop​=∫W1​W0​​ cηpr​​VVg​​DL​WdW​

    Rprop​=∫W1​W0​​ cηpr​​VV±Vwind​​DL​WdW​

    Rprop​=∫W1​W0​​ cηpr​​DL​WdW​±∫W1​W0​​ cηpr​​VVwind​​DL​WdW​

    Subtraction if headwind, addition if tailwind.

    Rjet​=∫W1​W0​​ ct​Vg​​DL​WdW​

    Rjet​=∫W1​W0​​ ct​V±Vwind​​DL​WdW​

    Rjet​=∫W1​W0​​ ct​V​DL​WdW​±∫W1​W0​​ ct​Vwind​​DL​WdW​

    Subtraction if headwind, addition if tailwind.

    Speed at which range is maximum is obtained by shifting the origin along the positive x-axis for a headwind, and along the negative x-axis for a tailwind. Drawing a tangent to the power required or drag curve from the new origin gives the the maximum range speed.
