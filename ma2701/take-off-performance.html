    MA2701 · FLIGHT PERFORMANCE

    Take-Off Performance

    #Take-Off Sequence

    Sequence of events:

    • Parking brake is released and full throttle is applied (maximum thrust)
    • Aircraft begins rolling forward with the maximum acceleration
    • Aircraft experiences resistance to motion — rolling resistance and drag
    • As speed increases, rolling resistance decreases while drag increases
    • Net thrust available for acceleration is thus constant during takeoff
    • Once liftoff speed is achieved, the aircraft will lift off safely from the ground

    For takeoff ground run, either zero flaps or partial flaps are deployed as recommended by
    the flight manual. Since flaps increase both     CL​ and CD​, there is an optimal value of flap deflection that will minimize the ground run.

    The takeoff distance is the sum of the ground run distance sg​ and the airborne distance sa​.
    The airborne distance is the additional ground distance that will be covered after the aircraft
    is airborne until it clears an obstacle of specified height.

    #Definition of the Various Speeds

    #Stall Speed

    • Minimum speed at which aircraft generates barely enough lift for flight
    • Flight at this speed will be unstable and will have poor climb rate
    • Practical liftoff speed is higher than Vstall​

    #Minimum Control Speed

    Description of control speed on the ground Vmcg​:

    • Minimum speed required for rudder to be effective in maintaining aircraft heading
    • In the event of a single engine failure on the runway, the rudder can be used to correct for the yawing caused by the lack of thrust from one of the engines

    Description of control speed in the air Vmca​:

    • Minimum speed required for rudder to be effective in maintaining aircraft heading
    • In the event of a single engine failure in the air, the rudder can be used to correct for the yawing caused by the lack of thrust from one of the engines

    #Decision Speed

    • If engine failure occurs after decision speed V1​, takeoff is aborted
    • Insufficient length of remaining runway to accelerate the aircraft to safety speed
    • If engine failure occurs after decision speed V1​, takeoff is to continue
    • Insufficient length of remaining runway to decelerate the aircraft to a full stop

    #Rotation Speed

    • Speed at which the pilot initiates rotation of the aircraft by deflecting the elevator
    • Rotation increases pitch attitude, which in turn increases the wing angle of attack
    • Wing is able to generate the lift necessary for takeoff

    #Minimum Unstick Speed

    • Minimum speed at which an aircraft can lift off with a reduced angle of attack
    • Maximum permissible rotation of aircraft is limited by ground clearance of the tail
    • Highest possible angle of attack is thus lower than that of takeoff configuration
    • Sufficient lift may not be generated for takeoff at rotation speed
    • Aircraft must accelerate further to a higher speed

    #Liftoff Speed

    • Typically 1.1 times stall speed
    • Liftoff speed is slightly higher than minimum unstick speed Vmu​
    • Additional lift generated, more than requried, acts as a safety margin

    #Takoff Safety Speed

    • Typically 1.2 times stall speed
    • Climb speed aircraft must attain in order to clear an
      obstacle of specified height with one engine failure

    #Equations of Motion

    Assume that there is no wind, thus airspeed V is equal to ground speed Vg​.

    Net normal reaction is given by,

    N=W−L

    Rolling resistance of the aircraft is,

    R=μr​N=μr​(W−L)

    Using Newton’s second law along the runway,

    💡
    gW​dtdVg​​=T−D−μr​(W−L)

    Acceleration of the aircraft is then,

    💡
    a=Wg[T−D−μr​(W−L)]​

    #Ground Run sg​

    Ground run consists of:

    • Ground acceleration distance sga​
    • Ground rotation distance sgr​

    Ground acceleration distance can be determined using the equation,

    a ds=V dV

    dsga​=aVg​ dVg​​

    sga​=∫0VLO​​aVg​ dVg​​

    sga​=21​∫0VLO​​ad(Vg​2)​

    Substituting the acceleration equation into the above,

    💡
    sg​=2gW​∫0VLO​​T−D−μr​(W−L)d(Vg​2)​

    #Approach 1

    Expression for thrust:

    • Turbojet engine — T remains relatively constant with V
    • Turbofan engine — T decreases slightly with V
    • Propeller — P remains relatively constant with V, thus T=VP​

      Thrust of a propeller aircraft decreases with increase in speed.

    Lift can be computed using the lift equation. If CL​ for ground run is not given,

    💡
    CL​=2kμr​​

    CD​ needs to be corrected for additional drag of the landing gear as well as ground effect, which reduces the induced drag.

    The corrected profile drag CD0​​ is,

    CD0​​=C~D0​​+ΔCD0​​

    💡
    CD0​​=C~D0​​+SW​Kuc​m−0.215

    where Kuc​=5.81×10−5 for zero flap deflection (for takoff) and
    Kuc​=3.16×10−5 for maximum flap deflection (for landing) and
    C~D0​​ is the uncorrected profile drag coefficient

    The total drag CD​ is then,

    CD​=CD0​​+kCL​2

    CD​=CD0​​+ϕk~CL​2

    💡
    CD​=CD0​​+[1+(16h/b)2(16h/b)2​]k~CL​2

    where ϕ is the ground effect factor and k~ is the uncorrected Oswald efficiency factor

    The ground acceleration distance is then,

    sga​=2gW​∫0VLO​​T−D−μr​(W−L)d(Vg​2)​

    sga​=2g1​∫0VLO​​(T/W)−(D/W)−μr​(1−L/W)d(Vg​2)​

    sga​=2g1​∫0VLO​​WT​−μr​−21​ρV2S(WCD​−μr​CL​​)d(Vg​2)​

    sga​=2g1​∫0VLO​​KT​+KA​V2d(Vg​2)​

    sga​=2gKA​1​∫0VLO​​u1​ du

    sga​=2gKA​1​ln(1+KT​KA​VLO​2​)

    Rotation typically takes 3s for large aircraft and 1s for small aircraft.
    Thus, the ground rotation distance can be approximated as     NVLO​.

    Total ground run distance is,

    💡
    sg​=2gKA​1​ln(1+KT​KA​VLO​2​)+NVLO​
    💡
    KA​=−2(W/S)ρ​(CD​−μr​CL​)
    💡
    KT​=WT​−μr​

    where N=3 for large aircraft and N=1 for small aircraft, and VLO​=1.1Vstall​

    #Approach 2

    From the ground acceleration distance equation, the denominator is the net thrust acting on the aircraft during the ground run. Weight of the aircraft is assumed to be constant.

    Since resistance to motion is relatively constant, the net thrust can be assumed to be
    a constant. Net thrust will then be evaluated at the effective speed of the ground run.

    💡
    Veff​=2​VLO​​=0.707VLO​

    Thus, the denominator is treated as a constant and the integration is simplified.

    sg​=2gW​T−D−μr​(W−L)1​∫0VLO​​d(Vg​2)+NVLO​

    💡
    sg​=2gWVLO​2​[T−D−μr​(W−L)1​]Veff​​+NVLO​

    whereby thrust, drag, and lift are evaluated at the effective speed Veff​

    Substituting VLO​=1.1Vstall​ into the above,

    sg​=gρCLmax​​[WT​−WD​−μr​(1−WL​)]Veff​​1.21(W/S)​+1.1NρSCLmax​​2W​​

    Assuming T≫D+μr​(W−L) and ignoring ground rotation distance,

    sg​≈gρCLmax​​(T/W)1.21(W/S)​=gρCLmax​​ST1.21W2​

    Observations of the above equation:

    • Ground run increases as wing loading SW​ increases
    • Ground run increases as air density ρ decreases
    • Ground run decreases as CLmax​​ increases
    • Gournd run decreases as WT​ increases
    • sg​∝W2

    #Flare and Climb sa​

    The airborne distance is the distance covered until the aircraft clears a specified height.

    Specified height for commercial aircraft is 35ft and for military aircraft is 50ft.

    Flight path during flare and climb is modeled as
    a pull-up maneuver with a uniform radius R and constant speed V.

    Recall from pull-up maneuver that radius is,

    💡
    R=g(n−1)V2​

    During the climb and flare, flight speed changes from VLO​ to V2​.
    Assume that velocity during this phase is the average of the two.

    💡
    V=1.15Vstall​

    Also, during this phase, CL​ is slightly less than CLmax​​ to maintain a safety margin.

    💡
    CL​=0.9CLmax​​

    Lift during the pull-up maneuver is then,

    L=nW=21​ρ(1.15Vstall​)2S(0.9CLmax​​)

    Lift of the aircraft during a level flight is,

    L=W=21​ρVstall​2SCLmax​​

    Load factor during the pull-up maneuver is,

    n=21​ρVstall​2SCLmax​​21​ρ(1.15Vstall​)2S(0.9CLmax​​)​

    💡
    n=1.19

    Substituting V and n into R,

    💡
    R=g6.96Vstall​2​

    Using Pythagoras theorem, the airborne distance is,

    sa​=R2−(R−hob​)2​

    sa​=R2−(R2−2Rhob​+hob​2)​

    💡
    sa​=2Rhob​−hob​2​

    #Balanced Field Length

    Suppose an engine failure occurs during the ground run and the pilot decides to abort. The accelerate-stop distance is the total distance it takes for the aircraft to begin its ground run, lose an engine, and bring the aircraft to a stop.

    Suppose the pilot decides to continue the takeoff instead. The accelerate-go distance is
    the total distance to accelerate from a standstill, lose an engine, and continue to takeoff
    and clear the specified height at the takeoff safety speed V2​.

    The balanced field length is the distance in which the accelerate-stop and accelerate-go distances are the same. That is, if an engine failure occurs at decision speed V1​, the pilot
    can choose to abort the takeoff or continue with the takeoff.

    Speed at which the distances are the same is known as the decision speed V1​.

    • Engine failue occurs before V1​, takeoff must be aborted
    • Insufficient runway to accelerate the aircraft to V2​
    • Engine failure occurs after V1​, takeoff must continue
    • Insufficient runway to decelerate the aircraft to a stop
    • Engine failure occurs at V1​, takeoff is either aborted or continued

    Computing balanced field length:

    • Assume a value for decision speed V1​ and determine distance D1​ required to attain V1​
    • Determine distance D2​ required for aircraft to clear specified height at V2​ (continue)
    • Determine distance D3​ required for aircraft to decelerate to a stop (abort)
    • Adjust V1​ accordingly until D2​=D3​
    • Balanced field length is D1​+D2​/D3​

    Note that the corresponding value of V1​ is the decision speed for the aircraft. The balanced
    field length is essentially the required runway length such that in the event of an engine
    failure, there is sufficient runway to either abort or continue the takeoff.

    #Effect of Wind

    #Effect of Steady Headwind

    Recall that a headwind decreases the ground speed of the aircraft. Since the ground acceleration distance is determined by sga​=2gW​∫T−D−μr​(W−L)d(Vg​2)​, the upper limit
    of the integral becomes a smaller value, which in turn gives a shorter ground run.

    #Effect of Steady Tailwind

    Recall that a tailwind increases the ground speed of the aircraft. Thus, the upper limit
    of the integral becomes a larger value, which in turn gives a longer ground run.
