    Glide Performance

    #Gliders

    Characteristics of gliders:

    • High aerodynamic efficiency (lift-to-drag ratio)
    • Quiet aircraft as there is no engine
    • Weight of glider is constant

    Performance of glide flight:

    • Endurance — duration of time the glider remains airborne
    • Range — ground distance covered by the glider

    Glider launching methods:

    • Air tow
    • Ground tow
    • Winch tow

    Description of air tow:

    • A towplane is used to tow the glider until the desired launch altitude is achieved
    • Glider is in the high/low two position to avoid the wake of the towplane

    Description of ground two:

    • A vehicle is used to tow the glider to the required altitude
    • Correct tow speed is typically determined through trial and error

    Description of winch tow:

    • A winch is used to tow the glider to the required altitude
    • Winch is powered by an engine that pulls the cable connected to the glider

    #Equilibirium Equations

    Terminologies for glide flight:

    • γ is the flight path (glide) angle
    • α is the angle of attack
    • θ is the pitch angle

    Resolving forces with respect to the flight path,

    L=Wcosγ

    D=Wsinγ

    Using small angle approximation for γ,

    tanγ=LD​

    💡
    γ=WD​

    For a small glide angle, lift is approximately equal to weight.

    For maximum range, γ must be a minimum.
    Minimum γ is acheived when WD​ is a minimum.

    #Hodograph for Glide Performance

    A hodograph plots an aircraft’s vertical speed Vvert​ against its horizontal speed Vhori​ for
    all possible glide flights. It also gives the direction and angle of the flight velocity vector
    (an arrow starting from origin to a point on the curve).

    Description of hodograph:

    • Maximum point on the curve gives the minimum sink rate, and thus maximum endurance
    • Tangent line of the curve gives the minimum glide angle, and thus maximum range
    • Angle that the flight velocity vector makes with the horizontal axis is the glide angle
