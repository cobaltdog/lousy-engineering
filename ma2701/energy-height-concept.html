    MA2701 · FLIGHT PERFORMANCE

    Energy Height Concept

    #Energy Height

    Excess thrust can be utilized for performing:

    • Steady climb — potential energy increases, while kinetic energy decreases
    • Level acceleration — potential energy is constant, while kinetic energy increases
    • Accelerated climb — both potential and kinetic energy increases

    Consider an aircraft of mass m at some altitude h moving with a velocity V.
    The total energy of the aircraft is the sum of its potential and kinetic energies.

    Total Energy=mgh+21​mV2

    Specific energy, or energy height, is defined as total energy per unit weight.

    💡
    He​=h+2gV2​

    Note that energy height has units of height.

    Energy height can be interpreted as the maximum altitude the aircraft is able to attain when all of its kinetic energy has been converted into potential energy.

    An aircraft travelling along the same
    energy height from B to A is said to perform a zoom climb. The reverse is
    called a dive.

    #Specific Excess Power

    The energy height of an aircraft can be increased by utilizing excess power, PA​−PR​.

    Applying Newton’s second law along the flight path,

    Tcosα−D−Wsinγ=gW​dtdV​

    Assuming small angles of attack, cosα≈1,

    T−D=W(sinγ+g1​dtdV​)

    WTV−DV​=V(sinγ+g1​dtdV​)

    SEP=Vsinγ+gV​dtdV​

    💡
    Ps​=dtdh​+gV​dtdV​

    where V is the instantaneous flight speed,
    and     dh/dt is the the instantaneous rate of climb,
    and     dV/dt is the instantaneous acceleration

    From the above equation, SEP can be utilized in three ways:

    • Steady climb, where Ps​=dtdh​
    • Level acceleration, where Ps​=gV​dtdV​
    • Accelerated climb, where Ps​=dtdh​+gV​dtdV​

    The specific excess power equation can also be written in terms of energy height.

    Ps​=dtdh​+2g1​dtd(V2)​

    Ps​=dtd(h+V2/2g)​

    💡
    Ps​=dtdHe​​

    The rate of change of energy height of an aircraft is equal to specific excess power. The time required to climb from one energy height to another can be obtained through integration.

    #Altitude Climb (Subsonic)

    At any given altitude, the maximum rate of altitude climb is possible at the point
    where Ps​ is maximum (tangential to Ps​ curve). Thus, by flying at the flight speed
    at the point of maximum Ps​, the pilot can minimize climb time.

    Note that the line segments joining the points tangential to the Ps​ curves for each
    altitude gives the best strategy to minimize the climb time in an altitude climb.

    #Energy Climb (Subsonic)

    At any given energy height, the maximum rate of energy climb is possible at the point
    where Ps​ is maximum (energy height curve is tangential to Ps​ curve). Thus, by flying
    at the flight speed at the point of maximum Ps​, the pilot can minimize climb time.

    Note that at different altitudes, the energy height level curves is tangent to the Ps​ curves
    at a different flight speed. Thus, for a pilot to climb from one energy height to another,
    flight speed of the aircraft needs to be adjusted between each level curve.

    #Energy Climb (Supersonic)

    The ability to reach supersonic speeds and high altitudes in the shortest possible time gives
    a fighter aircraft significant tactical advantage and air superiority. In the past, fighter aircraft
    had limited excess power and was unable to break the sound barrier.

    Energy climb technique to break sound barrier:

    • Level acceleration from A to B in the subsonic regime
    • Energy climb from B to C in the transonic regime (limited by maximum Ps​)
    • Dive along constant energy height from C to D to cross transonic region
    • Supersonic climb from D to E to desired energy height
