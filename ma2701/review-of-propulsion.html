    Review of Propulsion

    #Gas Turbine Engines

    Any engine that uses atmopheric air as the working fluid is an air-breathing engine.

    Engine spools:

    • A spool consists of a compressor and a turbine assembly
    • Each spool is mechanically independent; rotates at its own optimal speed
    • Categorized into low pressure, intermediate pressure, and high pressure spools

    Thrust producing engines:

    • Turbojet
    • Turbofan
    • Ram/scram jet

    Torque producing engines:

    • Turboprop
    • Turboshaft

    #Turbojet

    Characteristics of turbojet engine:

    • Accelerates a small mass of air in the axial direction to very high velocities
    • Thrust produced solely by acceleration of high pressure combustion products
    • Thrust developed is dependent on the exhaust nozzle area and fuel burn rate
    • All intake air passes through the compressor, combustor, and turbine

    AdvantagesDisadvantages
    High specific thrust (thrust to weight ratio)High thrust specific fuel consumption (HSFC)
    Small frontal area for better ground clearanceLimited in range and endurance
    Extremely loud

    Thrust specific fuel consumption is dependent on altitude and airspeed. It is highest
    at low altitudes and airspeeds but decreases when altitude and airspeed increase.

    #Turbofan

    Characteristics of turbofan engine:

    • Additional fan located before the low pressure compressor
    • Intake air is split between the engine core and bypass duct (bypass air)
    • Accelerates a large mass of air to relatively high velocities; slow, more
      dense bypass air mixes with the jet flow at the nozzle to produce thrust

    AdvantagesDisadvantages
    Better fuel efficiency compared to turbojetLarger frontal area
    Considerable reduction in noise levels

    #Turboprop

    Characteristics of turboprop:

    • Thrust mainly developed by the propeller with the exhaust producing some thrust
    • Propeller produces thrust by giving a very small acceleration to a large mass of air
    • Propeller is driven by its own turbine through a reduction gearbox

    AdvantagesDisadvantages
    Very high fuel efficiencyLarge frontal area due to long props
    Low thrust specific fuel consumptionNot suitable for high altitude flights

    #Ram Jet and Scram Jet

    Characteristics of ramjet:

    • Compression of air is achieved through ramming of air at high speeds
    • Simplest type of engine to operate due to the lack of moving components
    • Require relatively high speeds for optimal operation; most efficient at supersonic
    • Combustion occurs at subsonic speeds (incoming supersonic air is slowed down)
    • Efficiency drops with speed as inlet air temperature increases due to compression

    A ramjet can theoretically be started at low speeds but does not produce any significant
    thrust until airspeed reaches approximately     M0.5. Peak efficiency is only attained at supersonic speeds of M3. Its use is limited up to a maximum speed of M6 due to the shockwave induced pressure loss that occurs when slowing the intake air to subsonic.

    Characteristics of scramjet:

    • Variant of the ramjet — supersonic combustion ramjet
    • Combustion occurs under supersonic flow conditions
    • Airflow remains supersonic throughout the engine
    • Capable of operating above the M6 limiting speed of the ramjet

    #Fuel Consumption

    Thrust specific fuel consumption ct​ is defined as the weight of fuel consumed per unit thrust per unit time, expressed in terms of kg/kN/h. However, we shall use N/N/s.

    Power specific fuel consumption c is defined as the weight of fuel consumed per unit shaft power per unit time, expressed in terms of kg/kW/h. However, we shall use N/W/s.

    For propeller aircraft, the relationship between c and ct​ is,

    💡
    ct​=ηpr​cV​

    where V is the aircraft flight speed
