    MA2701 · FLIGHT PERFORMANCE

    Landing Performance

    #Landing Sequence

    Phases of landing:

    • Airborne phase — approach and flare (begins 50ft above ground and ends at touchdown)
    • Ground phase — free roll and braked roll (begins at touchdown and ends at the stop)
    • Landing distance is the sum of the airborne and ground run distances

    Approach segment:

    • Pilot performs landing checklist before beginning approach
    • Engine throttle is reduced and flaps are deployed in stages
    • Landing gear is extended

    Flaps allow for a lower approach speed Va​ without a decrease in lift as flaps increase the
    camber and thus, lift coefficient of the wing. Additionally, flaps increase induced drag
    which allows for a steeper descent angle without a corresponding increase in speed.

    Final approach:

    • Approach speed and descent rate is stabilized, aircraft is aligned with runway centerline
    • Va​=1.3Vstall​ for commercial aircraft, and Va​=1.2Vstall​ for military aircraft
    • Descent angle is typically maintained at 3°
    • Pitch attitude is typically 3°≤θ≤5°

    Flare segment:

    • Transition phase between final approach and touchdown
    • Flare is initiated at a height hf​ as recommended in flight manual
    • Pitch attitude is increased to 8°≤θ≤10° (maximum pitch angle limited by tail strike)
    • Engine power is reduced to a minimum (idle setting)
    • VTD​=1.15Vstall​ for commercial aircraft, and VTD​=1.1Vstall​ for military aircraft

    Due to the increased pitch, the main wheels touches down first. Flaring reduces the aircraft’s vertical speed (descent rate) at touchdown as close to zero as possible. The horizontal speed at touchdown is the touchdown speed. If the flare is not performed properly, the vertical speed at touchdown will be high, resulting in a hard landing.

    Free roll segment:

    • Aircraft rolls freely without any deceleration
    • Pilot performs a de-rotation to reduce the pitch angle to zero for nose gear touchdown
    • Speed is assumed to be constant and equal to VTD​ during the free roll

    De-rotation must be done without delay after touchdown before the elevator loses its
    effectiveness as speed decreases. This reduces the vertical speed of the nose gear and
    prevents the nose gear from slamming into the runway.

    Braked roll segment:

    • Deployment of thrust reversers, spoilers, and use of brakes to decelerate aircraft
    • Kinetic energy of the aircraft is dissipated

    Wheel brakes are applied after the aircraft’s speed is reduced to some extent by the thrust reversers as braking force is proportional to the ground normal reaction, which increases
    as lift reduces.

    #Landing Distance

    The governing equations for the different segments of landing are as shown.

    #Approach Distance

    Normal to flight path:

    Along the flight path:

    L+Tsin(θ+γa​)=Wcosγa​

    Tcos(θ+γa​)+Wsinγa​=D

    Since descent angle γa​=3° for commercial aircraft, and pitch attitude during approach
    is 3°≤θ≤5°, small angle approximation is valid. Furthermore, T is small for landing. Thus, the term Tsin(θ+γa​) is negligible.

    The equations become,

    L=W

    D=T+Wγa​

    💡
    γa​=WD−T​=L/D1​−WT​

    Note that if thrust is zero, the descent angle becomes the glide angle.

    Approach distance is then,

    💡
    sa​=tanγa​15.2−hf​​

    #Flare Distance

    Assuming a constant flare speed Vf​:

    • Vf​=1.225Vstall​ for commercial aircraft
    • Vf​=1.15Vstall​ for military aircraft
    • Flare load factor n=1.2

    Note that speed during the flare varies from approach speed at the start of the flare to touchdown speed.

    Recall from pull-up maneuver that radius is,

    R=g(n−1)V2​

    💡
    R=0.2gVf​2​

    The flare distance is then,

    💡
    sf​=Rsinγf​

    Note that γf​=γa​.

    If the flare height is required,

    💡
    hf​=R(1−cosγf​)

    #Free Roll Distance

    Speed during the free roll is assumed to be constant and equal to VTD​.
    Free roll typically occurs over a time period of 1s to 3s.

    💡
    sfr​=NVTD​

    #Braked Roll Distance

    Similar to the equation for takeoff,

    gW​dtdVg​​=−Trev​−D−μr​(W−L)

    a=Wg[−Trev​−D−μr​(W−L)]​

    Using the equation a ds=V dV,

    💡
    sbr​=2gW​∫0VTD​​Trev​+D+μr​(W−L)d(Vg​2)​

    The total resistance to motion is relatively constant. Reverse thrust is also a constant.
    Thus, the denominator is a constant and the integral can be simplified.

    Effective speed during the braked roll is,

    💡
    Veff​=0.707VTD​

    Braked roll distance is then,

    💡
    sbr​=2gWVTD​2​[Trev​+D+μr​(W−L)1​]Veff​​

    whereby reverse thrust, drag, and lift are evaluated at the effective speed Veff​

    Substituting VTD​=1.15Vstall​ into the above,

    sbr​=gρCLmax​​[WTrev​​+WD​+μr​(1−WL​)]Veff​​1.3225(W/S)​

    Observations of the above equation:

    • Braked roll distance increases as wing loading SW​ increases
    • Braked roll distance increases as air density ρ decreases
    • Braked roll distance decreases as CLmax​​ increases
    • Braked roll distance decreases as WTrev​​ increases

    #Effect of Wind

    #Effect of Steady Headwind

    Recall that a headwind decreases the ground speed of the aircraft. Since the ground acceleration distance is determined by sga​=2gW​∫Trev​+D+μr​(W−L)d(Vg​2)​, the upper limit
    of the integral becomes a smaller value, which in turn gives a shorter ground run.

    #Effect of Steady Tailwind

    Recall that a tailwind increases the ground speed of the aircraft. Thus, the upper limit
    of the integral becomes a larger value, which in turn gives a longer ground run.
