    MA2701 · FLIGHT PERFORMANCE

    Climb Performance

    #Equilibrium Equations

    Terminologies for steady climb flight:

    • FRL is the fuselage reference line
    • γ is the flight path (climb) angle
    • α is the angle of attack
    • θ is the pitch angle

    Note that lift is perpendicular to the flight path and drag is parallel to the flight path.

    Thrust is parallel to the fuselage reference line, instead of the flight path, since the engines are mounted on the fuselage.

    Resolving forces with respect to the flight path,

    L+Tsinα−Wcosγ=0

    Tcosα−D−Wsinγ=0

    Using small angle approximation for α,

    💡
    L=Wcosγ
    💡
    T=D+Wsinγ

    For climb flight, a portion of the weight is supported by the thrust. Thus, less lift is
    required for climb flight than for level flight, which in turn reduces induced drag.

    From the diagram,

    💡
    R/C=Vsinγ

    #Climb Rate and Climb Angle

    From the equilibrium equations,

    sinγ=WT−D​

    Substituting into the climb rate equation,

    💡
    R/C=WV(T−D)​=WTV−DV​

    The climb angle is then,

    💡
    γ=sin−1WT−D​

    From the equilibrium equations, the load factor for climb flight is,

    💡
    n=cosγ

    The above equation shows that load factor for climb flight is less than 1.

    #Maximum Climb Rate (Graphical)

    Since PA​=TA​V and PR​=DV,

    💡
    SEP=R/C=WPA​−PR​​

    where SEP represents specific excess power expressed in W/N

    The climb angle is then,

    💡
    γ=sin−1VSEP​

    To determine the maximum climb rate:

    • Calculate Vmin​ and Vmax​ for the chosen altitude
    • Calculate PA​ and PR​ for the range Vmin​≤V≤Vmax​
    • Calculate SEP for the range Vmin​≤V≤Vmax​
    • Plot SEP against flight speed V

    #Jet Aircraft

    To calculate power available PA​,

    PA​=TA​V

    PA​=(T0​σm)V

    To calculate power required PR​,

    PR​=TR​V

    PR​=DV

    PR​=(AV2+V2B​)V

    From the diagram, it can be seen that SEP is a parabolic curve.
    Thus, highest point on the curve gives the maximum climb rate.
    The climb speed that gives maximum climb rate is 600 km/h.

    Note that SEP is multiplied by a scale factor for readability.

    From climb angle equation, γmax​ is achieved when VSEP​ is maximum,
    which is obtained from the tangent line that cuts through the origin.
    The climb speed that gives the maximum climb angle is 460 km/h.

    Note that the climb speed for maximum climb rate and climb angle are different.

    SEP curves at different altitudes:

    • Maximum climb rate decreases with altitude
    • Maximum climb angle decreases with altitude
    • Speed that gives SEPmax​ and γmax​ increases with altitude

    As altitude increases, density, and thus thrust available, decreases. This results
    in a decrease in excess power which in turn reduces the maximum climb rate.

    Note that when there is deficit power, PA​<PR​, climb rate is negative, which
    indicates that the aircraft is descending instead of climbing. The curve (yellow)
    tangential to the horizontal axis occurs when aircraft is at its absolute ceiling.

    #Propeller Aircraft

    For a propeller aircraft, PA​ is a constant with respect to flight speed.
    Power required PR​ is dependent on the total drag of the aircraft.

    To calculate power available PA​,

    PA​=ηpr​(P0​σm)

    where ηpr​ is the propeller efficiency

    #Maximum Climb Rate (Analytical)

    Lift coefficient for climb flight is,

    L=21​ρV2SCL​

    Wcosγ=21​ρV2SCL​

    CL​=21​ρV2SWcosγ​

    Drag coefficient for climb flight is,

    CD​=CD0​​+kCL​2

    CD​=CD0​​+k(21​ρV2SWcosγ​)2

    Total drag for climb flight is,

    D=21​ρV2SCD​

    D=21​ρV2SCD0​​+21​ρV2SkW2cos2γ​

    Substituting into the climb rate equation,

    Vsinγ=V(WT−D​)

    Vsinγ=WV​(T−21​ρV2SCD0​​−21​ρV2SkW2cos2γ​)

    Vsinγ=V[WT​−21​W/SρV2CD0​​​−SW​(ρV22kcos2γ​)]

    💡
    R/C=Vsinγ=V[WT​−21​W/SρV2CD0​​​−SW​(ρV22k​)]

    CD0​​ term is dependent on parasite drag while k term is dependent on induced drag.
    Since climb angles for commercial aircraft are usually small,     cos2γ≈1.

    Effects on climb rate:

    • Increases as thrust-to-weight ratio increases
    • Decreases as drag characteristics CD0​​ and k increase
    • Decreases as altitude increases, due to decrease in density, and thus thrust
    • At low V, decreases as wing loading increases
    • At high V, increases as wing loading increases

    At low V, to generate the same lift, the aircraft needs to fly at a higher angle of attack.
    Thus,     CL​ is higher, which increases induced drag. The induced drag term in the climb
    rate equation becomes the dominant term, and decreases the climb rate.

    At high V, the aircraft is able to fly at a lower angle of attack. Thus, CL​ is lower, and
    parasite drag becomes the dominant term. As wing loading increases, the parasite
    drag term in the equation decreases, which in turn increases climb rate.

    #Jet Aircraft

    For maximum rate of climb, differentiate climb rate equation with respect to V.

    dVd​(R/C)=WT​−23​W/SρV2CD0​​​+SW​(ρV22k​)

    0=WT​−23​W/SρV2CD0​​​+SW​(ρV22k​)

    0=−2(W/S)3ρCD0​​​V4+WT​V2+ρS2kW​

    Solving the quadratic equation,

    💡
    VR/Cmax​​=3ρCD0​​Z(WT​)(SW​)​​
    💡
    Z=1+1+(DL​max​)2(WT​)23​​

    Substituting the velocity into the climb rate equation,

    💡
    R/Cmax​=3ρCD0​​(W/S)Z​​(WT​)23​[1−6Z​−2(T/W)2(L/D)max2​Z3​]

    #Propeller Aircraft

    For a propeller aircraft, PA​ is constant with respect to V.

    SEP=R/C=WPA​−PR​​

    To maximize climb rate, power required PR​ must be a minimum.

    💡
    VR/Cmax​​=VPmin​​=ρS2W​3CD0​​k​​​

    Substituting the velocity into the climb rate equation,

    💡
    R/Cmax​=WPA​​−(L/D)max​1.155​ρS2W​3CD0​​k​​​

    #Maximum Climb Angle (Analytical)

    #Jet Aircraft

    For a jet aircraft, TA​ is constant with respect to V.

    γ=sin−1WTA​−D​

    To maximize climb angle, total drag D must be a minimum.

    💡
    Vγmax​​=VDmin​​=ρS2W​CD0​​k​​​

    Note that Vγmax​​ may not be attainable in practice if it is less than stall speed.

    From the equilibrium equations,

    L=Wcosγ

    W=cosγL​

    Subsituting into the climb angle equation,

    γ=sin−1(WTA​​−L/Dcosγ​)

    γ=sin−1(WTA​​−L/D1​)

    💡
    γmax​=sin−1(WTA​​−(L/D)max​1​)

    For shallow climb angles, cosγ≈1.

    The corresponding climb rate for maximum climb angle is then,

    💡
    R/Cγmax​​=Vγmax​​sinγmax​

    #Propeller Aircraft

    The climb rate equation is,

    R/C=Vsinγ=V[WT​−21​W/SρV2CD0​​​−SW​(ρV22k​)]

    Comparing both sides of the equation,

    sinγ=WT​−21​W/SρV2CD0​​​−SW​(ρV22k​)

    sinγ=VWPA​​−21​W/SρV2CD0​​​−SW​(ρV22k​)

    Recall that PA​ is constant with respect to V for propeller aircraft.

    To maximize climb angle, differentiate the above with respect to V.

    dVd​(sinγ)=−V2WPA​​−W/SρVCD0​​​+SW​(ρV34k​)

    0=−V2WPA​​−W/SρVCD0​​​+SW​(ρV34k​)

    0=[−ρCD0​​(PA​/W)(W/S)​]V−V4+ρ2CD0​​4k(W/S)2​

    Comparing the terms, the second term is extremely small relative to the others.

    ρCD0​​V(PA​/W)(W/S)​=ρ2CD0​​4k(W/S)2​

    💡
    Vγmax​​=ρ(PA​/W)4k(W/S)​

    Note that Vγmax​​ may not be attainable in practice if it is less than stall speed.

    Substituting into the climb angle equation,

    sinγ=WPA​​[4k(W/S)ρ(PA​/W)​]−2(W/S)ρCD0​​​[ρ(PA​/W)4k(W/S)​]2−ρS2kW​[4k(W/S)ρ(PA​/W)​]2

    sinγ=4k(W/S)ρ(PA​/W)2​−ρ(PA​/W)28k2(W/S)CD0​​​−8k(W/S)ρ(PA​/W)2​

    💡
    γmax​=sin−1[4k(W/S)ρ(PA​/W)2​−ρ(PA​/W)28k2(W/S)CD0​​​−8k(W/S)ρ(PA​/W)2​]

    The corresponding climb rate for maximum climb angle is then,

    💡
    R/Cγmax​​=Vγmax​​sinγmax​

    #Hodograph for Climb Performance

    A hodograph plots an aircraft’s vertical speed Vvert​ against its horizontal speed Vhori​ for all possible climb flights that satisfy the climb flight equations. It also gives the direction and
    angle of the flight velocity vector (an arrow starting from origin to a point on the curve).

    Description of hodograph:

    • Maximum point on the curve gives the maximum climb rate R/Cmax​
    • Tangent line of the curve gives the climb rate for maximum climb angle R/Cγmax​​
    • Angle that the flight velocity vector makes with the horizontal axis is the climb angle

    Note that the flight speed V that gives the maximum climb rate and climb angle are
    different, which is shown by the length of the flight velocity vector. Maximum climb
    angle is crucial when the aircraft needs to clear an obstacle during takeoff.

    #Time to Climb

    The maximum climb rate reduces with altitude, but the curve shows slight non-linearity near
    the bottom end. Thus, to simplify the method for calculating time to climb, we shall assume
    the graph to be linear.

    The climb rate is given by,

    R/C=Vsinγ=dtdh​

    Rearranging the equation,

    dt=R/Cdh​

    💡
    Time=∫R/C1​ dh

    Derive an expression for R/C and evaluate the integral to determine the time taken (s).
    Since the graph is linear, the expression for     R/C is simply the equation of a straight line.
