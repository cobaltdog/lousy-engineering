    MA2701 · FLIGHT PERFORMANCE

    Level Turn Performance

    #Coordinated Level Turn

    For a level turn flight, the aircraft’s motion is along a circular path. Thus, there is centripetal acceleration. To maintain the circular path, the aircraft will bank at some angle ϕ, such that
    a component of lift balances the centrifugal force.

    Requirements for a level turn:

    • More lift is required for a level turn
    • More thrust is required to increase flight speed
    • Higher flight speed increases the lift generated

    Sideslip in steady level flight:

    • Deviation of aircraft heading from its flight path
    • Sideslip results in larger frontal area (increases drag)
    • Occurs due to sudden rudder deflection
      or a gust load on the horizontal stabilizer

    Sideslip in level turn flight:

    • Flight velocity vector is always tangential to the circular path
    • Negative sideslip angle if V is to the left of the plane of symmetry
    • Aircraft is said to be skidding (nose pointing towards the inside of the turn)
    • Positive sideslip angle if V is to the right of the plane of symmetry
    • Aircraft is said to be slipping (nose pointing towards the outside of the turn)

    Turn and sideslip indicator:

    • Rate of turn is too high for the bank angle which results in centrifugal force being
      greater than the force of gravity and thus, the ball rolls to the outside of the turn
    • Left rudder correction to reduce the rate of turn
    • Rate of turn is too low for the bank angle which results in force of gravity being
      greater than the centrifugal force and thus, the ball rools to the inside of the turn
    • Right rudder correction to increase the rate of turn

    #Equilibrium Equations

    Equilibrium of the vertical forces,

    Lcosϕ=W

    cosϕ=LW​

    cosϕ=n1​

    💡
    L=nW

    Since cosϕ=n1​, from trigonometry, sinϕ=nn2−1​​.

    Equilibrium of the horizontal forces,

    Lsinϕ=gW​RV2​

    R=gLW​sinϕV2​

    R=gV2​nn2−1​n​

    💡
    R=gn2−1​V2​

    Since V=Rω,

    💡
    ω=Vgn2−1​​

    A small turn radius can be achieved for a high load factor. Similarly, a higher turn rate
    can be achieved for a high load factor. However, a high load factor requires higher lift
    and a larger bank angle.

    Acceleration of the aircraft during a level turn is,

    a=ax​2+ay​2​=(RV2​)2+g2​

    a=[gn2−1​]2+g2​=ng

    Thus, a level turn maneuver with n=2 is said to be a 2g maneuver.

    #Bank Angle and Load Factor

    Practical limitations in achieving higher lift during a turn:

    • Aerodynamic capability — constrained by maximum CL​ achievable by the wings
    • Propulsive capability — constrained by maximum thrust that can be generated
    • Structural capability — constrained by maximum load the structure can carry

    #Aerodynamic Constraint

    The maximum load factor is constrained by CLmax​​.

    L=nW=21​ρV2SCLmax​​

    💡
    n=21​ρV2CLmax​​(WS​)

    #Propulsive Constraint

    For a level turn with load factor n,

    L=nW=21​ρV2SCL​

    CL​=ρV2S2nW​

    Thrust required for the level turn is,

    TR​=D=21​ρV2SCD​

    TR​=21​ρV2S(CD0​​+kCL​2)

    💡
    TR​=21​ρV2S[CD0​​+k(ρV2S2nW​)2]

    Rearranging the above, the load factor for a given thrust and flight speed is,

    💡
    n=2k(W/S)ρV2​(WT​−21​ρV2W/SCD0​​​)​

    The maximum load factor can be obtained when T is maximum.

    #Structural Constraint

    The maximum load factor is constrained by nmax​ as specified by regulators. Limit load
    factor (LLF) is where permanent structural damage begins to occur. Ultimate load factor
    (ULF) is where structural failure can occur.

    #Turn Radius and Turn Rate

    For a level turn with load factor n, the stall speed is,

    💡
    Vstall​=ρSCL​2nW​​

    Stall speed increases with the square root of load factor n, thus
    stall speed is higher for level turn flight than that in level flight.

    The minimum turn radius will be determined based on propulsive constraint.
    The maximum turn rate will be determined based on propulsive constraint.

    #Minimum Turn Radius

    From equilibrium equations,

    R=gn2−1​V2​

    R=21​ρgn2−1​21​ρV2​

    R=ρgn2−1​2q​

    Note that q is dynamic pressure.

    Load factor for a given thrust and flight speed is,

    n=2k(W/S)ρV2​(WT​−21​ρV2W/SCD0​​​)​

    n2=k(W/S)21​ρV2​(WT​−21​ρV2W/SCD0​​​)

    n2=k(W/S)q​(WT​−W/SqCD0​​​)

    Substituting n2 into R,

    R=ρgk(W/S)q​(WT​−W/SqCD0​​​)−1​2q​

    To obtain the minimum turn radius, set dqdR​=0 and we get,

    💡
    q=T/W2k(W/S)​

    Value of dynamic pressure that gives minimum turn radius.

    Flight speed to achieve minimum turn radius is then,

    21​ρV2=T/W2k(W/S)​

    V2=ρ(T/W)4k(W/S)​

    💡
    VRmin​​=ρ(T/W)4k(W/S)​​

    Substituting q into n,

    n2=T/W2​(WT​−T/W2kCD0​​​)

    n2=2−(T/W)24kCD0​​​

    💡
    nRmin​​=2−(T/W)24kCD0​​​​

    Substituting q and n into R,

    💡
    R=ρg(T/W)1−(T/W)24kCD0​​​​4k(W/S)​

    The smallest possible turn radius can be obtained by subsituting T=Tmax​.

    #Maximum Turn Rate

    From equilibrium equations,

    ω=Vgn2−1​​

    ω2=V2g2(n2−1)​

    ω2=21​ρV221​ρg2(n2−1)​

    ω2=2qρg2(n2−1)​

    Similarly, substitute n2 into ω2 and set dqd(ω2)​=0 to obtain the maximum turn rate.

    💡
    ωmax​=gW/Sρ​(2kT/W​−kCD0​​​​)​

    The load factor for maximum turn rate is then,

    💡
    nωmax​​=kCD0​​​T/W​−1​

    The flight speed at which turn rate is maximum is,

    💡
    Vωmax​​=(CD0​​k​)41​ρ2(W/S)​​

    #Corner/Maneuver Point

    As minimum turn radius and maximum turn rate are determined based on propulsive constraint, the values obtained may fall outside the safe region on the V-n diagram
    (dotted red line).

    • nRmin​​, nωmax​​>nCLmax​​​ for the given flight conditions (aerodynamic constraint)
    • nRmin​​, nωmax​​>nlimit​ for the given flight conditions (structural constraint)

    From observation, if the computed load factor for Rmin​ or ωmax​ is larger than the load
    factors based on aerodynamic and structural constraint, the smallest possible R and
    highest possible ω is only achievable at A, the corner/maneuver point.

    #Rmin​ and ωmax​ on Stall Boundary

    On the stall boundary where CL​ is maximum, the load factor is,

    n=21​ρV2CLmax​​(WS​)

    Substituting n into R,

    R=gn2−1​V2​

    R=g(WρV2SCLmax​​​)2−1​V2​

    💡
    R=g(2WρSCLmax​​​)2−V41​​1​

    Minimum turn radius is achieved when V is maximum,
    which occurs only at point     A on the stall boundary.

    Substituting n into ω,

    ω=Vgn2−1​​

    ω=Vg(2WρV2SCLmax​​​)2−1​​

    💡
    ω=gV2(2WρSCLmax​​​)2−V21​​

    Maximum turn rate is achieved when V is maximum,
    which occurs only at point     A on the stall boundary.

    #Rmin​ and ωmax​ on Structural Boundary

    The structural boundary is defined by a constant load factor.

    The equations for turn radius and turn rate are,

    R=gn2−1​V2​

    ω=Vgn2−1​​

    From observation, since n is fixed, the equations are purely functions of V.
    Minimum turn radius is achieved when V is minimum, which occurs only at point A.
    Maximum turn rate is achieved when V is minimum, which occurs only at point A.
