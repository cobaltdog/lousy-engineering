    #Work and Energy Principles

    #Work and Kinetic Energy

    Work done by a force F during displacement dr is,

    💡
    dW=F⋅dr=Fcosθ ds

    The component of force normal to the displacement does no work, thus, only the tangential component does work.

    💡
    W=∫s1​s2​​ Fcosθ ds

    Work is positive if the component of force is in the direction of displacement and vice versa.

    If the altitude variation is sufficiently small, g can be considered constant.

    W=∫y1​y2​​ (−mgj​)⋅(dxi^+dyj​)

    W=−mg∫y1​y2​​ dy

    💡
    W=−mg(y2​−y1​)

    If the body falls, the work is positive.

    If large changes in altitude occur,
    then g is no longer a constant.

    W=∫r1​r2​​ −r2Gme​m​ er​⋅drer​

    W=−Gme​m∫r1​r2​​ r21​ dr

    W=Gme​m(r2​1​−r1​1​)

    💡
    W=mgRe​2(r2​1​−r1​1​)

    The work-energy theorem states that work always results in a change in kinetic energy.

    💡
    Wtot​=ΔK

    The power developed by a force F which does an amount of work W is,

    💡
    P=F⋅v=Fvcosθ

    The mechanical efficiency at any instant of time may be expressed as,

    💡
    emech​=Pinput​Poutput​​

    If there is also electrical and thermal energy loss, the overall efficiency is,

    💡
    e=emech​eelec​ethermal​

    #Potential Energy

    Gravitational potential energy of a particle is the
    work done against the gravitational field to elevate
    the particle a distance h above some arbitrary datum.

    💡
    Ugrav​=mgh

    When large changes in altitude are encountered,
    the gravitational force is no longer constant.

    Fg​=r2Gme​m​=r2mgRe​2​

    ΔUgrav​=mgRe​2∫r1​r2​​r21​ dr

    ΔUgrav​=mgRe​2(−r2​1​+r1​1​)

    ΔUgrav​=−mgRe​2(r2​1​−r1​1​)

    💡
    Ugrav​=−rmgRe​2​

    When only conservative forces do work, the total mechanical energy is constant.

    💡
    E=K+U

    #Impulse and Momentum

    #Linear Impulse and Momentum

    Consider the general curvilinear motion of a particle of mass m. The velocity of the particle is v=r˙ and is tangent to its path. The ∑F of all forces is in the direction of its acceleration a=v˙.

    ∑F=mv˙=dtd​ mv=dtd​ L

    💡
    L=mv

    ∫t1​t2​​ ∑F dt=dL

    ∫t1​t2​​∑F dt=L2​−L1​

    💡
    L1​+∫t1​t2​​∑F dt=L2​

    SI unit of linear impulse is N⋅s.

    Impulsive forces are forces that are very large and act over a short duration. Thus, they are assumed
    to be constant and be brought outside the integral.

    If a force acting on a particle varies with time, the impulse of the force is equal to the area under the curve from t1​ to t2​.

    If the net force on a particle is zero during an interval of time,

    💡
    L1​=L2​

    #Angular Impulse and Momentum

    The moment of the linear momentum vector mv about the origin O is the angular momentum H.

    💡
    H=r×mv
    💡
    H=mvrsinθ

    H=r×mv=m​ixvx​​jyvy​​kzvz​​​

    Hx​=m(vz​y−vy​z)i

    Hy​=m(vx​z−vz​x)j

    Hz​=m(vy​x−vx​y)k

    dtdH​=(dtd​ r×mv)+(r×dtd​ mv)

    dtdH​=(v×mv)+(r×ma)

    💡
    dtdH​=r×∑F=∑M

    The rate of change of angular momentum of a particle equals the torque of the net force.

    ∫t1​t2​​ ∑M dt=dH

    ∫t1​t2​​ ∑M dt=H2​−H1​

    💡
    H1​+∫t1​t2​​ ∑M dt=H2​

    SI unit of angular impulse is Nm⋅s.

    For a particle moving along a curved path in the x-y plane, angular momenta about the origin have magnitudes,

    H1​=∣r×mv1​∣=mv1​d1​

    H2​=∣r×mv2​∣=mv2​d2​

    M=∣r×F∣=Frsinθ

    💡
    mv1​d1​+∫t1​t2​​ ∑Frsinθ dt=mv2​d2​

    If the net moment is zero during an interval of time,

    💡
    H1​=H2​

    The total angular momentum for the system of two particles is constant.
