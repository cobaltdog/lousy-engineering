    Kinetics of Rigid Bodies

    A body which can be approximated as a thin slab with its motion confined to the plane of the slab will be considered to be in plane motion. The plane of motion will contain the mass center, and all forces which act on the body will be projected onto the plane of motion.

    A body which has appreciable dimensions normal to the plane of motion but is symmetrical about that plane of motion through the mass center may be treated as having plane motion.

    #Force, Mass and Acceleration

    #General Equations of Motion

    Mass center G has an acceleration of aˉ. The body has an angular velocity ω=ωk and an angular acceleration α=αk.

    Velocity of mi​ relative to G is ρi​˙​, with a magnitude of ρi​ω. The product ρi​×ρi​˙​ is a vector normal to the x-y plane and has a magnitude of ρi​2ω.

    ∣L∣=∑ mi​ρi​2ω

    L=ω∑ mi​ρi​2

    L=Iω

    M=dtdL​

    M=I dtdω​

    M=Iα

    💡
    ∑F=maˉ
    💡
    ∑MG​=Iˉα

    The bar represents a variable of or about the mass center.

    ∑MP​=Iˉα+maˉd

    ∑MP​=IP​α+ρˉ​×maP​

    When P becomes a point O fixed in an inertial reference system and attached
    to the body, then aP​=0.

    💡
    ∑MO​=IO​α

    #Unconstrained and Constrained Motion

    For a body undergoing unconstrained motion, the components aˉx​ and aˉy​ of the
    mass-center acceleration and the angular acceleration α can be determined independently of one another using the above equations.

    For a body undergoing constrained motion, it requires a kinematic analysis relating linear to angular acceleration before the force and moment equations of motion
    can be solved.

    #Systems of Interconnected Bodies

    In problems dealing with two or more connected rigid bodies whose motions are related kinematically, it is convenient to analyze the bodies as an entire system.

    💡
    ∑F=∑maˉ
    💡
    ∑MP​=∑Iˉα+∑maˉd

    #Translation

    For a translating body, since there
    is no angular motion of the body,

    💡
    ∑F=maˉ
    💡
    ∑MG​=Iˉα=0

    In rectilinear translation, all points move
    in straight lines, whereas in curvilinear translation, all points move in concentric
    curved paths.

    #Work and Energy Principles

    #Work of Forces and Couples

    Work done by a force F is given by, W=∫F⋅dr=∫Fcosθ ds.

    Consider a couple M=Fb acting on a rigid body which moves in the plane of the couple. During time
    dt, the body rotates an angle dθ, and line AB also moves to A′B′.

    Work done during the translation from AB to A′B′′
    is zero. Thus, net work done is dW=F(b dθ) due
    to rotational motion of the body.

    💡
    W=∫θ1​θ2​​ M dθ

    #Kinetic and Potential Energy

    For a translating rigid body of mass m,

    💡
    K=21​mv2

    Applies to both rectilinear and curvilinear motion.

    For a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis,

    💡
    K=21​IO​ω2

    For the general plane motion of a rigid body,

    💡
    K=21​mvˉ2+21​Iˉω2

    The work-energy equation for a rigid body is,

    💡
    Ki​+Ui​+W=Kf​+Uf​

    #Power

    For a couple M acting on a body, the power developed by the couple is,

    💡
    P=Mω

    If M and ω have the same directions, power is positive and energy is supplied to the body.

    If a force F and a couple M acts simultaneously, the total power is,

    💡
    P=F⋅v+Mω

    dW=dK+dU

    P=dtd​(K+U)

    dtdK​=dtd​(21​mvˉ⋅vˉ+21​Iˉω2)

    dtdK​=21​m(aˉ⋅vˉ+vˉ⋅aˉ)+Iˉωω˙

    dtdK​=maˉ⋅vˉ+Iˉαω

    dtdK​=R⋅vˉ+Mˉω

    R is the resultant of all forces acting on the body while Mˉ is the resultant moment about the mass center of all forces. The dot product accounts for the case of curvilinear motion of the mass center, where aˉ and vˉ are not in the same direction.

    #Impulse and Momentum

    #Linear and Angular Momentum

    Linear and angular momentum are simply,

    💡
    L=mv
    💡
    L1​+∫t1​t2​​∑F dt=L2​
    💡
    H=r×mv
    💡
    H1​+∫t1​t2​​ ∑M dt=H2​

    H=r×mv

    H=r×m(ω×r)

    H=mr2ω

    💡
    H=Iω

    Dividing by time,

    💡
    M=Iα

    Linear momenta of all particles about G is accounted for by HG​=Iˉω. Linear momentum of the mass center can be represented as a vector L=mvˉ, acting through the mass center G.

    Angular momentum about any point O is,

    💡
    HO​=Iˉω+mvˉd

    O may be a fixed or moving point on or off the body.
    If O is a fixed point, HO​=IO​ω.

    #Conservation of Momentum

    If ∑F=0 for an interval of time, L1​=L2​.

    Linear-momentum vector undergoes no change if there is no net linear impulse.

    If ∑M=0 for an interval of time, H1​=H2​.

    Angular momentum undergoes no change if there is no net angular impulse.
