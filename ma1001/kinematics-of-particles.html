    its coordinates becomes s+Δs.

    💡
    v=dtds​

    #Plane Curvilinear Motion

    #Velocity and Acceleration

    Consider the continuous motion of a particle along a plane curve.

    At time t, the particle is at A, which is located by the position vector r.
    At time t+Δt, the particle is at A′, which is located by the position vector r+Δr.
    The distance actually travelled by the particle as it moves from A to A′ is the scalar Δs.

    As Δt→0, the instantaneous velocity of the particle is,

    💡
    v=dtdr​

    The velocity v at A plus the change Δv must equal the velocity v′ at A′.
    Δv depends on the change in magnitude of v and change in direction of v.

    As Δt→0, the instantaneous acceleration of the particle is,

    💡
    a=dtdv​

    In general, the direction of acceleration of a particle in curvilinear motion is neither
    tangent to the path nor normal to the path. The acceleration component which is
    normal to the path points toward the center of curvature of the path.

    #Constrained Motion of Connected Particles

    #One Degree of Freedom

    The total length of the cable is given by,

    L=x+r2​(2π​)+2y+r1​π+b

    Taking the time derivatives of the equation,

    0=x˙+2y˙​ ⟶ 0=vA​+2vB​

    0=x¨+2y¨​ ⟶ 0=aA​+2aB​

    The above equations indicate that the velocity of A must have a sign which is opposite to that of B, and similarly for the accelerations.

    #Two Degrees of Freedom

    The lengths of each cable is,

    LA​=yA​+2yD​+constant

    LB​=yB​+yC​+(yC​−yD​)+constant

    0=y˙​A​+2y˙​D​

    0=y¨​A​+2y¨​D​

    0=y˙​B​+2y˙​C​−y˙​D​

    0=y¨​B​+2y¨​C​−y¨​D​

    0=y˙​A​+2y˙​B​+4y˙​C​ → 0=vA​+2vB​+4vC​

    0=y¨​A​+2y¨​B​+4y¨​C​ → 0=aA​+2aB​+4aC​
