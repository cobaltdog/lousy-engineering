    MA4705 · AIRCRAFT NAV. & FLIGHT COMP.

    Avionics Operation

    #Electrostatic Discharge Sensitive Devices

    Electrostatic discharge sensitive (ESDS) devices:

    • Contain micro-circuits that may be damaged internally by ESD
    • Physical or electrical characteristics can be altered by ESD

    Static electricity can be generated by:

    • Friction between two dissimilar materials
    • Contact between a neutral body and a charged body

    Amount of charge created depends on:

    • Area of contact
    • Speed of separation
    • Relative humidity of environment

    Types of failure:

    • Hard/catastrophic failure — completely destroyed
    • Soft failure/latent defect — stresses a component but does not destroy it
      • Early failure — damaged part is installed and expected to be operational
      • Altered response — components in the part work intermittently (difficult to isolate)

    As the components in a damaged part work intermittently, it can be difficult to determine
    the root cause of the issue, which results in the LRU to cycle from line to shop multiple
    times before the faulty component is found.

    #Electrostatic Control

    Basic principles:

    • Eliminate and reduce generation of static charge
    • Design device to be immune to ESD
    • Dissipate and neutralize ESD
    • Protect device from ESD

    Controlling ESD:

    • Wrist straps — connected to aircraft’s ground to discharge electrostatic build-up
    • Mats, floors, clothing, shoes, casters — reduce generation of static charge
    • Protective packaging

    #Electromagnetic Interference (EMI)

    Aircraft avionics can be susceptible to high intensity radiation fields (HIRF).
    Radiated electromagnetic energy may induce significant current in conductors.

    Effects of a lightning strike:

    • Overloading of avionics caused by high currents and voltages
    • Vaporized wires, electrical components, or insulation
    • Holes burned through metallic skins
    • Hinges and bearings welded together
    • Puncturing or non-metallics (e.g. radome)

    Protection against lightning:

    • Indirect effects in cables and equipment are averted by careful shielding,
      grounding, and the use of surge suppression devices when necessary
    • Precautions are taken to ensure no sparks occur in the fuel system
    • Area struck by lightning can become magnetized, which can affect
      certain aircraft systems and must be removed by “degaussing”

    Protection against HIRF (structure):

    • RF gaskets — are conductive to maintain bonding integrity (used on removable panels)
    • Shielding for non-conducting surfaces
    • Structural bonding

    Protection against HIRF (equipment):

    • Resistors
    • EMI filters
    • Zener diodes
    • Thickness of LRU casing
    • Shielding of wiring — eliminate interference caused by electromagnetic energy

    #Aircraft Bonding

    Bonding is the electrical interconnection of metallic parts of an aircraft.

    • Ensure safe distribution of electrical charges and currents
    • Minimize damage to the aircraft structure and equipment
    • Prevent passage of electrical currents that would cause
      dangerous malfunction of the aircraft or its equipment
    • Prevent formation of high potential differences
    • Reduce interference to navigation aids
    • Reduce radio interference

    #Electromagnetic Compatibility (EMC)

    EMC is the condition in which a device performs its designed function in a common electromagnetic environment without causing/suffering unacceptable degradation
    due to unintentional EMI to/from other devices in the environment.

    Criteria for an EMC device:

    • Does not cause EMI with itself
    • Does not cause EMI to other devices
    • Not affected by EMI from other devices

    Elements of an EMC problem:

    • Source (lightning, ESD, RF transmitters)
    • Coupling path (conduction, radiation)
    • Receiver (analog/digital circuits)

    Types of coupling path:

    • Conducted (electric current)
    • Inducted (magnetic field)
    • Capacitive (electric field)
    • Radiated (EM field)
