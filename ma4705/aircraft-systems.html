    Aircraft Systems

    #Flight Management System

    Functions of management system:

    • Automatic flight control
      • Under autopilot, automatically move and control flight control surfaces
      • Under manual, display Flight Director commands for pilot to follow
    • Performance management
    • Navigation and guidance
      • Loaded with data regarding flight routes, navigation aids, and airports
      • Allow routes to be pre-programmed using information from database
      • Automatically tune to communication and navigation aid stations
    • Status and warnings

    Communication → Aircraft Communication Addressing and Reporting System (ACARS)
    Aircraft information → Engine Indicating and Crew Alerting System (EICAS)
    Aircraft information → Centralized Maintenance Computer (CMC)
    Engine control → Full Authority Digital Engine Control (FADEC)
    Flight displays → Electronic Flight Instrument System (EFIS)

    Strategic operating modes:

    1. ECON — minimum operating cost per mile travelled
    2. MIN FUEL — speed targets for minimum fuel used
    3. MIN TIME — speed based on flight envelope

    #Autopilot

    Autopilot:

    • Guides an aircraft without direct assistance from the pilot
    • Integrated with navigation systems to control all flight phases
    • Takeoff and climb — automatically adjust takeoff power and speed
    • Cruise — adjust power to achieve the desired flight characteristics
    • Decent and approach — descend at appropriate speed while following flight plan

    Autoland system:

    • Incorporates autopilot, autothrust, radio altimeter, and nose wheel steering
    • Designed as a “Fail Operational” or “Fail Passive” system
    • Fail Operational
      • Must have at least 2 autopilots engaged for the approach
      • Must have 3 autopilot systems such that autoland can still be executed if one fails
      • Allows for a “no decision height” approach to be conducted
    • Fail Passive
      • Single autopilot approach
      • Failure of autopilot will not result in deviation from the desired flight path
      • Pilot must immediately assume control of the aircraft and carry out a missed approach
      • If pilot has sufficient visual reference to land, continue with landing
      • Lowest allowable decision height for a fail-passive system is 50ft

    On a Cat 3 ILS approach (autoland), the autopilot controls the aircraft flight path to follow
    the localizer and glide slope, adjusting the power to maintain the appropriate speeds, and commencing flare as required to achieve a safe landing without the runway being visible
    until the final stage of the approach.
