    Strain

    #Strain

    Strain is used to describe the deformation of a body by changes in the lengths of segments.

    💡
    ε=L0​L−L0​​=L0​ΔL​

    #Stress-Strain Curves

    Description of curve (ductile):

    • Length increases linearly with increasing load
    • Applied load increases to yield point of material
    • Material undergoes large deformation with relatively small increase in load
    • Deformation caused by slippage of material along oblique surfaces (shear stresses)
    • Necking occurs when applied load reaches ultimate tensile stress of the material

    Description of curve (brittle):

    • Rupture occurs without any noticeable change in the rate of elongation
    • Rupture occurs along a surface perpendicular to the load
    • Strain at rupture is much smaller than ductile materials
    • Normal stresses responsible for failure of material

    #Modulus of Elasticity

    The modulus of elasticity is used to describe the linear portion of the stress-strain curve. It represents the stiffness of a material and is not affected by alloying, heat treatment, etc.

    💡
    E=εσ​

    #Fatigue

    When loadings are repeated thousands or millions of times, rupture may occur at a stress much lower than the material’s ultimate tensile strength. This is known as fatigue. A fatigue failure is of a brittle nature, even for materials that are normally ductile.

    The endurance limit of a material is defined as the stress below which a material can endure an infinite number of repeated load cycles without exhibiting failure.

    #Generalized Hooke’s Law

    Consider an element of an isotropic material in the shape of a cube. Under multiaxial loading, the element will deform in the direction of all three axes.

    The effect of a combined loading on a structure can be obtained by determining separately the effects of various loads and combining the results obtained, provided that the following conditions are satisfied:

    • Each effect is linearly related to the load that produces it
    • Deformation resulting from any given load is small and does not affect the other loads

    💡
    εx​=+Eσx​​−Eυσy​​−Eυσz​​
    💡
    εy​=+Eσy​​−Eυσx​​−Eυσz​​
    💡
    εz​=+Eσz​​−Eυσx​​−Eυσy​​

    The equations are valid only as long as the stresses do not exceed the yield limit of the material, and as long as the deformations involved remain small.
