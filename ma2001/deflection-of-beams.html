    #Elastic Curve (Double Integration Method)

    The elastic curve passes through the centroid of each cross section of the beam.

    Supports that resist a force, restrict displacement, and those that restrict a moment, restrict slope as well as displacement.

    If the elastic curve for a beam seems difficult to establish, the bending moment diagram for the beam must be drawn first.

    Description of elastic curve:

    • Positive bending moment → Concave up
    • Negative bending moment → Concave down
    • Slope is zero → Deflection may be maximum

    #Moment-Curvature Relationship

    The deformation of a member caused by a bending moment is measured by the curvature of the neutral surface. The curvature is defined as the reciprocal of the radius of curvature ρ.

    εmax​=ρc​

    ρ1​=cεmax​​=Ecσmax​​

    💡
    ρ1​=EIM(x)​

    Sign convention for ρ:

    • Positive M → positive ρ
    • Negative M → negative ρ

    #Slope and Deflection

    The deflection of the beam can be expressed as y=f(x). The curvature of a plane curve at a point Q(x,y) of the curve can be expressed as the following. As the slope dxdy​ of the elastic curve is very small, we can neglect this term.

    ρ1​=[1+(dxdy​)2]3/2dx2d2y​​≈dx2d2y​

    💡
    dx2d2y​=EIM(x)​

    The product EI is known as the flexural rigidity.

    The maximum slope θ<0.1 rad (5.73°).

    EI dx2d2y​=M(x)

    EI dxdy​=∫M(x) dx+C1​

    EI y=∫M(x) dx+C1​x+C2​

    Alternatively, dxdy​=tanθ≈θ(x).

    EI θ(x)=∫M(x) dx+C1​

    The constants C1​ and C2​ are determined from the boundary conditions — the conditions imposed on the beam by its supports.

    This limits the analysis to statically determinate beams — simply supported beams, overhanging beams, and cantilever beams.

    For roller and pin support:

    • Displacement is zero

    For fixed supports:

    • Displacement is zero
    • Slope is zero

    #Singularity Functions (Macaulay’s Method)

    The integration method provides a convenient and effective way of determining the slope and deflection at any point of a prismatic beam, as long as the bending moment can be expressed as a continuous function throughout the entire beam’s length.

    #Shear and Bending Moment

    Consider a simply supported beam AB of length 2a which carries a uniformly distributed load w0​.

    ∑MB​=0

    0=w0​a(21​a)−RA​(2a)

    RA​=41​w0​a

    Cutting the beam at a point D between A and C, we draw the FBD of AD.

    V1​(x)=41​w0​a

    M1​(x)=41​w0​ax

    Cutting the beam at a point E between C and B, we draw the FBD of AE.

    V2​(x)=41​w0​a−w0​(x−a)

    M2​(x)=41​w0​ax−21​w0​(x−a)2

    Shear and bending moment can be represented by a single expression.

    V(x)=41​w0​a−w0​⟨x−a⟩

    M(x)=41​w0​ax−21​w0​⟨x−a⟩2

    Thus, the second term should be included when x≥a and ignored when x<a.

    The expression ⟨x−a⟩n is called a singularity function.

    💡
    ⟨x−a⟩n={(x−a)n     when x≥a0                 when x<a​
    💡
    ∫⟨x−a⟩n dx=n+11​⟨x−a⟩n+1
