    Strain Transformations

    The general state of strain at a given point is represented by a combination of three components of normal strain, εx​, εy​ and εz​, and three components of shear strain, γxy​, γxz​ and γyz​.

    Mechanics of Materials Lecture 21: Plain strain transformationhttps://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xtVOO2Wwd14

    #Transformation of Plane Strain

    Sign conventions:

    • θ is positive in the anticlockwise direction
    • Normal strains are positive if they cause elongation along the axes
    • Shear strain is positive if the interior angle of the element becomes smaller than 90°

    #Strain Equations

    Let us assume a state of plane strain on an element where εz​=γxz​=γyz​=0, and is defined by the strain components εx​, εy​ and γxy​.

    To determine the strain components, we must find the elongation of line dx′ that lies along the x′ axis.

    dx=dx′cosθ

    dy=dx′sinθ

    When positive εx​ occurs, dx its elongated ex​dx.

    When positive εy​ occurs, dx its elongated ey​dy.

    ex​dx=ex​dx cosθ

    ey​dx=ey​dy sinθ

    Assuming that dx remains fixed in position, γxy​ causes the top of line dy to be displaced γxy​dy to the right. This causes dx′ to elongate γxy​dy cosθ.

    If all three (red) elongations are summed, we can determine the resultant elongation of dx′.

    Δx′=εx​dx cosθ+εy​dy sinθ+γxy​dy cosθ

    Δx′=εx​dx′cos2θ+εy​dx′sin2θ+γxy​dx′sinθcosθ

    dx′Δx′​=εx​cos2θ+εy​sin2θ+γxy​sinθcosθ

    💡
    εx′​=εx​cos2θ+εy​sin2θ+γxy​sinθcosθ

    To determine γx′y′​, we must find the rotation of each of the lines dx′ and dy′ when subjected to strain.

    Line dx′ rotates by an amount α.

    tanα=dx′Δy′​ ⟶ α≈dx′Δy′​

    Δy′=−εx​dx sinθ+εy​dy cosθ−γxy​dy sinθ

    Δy′=(−εx​+εy​)dx′sinθcosθ−γxy​dx′sin2θ

    α=(−εx​+εy​)sinθcosθ−γxy​sin2θ

    Line dy′ rotates by an amount β.

    Substituting θ+90° for θ.

    β=(−εx​+εy​)cosθ(−sinθ)−γxy​cos2θ

    β=−(−εx​+εy​)sinθcosθ−γxy​cos2θ

    Since α and β must represent the rotation of the lines dx′ and dy′, γx′y′​=α−β.

    γx′y′​=2(−εx​+εy​)sinθcosθ+γxy​(cos2θ−sin2θ)

    γx′y′​=−2(εx​−εy​)sinθcosθ+γxy​(cos2θ−sin2θ)

    Substituting identities sin2θ=2sinθcosθ, cos2θ=21+cos2θ​ and sin2θ+cos2θ=1.

    💡
    εx′​=2εx​+εy​​+2εx​−εy​​cos2θ+2γxy​​sin2θ
    💡
    εy′​=2εx​+εy​​−2εx​−εy​​cos2θ−2γxy​​sin2θ
    💡
    γx′y′​=−(εx​−εy​)sin2θ+γxy​cos2θ
    💡
    2γx′y′​​=−2εx​−εy​​sin2θ+2γxy​​cos2θ

    #Principal Strains

    The axes along which principal strains occur will coincide with the axes of principal stress.

    Principal plane of strain:

    💡
    tan2θP​=εx​−εy​γxy​​

    Principal strains:

    💡
    ε=2εx​+εy​​±(2εx​−εy​​)2+(2γxy​​)2​

    #Maximum Shear Strain

    Plane for maximum in-plane shear strain:

    💡
    tan2θs​=−γxy​εx​−εy​​

    Corresponding maximum in-plane shear strain:

    💡
    2γmax(in−plane)​​=(2εx​−εy​​)2+(2γxy​​)2​

    Corresponding average normal strain:

    💡
    εavg​=2εx​+εy​​

    #Mohr’s Circle for Plane Strain

    The sign convention for shear strain follows the convention used to draw Mohr’s circle for plane stress.

    If the shear deformation causes a given side to rotate clockwise, that corresponding point will be plotted above the the horizontal axis and vice versa.

    If εx​ and εy​ are of opposite signs, the absolute maximum shear strain is in-plane.

    If εx​ and εy​ are of the same signs, the absolute maximum shear strain is out-of-plane.

    #Measurement of Strain — Strain Rosettes

    The normal strain on the free surface of a body can be measured in a particular direction using an electrical resistance strain gauge.

    Unfortunately, shear strain cannot be directly measured with a strain gauge. Thus, we require a cluster of three strain gauges — strain rosette.

    Consider the general case of arranging the strain gauges at the angles θa​, θb​ and θc​.

    εa​=εx​cos2θa​+εy​sin2θa​+γxy​sinθa​cosθa​

    εb​=εx​cos2θb​+εy​sin2θb​+γxy​sinθb​cosθb​

    εc​=εx​cos2θc​+εy​sin2θc​+γxy​sinθc​cosθc​
