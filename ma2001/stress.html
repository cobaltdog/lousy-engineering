    #Forces

    #Loads

    A body can be subjected to both surface loads and body loads.

    #Surface Loads

    Description of load:

    • Concentrated forces are loads that act on a small area of contact
    • Distributed loadings act over a larger surface area of the body
    • Resultant force of a distributed load acts through the geometric center of the area

    #Body Loads

    Description of load:

    • No physical contact between the two bodies
    • Force that is developed when one body exerts a force on another body

    #Support Reactions

    As a general rule, if the support prevents movement in a given direction, then a force must be developed on the member in that direction. Likewise, if rotation is prevented, a couple moment must be exerted on the member.

    #Equations of Equilibrium

    Equilibrium of a body requires both a balance of forces, to prevent the body from moving or accelerating along a straight or curved path, and a balance of moments.

    💡
    ∑Fx​=0      ∑Fy​=0      ∑Fz​=0
    💡
    ∑Mx​=0     ∑My​=0     ∑Mz​=0

    #Two-Force Member

    Description of member:

    • Joined at the ends with pins
    • No transverse load between pinned ends
    • Only axial stresses due to axial load (no bending)

    #Stresses

    #Normal Stress

    When forces act along the length of a member, it is called axial loading.

    💡
    σaxial​=AF​

    However, in general, the normal stress at a given point varies from section to section. In practice, it will be assumed that the distribution of normal stresses in an axially loaded member is uniform, except in the imminent vicinity of the points of application of loads.

    A uniform distribution of stress is only possible only if the concentrated loads act through the center of the section. This is called concentric loading and is assumed to take place in all straight, two-force members found in trusses and pin-connected structures.

    If a two-force member is eccentrically loaded, then the resultant of the stress distribution in a section must yield an axial force and a moment.

    #Tension and Compression

    Depending on whether a member is in tension or compression, the cross-sectional area changes. In tension, the pins are pushing outwards and vice versa for compression.

    #Shear Stress

    Shear stress is the result of transverse forces acting on a member.

    The distribution of shearing stresses across a section cannot be assumed to be uniform. The actual value of shear stress varies from zero at the surface of the member to a maximum value that may be larger than the calculated average value.

    There are two types of shear — single and double shear.

    💡
    τshear​=AF​
    💡
    τshear​=2AF​

    #Bearing Stress

    Bolts, pins, rivets create stresses in the members they connect, along the bearing surface.

    This formula only applies to symmetrical holes.

    Looking from the top view, resolving each force into its x and y components, the y components of each force cancel each other out. This leaves resultant forces in the x direction. Thus, the force is acting over the projected area.

    #Allowable Load and Allowable Stress

    The maximum load that a structural member will be allowed to carry under normal conditions of utilization is considerably smaller than the ultimate load. A portion of the load-carrying capacity of the member is kept in reserve to assure its safe performance.
