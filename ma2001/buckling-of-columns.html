    Buckling of Columns

    Understanding BucklingGet access to the companion video on self-buckling on Nebula and documentaries like The World of Bridges on CuriosityStream with the bundle deal - visit https://curiositystream.com/efficientengineer and use the promo code 'efficientengineer'! Buckling is a failure mode that occurs in columns and other members that are loaded in compression.https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=21G7LA2DcGQ&t=782s

    #Euler’s Formula

    Suppose we are to design a column to support a given load. The column will be pin-connected at both ends and we assume that the load is a centric axial load.

    If the cross-sectional area A of the column is selected such that the value of σ<σallowable​ for the material used and if the deformation ΔL=AEFL0​​ falls within the given specifications, we might conclude that the column has been properly designed.

    However, it may happen that, as the load is applied, the column will buckle; instead of remaining straight, it will suddenly become sharply curved.

    #Pin-Ended Columns

    The critical load is the load at which a pin-ended column will buckle.

    💡
    Pcr​=L2π2EI​

    I = least moment of inertia for the column’s cross-sectional area
    L = unsupported length of the column, whose ends are pinned

    A column having a rectangular cross, as shown on the right, will buckle about the a-a axis, and not the b-b axis.

    The critical load equation can be rewritten in terms of stress, by using I=Ar2, where A is the cross-sectional area and r is the radius of gyration of the cross-sectional area.

    Pcr​=L2π2E(Ar2)​

    APcr​​=L2π2Er2​

    💡
    σcr​=(L/r)2π2E​

    The geometric ratio L/r is known as the slenderness ratio of the column.

    Assuming E=200 GPa and σyield​=250 MPa, if σcr​>σyield​, this value is of no interest, since the column will yield in compression and cease to be elastic before it has a chance to buckle.

    #Columns with Other End Conditions

    To use Euler’s formula for columns having different types of supports, we will have to use the effective length Le​ to represent the distance between two points of zero moment on the column.

    💡
    Pcr​=Le​2π2EI​

    Alternatively, we can use a dimensionless coefficient K called the effective-length factor.

    💡
    Pcr​=(KL)2π2EI​
