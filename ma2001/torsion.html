    Torsion

    #Torque

    Consider a system, which consists of a turbine and a generator, connected by a transmission shaft. The turbine exerts a torque on the shaft, which exerts an equal torque on the generator. The generator reacts by exerting an equal and opposite torque on the shaft.

    dAdF​=τ

    dF=τ dA

    ρ⋅dF=ρ⋅τdA

    ∫ρ (dF)=∫ρ (τdA)

    T=∫ρ(τdA)

    where ρ is a point along the radius

    Unlike the normal stresses due to axial loads, the distribution of shear stresses due to
    torsional loads cannot be assumed to be uniform. To identify the direction of torque,
    we use the Right-Hand-Screw rule.

    #Shear Strain and Stress

    Consider a circular shaft of length L and radius c that has been twisted through an angle ϕ. Taking a small square element on the internal surface of radius ρ, and subjecting the cylinder to a torsional load, the element deforms into a rhombus.

    From observation, shear strain is proportional to the radius, angle of twist, and length of shaft.

    Arc Length=Ltanγ≈Lγ

    Arc Length=ρϕ

    Lγ=ρϕ

    💡
    γ=Lρϕ​
    💡
    γmax​=Lcϕ​
    💡
    γ=cρ​ γmax​

    where ρ is the radius at any point, ϕ is the angle of twist
    and     c is radius of shaft where shear strain is maximum

    #Shear Stress

    If the torque is such that all shearing stresses in the shaft remain below the yield point
    of the material, Hooke’s Law will apply and there will be no permanent deformation.

    Multiplying G on both sides of the equation,

    Gγ=cρ​ Gγmax​

    💡
    τ=cρ​ τmax​

    The shear stress varies linearly with the radial position in the section of the shaft.

    T=∫ρ(τdA)=∫ρ(cρ​τmax​) dA

    #Angle of Twist in Elastic Range

    In the elastic range, the yield stress of the material is not exceeded anywhere in the shaft. Thus, Hooke’s Law applies.

    τmax​=Gγmax​

    γmax​=Gτmax​​=JGTc​

    γmax​=Lcϕ​

    Lcϕ​=JGTc​

    ϕ=JGTL​ where ϕ is expressed in rad

    For a shaft containing multiple sections and multiple torques, the total angle of twist of the shaft is obtained by adding algebraically the angles of twist of each component part.

    #Axial Loading vs Torsion

    Shaft subjected to normal stresses — tension/compression:

    • Upright element (square) will experience maximum normal stresses in both directions
    • Rotated element (rhombus) inclined surfaces will experience both normal and shear stress

    Shaft subjected to torsion:

    • Upright element (square) will experience maximum shear stresses
    • Rotated element (rhombus) inclined surfaces will only experience tension and compression

    #Prove for Torsion

    Taking half of the square, we get:

    τmax​=A0​Fshear​​

    Fshear​=τmax​A0​

    where A0​ is the surface area
    on the left and bottom

    Resolving the forces, we get:

    ∑F45°​=0

    F=2(τmax​A0​)cos45°=τmax​A0​2​

    σ45°​=AF​=A0​2​τmax​A0​2​​=τmax​

    Thus, for an element in pure shear, it experiences only normal stresses on the 45° plane.

    #Failure Modes

    Ductile materials usually fail by shear while brittle materials fail by tension/compression.

    Ductile materials:

    • Axial loads — fails at an angle of 45° as maximum shear occurs on that plane
    • Torsion — fails perpendicular to the shaft axis as maximum shear occurs on that plane

    Brittle materials:

    • Axial loads — fails perpendicular to the shaft axis due to maximum normal stresses
    • Torsion — fails at an angle of 45° as maximum normal stress occurs at that plane

    #Design of Transmission Shafts

    Shaft performance specifications:

    • Power
    • Speed

    A suitable material and cross section must be selected to meet the performance specifications of the shaft without exceeding allowable shearing stress.

    T=ωPower​=2πfPower​
