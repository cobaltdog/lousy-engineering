    #Fluid Mechanics

    #Fluid Viscosity

    One-dimensional flow: τ=μ∂y∂u​ where μ is dynamic viscosity expressed in Pa⋅s

    Two-dimensional flow: τ=μ(∂y∂u​+∂x∂v​) when velocity gradient is non-linear

    ν=ρμ​ where ν is kinematic viscosity expressed in m2/s

    Dimensionless Numbers

    Reynolds number (most fluid problems)Re=Viscous ForceInertia Force​=μρUL​=νUL​
    Froude number (free surface flows)Fr=Gravitational ForceInertia Force​=gL​U​
    Euler number (flows with pressure differentials)Eu=Inertia ForcePressure Force​=ρU2p​
    Strouhal number (compressible flows)St=Convective Inertia ForceLocal Inertia Force​=UfL​
    Weber number (flows with surface tension)We=Surface Tension ForceInertia Force​=σρU2L​

    where U is flow velocity and L is characteristic length

    #Pressure, Lift and Drag Coefficients

    Cp​=21​ρ∞​U∞​2p−p∞​​=1−U∞​2U2​

    CL​=21​ρ∞​U∞​2AL​

    CD​=21​ρ∞​U∞​2AD​

    p∞​+21​ρ∞​U∞​2=p+21​ρ∞​U2

    p−p∞​=21​ρ∞​U∞​2(1−U∞​2U2​)

    21​ρ∞​U∞​2p−p∞​​=1−U∞​2U2​

    where ∞ represents the freestream property

    #Governing Equations

    Conservation of Mass

    Differential form of continuity equation:

    ∂t∂ρ​+∂x∂(ρu)​+∂y∂(ρv)​+∂z∂(ρw)​=0

    For incompressible fluids where ρ=const:

    ∂x∂u​+∂y∂v​+∂z∂w​=0

    Conservation of Momentum

    Navier-Stokes equations:

    ρDtDu​=ρgx​−∂x∂p​+∂x∂​[μ(2∂x∂u​−32​∇⋅V)]+∂y∂​[μ(∂z∂u​+∂x∂w​)]+∂z∂​[μ(∂z∂u​+∂x∂w​)]

    ρDtDv​=ρgy​−∂y∂p​+∂x∂​[μ(∂y∂u​+∂x∂v​)]+∂y∂​[μ(2∂z∂v​−32​∇⋅V)]+∂z∂​[μ(∂z∂v​+∂y∂w​)]

    ρDtDw​=ρgz​−∂z∂p​+∂x∂​[μ(∂z∂u​+∂x∂w​)]+∂y∂​[μ(∂z∂v​+∂y∂w​)]+∂z∂​[μ(2∂z∂w​−32​∇⋅V)]

    If viscosity is taken to be negligible:

    ρDtDu​=ρ(∂t∂u​+u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​+w∂z∂u​)=ρgx​−∂x∂p​

    ρDtDv​=ρ(∂t∂v​+u∂x∂v​+v∂y∂v​+w∂z∂v​)=ρgy​−∂y∂p​

    ρDtDw​=ρ(∂t∂w​+u∂x∂w​+v∂y∂w​+w∂z∂w​)=ρgz​−∂z∂p​

    The above equations are known as Euler’s equations.

    Conservation of Energy

    Bernoulli’s equation (valid for only inviscid and incompressible flows):

    p1​+21​ρu1​2+ρgz1​=p2​+21​ρu2​2+ρgz2​

    #Potential Flow Theory

    #Vorticity ζ

    For a 2-dimensional flow:

    ζ=2ωz​=∂x∂v​−∂y∂u​

    If ζ=0, the flow is called irrotational (ideal).
    If     ζ=0, the flow is called rotational (real).

    #Circulation Γ

    For a 2-dimensional flow:

    Γ=ζA

    Circulation is the measure of the strength of the fluid flow.
    It is the sum of the vorticity enclosed within the area of interest.

    #Stream Function ψ

    A 2-dimensional flow that satisfies the continuity equation is a steady flow.

    Cartesian Coordinates

    Continuity equation:

    ∂x∂u​+∂y∂v​=0

    Velocity components:

    u=∂y∂ψ​

    v=−∂x∂ψ​

    Polar Coordinates

    Continuity equation:

    r1​∂r∂(rvr​)​+r1​∂θ∂vθ​​=0

    Velocity components:

    vr​=r1​∂θ∂ψ​

    vθ​=−∂r∂ψ​

    Laplace Equation

    ∂x2∂2ψ​+∂y2∂2ψ​=0

    Interpretation of Stream Function

    dψ=u dy−v dx=0

    Change in dψ along a streamline is zero.

    The change in ψ is zero along a streamline;
    lines along which ψ is a constant are streamlines.

    #Velocity Potential ϕ

    Cartesian Coordinates

    Velocity components:

    u=∂x∂ϕ​

    v=∂y∂ϕ​

    Polar Coordinates

    Velocity components:

    vr​=∂r∂ϕ​

    vθ​=r1​∂θ∂ϕ​

    Note that potential function share common velocity components with stream function.

    u=∂y∂ψ​=∂x∂ϕ​

    v=−∂x∂ψ​=∂y∂ϕ​

    vr​=r1​∂θ∂ψ​=∂r∂ϕ​

    vθ​=−∂r∂ψ​=r1​∂θ∂ϕ​

    Laplace Equation

    ∂x2∂2ϕ​+∂y2∂2ϕ​=0

    Relationship with Stream Function

    For a streamline where dψ=0:

    (dxdy​)ψ=const​=uv​

    For a potential line where dϕ=0:

    (dxdy​)ϕ=const​=−vu​

    Slope of streamline is the negative reciprocal of potential line:

    (dxdy​)ψ=c​=−(dxdy​)ϕ=c​1​

    #Basic Plane Potential Flows

    A potential flow is characterized by incompressibility and irrotationality ζ=0.

    Uniform Flow

    Velocity components:

    u=∂y∂ψ​=U

    v=−∂x∂ψ​=0

    For a horizontal uniform flow:

    ψ=Uy=Ursinθ

    ϕ=Ux=Urcosθ

    For a uniform flow at some arbitrary angle:

    ψ=U(ycosα−xsinα)

    ϕ=U(xcosα+ysinα)

    Source and Sink

    Q=(2πr)vr​

    vr​=2πrQ​

    where Q is volume flow rate per unit length
    and     r is the radial distance from the source

    ψ=2πQ​⋅θ

    ϕ=2πQ​ ln(r)

    vr​=r1​∂θ∂ψ​=∂r∂ϕ​=2πrQ​

    Vortex

    vθ​=−∂r∂ψ​=r1​∂r∂ϕ​=2πrΓ​

    where the constant K=2πΓ​

    ψ=−2πΓ​ ln(r)

    ϕ=2πΓ​ θ

    Doublet

    For a souce-sink pair:

    ψ=−π(r2−a2)Qarsinθ​

    where a is the half-distance
    between source and sink

    For small values of a — doublet:

    ψ=−rKsinθ​

    ϕ=rKcosθ​

    where r is the radius from the center of
    the doublet, with the constant     K=πQa​

    #Superposition of Basic Flows

    #Rankine Half-Body (Source in Uniform Flow)

    ψ=Ursinθ+2πQ​⋅θ

    ϕ=Urcosθ+2πQ​ ln(r)

    Note that x=rcosθ and y=rsinθ.

    vr​=Ucosθ+2πrQ​

    vθ​=−Usinθ

    Distance between stagnation point and source:

    b=2πUQ​

    Streamline on which stagnation point is located on:

    ψstagnation​=2Q​=πbU

    Boundary of the half-body:

    r=sinθb(π−θ)​

    Note that the boundary of the half-body is essentially the
    streamline on which the stagnation point is located on.

    Since r=sinθ, the half-width of the body:

    y=b(π−θ)

    As x approaches infinity, the half-width becomes:

    y=±bπ

    The angle θ of the boundary, with respect to the x-axis, tends towards zero.

    Velocity at any point in the flow:

    V2=U2+πrUQcosθ​+(2πrQ​)2

    V2=U2(1+r2bcosθ​+r2b2​)

    We can make use of Bernoulli’s equation to determine the pressure at any point.

    #Rankine Oval (Source and Sink in Uniform Flow)

    ψ=Ursinθ−2πQ​tan−1(x2+y2−a22arsinθ​)

    ϕ=Urcosθ−2πQ​(ln r1​−ln r2​)

    Note that r2=x2+y2.

    u=U+2πQ​[(x−a)2+y2x−a​−(x+a)2+y2x+a​]

    v=−2πQ​[(x−a)2+y2y​−(x+a)2+y2y​]

    Half-length of the body:

    l=πUQa​+a2​

    al​=πUaQ​+1​

    Half-width of the body:

    h=2ah2−a2​ tanQ2πUh​

    ah​=21​[(ah​)2−1]tan[2(QπUa​)ah​]

    #Flow around Cylinder (Doublet in Uniform Flow)

    ψ=Ursinθ(1−r2a2​)

    ϕ=Urcosθ(1+r2a2​)

    vr​=Ucosθ(1−r2a2​)

    vθ​=−Usinθ(1+r2a2​)

    At the boundary of the body where r=a:

    vr​=0

    vθ​=−2Usinθ

    We can make use of Bernoulli’s equation to determine pressure
    at any point along the boundary of the Rankine oval.

    #Rotating Cylinder (Free Vortex and Cylinder)

    ψ=Ur(1−r2a2​)sinθ−2πΓ​ ln(r)

    ϕ=Ur(1+r2a2​)cosθ+2πΓ​ θ

    where Γ is the circulation of the free vortex

    vr​=Ucosθ(1−r2a2​)

    vθ​=−Usinθ(1+r2a2​)+2πrΓ​

    Tangential velocity on the surface:

    vθs​=−∂r∂ψ​∣r=a​=−2Usinθ+2πaΓ​

    Location of stagnation points:

    sinθstagnation​=4πUaΓ​

    where vθs​=0

    #Airfoil Characteristics

    #NACA 4-Digit Series

    • 1st digit specifies the maximum camber in % of chord
    • 2nd digit ×10 specifies position of maximum camber from leading edge in % of chord
    • Last two digits specifies maximum thickness of the airfoil in % of chord

    The NACA 2415 airfoil has a maximum camber of 2% chord length, which is located 40%
    of chord length from the airfoil leading edge, and has a maximum thickness of 15%.

    #NACA 5-Digit Series

    • 1st digit ×0.15 specifies the design lift coefficient
    • 2nd and 3rd digits ×0.5 specifies position of maximum camber in % of chord
    • 4th and 5th digits specifies position of maximum thickness in % of chord

    The NACA 23012 airfoil has a design lift coefficient of 0.3, a maximum camber located
    15% of chord length from the airfoil leading edge, and a maximum thickness of 12%.

    #NACA 6-Digit Series

    • 1st digit denotes the series and indicates this is designed for greater laminar flow
    • 2nd digit ×10 specifies position of minimum pressure in % of chord
    • 3rd digit ×0.1 specifies the acceptable range of Cl​ for low drag performance
    • 4th digit ×0.1 specifies the design lift coefficient
    • Last two digits specifies position of maximum thickness in % of chord

    The NACA 632-315 airfoil has a minimum pressure located 30% of chord length from the
    leading edge, has a 0.3 design lift coefficient,     ±0.2 of design lift coefficient for low drag performance, and maximum thickness located 15% of chord length.

    #Shockwaves

    #Isentropic Flow Properties

    For a given Mach number, the ratios are:

    pp0​​=[1+21​(γ−1)M2]γ−1γ​

    ρρ0​​=[1+21​(γ−1)M2]γ−11​

    TT0​​=1+21​(γ−1)M2

    #Normal Shock Properties

    For a given Mach number, the ratios are:

    p1​p2​​=1+γ+12γ​(M1​2−1)

    ρ1​ρ2​​=2+(γ−1)M1​2(γ+1)M1​2​

    T1​T2​​=[1+γ+12γ​(M1​2−1)][(γ+1)M1​22+(γ−1)M1​2​]

    Upstream Mach number is:

    M2​2=γM1​2−21​(γ−1)1+21​(γ−1)M1​2​

    Entropy change across shockwave is:

    Δs=cp​ ln{[1+γ+12γ​(M1​2−1)][(γ+1)M1​22+(γ−1)M1​2​]}           −R ln[1+γ+12γ​(M1​2−1)]

    #Prandtl-Meyer Function

    Solving for properties across an oblique shock:

    • Determine deflection angle θ, which is with respect to the upstream flow direction
    • Determine shockwave angle β from θ-β-M diagram (weak or strong shock angle)
    • Compute normal component of upstream Mach number using M1n​=M1​sinβ
    • Obtain properties corresponding to M1n​ from “Normal Shock Properties” table
    • Compute the downstream Mach number M2​ using M2​=sin(β−θ)M2n​​

    Note that for oblique shockwaves, the properties depend only on the normal component
    of the upstream Mach number. Thus, the downstream Mach number computed using the
    normal shock properties is the normal component of the actual Mach number.

    Solving for properties across an expansion wave:

    • Determine deflection angle θ, which is with respect to the upstream flow direction
    • For the given M1​, obtain ν(M1​) and μ1​ from the “Prandtl-Meyer Function” table
    • Compute ν(M2​) using the Prandtl-Meyer equation θ=ν(M2​)−ν(M1​)
    • Obtain properties corresponding to ν(M2​) from “Isentropic Flow Properties” table
    • Compute the properties downstream of the expansion wave

    Note that expansion waves are isentropic, thus, p01​​=p02​​, ρ01​​=ρ02​​, and T01​​=T02​​.

    #Compressibility Corrections

    Prandtl-Glauert correction:

    Cp​=1−M∞​2​Cp0​​​

    Cl​=1−M∞​2​Cl0​​​

    Cm​=1−M∞​2​Cm0​​​

    where Cp0​​ is the pressure coefficient under incompressible flow conditions and
    Cp​ is the predicted pressure coefficient under compressible flow conditions

    Karmen-Tsien correction:

    Cp​=1−M∞​2​+2Cp0​​​(1+1−M∞​2​M∞​2​)Cp0​​​

    Laitone correction:

    Cp​=1−M∞​2​+Cp0​​⋅21−M∞​2​M∞​2[1+21​(γ−1)M∞​2]​Cp0​​​

    #Critical Mach Number

    Pressure coefficient at any point in the flow:

    CpA​​=γM∞​22​{[1+21​(γ−1)MA​21+21​(γ−1)M∞​2​]γ−1γ​−1}

    Pressure coefficient at the point where MA​=1:

    Cpcr​​=γMcr​22​{[1+21​(γ−1)1+21​(γ−1)Mcr​2​]γ−1γ​−1}

    When freestream Mach number reaches the critical Mach number, there will be a point in the
    flow whereby the local Mach number is sonic. Thus, the pressure coefficient at that point is
    the critical pressure coefficient (minimum pressure coefficient).
