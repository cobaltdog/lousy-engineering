    Review of Fluid Mechanics

    #The Total Derivative

    Consider a particle moving along a path with a velocity VA​.
    The particle’s velocity is a function of its location and the time.

    VA​=VA​(rA​, t)=VA​[xA​(t), yA​(t), zA​(t), t]

    Acceleration of a particle is the time rate of change of velocity. Since velocity is a function of both position and time, acceleration may change due to the change in time and a change in
    the particle’s position.

    Using chain rule of differentiation to obtain the acceleration of particle A,

    aA​=dtdVA​​=∂t∂VA​​+∂x∂VA​​dtdxA​​+∂y∂VA​​dtdyA​​+∂z∂VA​​dtdzA​​

    aA​=∂t∂VA​​+uA​∂x∂VA​​+vA​∂y∂VA​​+wA​∂z∂VA​​

    Thus, for any particle, its acceleration is given by,

    💡
    a=∂t∂V​+u∂x∂V​+v∂y∂V​+w∂z∂V​

    Acceleration in the x-direction: ax​=∂t∂u​+u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​+w∂z∂u​

    Acceleration in the y-direction: ay​=∂t∂v​+u∂x∂v​+v∂y∂v​+w∂z∂v​

    Acceleration in the z-direction: az​=∂t∂w​+u∂x∂w​+v∂y∂w​+w∂z∂w​

    The above equation can be written in the shorthand notation,

    💡
    DtDV​=∂t∂V​+V⋅∇V

    where V=ui+vj+wk and the gradient operator ∇V=∂x∂V​i+∂y∂V​j+∂z∂V​k

    Breaking down the equation, the left-hand term represents the total acceleration, while the right-hand terms represents local (1st) and convective accelerations (2nd) respectively.

    #Strealimes, Streaklines, and Pathlines

    Streamline, streakline, and pathline:

    • Streamline — a line which is everywhere tangent to the velocity field
    • Streakline — a line consisting of all particles that originated from a common point
    • Pathline — a line traced out by a given particle as it flows from one point to another

    #Conservation of Mass

    Conservation of mass requires that the mass of a system remain constant as the system
    moves through a flow field. In equation form, it is expressed as the continuity principle.

    💡
    ∂t∂​∫CV​ ρ dVˉ+∫CS​ ρVcosθ dA=0

    where the continuity equation can be applied to a finite control volume

    For a fixed, non-deforming finite control volume, the equation simplifies to,

    💡
    ∑m˙out​−∑m˙in​=0

    The time rate of change of the mass of contents of the control volume becomes zero.

    #Differential Form of Continuity Equation

    We will now take the control volume as an infinitesimal fluid element. At the center of
    the fluid element, the fluid density is ρ, and the velocity has components u, v, and v.

    Since the element is small, the volume integral can be expressed as,

    💡
    ∂t∂​∫CV​ ρ dVˉ≈∂t∂ρ​δxδyδz

    The above represents the first term of the continuity equation.

    The massflow rate through the surfaces of the element can be obtained by considering the
    flow in each of the coordinate directions separately. Let ρu represent the x-component of massflow rate at the center of the element.

    Thus, he net massflow rate in the x-direction is given by,

    m˙x​=[ρu+∂x∂(ρu)​2δx​]δyδz−[ρu−∂x∂(ρu)​2δx​]δyδz

    m˙x​=∂x∂(ρu)​δxδyδz

    Similarly, the net massflow rate in the y- and z-directions are,

    m˙y​=∂y∂(ρv)​δxδyδz

    m˙z​=∂z∂(ρw)​δxδyδz

    Combining the net massflow rate in all directions,

    m˙net​=[∂x∂(ρu)​+∂y∂(ρv)​+∂z∂(ρw)​]δxδyδz

    Hence, the differential form of the equation is,

    💡
    ∂t∂ρ​+∂x∂(ρu)​+∂y∂(ρv)​+∂z∂(ρw)​=0

    The above equation is valid for both incompressible and compressible flows.

    For incompressible fluids, since ρ is a constant,

    💡
    ∂x∂u​+∂y∂v​+∂z∂w​=0
    💡
    ∇⋅V=0

    #Conservation of Momentum

    The equation for conservation of momentum is from Newton’s second law,

    💡
    F=mdtdV​

    #Navier-Stokes Equations

    The principal forces that act on a fluid element are, the body forces, which act directly
    on the mass of the element, and pressure and shear forces, which act on its surface.

    A body force is a force that acts throughout the volume of a body such as gravity.

    The shear stressess are assigned subscripts. The first subscript indicates the direction of a surface’s normal, while the second subscript indicates the direction in which the stress acts.

    Let fx​ be the body force per unit mass in the x-direction. The force in the x-direction is,

    Fx​=ρfx​δxδy         +[τxx​+∂x∂​(τxx​)2δx​]δy−[τxx​−∂x∂​(τxx​)2δx​]δy         +[τyx​+∂y∂​(τyx​)2δy​]δx−[τyx​−∂y∂​(τyx​)2δy​]δx

    Fx​=ρfx​δxδy+∂x∂​(τxx​)δxδy+∂y∂​(τyx​)δxδy

    Including the shear stress in the z-direction,

    Fx​=ρfx​δxδyδz+∂x∂​(τxx​)δxδyδz+∂y∂​(τyx​)δxδyδz+∂z∂​(τzx​)δxδyδz

    From Newton’s second law,

    max​=ρδxδyδz⋅dtdu​

    max​=ρδxδyδz[∂t∂u​+V⋅∇u]

    Equating the two and dividing by volume of the element,

    ρ∂t∂u​+ρ(V⋅∇u)=ρfx​+∂x∂​(τxx​)+∂y∂​(τyx​)+∂z∂​(τzx​)

    ρ(∂t∂u​+V⋅∇u)=ρfx​+∂x∂​(τxx​)+∂y∂​(τyx​)+∂z∂​(τzx​)

    ρDtDu​=ρfx​+∂x∂​(τxx​)+∂y∂​(τyx​)+∂z∂​(τzx​)

    Similarly, the equations for the y- and z-directions are,

    ρDtDv​=ρfy​+∂x∂​(τxy​)+∂y∂​(τyy​)+∂z∂​(τzy​)

    ρDtDw​=ρfz​+∂x∂​(τxz​)+∂y∂​(τyz​)+∂z∂​(τzz​)

    Representing normal stresses in terms of pressure, viscous strains, and viscosity,

    τxx​=−p−32​μ∇⋅V+2μ∂x∂u​=−p+μ(2∂x∂u​−32​∇⋅V)

    τyy​=−p−32​μ∇⋅V+2μ∂y∂v​=−p+μ(2∂y∂v​−32​∇⋅V)

    τzz​=−p−32​μ∇⋅V+2μ∂z∂w​=−p+μ(2∂z∂w​−32​∇⋅V)

    Representing shear stresses as a product of viscosity and velocity gradients,

    τxy​=τyx​=μ(∂y∂u​+∂x∂v​)

    τxz​=τzx​=μ(∂z∂u​+∂x∂w​)

    τyz​=τyz​=μ(∂z∂v​+∂y∂w​)

    Substituting the stress terms into the above equations,

    💡
    ρDtDu​=ρgx​−∂x∂p​            +∂x∂​[μ(2∂x∂u​−32​∇⋅V)]            +∂y∂​[μ(∂z∂u​+∂x∂w​)]+∂z∂​[μ(∂z∂u​+∂x∂w​)]
    💡
    ρDtDv​=ρgy​−∂y∂p​            +∂x∂​[μ(∂y∂u​+∂x∂v​)]            +∂y∂​[μ(2∂z∂v​−32​∇⋅V)]+∂z∂​[μ(∂z∂v​+∂y∂w​)]
    💡
    ρDtDw​=ρgz​−∂z∂p​            +∂x∂​[μ(∂z∂u​+∂x∂w​)]            +∂y∂​[μ(∂z∂v​+∂y∂w​)]+∂z∂​[μ(2∂z∂w​−32​∇⋅V)]

    Assuming gravity is the only body force, ρf becomes ρg.

    To simplify the Navier-Stokes equations, viscosity can be taken to be negligible.

    💡
    ρDtDu​=ρ(∂t∂u​+u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​+w∂z∂u​)=ρgx​−∂x∂p​
    💡
    ρDtDv​=ρ(∂t∂v​+u∂x∂v​+v∂y∂v​+w∂z∂v​)=ρgy​−∂y∂p​
    💡
    ρDtDw​=ρ(∂t∂w​+u∂x∂w​+v∂y∂w​+w∂z∂w​)=ρgz​−∂z∂p​

    The above equations are known as the Euler equations.

    #Conservation of Energy

    The simplest equation is the Bernoulli’s equation.

    💡
    p1​+21​ρu1​2+ρgz1​=p2​+21​ρu2​2+ρgz2​

    Equation is valid only for inviscid and incompressible flows.
