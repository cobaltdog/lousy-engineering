    Oblique Shockwaves

    #Shockwaves

    In general, a shockwave will make an oblique angle with respect to the upstream flow.
    A normal shockwave is simply a special case of the family of oblique shocks, whereby
    the wave angle is 90° with respect to the flow direction.

    Consider a supersonic flow over a concave corner.

    • Flow at the wall must be tangent to the wall
    • Streamlines are deflected upward by angle θ
    • Supersonic flow is “turned into itself”

    Across the wave:

    • Mach number discontinuously decreases
    • p, ρ, and T discontinuously increase

    The originally horizontal streamlines ahead of the wave are uniformly deflected in crossing
    the wave, such that streamlines behind the wave are parallel to each other and inclined
    upward by the deflection angle     θ.

    Consider a supersonic flow over a convex corner.

    • Flow at the wall must be tangent to the wall
    • Streamlines are deflected downward by angle θ
    • Supersonic flow is “turned away from itself”
    • An expansion wave (shape of a fan) occurs
    • Fan, centered at the corner, continuously
      opens in the direction away from corner

    Across the wave:

    • Mach number increases
    • p, ρ, and T decrease

    The originally horizontal streamlines ahead of the expansion wave are deflected smoothly
    and continuously through the fan such that streamlines behind the wave are parallel to
    each other and inclined downward by the deflection angle     θ.

    #Mach Angle

    When U>a, the wave fronts, family of circular sound waves, form a disturbance
    envelope given by the straight line BC — Mach cone. The angle that the Mach
    cone makes with respect to the direction of motion is the Mach angle μ.

    Mach angle can be determined from geometry.

    sinμ=Utat​=Ua​=M1​

    💡
    μ=sin−1M1​

    If the disturbances are stronger than a sound wave, the wave front becomes stronger
    than a Mach cone, creating an oblique shockwave at an angle β to the freestream,
    where β>μ. Thus, the Mach cone is an infinitely weak oblique shock.

    #Oblique Shock (Concave Corner)

    Consider the normal component of the upstream Mach number M1n​.

    💡
    M1n​=M1​sinβ

    Similar to the ratios for a normal shockwave, for an oblique shockwave,

    M2n​2=γM1n​2−21​(γ−1)1+21​(γ−1)M1n​2​

    p1​p2​​=1+γ+12γ​(M1n​2−1)

    ρ1​ρ2​​=2+(γ−1)M1n​2(γ+1)M1n​2​

    The above equations state that oblique shockwave properties depend only on normal
    component of the upstream Mach number, which itself is dependent on     M1​ and β.

    The downstream Mach number is,

    💡
    M2​=sin(β−θ)M2n​​

    From the geometry,

    tanβ=w1​U1​​

    tan(β−θ)=w2​U2​​

    where w is the tangential velocity of the flow across an oblique shockwave

    Since tangential component of flow velocity is constant, w1​=w2​,

    tanβtan(β−θ)​=U1​U2​​=ρ2​ρ1​​

    tanβtan(β−θ)​=(γ+1)M1n​22+(γ−1)M1n​2​

    tanβtan(β−θ)​=(γ+1)M1​2sin2β2+(γ−1)M1​2sin2β​

    #Physical Phenomena of Oblique Shockwaves

    For any given upstream Mach number M1​:

    • A maximum deflection angle θmax​
    • If physical geometry such that θ>θmax​, an oblique shockwave cannot exist
    • Nature establishes a curved shockwave, detached from the corner or the nose

    For any given θ less than θmax​:

    • Formation of two straight oblique shockwaves for a given upstream Mach number
    • Smaller β is called the weak shock and larger β is called the strong shock
    • Mach number downstream of a weak shock is supersonic 1<M2​<M1​
    • Mach number downstream of a strong shock is always subsonic M2​<1
    • Straight, attached oblique shockwave is generally a weak shock

    For a given Mach number, the larger the wave angle β, the larger the M1n​, and the larger
    the pressure ratio     p1​p2​​. Thus, the larger-angle shockwave will compress the air more and is termed the strong shock.

    For a given deflection angle θ:

    • As freestream Mach number M1​ increases, wave angle β decreases
    • Larger M1​ compensates for the decreased β, thus it is a stronger shock

    For a given Mach number M1​:

    • As deflection angle θ increases, wave angle β also increases
    • Normal component of Mach number M1n​ also increases
    • Stronger shock is produced

    However, once θ exceeds θmax​, the shockwave becomes detached.

    #Prandtl-Meyer Expansion Waves

    Expansion fan is bounded upstream by a Mach wave which makes the angle μ1​ with respect to the upstream flow.

    It is also bounded downstream by another Mach wave which makes the angle μ2​ with respect to the downstream flow.

    Since the expansion through the wave takes place across a continuous succession of Mach waves, and since ds=0 for each Mach wave, the expansion is isentropic.

    Consider a very weak wave produced by an infinitesimally small flow deflection dθ. The
    flow velocity behind the wave is U+dU inclined at angle dθ, represented by line AC.

    Any change in velocity across a wave takes place normal to the wave. Then, the line BC represents the line along which a change in velocity occurs, since it is normal to the wave.

    Applying the sine rule to triangle ABC,

    💡
    UU+dU​=sin(2π​−μ−dθ)sin(2π​+μ)​

    From trigonometric identities,

    sin(2π​+μ)=sin(2π​−μ)=cosμ

    sin(2π​−μ−dθ)=cos(μ+dθ)=cosμ⋅cos(dθ)−sinμ⋅sin(dθ)

    Substituting the identities into the above equation,

    1+UdU​=cosμ⋅cos(dθ)−sinμ⋅sin(dθ)cosμ​

    For small dθ, we can make small-angle assumptions,

    1+UdU​=cosμ−dθ⋅sinμcosμ​

    1+UdU​=1−dθ⋅tanμ1​

    Since the function 1−x1​ can be expanded in a power series,

    1+UdU​=1+dθ⋅tanμ+...

    💡
    dθ=tanμdU/U​

    Since Mach angle μ=sin−1M1​,

    tanμ=M2−1​1​

    Substituting tanμ into dθ,

    💡
    dθ=M2−1​UdU​

    U=Ma

    #Shock Expansion Theory

    #Flat Plate

    Consider a flat plate of length c at an angle of attack α in supersonic flow.

    On the top surface, the flow is turned away from itself and an expansion wave occurs at
    the leading edge such that p2​<p1​. At the trailing edge, for the flow to return to the freestream direction, it is turned into itself and an oblique shockwave occurs.

    On the bottom surface, the flow is turned into itself and an oblique shockwave occurs
    at the leading edge such that p3​>p1​. At the trailing edge, the flow is turned into the freestream direction by means of an expansion wave.

    The top and bottom surfaces experience uniform pressure distributions of p2​ and p3​.
    Since p3​>p2​, there is a net pressure imbalance that generates the resultant force R.

    💡
    R′=(p3​−p2​)c
    💡
    L′=(p3​−p2​)c⋅cosα
    💡
    D′=(p3​−p2​)c⋅sinα

    #Wedge

    Consider a diamond-wedge airfoil at zero angle of attack in a supersonic flow.

    The flow is first compressed and deflected through the angle ε by the oblique shockwave
    at the leading edge. At the midchord, the flow is expanded through an angle 2ε, creating an expansion wave. At the trailing edge, the flow is turned back into the freestream direction through another oblique shockwave.

    In the lift direction, the pressure distributions on the top and bottom faces exactly cancel out such that L′=0. In the drag direction, however, there is a finite drag due to the inclined pressure distributions.

    D′=2(p2​⋅l⋅sinε−p3​⋅l⋅sinε)

    D′=2(p2​−p3​)2t​

    💡
    D′=(p2​−p3​)t
