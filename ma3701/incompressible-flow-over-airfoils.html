    Incompressible Flow over Airfoils

    #Circulation Theory of Lift

    #Kutta-Joukowski Theorem

    Let curve A be any curve in the flow enclosing the airfoil.

    If the airfoil is producing lift, velocity field around the airfoil will be such that the line
    integral of velocity around A will be finite whereby the circulation Γ=∮A​ V⋅ds.

    Thus, the lift per unit span on the airfoil will be given by the Kutta-Joukowski theorem,

    💡
    L′=ρ∞​U∞​Γ

    The theorem states that lift per unit span on a 2-dimensional body
    is directly proportional to the circulation around the body.

    #Vortex Sheet

    Consider a point vortex of strength Γ located at a given point O.

    Imagine a straight line perpendicular to the page, going through point O, and extending
    to infinity into and out of the page, forming a vortex filament of strength Γ.

    Flow induced in any plane perpendicular to
    the filament by the filament is identical to
    that induced by a point vortex.

    Flows perpendicular to the filament at points
    O and O′ are identical to each other and are identical to the flow induced by a point vortex.

    Now, consider an infinite number of straight vortex filaments side-by-side, where the strength of each filament is infinitesimally small. These side-by-side vortex filaments
    form a vortex sheet.

    Let s be the distance measured along the vortex sheet in the edge view.
    Let γ=γ(s) be the strength of the vortex sheet, per unit span, along s.

    💡
    L′=ρ∞​U∞​ ∫ab​γ ds

    The circulation around a vortex sheet is the sum of the elemental vortices.

    Imagine that the airfoil is made very thin. If you were to stand back and look at such a
    thin airfoil from a distance, the portions of the sheet on the top and bottom surface of
    the airfoil would almost coincide. Thus, we can approximate a thin airfoil with a single vortex sheet distributed over the camber line of the airfoil.

    #The Kutta Condition

    For a given airfoil at a given angle of attack, there is an infinite number of valid
    theoretical solutions, corresponding to an infinite number of values for Γ. But,
    we know that an airfoil at a given angle of attack produces a single value of lift.
    Thus, we need an additional condition that fixes Γ for a given airfoil at a given α.

    As the flow begins to develop, the flow tries to curl around the sharp trailing edge from the bottom surface to the top surface. This is not tolerated
    very long by nature.

    As the real flow develops, the stagnation point on
    the upper surface moves toward the trailing edge.

    Once the flow has developed and steady flow is reached, the flow is smoothly leaving the top
    and the bottom surfaces at the leading edge.

    The Kutta condition can be summarized as:

    • For a given airfoil at a given angle of attack, the value of Γ around
      the airfoil is such that the flow leaves the trailing edge smoothly.
    • If the trailing edge angle is finite, then the trailing edge is a stagnation point.
    • If the trailing edge is cusped, then the velocities leaving the top and bottom
      surfaces at the trailing edge are finite and equal in magnitude and direction.

    #Thin Airfoil Theory

    Assumptions of thin airfoil theory:

    • Inviscid flow (theory is based on potential flow theory)
    • Lift characteristics below stall are negligibly affected by boundary leayer
    • Airfoil is operating at a small angle of attack where no flow separation occurs
    • Very thin airfoils — maximum mean camber is small and ct​ is small

    The boundary layer is assumed to be thin and does not cause the flow to separate when it encounters an adverse pressure gradient. For thin airfoil theory, the effects of thickness
    will be neglected. Instead, the airfoil will be represented by its mean camber line.

    If the airfoil is thin, the camber line is close to
    the chord line. Thus, the vortex sheet appears
    to fall approximately on the chord line.

    The strength of the vortex sheet on the chord
    line is determined such that the camber line is
    a streamline.

    For a camber line to be a streamline, the component of velocity normal to the camber line
    must be zero at all points along the camber line. Velocity at any point in the flow is the
    sum of the uniform freestream velocity and the velocity induced by the vortex sheet.

    💡
    U∞, n​+w′(s)=0

    where U∞, n​ is the component of freestream velocity normal to camber line and
    w′(s) is the component of velocity normal to camber line induced by the vortex sheet

    At any point P on the camber line, the slope of the camber line is dxdz​.

    U∞, n​=U∞​sin[α+tan−1(−dxdz​)]

    At small angles of attack, both α and tan−1(−dxdz​) are small values. Using
    the approximation that sinθ≈tanθ≈θ, where θ is expressed in radians.

    U∞, n​=U∞​(α−dxdz​)

    Let w(x) denote the component of velocity normal to the camber line induced by
    the vortex sheet. Since the airfoil is thin, the camber line is close to the chord line,

    w′(s)≈w(x)

    Consider an elemental vortex of strength γ dζ located at a distance ζ from the origin
    along the chord line. The strength of the vortex sheet γ varies with the distance along
    the chord; γ=γ(ζ).

    The velocity dw at point x induced by the elemental vortex at point γ is,

    dw=−2π(x−ζ)γ(ζ) dζ​

    Thus, the velocity induced at point x by all elemental vortices along the chord line is,

    w(x)=−∫0c​2π(x−ζ)γ(ζ) dζ​

    Substituting the terms back into the initial equation,

    U∞, n​+w′(s)=0

    U∞​(α−dxdz​)−∫0c​2π(x−ζ)γ(ζ) dζ​=0

    💡
    2π1​∫0c​x−ζγ(ζ) dζ​=U∞​(α−dxdz​)

    The final equation simply states that the camber line is a streamline of the flow. Note
    that     ζ is simply a dummy variable of integration which varies from 0 to c along the
    chord line. The vortex strength     γ=γ(ζ) is a variable along the chord line.

    #Symmetric Airfoil

    As a symmetric airfoil has no camber, the camber line is coincident with the
    chord line. Thus, the term dxdz​=0 and the equation can be reduced to,

    💡
    2π1​∫0c​x−ζγ(ζ) dζ​=U∞​α

    To integrate the above equation, we need to transform ζ into θ using,

    ζ=2c​(1−cosθ)

    dζ=2c​sinθ dθ

    Since x is a fixed point, it corresponds to a particular value of θ, namely θ0​.
    Thus, θ corresponds to a location along the chord of the airfoil.

    x=2c​(1−cosθ0​)

    Substituting the transformed variables,

    2π1​∫0π​cosθ−cosθ0​γ(θ)sinθ dθ​=U∞​α

    The solution to the integral is,

    💡
    γ(θ)=2αU∞​(sinθ1+cosθ​)

    whereby at the trailing edge, θ=π

    #Lift Coefficient

    The total circulation around the airfoil using variable transformation is,

    Γ=∫0c​γ(ζ) dζ

    Γ=2c​∫0π​γ(θ)sinθ dθ

    Substituting the vortex sheet stength γ(θ) into Γ,

    Γ=αcU∞​∫0π​(1+cosθ) dθ

    Γ=παcU∞​

    Substituting Γ into the Kutta-Joukowski theorem,

    L′=ρ∞​U∞​(παcU∞​)

    💡
    L′=παcρ∞​U∞​2

    Assuming a wing span of 1 m, substitute L′ into the lift coefficient equation,

    Cl​=21​ρ∞​U∞​2SL′​

    Cl​=21​ρ∞​U∞​2(1×c)L′​

    Cl​=21​ρ∞​U∞​2cπαcρ∞​U∞​2​

    💡
    Cl​=2πα

    whereby angle of attack α is expressed in radians

    The lift slope is then,

    💡
    dαdCl​​=2π/rad=0.11/°

    The equations state that the lift coefficient is linearly proportional to angle of attack, which is supported by experimental results. They also state that the theoretical lift
    slope is equal to     2π per degree radian of angle of attack.

    #Moment Coefficient

    Consider an elemental vortex of strength γ(ζ) dζ located at a distance ζ from the leading edge. The circulation associated with this elemental vortex is dΓ=γ(ζ) dζ. The lift dL contributed by the elemental vortex is dL=ρ∞​U∞​dΓ.

    The moment about the leading edge due to dL is,

    dM=−ζ⋅dL

    The total moment, per unit span, about the leading edge is then,

    MLE′​=−∫0c​ζ⋅dL

    MLE′​=−ρ∞​U∞​∫0c​ ζ⋅γ(ζ) dζ

    Using variable transformation and performing the integration,

    💡
    MLE′​=−(21​ρ∞​U∞​2)c22πα​

    Assuming a wing span of 1 m, substitute MLE′​ into moment coefficient equation,

    CmLE​​=21​ρ∞​U∞​2ScMLE​​

    CmLE​​=21​ρ∞​U∞​2c2MLE′​​

    💡
    CmLE​​=−2πα​

    Since Cl​=2πα, rearranging the lift coefficient and substituting into CmLE​​,

    CmLE​​=−4Cl​​

    The moment about the quarter-chord point is,

    Cmc/4​​=CmLE​​+4Cl​​

    Thus, substituting the moment coefficient about the leading edge,

    Cmc/4​​=−4Cl​​+4Cl​​=0

    The thin airfoil theory shows that the center of pressure for a symmetric airfoil is
    at the quarter-chord point. Furthermore, since the moment about the quarter
    -chord is independent of angle of attack, it is also the aerodynamic center.

    #Cambered Airfoil

    For a cambered airfoil, the term dxdz​ is finite. Using variable transformation,

    2π1​∫0π​cosθ−cosθ0​γ(θ)sinθ dθ​=U∞​(α−dxdz​)

    The solution to the integral is,

    💡
    γ(θ)=2U∞​[A0​sinθ1+cosθ​+n=1∑∞​An​sin(nθ)]

    The equation consists of a leading term similar to that for a symmetric airfoil, plus a
    Fourier sine series with coefficients     An​. The values of An​ depend on the shape of
    the camber line     dxdz​, while A0​ depends on both dxdz​ and α.

    To obtain the coefficents A0​ and An​, substitute γ(θ) into the transformed equation,

    π1​∫0π​cosθ−cosθ0​A0​(1+cosθ) dθ​+π1​n=1∑∞​∫0π​cosθ−cosθ0​An​sin(nθ)sinθ dθ​=α−dxdz​

    Reducing the above equation,

    A0​−n=1∑∞​An​cos(nθ0​)=α−dxdz​

    dxdz​=(α−A0​)+n=1∑∞​An​cos(nθ0​)

    Using Fourier analysis,

    💡
    A0​=α−π1​∫0π​dxdz​ dθ0​
    💡
    An​=π2​∫0π​dxdz​cos(nθ0​) dθ0​

    where dxdz​ is the camber of the airfoil

    #Lift Coefficient

    The total circulation due to the entire vortex sheet from the leading to trailing edge is,

    Γ=∫0c​γ(ζ) dζ

    Γ=2c​∫0π​γ(θ)sinθ dθ

    Substituting the vortex sheet strength γ(θ) into Γ,

    Γ=cU∞​[A0​∫0π​(1+cosθ) dθ+n=1∑∞​An​∫0π​sin(nθ)sinθ dθ]

    Γ=cU∞​(πA0​+2π​A1​)

    The lift per unit span is then,

    💡
    L′=ρ∞​U∞​2c(πA0​+2π​A1​)

    The lift coefficient is in the form,

    💡
    Cl​=π(2A0​+A1​)

    Substituting the equations for A0​ and An​ into the lift coefficient,

    💡
    Cl​=2π[α+π1​∫0π​dxdz​(cosθ0​−1) dθ0​]

    The lift slope is then,

    💡
    dαdCl​​=2π/rad=0.11/°

    Similar to a symmetrical airfoil, the theoretical lift slope of a cambered airfoil is equal to 2π per degree radian of angle of attack.

    #Zero-Lift Angle

    The angle of zero lift is denoted by αL=0​ and is a negative value.

    The lift slope from the graph is,

    dαdCl​​=α−αL=0​Cl​−0​

    Cl​=dαdCl​​(α−αL=0​)

    Cl​=2π(α−αL=0​)

    Comparing the lift coefficient equations,

    αL=0​=−π1​∫0π​dxdz​(cosθ0​−1) dθ0​

    αL=0​=−π1​∫0π​dxdz​cosθ0​−dxdz​ dθ0​

    αL=0​=−π1​∫0π​dxdz​cosθ0​ dθ0​+π1​∫0π​dxdz​ dθ0​

    💡
    αL=0​=−21​A1​+(α−A0​)

    The thin airfoil theory provides a means to predict the angle of zero lift. The more
    highly cambered the airfoil, the larger will be the absolute magnitude of     αL=0​.

    #Moment Coefficient

    Substituting vortex sheet strength γ(θ) into the total moment equation and solving,

    💡
    CmLE​​=−2π​(A0​+A1​−2A2​​)

    Substituting the lift coefficient,

    💡
    CmLE​​=−[4Cl​​+4π​(A1​−A2​)]

    Note that when dxdz​=0, A1​=A2​=0, and the equation reduces to the moment coefficient equation for a symmetric airfoil.

    Substituting the moment coefficient about the leading edge,

    Cmc/4​​=CmLE​​+4Cl​​

    Cmc/4​​=−[4Cl​​+4π​(A1​−A2​)]+4Cl​​

    💡
    Cmc/4​​=4π​(A2​−A1​)

    The equation shows that the quarter-chord point is not the center of pressure for a cambered airfoil. As Cmc/4​​ is independent of α, the quarter-chord is the theoretical location of the aerodynamic center for a cambered airfoil.

    The location of the center of pressure is given by,

    xcp​=−L′MLE′​​=−Cl​CmLE​​×c​

    💡
    xcp​=4c​[1+Cl​π​(A1​−A2​)]

    The equation shows that the center of pressure for a cambered airfoil varies with the
    lift coefficient. As the angle of attack changes, the center of pressure also changes.
