    Normal Shockwaves

    #Review of Thermodynamics

    #Ideal Gas

    For an ideal gas, pressure, density, and temperature are related through,

    💡
    p=ρRT

    where the gas constant Rs​=0.287 kJ/kg⋅K

    #Internal Energy and Enthalpy

    The internal energy per unit mass of gas is defined as the specific internal energy e.
    A related quantity is the specific enthalpy, denoted by h, and is defined as,

    💡
    h=e+pv

    For moderate temperatures, specific heats of ideal gases are relatively constant.

    💡
    e=cv​T
    💡
    h=cp​T

    For ideal gases, cp​ and cv​ are related through the equation,

    cp​−cv​=R

    1−cp​cv​​=cp​R​

    1−γ1​=cp​R​

    💡
    cp​=γ−1γR​

    cp​−cv​=R

    cv​cp​​−1=cv​R​

    γ−1=cv​R​

    💡
    cv​=γ−1R​

    #First Law of Thermodynamics

    Let dq be an incremental amount of heat added to the system across the boundary.
    Let dw denote the work done on the system by the surroundings.

    💡
    dq+dw=de

    where heat input and work input are positive

    For a reversible process,

    💡
    dq−p dv=de

    #Second Law of Thermodynamics

    To ascertain the proper direction of a process, we define the property, entropy.

    💡
    ds=Tdq​+dsgen​

    where dsgen​ is zero for an reversible process

    #T ds Relations

    Rewriting the first law of thermodynamics,

    dq−p dv=de

    T ds−p dv=de

    💡
    T ds=de+p dv

    Eliminating du using h=u+pv,

    dh=du+p dv+v dp

    dh=T ds+v dp

    💡
    T ds=dh−v dp

    #Entropy Change of Ideal Gases

    Substituting du=cv​ dT and p=vRT​ into the first relation,

    T ds=du+p dv

    T ds=cv​ dT+RT vdv​

    ds=cv​ TdT​+R vdv​

    Δs=cv​∫12​T1​ dT+R∫12​v1​ dv

    💡
    Δs=cv​ lnT1​T2​​+R lnv1​v2​​

    Substituting dh=cp​ dT and v=pRT​ into the second relation,

    T ds=dh−v dp

    T ds=cp​ dT−RT pdp​

    ds=cp​ TdT​−R pdp​

    Δs=cp​∫12​T1​ dT−R∫12​p1​ dp

    💡
    Δs=cp​ lnT1​T2​​−R lnp1​p2​​

    #Isentropic Relations of Ideal Gases

    For an isentropic process of an ideal gas, equate the above equations to zero.

    💡
    v1​v2​​=(T1​T2​​)−γ−11​
    💡
    p1​p2​​=(T1​T2​​)γ−1γ​

    Relating density to specific volume,

    v1​v2​​=ρ2​ρ1​​

    Substituting into the left equation,

    ρ2​ρ1​​=(T1​T2​​)−γ−11​

    (ρ2​ρ1​​)−γ=(T1​T2​​)γ−1γ​

    💡
    p1​p2​​=(ρ1​ρ2​​)γ

    #Speed of Sound and Shockwaves

    Consider a motionless point source of disturbance. Small disturbances generated by the
    point move outward in a spherically symmetric pattern. Distance between wave fronts is determined by the the frequency of the disturbance.

    The radius of a given wave front is given by,

    💡
    r=at

    where a is the speed of sound and t is time since the particular disturbance was generated

    Suppose the point source moves from right to left at a constant speed U which is less than
    the speed of sound a. A stationary observer ahead of the source will detect an increase in frequency of the sound, while an observer behind will detect a decrease in frequency. This
    is known as the Doppler effect.

    If U=a, the wave fronts coalesce at the position of the source. As U increases further,
    such that U>a, the wave fronts form a conical envelope — Mach cone. Outside of this
    cone is the “zone of silence”, where the pressure pulses have not arrived and an observer cannot sense the source.

    In subsonic flow, the effect of a disturbance propagates upstream of its location, and “warns”
    the upstream flow of the approach of the disturbance. In supersonic flow, there is no such
    warning as the disturbances cannot propagate upstream.

    #Derivation of the Speed of Sound

    Based on conservation of mass, the continuity equation for the control volume is,

    ρa=(ρ+dρ)(a+dU)

    ρa=ρa+a dρ+ρdU+dρdU

    a=−ρdρdU​

    Note that the product of two differentials dρdU can be neglected.

    Based on Newton’s second law, the conservation of momemtum is,

    p+ρa2=(p+dp)+(ρ+dρ)(a+dU)2

    p+ρa2=(p+dp)+(ρ+dρ)(a2+2a dU)

    dp=−2aρ dU−a2 dρ

    dU=−2aρdp+a2 dρ​

    Substituting dU into a,

    a=−ρ−2aρdρdp​+a2​

    a=2adρdp​+a2​

    a2=dρdp​

    Assuming flow through a sound wave is isentropic, dρdp​ is an isentropic change.

    💡
    a=(∂ρ∂p​)s​​

    From isentropic relations of an ideal gas,

    p1​p2​​=(ρ1​ρ2​​)γ

    ρ2​γp2​​=ρ1​γp1​​

    ργp​=const

    p=cργ

    Differentiating the equation for p,

    dρdp​=cγργ−1

    dρdp​=ργp​⋅γργ−1

    dρdp​=ργp​

    Substituting the result of the differential into a,

    💡
    a=ργp​​

    Since p=ρRT,

    💡
    a=γRT​

    #Shockwaves

    A shockwave is an extremely thin region across which the flow properties can change drastically.

    Normal shockwave:

    • Shockwave is normal to direction of the flow
    • Downstream flow is always subsonic

    Oblique shockwave:

    • Shockwave is at some angle to flow direction
    • Downstream flow is typically supersonic

    In some special cases, an oblique shock is strong enough to decelerate the downstream flow to a
    subsonic Mach number.

    #Basic Compressible Flow Equations

    For a steady, adiabatic, inviscid flow, the total enthalpy along a streamline is constant.

    💡
    h0​=h1​+21​U1​2=h2​+21​U2​2

    where U1​ and U2​ are velocities at any two points along a streamline

    For an ideal gas, since h=cp​T,

    cp​T0​=cp​T+21​U2

    cp​(T0​−T)=21​U2

    T0​−T=2cp​U2​

    💡
    TT0​​=1+2Tcp​U2​

    Substituting cp​=γ−1γR​ into the above equation,

    TT0​​=1+2TU2​⋅γRγ−1​

    TT0​​=1+2γRT(γ−1)U2​

    TT0​​=1+2a2(γ−1)U2​

    TT0​​=1+2γ−1​(aU​)2

    💡
    TT0​​=1+21​(γ−1)M2

    where T0​ is the total temperature and T is the static temperature

    Using isentropic relations of ideal gases,

    pp0​​=(ρρ0​​)γ=(TT0​​)γ−1γ​

    💡
    pp0​​=[1+21​(γ−1)M2]γ−1γ​
    💡
    ρρ0​​=[1+21​(γ−1)M2]γ−11​

    The above equations show that these ratios are dependent on Mach number only.
    The equations are applicable only for an isentropic compression of a flow.

    #Properties of a Normal Shockwave

    #Pressure Ratio across Shockwave

    Consider the conservation of momentum equation used to derive the speed of sound.

    p1​+ρ1​U1​2=p2​+ρ2​U2​2

    p1​−p2​=ρ2​U2​2−ρ1​U1​2

    Rearranging the Mach number equation,

    M=aU​=γRT​U​=γp/ρ​U​

    M2=γpρU2​

    ρU2=γpM2

    Substituting into the conservation of momentum equation,

    p1​−p2​=γp2​M2​2−γp1​M1​2

    1−p1​p2​​=γp1​p2​​M2​2−γM1​2

    p1​p2​​+γp1​p2​​M2​2=1+γM1​2

    💡
    p1​p2​​=1+γM2​21+γM1​2​

    #Temperature Ratio across Shockwave

    Assuming no heat transfer across the shockwave, total enthalpy is constant.

    h1​+21​U1​2=h2​+21​U2​2

    cp​T1​+21​U1​2=cp​T2​+21​U2​2

    cp​(T2​−T1​)=21​(U1​2−U2​2)

    Rearranging the Mach number equation,

    M=aU​=γRT​U​

    M2=γRTU2​

    U2=M2(γRT)

    Substituting into the total enthalpy equation,

    cp​(T2​−T1​)=21​(M1​2γRT1​−M2​2γRT2​)

    cp​(T1​T2​​−1)=21​(M1​2γR−M2​2γRT1​T2​​)

    T1​T2​​(cp​+21​M2​2γR)=cp​+21​M1​2γR

    T1​T2​​(γ−1γR​+21​M2​2γR)=γ−1γR​+21​M1​2γR

    💡
    T1​T2​​=1+21​(γ−1)M2​21+21​(γ−1)M1​2​

    #Mach Number of Downstream Flow

    Recall the ideal gas equation p=ρRT.

    T1​T2​​=p1​ρ2​p2​ρ1​​=p1​U1​p2​U2​​

    T1​T2​​=p1​p2​​⋅M1​γRT1​​M2​γRT2​​​

    T1​T2​​=(p1​M1​p2​M2​​)2

    1+21​(γ−1)M2​21+21​(γ−1)M1​2​=(1+γM2​21+γM1​2​)2M1​2M2​2​

    💡
    M2​2=γM1​2−21​(γ−1)1+21​(γ−1)M1​2​

    The Mach number behind the wave is a function only of the Mach number ahead.

    Substituting M2​2 into previous ratios,

    💡
    p1​p2​​=1+γ+12γ​(M1​2−1)
    💡
    T1​T2​​=[1+γ+12γ​(M1​2−1)][(γ+1)M1​22+(γ−1)M1​2​]
    💡
    ρ1​ρ2​​=2+(γ−1)M1​2(γ+1)M1​2​

    #Entropy Change across Shockwave

    Recall that entropy change of an ideal gas is,

    Δs=cp​ lnT1​T2​​−R lnp1​p2​​

    Substituting the pressure and temperature ratios,

    💡
    Δs=cp​ ln{[1+γ+12γ​(M1​2−1)][(γ+1)M1​22+(γ−1)M1​2​]}           −R ln[1+γ+12γ​(M1​2−1)]

    When M1​=1, there is no change in entropy across the shockwave.

    When M1​>1, there is an increase in entropy across the shockwave.

    When M1​<1, there is a decrease in entropy across the shockwave.

    However, a decrease in entropy would violate the second law of thermodynamics.
    Hence, it implies that a normal shockwave can only occur in supersonic flow.

    Normal shockwaves are not isentropic as:

    • Large velocity and temperature changes within a very thin region
    • Friction and thermal conduction render them irreversible
