    Airfoil and Wing Characteristics

    #Characteristics of NACA Airfoils

    #NACA 4-Digit Series

    Description of series:

    • First digit specifies the maximum camber in percentage of chord
    • Second digit ×10 indicates the position of maximum camber from leading edge
    • Last two digits provide the maximum thickness of the airfoil in percentage of chord

    The NACA 2415 airfoil has a maximum camber of 2% chord length, which is located 40%
    of chord length from the airfoil leading edge, and has a maximum thickness of 15%.

    The equation for a symmetric 4-digit airfoil is,

    💡
    y=0.2t​⋅c[0.2969cx​​−0.126(cx​)                   −0.3516(cx​)2+0.2483(cx​)3−0.1015(cx​)4]

    where y is the half-thickness of the aerofoil and x along the chord from leading edge

    The equations for a cambered 4-digit airfoil are,

    💡
    yc​=m⋅p2x​(2p−cx​) where 0<x<p
    💡
    yc​=m⋅(1−p)2c−x​(1+cx​−2p) where p<x<c

    where m is the maximum camber as fraction of the chord and
    p is location of maximum camber as fraction of the chord

    #NACA 5-Digit Series

    Description of series:

    • First digit ×0.15 gives the design lift coefficient
    • Second and third digits ×0.5 give the position of maximum camber
    • Fourth and fifth digits give the position of maximum thickness

    The NACA 23012 airfoil has a design lift coefficient of 0.3, a maximum camber located
    15% of chord length from the airfoil leading edge, and a maximum thickness of 12%.

    #NACA 6-Digit Series

    Description of series:

    • First digit denotes the series and indicates this is designed for greater laminar flow
    • Second digit ×10 gives the position of minimum pressure in percentage chord
    • Third digit ×0.1 gives the acceptable range of Cl​ for low drag performance
    • Fourth digit ×0.1 gives the design lift coefficient
    • Last two digits gives the location of maximum thickness

    The NACA 632-315 airfoil has a minimum pressure located 30% of chord length from the leading edge, has a 0.3 design lift coefficient, ±0.2 of design lift coefficient for low drag performance, and maximum thickness located 15% of chord length.

    #Airfoil Characteristics

    Description of lift curve:

    • At low to moderate angles of attack, Cl​ varies linearly with α (lift slope)
    • As α becomes large, flow tends to separate from the surface of the airfoil
    • Flow separation results in a decrease in lift and an increase in drag

    As angle of attack increases beyond stall angle, turbulence re-energizes the boundary
    layer, causing the flow to reattach to the surface of the airfoil. Thus, there is a gradual increase in lift coefficient.

    #Aerodynamic Forces and Moments

    Consider the forces and moments in the plane of symmetry (the pitch plane).

    Lift is perpendicular to the U∞​ while drag is parallel to U∞​. Resovling these two forces in the horizontal and vertical axes gives us body-oriented components of forces N and A. These components are the normal force, perpendicular to the vehicle axis, and the axial force, parallel to the vehicle axis.

    💡
    L=Ncosα−Asinα
    💡
    D=Nsinα+Acosα

    Taking summation of moments about the leading edge,

    💡
    MLE​=Mc/4​−4c​L

    Note that a nose-up moment is positive and a nose-down moment is negative.

    #Characteristics of Wings

    A wing is a three-dimensional shape made up of two-dimensional airfoil sections. By
    placing the airfoil sections in various spanwise combinations, wings, horizontal and
    vertical stabilizers, canards, and/or other lifting surfaces are formed.

    • Wing span b is the straight-line distance measured from wing tip to wing tip
    • Average chord cˉ is determined such that the product b×cˉ=S
    • Aspect ratio is the ratio of the span to chord length AR=Sb2​
    • Taper ratio is the ratio of tip chord to root chord λ=cr​ct​​
    • Sweep angle Λ is the angle between the quarter chord line and root chord
    • Washout — angle of incidence decreases from the root to tip
    • Washin — angle of incidence increases from root to tip

      The angle of incidence is the angle of the airfoil relative to the vehicle axis.

    #The Delta Wing

    A special case of swept wings is the delta wing.

    Though delta-winged aircraft are high-speed aircraft, these aircraft spend a majority of their flight time at subsonic speeds — takeoff, landing. Thus, the low-speed aerodynamics of delta wings are of great importance.

    Planform area of a delta wing is,

    💡
    S=21​bcr​

    tanΛLE​=b/2cr​​

    cr​=2b​ tanΛLE​

    💡
    S=4b2​ tanΛLE​

    AR=Sb2​

    💡
    AR=tanΛLE​4​

    A counter-rotating vortex pair forms along the highly swept leading edges, which are a
    source of high energy and relatively high-vorticity flow. Thus, the local static pressure is
    low, creating a strong “suction” on the top surface near the leading edges. The suction
    effect of the vortices enhances lift.

    At a high enough angle of attack, the vortices begin to fall apart somewhere along the length of the vortex; this is called vortex breakdown. When vortex breakdown occurs,
    the lift and pitching moment of the delta wing decrease, the flow becomes unsteady,
    and buffeting of the wing occurs; turbulent airflow over the wing.
