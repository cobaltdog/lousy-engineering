    Crystal Structure of Solids

    #Crystal Structures

    Solid materials are classified according to the regularity of atom arrangement.

    • Crystalline material
      • Atoms are arranged in a repeating array over large atomic distances
      • Long-range order exists, such that upon solidification, atoms will
        position themselves in a repetitive 3-dimensional pattern
    • Non-crystalline/amorphous material
      • Only short-range order exists
      • Irregular arrangement of atoms

    Crystal structures are categorized
    based on unit cell configurations.

    • Cubic
    • Hexagonal
    • Tetragonal
    • Rhombohedral
    • Monoclinic
    • Triclinic

    #Metallic Crystal Structures

    Crystal structures for most common metals:

    • Face-centered cubic (FCC)
    • Body-centered cubic (BCC)
    • Hexagonal closed-packed (HCP)

    An important characteristic of a crystal structure is the coordination number. For metals,
    each atom has the same number of nearest-neighbor (touching atoms). For an FCC unit
    cell, the front face atom touches 4 corner atoms surrounding it and 4 face atoms, and
    another 4 face atoms in the next unit cell to the front.

    Atomic packing factor (APF) is the sum of the sphere volumes
    of all atoms within a cell unit divided by the unit cell volume.

    💡
    APF=Unit Cell VolumeVolume of Atoms​

    Density can be computed using knowledge of the crystal structure.

    💡
    ρ=Vcell​NA​nA​

    where n is the number of atoms in each unit cell and A is the atomic weight

    #Face-Centered Cubic (FCC)

    Description of FCC unit cell:

    • Atoms located at each corner and at the centers of each cube face
    • Coordination number of 12
    • Atomic packing factor of 0.74
    • Each unit cell has 4 atoms

    Relation between unit cell length a and atomic radius R can be obtained through geometry.

    💡
    a=2​4R​

    Computing the atomic packing factor,

    Vatoms​=4×34​πR3=16.755R3

    Vcell​=a3=(2​4R​)3=22.627R3

    APF=22.627R316.755R3​=0.74

    #Body-Centered Cubic (BCC)

    Description of BCC unit cell:

    • Atoms located at all eight corners and a single atom at the cube center
    • Coordination number of 8 (center atom touches eight corner atoms)
    • Atomic packing factor of 0.68
    • Each unit cell has 2 atoms

    Relation between unit cell length a and atomic radius R can be obtained through geometry.

    💡
    a=3​4R​

    Computing the atomic packing factor,

    Vatoms​=2×34​πR3=8.378R3

    Vcell​=a3=(3​4R​)3=12.32R3

    APF=12.32R38.378R3​=0.68

    #Hexagonal Closed-Packed (HCP)

    Description of HCP unit cell:

    • Atom at each of the 12 corners, 2 atoms at top and bottom faces
    • 3 additional atoms located on a plane between the top and bottom faces
    • Coordination number of 12
    • Atomic packing factor of 0.74
    • Each unit cell has 6 atoms

    #Crystallographic Indices

    #Crystallographic Directions

    Description of crystallographic direction:

    • Defined as a line directed between two points, a vector
    • Resolved along each axes and to the smallest integer
    • Represented by 3 indices [U,V,W]

    Note that direction indices are denoted by square brackets. Negative indices are
    represented by a bar over the appropriate index. For example,     [11ˉ1] direction
    has a component in the     −y direction.

    #Crystallographic Planes

    Description of Miller indices:

    • Represents a specific lattice of atoms
    • Obtained by taking reciprocal of the x, y, and z intercepts of the plane

    Note that Miller indices are denoted by parentheses. Similarly, negative indices
    are represented by a bar over the appropriate index. Origin of the plane need
    not always be located at the coordinates     0,0,0.

    #Hexagonal Unit Cells

    Description of Miller-Bravais indices:

    • Three basal axes at 120° to each other
    • z-axis is normal to the basal plane
    • Represented by 4 indices [u,v,t,w]

    To convert from a 3-axis to a 4-axis coordinate system,

    💡
    u=32U−V​
    💡
    v=32V−U​
    💡
    t=−3U+V​

    Crystallographic planes for a hexagonal unit cell:

    • Obtained by taking reciprocal of the intercepts
    • Given plane may intersect a1​, a2​, a3​, and c axes

    #Polymorphism and Allotropy

    Some metals may have more than one crystal structure. The type of crystal structure
    depends on both the temperature and the external pressure. For example, iron exists
    in both BCC and FCC crystal structures at different temperatures.
