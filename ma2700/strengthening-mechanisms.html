    Strengthening Mechanisms

    #Diffusion Mechanisms

    Diffusion is the migration of atoms from lattice site to lattice side.

    At a specific temperature, some small fraction of the total number of atoms is capable
    of diffusive motion, by virtue of the magnitudes of their vibrational energies, which
    increases with rising temperature.

    For an atom to diffuse:

    • There must be an empty adjacent site (vacancy or interstitial position)
    • Atom must have sufficient energy to break bonds with its neighbor atoms

    Vacancy diffusion:

    • Involves the movement of atoms into vacancies present in the lattice
    • Significant concentrations of vacancies may exist at elevated temperatures
    • Diffusion rate is thus higher due to larger number of defects present in the lattice

    Interstitial diffusion:

    • Atoms migrate from an interstitial position to a neighboring one that is empty
    • Atoms that move must be small enough to fit into the interstitial positions
    • Occurs more rapidly than vacancy diffusion as interstitial atoms are smaller
      and there are also more empty interstitial positions than vacancies

    #Solid-Solution Strengthening

    Resistance to slip is greater when impurity atoms are present as the overall lattice strain
    must increase for a dislocation to move. Thus, a greater applied stress is required for
    plastic deformation of the alloy to occur.

    Effect of impurity atoms:

    • Impose lattice strains on the surrounding host atoms
    • Impurity atoms interact restrict the movement of dislocations
    • Impurity atoms tend to diffuse to site of dislocation to reduce overall strain energy

    Impurity atom that is smaller than host atom:

    • Impurity atom exerts tensile strains on the surrounding crystal lattice
    • Tensile strain partially nullifies some of the dislocation’s compressive strain

    Impurity atom that is larger than host atom:

    • Imposes compressive strains on the surrounding crystal lattice
    • Compressive strain nullifies some of the dislocation’s tensile strain

    #Precipitation Strengthening

    Strengthening mechanism:

    • Create a dense and fine dispersion of particles of different phase
      in a matrix of deformable metal through proper heat treatment
    • Precipitated particles act as obstacles to dislocation movement
    • Strength of the alloy is increased

    Non-coherent precipitate:

    • Has no relationship with the crystal structure of surrounding matrix
    • May not significantly disrupt the surrounding matrix structure
    • Precipitate blocks slip only if it lies directly in the path of the dislocation

    Coherent precipitate:

    • Planes of atoms in the crystal structure of the precipitate are related to,
      or even continuous with, the planes in the crystal structure of the matrix
    • Creates a widespread disruption of the matrix crystal structure
    • Impedes dislocation even if it merely passes near precipitate

    #Grain Size Refinement

    During plastic deformation, dislocation movement must take place across a grain boundary.

    Grain boundary acts as a barrier by:

    • Different orientations of grains — dislocation must change its direction of motion,
      which becomes more difficult as crystallographic misorientation increases
    • Atomic disorder within grain boundary region results in a
      discontinuity of slip planes from one grain into the other

    Relationship between yield strength and grain size (Hall-Petch equation).

    💡
    σy​=σ0​+d1/2k​

    where d is the average grain diameter and, σ0​ and k are material constants

    #Strain Hardening

    Strain hardening is where a ductile metal becomes harder and stronger as
    it is plastically deformed. This occurs as only some fraction of the total
    deformation is recovered as elastic strain.

    Note that the new yield strength of the metal is higher than
    its initial yield strength after undergoing plastic deformation.

    Strengthening mechanism:

    • Cold working increases density of dislocations
    • Dislocation movement is hindered by other dislocations
    • Increase in strength and hardness comes at the cost of ductility

    #Recovery and Recrystallization

    Strain hardening results in microstructural and property changes:

    • Change in grain shape — elongated grains
    • Strain hardening (increase in strength)
    • Increase in dislocation density

    To revert a cold worked metal back to its pre-cold worked state:

    • Annealing (reheating) is carried out on the metal
    • Metal is held at the annealing temperature for a prolonged period
    • Recovery and recrystallization occurs, which increases ductility of the metal

    Recovery process:

    • Cold-worked metal is heated to just below recrystallization temperature
    • Heating to recovery temperature results in enhanced atomic diffusion
    • Dislocations are allowed to rearrange into lower-energy configurations
    • Internal strain energy of recovered metal is reduced
    • Strength is reduced only slightly
    • Ductility is significantly increased

    Recrystallization process:

    • Formation of strain-free and equiaxed grains with low dislocation densities
    • New grains form as small nuclei and grow until they consume the parent material
    • Cold-worked microstructure is eventually replaced with recrystallized structure
