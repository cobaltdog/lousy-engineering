    #EST S2 (2020 - 2021)

    #Question 1

    #Part (a)

    Ionic bonds:

    • Strong electrostatic forces of attraction between positive and negative ions
    • Movement of ions within the lattice structure is difficult and slow
    • When stress is applied, fracture occurs rather than deformation
    • Low ductility due to the strong ionic bonds

    Metallic bonds:

    • Positive ions bonded by attraction to a sea of negatively-charged electrons
    • Rows of atoms can shear continuously without a sudden change in bonds
    • Good ductility as the metal is capable of deforming to a certain extent

    #Question 4

    #Part (a)

    • Extrusion results in elongated grains with directional properties
    • Cold and warm extrusion additionally results in work hardening
    • Work hardening occurs due to increase in dislocation density
    • Dislocation movement is hindered by other dislocations
    • Strength and hardness of the material thus increases

    #Part (b)

    Misrun:

    • Casting solidifies before completely filling the mold cavity
    • Higher volume-to-surface area ratio to increase solidification time
    • Molten metal will take a longer time to solidify, thus filling the cavity

    Cold shots:

    • Solid globules of metal entrapped in the casting
    • Proper design of gating system to minimize splashing and turbulence
    • Gating system will promote uniform molten metal flow into the cavity

    Shrinkage cavity:

    • Depression in the casting surface or internal void
    • Proper design of the riser to compensate for shrinkage
    • As molten metal solidifies and shrinks, riser serves as a source of molten metal

    Hot cracking:

    • Mold does not allow casting to contract, resulting in high tensile stresses
    • Proper casting design with rounded corners, fillets, and gradual transitions
    • Reduce stress concentrations and minimize risk of cracking

    #Part (c)

    Roughing cut:

    • Removes a large amount of material quickly
    • High feed rate, high depth of cut, and low cutting speed
    • Component is cut to its rough size and shape
    • Cutting operation is less precise
    • Low quality surface finish

    Finishing cut:

    • Removes a small amount of material
    • Low feed rate, low depth of cut, and high cutting speed
    • Component is cut to its desired dimensions and shape
    • High dimensional accuracy (more precise)
    • High quality surface finish

    #Question 3

    #Part (a)

    • Ability to deform extensively without fracture
    • Superplasticity is due to grain boundary sliding and diffusion
    • Capable of producing net shape components without further processing
    • Does not require annealing, unlike cold working, as grains are not deformed
    • Complex geometries can be formed with good surface finish
    • Good formability without springback

    #Part (b)

    Precipitation strengthening:

    • Solution heat treated and aged to produce fine precipitates
    • Precipitates hinder the movement of dislocations
    • Higher stress is required for a crack to propagate

    Carbide strengthening:

    • Presence of carbides along the grain boundaries
    • Carbides restrict grain boundary sliding and impede dislocations
    • Higher stress is required to propagate a crack along grain boundaries
