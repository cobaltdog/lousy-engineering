    Powder Metallurgy

    #Powder Metallurgy

    Powder metallurgy is a metal processing technology
    in which parts are produced from metallic powders.

    PM production sequence:

    • Pressing — powders are compressed into the desired shape to produce a green compact
    • Pressing is accomplished using a punch-and-die tooling designed for that specific part
    • Sintering — green compact is heated to bond the particles into a hard, rigid mass
    • Sintering is performed at temperatures below the melting point of the metal

    Advantages of powder metallurgy:

    • Parts can be mass-produced to net shape or near net shape
    • Eliminates or reduces the need for subsequent processing
    • Very little wastage of material compared to casting
    • Parts having a specified level of porosity can be made (porous metal parts)
    • Certain metals that are difficult to fabricate by other methods can be shaped by PM

    Disadvantages of powder metallurgy:

    • High tooling and equipment costs
    • Metallic powders are expensive
    • Difficulties in storing and handling metal powders (degradation, fire hazards)
    • Variations in density throughout the part, especially for complex geometries

    #Engineering Powders

    Geometric features of powders:

    • Particle size and distribution
    • Particle shape and internal structure
    • Surface area

    #Packing, Density, and Porosity

    Particle density measures:

    • True density — density of the material if powders were melted into a solid mass
    • Bulk density — density of the powders in the loose state after pouring
    • Due to pores between particles, bulk density is less than true density

    Packing factor:

    • Bulk density divided by true density
    • Dependent on particle shape and the distribution of particle sizes
    • Smaller powders will fit into the spaces between larger powders (high packing factor)
    • Packing can be increased by vibrating the powders, causing them to settle more tightly
    • Pressing greatly increases packing through rearrangement and deformation of particles

    Porosity is defined as the ratio of the volume of pores in the powder to the bulk volume.

    💡
    Porosity+Packing Factor=1

    #Pressing and Sintering

    Steps in a conventional PM sequence:

    • Blending and mixing of the powders
    • Compaction — powders are pressed into the desired shape
    • Sintering — heating to below melting point to cause solid-state bonding of particles
    • Secondary operations — performed to improve dimensional accuracy, increase density

    #Blending and Mixing

    Description of process:

    • Powders are homogenized for successful results in compacting and sintering
    • Blending — powders of the same chemistry but different sizes are mixed
    • Blending of different particle sizes reduce porosity (high packing factor)
    • Mixing — powders of different chemistries are combined

    Other ingredients are usually added to the metallic powders during blending and mixing.
    These include lubricants to reduce friction between particles and at the die wall during compaction, binders to achieve adequate strength in the pressed but unsintered part.

    #Compaction

    Description of process:

    • High pressure is applied to the powders to form the required shape
    • Pressing — opposing punches squeeze the powders in a die
    • Work part after pressing is called a green compact (not yet fully processed)
    • Green strength of the part is adequate for handling but far less than after sintering

    #Sintering

    Description of process:

    • Green compact lacks strength and hardness after pressing, it is easily crumbled
    • Heat treatment to bond the metallic particles, increase strength and hardness
    • Usually carried out at temperatures between 0.7Tm​<T<0.9Tm​
    • Driving force for sintering is reduction of surface energy
    • Part shrinkage occurs due to pore size reduction

    The green compact consists of many distinct particles, each with its own individual surface,
    and so the total surface area contained in the compact is very high. Under the influence of
    heat, the surface area is reduced through the formation and growth of bonds between the particles, with associated reduction in surface energy.
