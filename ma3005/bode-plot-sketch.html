    #Principal Components

    A complex number can be expressed in the form,

    σ+jω=rejθ

    where magnitude r=σ2+ω2​,
    and phase angle     θ=tan−1σω​

    A transfer function of a system can be expressed as,

    G(s)=K⋅Πj=1​ (s+pj​)Πi=1​ (s+zi​)​

    Substituting s=jω into G(s),

    G(s)=K⋅Πj=1​ (jω+pj​)Πi=1​ (jω+zi​)​

    G(s)=K⋅Πj=1​ ω2+pj​2​ej tan−1(ω/pj​)Πi=1​ ω2+zi​2​ej tan−1(ω/zi​)​

    G(s)=K⋅ω2+pj​2​ω2+zi​2​​ej[∑tan−1(ω/zi​)−∑tan−1(ω/pj​)]

    The 1st term represents the magnitude, while the 2nd term represents the phase angle.

    Taking logarithm of G(s), the linear combination of the magnitude is,

    💡
    20lg∣G∣=20lg(K)+∑20lg(ω2+zi​2​)−∑20lg(ω2+pj​2​)

    Similarly, the linear combination of the phase angle is,

    💡
    ∠G=∑tan−1(zi​ω​)−∑tan−1(pj​ω​)

    20lg∣G(jω)∣=20lgK

    ∠G(jω)=0

    #Complex Function

    Consider the 2nd order transfer function G(s)=s2+s+1001000s​.

    G(s)=1001000​⋅s⋅s2+s+100100​=K⋅s⋅s2+2ζωn​s+ωn​2ωn​2​

    Transfer function is the product of a constant, a differentiator, and a 2nd order pole.

    Damping ratio of the transfer function is,

    1=2ζ(10)

    ζ=0.05

    Since the damping ratio is low, the bode plot will not follow the asymptotes.

    #Bode Plot Analysis

    For a 2nd-order system, a smaller damping ratio ζ results in a sharper resonance
    peak on the magnitude plot, and results in a steeper gradient on the phase plot.

    💡
    ωr​=ωn​1−2ζ2​

    For resonance frequency to be zero,

    0=ωn​1−2ζ2​

    0=1−2ζ2​

    ζ=2​1​=0.707

    Thus, resonance occurs when damping ratio lies in the range 0<ζ<0.707.

    #Low-Pass Filter

    Low-pass filter:

    • Only allows low frequency signals to pass through and produce a valid output
    • High frequency signals are filtered out and produce a negligible output
    • Largest magnitude is located in the low-frequency regime

    Cutoff frequency ωc​ is obtained by drawing a horizontal X−3 db line.
    For any frequencies, which are smaller than ωc​, they fall within the pass band.
    For any frequencies, which are larger than ωc​, they fall within the no-pass band.

    #High-Pass Filter

    High-pass filter:

    • Only allows high frequency signals to pass through and produce a valid output
    • Low frequency signals are filtered out and produce a negligible output
    • Largest magnitude is located in the high-frequency regime

    Cutoff frequency ωc​ is obtained by drawing a horizontal X−3 db line.
    For any frequencies, which are larger than ωc​, they fall within the pass band.
    For any frequencies, which are smaller than ωc​, they fall within the no-pass band.

    #Unity Feedback System

    Bode plot of an open-loop transfer function:

    • Determine whether system is stable
    • Determine transient response of system
    • Determine steady-state error of system

    #Gain and Phase Margins

    Gain margin of a unity feedback system is,

    💡
    GM=0−∣G(s=jωgm​)∣

    Occurs at the frequency ωgm​ where ∠G=−π.
    Intersection is called the phase crossover frequency.

    Phase margin of a unity feedback system is,

    💡
    γ=∠G(s=jωpm​)−(−π)

    Occurs at the frequency ωpm​ where 20lg∣G∣=0.
    Intersection is called the gain crossover frequency.

    Unity feedback system is stable if and only if GM>0 and γ>0.
    As γ increases, damping ratio ζ of the closed-loop increases as well.

    #Bandwidth

    Frequency at which 20lg∣G∣=X−3 is denoted as the bandwidth frequency ωbw​.
    As ωbw​ increases, natural frequency ωn​ of the closed-loop increases as well.

    #Steady-State Errors

    Recall that a Type 0 system has no integrators. A zero slope on
    the magnitude plot indicates a static position error constant Kp​.

    From the bode plot, at ω≈0, the magnitude is 20lg∣G(s=0j)∣=20lgKp​.

    Therefore, solving for the static position error constant, Kp​=102020lg∣G∣​.

    Recall that a Type 1 system has 1 integrator, indicated by the −20 db/dec slope.
    Extending this line until it cuts the 0 db line, intersection point is then ωv​=Kv​.

    Recall that a Type 2 system has 2 integrators, indicated by the −40 db/dec slope.
    Extending this line until it cuts the 0 db line, intersection point is then ωa​=Ka​​.
