    Root Locus Sketch

    #Sketching the Root Locus

    Consider a typical closed-loop feedback control system, with the transfer function,

    💡
    T(s)=1+KG(s)⋅H(s)KG(s)​

    Characteristic equation of the system is,

    CE=1+KG(s)⋅H(s)

    0=1+KG(s)⋅H(s)

    −1=KG(s)⋅H(s)

    −1=K(s+p1​)(s+p2​)…(s+pn​)(s+z1​)(s+z2​)…(s+zm​)​

    −1=KD(s)N(s)​

    where N(s) contain the open-loop zeros (end points)
    and     D(s) contain the open-loop poles (start points)

    To determine the start and end points of a root locus, express the
    characteristic equation of the control system in the following form.

    💡
    KD(s)N(s)​

    If there are more poles than zeros, the additional poles will end at infinity zeros.

    To determine where a root locus lies on the real-axis, apply the phase condition.

    💡
    i=1∑m​∠(s+zi​)−j=1∑n​∠(s+pj​)=±π

    In general, label the zeros and poles located on the real-axis from right to left.
    A root locus exists in the region to the left of odd-numbered zeros and poles.

    #Refining the Locus Sketch

    When there are more poles than zeros, the additional poles will approach some asymptote.

    To determine the location of the asymptote of infinity zeros,

    💡
    σa​=n−m∑pi​−∑zj​​

    where n is the number of poles and m is the number of zeros

    To determine the angle of the asymptotes of infinity zeros,

    💡
    ∠s=n−mπ(1+2q)​

    where q=0,1,2,3…

    #Break-Out and Break-In Points

    For poles that end at infinity zeros, a break-out point exist along the real axis, whereby it
    is the maximum point before the locus breaks out of the real-axis and becomes complex.

    To determine the break-out points:

    • Rearrange the characteristic equation with K as the subject
    • Differentiate K with respect to s and equate to zero
    • Solve for the roots of the new polynomial equation
    • Reject roots which do not lie on real part of locus

    Similarly, to determine the break-in points of poles, perform the same process.
    A break-in point is the minimum point at which the locus enters the real-axis.

    #Departure and Arrival Angles

    To determine the departure angle of poles, apply the phase condition.

    💡
    i=1∑m​∠(s+zi​)−j=1∑n​∠(s+pj​)=−π

    To determine the arrival angle of poles at zeros, apply the phase condition.

    💡
    i=1∑m​∠(s+zi​)−j=1∑n​∠(s+pj​)=π

    #Intersection with jω-Axis

    For certain control systems, as K varies, the poles may cross from
    the stable region (left) to the unstable region (right) or vice versa.

    To determine the intersection the path of a pole makes with the jω-axis,
    substitute s=jω into the characteristic equation of the transfer function.
