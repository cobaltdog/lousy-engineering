    Parameters of translational motion:

    • Displacement — change in position of an object, x(t)
    • Velocity — rate of change of displacement, v=x˙(t)
    • Acceleration — rate of change of velocity, a=x¨(t)
    • Force — push or pull on an object, f(t)

    #System Elements

    Elements of a translational system:

    • Mass — property that stores kinetic energy
    • Spring — elastic object that stores potential energy
    • Damper/friction — force which opposes the relative motion

    #Mass

    An object accelerates in the direction of an applied force
    and this acceleration is proportional to the applied force.

    💡
    f(t)=ma=mx¨

    When accelerating a mass, there is an inertial force acting
    in the opposite direction to the defined positive direction.

    #Spring

    A linear spring deforms proportional to the applied force.
    Ideally, the mass of a spring is considered to be negligible.

    💡
    f(t)=kx

    Equivalent spring constant for springs in parallel,

    💡
    keq​=k1​+k2​+⋯+kn​

    Equivalent spring constant for springs in series,

    💡
    keq​=k1​+k2​+⋯+kn​k1​k2​⋯kn​​

    #Damper/Friction

    An opposing force can be due to a damper or friction between two surfaces. If it is a damper, the mass of
    the damper is considered negligible.

    💡
    f(t)=bv=bx˙

    where b is the frictional constant (N⋅s/m)

    Equivalent damping constant for dampers in parallel,

    💡
    ceq​=c1​+c2​+⋯+cn​

    Equivalent damping constant for dampers in series,

    💡
    ceq​=c1​+c2​+⋯+cn​c1​c2​⋯cn​​

    #Rigid Connection between Two Masses

    Consider two blocks of masses m1​ and m2​ connected via a rigid connection.
    A force is applied on one of the blocks. Since both blocks are connected via
    the rigid connection, they can be treated as one body.

    Laplace transform of the system is,

    F=mtot​x¨

    F=(m1​+m2​)x¨

    💡
    F(s)=(m1​+m2​)⋅s2X(s)

    #Spring with Two Free Ends

    Consider two blocks of masses m1​ and m2​ connected via a spring. A force is applied
    on one of the blocks. However, the force applied on each block will vary as the spring
    will store some potential energy. Thus, displacement of each block will differ.

    Laplace transform of the system is,

    F=kx1​−kx2​

    💡
    F(s)=k[X1​(s)−X2​(s)]

    If x1​>x2​, spring is being stretched.
    If     x1​<x2​, spring is being compressed.

    #Damper with Two Free Ends

    Consider two blocks of masses m1​ and m2​ placed on a surface with friction.
    If a force is applied on the system, the displacement of each mass will vary
    due to the friction.

    Laplace transform of the system is,

    F=bx˙1​−bx˙2​

    💡
    F(s)=b[sX1​(s)−sX2​(s)]

    If x˙1​>x˙2​, damper is in tension.
    If     x˙1​<x˙2​, damper is in compression.

    #Control System Examples

    As a rule of thumb, for springs and dampers with two free ends,
    assume input displacement is larger than output displacement.

    #Rotational Mechanical System

    Parameters of rotational motion:

    • Angular displacement — angle through which a body rotates about a fixed axis, θ(t)
    • Angular velocity — rate of change of angular displacement, ω=θ˙(t)
    • Angular acceleration — rate of change of angular velocity, α=θ¨(t)
    • Torque — force that causes the body to rotate, T(t)

    #System Elements

    Elements of a rotational system:

    • Moment of inertia — property which opposes the change in angular position
    • Torsional spring
    • Damper/friction

    #Moment of Inertia

    Angular acceleration is proportional to applied torque.

    💡
    T(t)=Jα=Jθ¨

    where J is the moment of inertia of the body (kg⋅m2)

    #Torsional Spring

    A torsional spring deforms proportional to applied torque.
    In an ideal system, the spring has no inertia.

    💡
    T(t)=kr​θ

    where kr​ is the spring constant (N⋅m/rad)

    #Damper/Friction

    A rotation friction element can be an object moving
    in a fluid, or friction between a flywheel and a surface.

    💡
    T(t)=br​ω=br​θ˙

    where br​ is the frictional constant (N⋅m⋅s/rad)

    #Transfer Functions

    Performing Laplace transform,

    L{T(t)}=L{J⋅θ¨(t)}

    T(s)=J⋅s2θ(s)

    Performing Laplace transform,

    L{T(t)}=L{kr​⋅θ(t)}

    T(s)=kr​⋅θ(s)

    Performing Laplace transform,

    L{T(t)}=L{br​⋅θ˙}

    T(s)=br​⋅sθ(s)

    For springs and dampers with two free ends, the free end with the smaller angular displacement will create an opposing torque that acts against the other end with
    the higher angular displacement.

    #Transfer Functions (Gears)

    Relationship between the variables is,

    💡
    T1​T2​​=N1​N2​​=r1​r2​​=θ2​θ1​​=ω2​ω1​​=n

    whereby n is the gear ratio

    Consider an input torque T1​(t) that results in angular displacement of the driver gear θ1​(t). Driven gear experiences a displacement θ2​(t).

    Using the gear relations,

    T1​(t)T2​(t)​=N1​N2​​

    T2​(t)=T1​(t)N1​N2​​

    Torque experienced at the output shaft.

    Using Newton’s 2nd law,

    Jθ¨2​=T

    Jθ¨2​=T1​(t)N1​N2​​−bθ˙2​−kθ2​

    Jθ¨2​+bθ˙2​+kθ2​=T1​(t)N1​N2​​

    Performing Laplace transform,

    (Js2+bs+k)⋅θ2​(s)=T1​(s)N1​N2​​

    Expressing θ2​(s) in terms of θ1​(s),

    (Js2+bs+k)⋅θ1​(s)N2​N1​​=T1​(s)N1​N2​​

    n21​(Js2+bs+k)⋅θ1​(s)=T1​(s)

    #Electromechanical System

    A motor is an electromechanical component that produces a displacement output
    for a voltage input, that is, a mechanical output generated by an electrical input.

    A motor operates by having a current-carrying circuit (armature) rotating in a
    magnetic field, which results in a torque that turns the rotor inside the motor.

    Since the armature rotates in a magnetic field, its voltage is proportional to speed.

    vb​(t)=Kb​dtdθm​(t)​

    Vb​(s)=Kb​⋅sθm​(s)

    where vb​ is the back e.m.f.,
    and     Kb​ is the back e.m.f. constant,
    and     dtdθm​​ is the angular velocity of the motor

    Torque produced by the motor is proportional to the armature current.

    Tm​(s)=Kt​Ia​(s)

    where Tm​ is the torque developed by the motor,
    and     Kt​ is the motor torque constant

    Relationship between armature current and voltage is given by,

    Ea​(s)=Ra​Ia​(s)+La​⋅sIa​(s)+Vb​(s)

    where Ea​ is the applied armature voltage

    Substituting Vb​ and Ia​ into the above equation,

    Ea​(s)=Kt​(Ra​+La​s)Tm​(s)​+Kb​⋅sθm​(s)

    Ea​(s)=Kt​(Ra​+La​s)(Jm​s2+bm​s)θm​(s)​+Kb​⋅sθm​(s)

    Note that Tm​ can be obtained from rotational system modelling.

    Assuming the armature inductance is negligible,

    Ea​(s)=[Kt​Ra​​(Jm​s+bm​)+Kb​]sθm​(s)

    Rearranging the above equation gives the transfer function.

    💡
    Ea​(s)θm​(s)​=s[s+Jm​1​(bm​+Ra​Kt​Kb​​)]Kt​/(Ra​Jm​)​

    #Torque-Speed Curve

    The constants Kb​ and Kt​ can be obtained through a dynamometer test,
    where a dynamometer measures the torque and speed of a motor under
    the condition of a constant applied voltage.

    Relationship between armature current and voltage is,

    Ea​(s)=Kt​(Ra​+La​s)Tm​(s)​+Kb​⋅sθm​(s)

    Ea​(s)=Kt​Ra​​Tm​(s)+Kb​⋅sθm​(s)

    Assuming armature inductance is negligible.

    Performing inverse Laplace transform of the above,

    ea​(t)=Kt​Ra​​Tm​(t)+Kb​ωm​(t)

    Note that inverse Laplace transform of sθm​(s)=dtdθm​(t)​.

    Rearranging the above equation gives the torque-speed curve.

    💡
    Tm​=−Ra​Kb​Kt​​ωm​+Ra​Kt​​ea​

    where Tm​ is plotted against ωm​

    The torque at which the angular
    velocity is zero is the stall torque.

    Tstall​=Ra​Kt​​ea​

    💡
    Ra​Kt​​=ea​Tstall​​

    The angular velocity occurring when
    torque is zero is the no-load speed.

    ωno load​=Kb​ea​​

    💡
    Kb​=ωno load​ea​​
