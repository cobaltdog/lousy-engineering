    #In-Service Loads

    Relevant unit conversions:

    • 1 pound (mass)=0.4536 kg (mass)
    • 1 ft2=0.0929 m2
    • 1 mile=1.609 km

    Kinematic equations:

    • v=v0​+at
    • s=s0​+v0​t+21​at2
    • v2=v0​2+2a(s−s0​)

    To convert a dynamic problem into a static problem, we define an
    inertia force Fi​=−ma due to the acceleration of the aircraft.

    #Lift, Drag, and Moment Coefficients

    Lift Coefficient

    L=21​ρV∞​2⋅S⋅CL​

    where ρ is air density at that altitude,
    and     S is planform area of the wing

    Lift curve provides information such as:

    • Maximum CLmax​​ and its αmax​
    • Minimum CLmin​​ and its αmin​
    • Lift slope CLα​

    Drag Coefficient

    D=21​ρV∞​2⋅S⋅CD​

    AR=Sb2​=cb​

    CD​=CD0​​+πeARCL​2​

    where CD0​​ is profile drag constant,
    e is span effectiveness factor and
    b is wing span

    For maximum DL​, parasitic drag is equal
    to induced drag. Thus, CD0​​=πeARCL​2​.

    Pitch Moment Coefficient

    Mac​=21​ρV∞​2⋅S⋅c⋅CM​

    where c is average chord length of airfoil,
    and     CM​ is pitch moment coefficient

    #Load Factor during Maneuvers

    Load Factor

    n=WL​=1+gay​​

    Second form of equation can be determined from Newton’s
    second law, with a given acceleration in the     y-direction.

    n=21​ρ∞​V∞​2⋅W/SCL​​

    where SW​ is the wing loading

    Load factor as given by aviation authority:

    n1​=2.1+2.20462W+1000024000​

    where W is in kilograms

    Parameters in flight envelope:

    • Positive limit load is denoted by n1​
    • Negative limit load is denoted by n3​
    • Proof load is denoted by np​=1.25n1​
    • Ultimate load is denoted by nu​=1.5n1​
    • Design dive speed is defined as Vdive​=1.2Vcruise​

    Banking Turn

    R=gn2−1​V∞​2​

    ω=V∞​gn2−1​​

    ϕ=tan−1gRV∞​2​

    where ϕ is bank angle

    Pull-Up Maneuver

    R=g(n−1)V∞​2​

    ω=V∞​g(n−1)​

    n=gRV∞​2​+cosθ

    where θ is angle from lowest position of dive

    Pull-Down Maneuver

    R=g(n+1)V∞​2​

    ω=V∞​g(n+1)​

    Minimum Turn Radius and Maximum Turn Rate

    When n is very large, the formulas can be simplified to:

    R=gnV∞​2​=g(L/W)V∞​2​=ρ∞​CL​⋅g2​⋅SW​

    ω=V∞​gn​=V∞​g(L/W)​=g2(W/S)ρ∞​CL​⋅n​​

    #Gust V-n Diagram

    Change in angle of attack due to gust:

    Δα=tan−1V∞​u​=V∞​u​

    where u is gust velocity (positive for upwards, negative for downwards)

    Change in lift due to gust:

    ΔL=21​ρ∞​V∞​2SCLα​⋅Δα

    ΔL=21​ρ∞​V∞​SCLα​u

    Change in load factor due to gust:

    Δn=WΔL​=21​Wρ∞​V∞​SCLα​u​

    Total load factor accounted for gust load:

    nG​=1+Δn=1+21​Wρ∞​V∞​SCLα​u​

    Maxmimum speed in the presence of a gust:

    n1​=nG​

    n1​=1+21​Wρ∞​VP​SCLα​u​

    VP​=ρ∞​CLα​u2W(n1​−1)​

    where VP​ is penetration speed

    #Torsion of Thin-Walled Structures

    rτ​=JT​=Gϕ

    Jsolid​=2πr4​=32πd4​

    Jhollow​=2π​(ro​4−ri​4)

    Jthin​=2πr0​3t

    Ithin​=πr0​3t

    #Closed Section

    Shear stress for a thin-walled section:

    τ=tq​=2A0​tT​

    where A0​ represents the mean area

    Torque for a thin-walled section:

    Tsingle−cell​=2A0​q

    Tmulti−cell​=2∑Ai​qi​

    Angle of twist for a thin-walled section:

    ϕsingle−cell​=4A0​2GT​∮tds​=2A0​G1​∮tq​ ds

    ϕmulti−cell​=4Ai​2GT​(∮tds​)i​=2Ai​G1​(∮tq​ ds)i​

    Note that ϕ is expressed in rad/m.

    Alternative expression for polar moment of inertia:

    J=GϕT​=∮tds​4A0​2​

    #Open Section

    Polar moment of ineratia:

    JR​=31​∑bi​ti​3

    Shear stress for a thin-walled section:

    τ=2Ghϕ=Gtϕ

    where t is the thickness of the section when unfolded

    Maximum shear stress:

    τmax​=±JR​Tt​

    Torque for a thin-walled section:

    T=GϕJR​

    Angle of twist for a thin-walled section:

    ϕ=GJR​T​

    #Hybrid Section

    Combined polar moment of inertia:

    JC​=∮tds​4A0​2​+31​∑bi​ti​3

    Angle of twist for closed section:

    ϕ=GJT​=GJ2A0​q​

    Maximum shear stress for open section:

    τmax​=±GJGTt​

    #Analysis of Thin-Walled Structures

    #Bending in Beams

    Unsymmetrical Bending Theory

    General equation for bending stress:

    σz​=Ixx​Iyy​−Ixy​2Mx​(Iyy​y−Ixy​x)​+Ixx​Iyy​−Ixy​2My​(Ixx​x−Ixy​y)​

    If beam section has an axis of symmetry:

    σz​=Ixx​Mx​​y+Iyy​My​​x

    Inclination of neutral axis:

    tanα=−Mx​Iyy​−My​Ixy​My​Ixx​−Mx​Ixy​​

    #Shearing in Beams

    General equation for shear flow:

    qs​−q0​=−(Ixx​Iyy​−Ixy​2Sy​Iyy​−Sx​Ixy​​)∫0s​yt ds−(Ixx​Iyy​−Ixy​2Sx​Ixx​−Sy​Ixy​​)∫0s​xt ds

    If beam section has an axis of symmetry:

    qs​−q0​=−Ixx​Sy​​∫0s​yt ds−Iyy​Sx​​∫0s​xt ds

    Note that q0​=0 for an open section beam (open edge of the cross section)
    and     q0​ is an unknown and must be computed for a closed section beam.

    Shear Center of Closed Section

    qs​=qb​+q0​

    If shear forces act through shear center:

    q0​=−∮ds/t∮(qb​/t) ds​

    where qb​ represents the basic shear flow (shear flow assuming an open section)

    If shear forces do not act through shear center:

    Sx​y−Sy​x=∮rqs​ ds

    Sx​y−Sy​x=∮r(qb​+q0​) ds

    Sx​y−Sy​x=∮rqb​ ds+2A0​q0​

    q0​=2A0​Sx​y−Sy​x−∮rqb​ ds​

    When shear forces do not act through the shear center, it results in a torque about the
    shear center. Thus, the problem can be broken down into shear flow due to the vertical
    shear force and shear flow due to torsion. Hence, the second term     2A0​q0​.

    #Analysis of Fuselage Structures

    #Thin-Walled Pressure Vessels

    Cylindrical Vessel

    σ1​=tpr​ (hoop stress)

    σ2​=2σ1​​=2tpr​ (longitudinal stress)

    ε1​=rΔr​=E1​(σ1​−vσ2​)

    ε2​=LΔL​=E1​(σ2​−vσ1​)

    ε3​=tΔt​=−Ev​(σ1​+σ2​)

    Spherical Vessel

    σ1​=σ2​=2tpr​

    #Bending with Internal Pressure

    σz​=2tpr​±πr2tM​ (longitudinal stress and bending stress)

    σy​=tpr​ (hoop stress)

    #Twisting with Internal Pressure

    τzy​=2A0​tT​ (shear flow due to torque in a single-cell closed section)

    σz​=2tpr​ (longitudinal stress)

    σy​=tpr​ (hoop stress)

    #Combined Loading with Internal Pressure

    σz​=2tpr​±πr2tMx​​±πr2tMy​​ (longitudinal stress and bending stresses)

    τzy​=2A0​tT​+tqs​​ (shear flow due to torque and shear flow due to shear forces)

    Note that qs​ is the shear flow for a closed section, whereby qs​=qb​+q0​.

    #Practical Aircraft Stress Analysis

    #Structural Idealization

    To idealize a structure into an arrangement of booms and skin, the
    cross-sectional area of each boom needs to be determined first.

    Am​=6bt​(2+σm​σn​​)

    where b is the length of the skin between booms m and n,
    and     t is the thickness of that skin

    For a skin between booms m and n, the cross-sectional area of boom m is given by
    the above equation. If a stiffener is present at the joint between two skins, the cross-
    sectional area of the stiffener needs to be added.

    Sectional properties of an idealized structure:

    Ixx​=∑Br​yr​2

    Iyy​=∑Br​xr​2

    Ixy​=∑Br​xr​yr​

    General equation for bending stress:

    σz​=Ixx​Iyy​−Ixy​2Mx​(Iyy​yr​−Ixy​xr​)​+Ixx​Iyy​−Ixy​2My​(Ixx​xr​−Ixy​yr​)​

    where subscript r represents the boom

    General equation for shear flow:

    qs​−q0​=−(Ixx​Iyy​−Ixy​2Sy​Iyy​−Sx​Ixy​​) r=1∑n​Br​yr​−(Ixx​Iyy​−Ixy​2Sx​Ixx​−Sy​Ixy​​) r=1∑n​Br​xr​

    where subscript r represents the boom

    #Torsion of Multicell Wing Section

    Angle of twist for a wing section in pure torque:

    ϕ=2AR​G1​[−qR−1​δR−1,R​+qR​δR​−qR+1​δR+1,R​]

    where δ=∫tds​

    If wing section has different shear modulus:

    ϕ=2AR​Gref​1​[−qR−1​δR−1,R​+qR​δR​−qR+1​δR+1,R​]

    where δ=∫t∗ds​ and t∗=Gref​Gt​

    Note that δR−1,R​ and δR+1,R​ refers to the partition walls between the cells.
    For a section with different shear modulus,     Gref​ is a convenient reference
    value of the shear modulus.

    #Shear of Multicell Wing Section

    Angle of twist for a wing section subjected to transverse stress:

    ϕ=2AR​G1​[−q0,R−1​δR−1,R​+q0,R​δR​−q0,R+1​δR+1,R​+∮R​tqb​​ ds]

    where δ=∫tds​

    If wing section has different shear modulus:

    ϕ=2AR​Gref​1​[−q0,R−1​δR−1,R​+q0,R​δR​−q0,R+1​δR+1,R​+∮R​t∗qb​​ ds]

    where δ=∫t∗ds​ and t∗=Gref​Gt​

    Moments equation of shear flow in wing section:

    Sx​y−Sy​x=R=1∑N​(∮rqb​ ds)+R=1∑N​(2A0​q0,R​)

    The above equation accounts for the combined effects of shear forces and
    torsion due to shear forces not acting through the shear center of the section.

    Moments equation of shear flow for a tapered wing section:

    Sx​y−Sy​x=R=1∑N​(∮rqb​ ds)+R=1∑N​(2A0​q0,R​)−r=1∑n​Px,r​yr​+r=1∑n​Py,r​xr​

    For a tapered wing section, normal stresses in a boom can be resolved
    into     x-, y- and z-components, which may contribute to shear forces.

    #Deflection of Beams

    #Symmetrical Section

    Deflection (moment-curvature) equation:

    EI dz2d2v​=M

    Integrating once gives the equation for the slope θ.
    Integrating twice gives the equation for the deflection     v.

    Higher-order deflection equation:

    EI ∂z4∂4v​=∂z∂Sy​​=p

    where p is the applied transverse load

    Integrating once gives the equation for shear force Sy​.
    Integrating twice gives the equation for moment     Mx​.

    #Arbitrary Section

    Horizontal deflection equation:

    dz2d2u​=−E(Ixx​Iyy​−Ixy​2)My​Ixx​−Mx​Ixy​​

    Vertical deflection equation:

    dz2d2v​=−E(Ixx​Iyy​−Ixy​2)Mx​Iyy​−My​Ixy​​

    #Unit Load Method

    Unit load equation for a beam subjected to bending:

    ΔM​=∫0L​⟨EIMm​⟩ dz

    where M is the actual moment, and m is the virtual moment

    Unit load equation for a beam subjected to transverse shear forces:

    ΔQ​=∫0L​(∫GtQs​qs​​ ds)dz

    where Qs​ is the actual shear flow, and qs​ is the virtual shear flow

    For a beam subjected to both bending and transverse shear:

    Δcomb​=∫0L​⟨EIMm​⟩ dz+∫0L​(∫GtQs​qs​​ ds)dz

    Actual shear flow Qs​ needs to be determined first using theories in previous chapters.

    #Buckling of Columns

    #Euler Buckling Load

    A Euler column is a perfect column; ideally straight.

    Critical buckling load of a column:

    Pcr​=L2m2π2​EI=m2⋅PEuler​

    where m is the mode of the column (number of constraints)

    For columns with different end conditions:

    Pcr​=KL2m2π2​EI=Km2⋅PEuler​

    where K=4 for a beam fixed at both ends (C-C),
    and     K=1 for a beam simply supported at both ends (S-S),
    and     K=0.25 for a beam fixed at one end and free at the other (C-F),
    and     K=2.05 for a beam fixed at one end and simply supported at the other (C-S)

    #Effect of Imperfections

    Initial imperfection (deflection) of column:

    v0​=a0​sinLπz​

    where a0​ is a constant

    Ratio of Euler buckling load with actual buckling load:

    α1​=PPEuler​​

    Deflection of an imperfect column due to applied compressive load:

    v=1−αα​a0​sinLπz​

    Total deflection of an imperfect column:

    vtot​=v0​+v

    vtot​=1−α1​a0​sinLπz​

    #Beam Columns

    Beam columns are structural members that support both axial and transverse loads.

    Deflection at any point of beam column:

    v=2PλW​[cos(λL/2)sinλz​−λz]

    where λ2=EIP​

    Deflection at midpoint of beam column:

    δ=kW​⋅1−α1​

    where W is the concentrated transverse load and stiffness k=L348EI​

    #Energy Approach to Buckling

    Elastic strain energy equation for a column:

    U=21​∫EI(∂z2∂2v​)2 dz

    Work done by compressive load on column:

    We​=21​∫Pcr​(∂z∂v​)2 dz

    Total potential energy in a buckled system:

    Π=21​∫EI(∂z2∂2v​)2 dz−21​∫Pcr​(∂z∂v​)2 dz

    where Π is a constant
