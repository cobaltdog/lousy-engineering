    Analysis of Fuselage Structures

    #Thin-Walled Pressure Vessels

    #Cylindrical Vessel

    Recall that hoop stress σ1​ and longitudinal stress σ2​ for a cylindrical vessel are,

    💡
    σ1​=tpr​
    💡
    σ2​=2σ1​​=2tpr​

    Generalized Hooke’s law equations are,

    ε1​=E1​(σ1​−vσ2​)

    ε2​=E1​(σ2​−vσ1​)

    ε3​=−Ev​(σ1​+σ2​)

    Hoop strain can also be computed using,

    ε1​=Original CircumferenceChange in Circumference​

    ε1​=2πr2π(r+Δr)−2πr​

    ε1​=rΔr​

    Applying the same concept for the other strains,

    💡
    ε1​=rΔr​=E1​(σ1​−vσ2​)
    💡
    ε2​=LΔL​=E1​(σ2​−vσ1​)
    💡
    ε3​=tΔt​=−Ev​(σ1​+σ2​)

    #Spherical Vessel

    Recall that hoop stress σ1​ and longitudinal stress σ2​ for a spherical vessel are,

    💡
    σ1​=σ2​=2tpr​

    #Deformation of Vessel

    Geometrical changes in shape results in bending and shear stresses in the region
    around junctures (joints) as cylindrical and spherical pressure vessels deform by
    a different amount when subjected to the same pressure.

    Thus, to maintain physical continuity of the wall, it needs to bend. If thick flat end
    plates are used instead of hemispherical bulkheads, the local bending and shear
    stresses will increase considerably.

    Let Δrc​ be the change in cylinder radius.
    Let Δrh​ be the change in head (hemisphere) radius.

    Hoop strain of the cylinder is,

    ε1​=rc​Δrc​​=E1​(σ1​−vσ2​)

    rc​Δrc​​=Eσ1​​(1−2v​)

    rc​Δrc​​=Etc​prc​​(1−2v​)

    Δrc​=Etc​prc​2​(1−2v​)

    Strain in the head (hemisphere) is,

    ε=rh​Δrh​​=E1​(σ−vσ)

    rh​Δrh​​=Eσ​(1−v)

    rh​Δrh​​=2Eth​prh​​(1−v)

    Δrh​=2Eth​prh​2​(1−v)

    To minimize distortion at the juncture (joint),

    Δrc​=Δrh​

    Etc​prc​2​(1−2v​)=2Eth​prh​2​(1−v)

    💡
    tc​th​​=2−v1−v​

    By ensuring compatible circumference deformation of the cylinder and
    hemispherical head, distortion of the junctures will be minimized.

    #Fuselage Structures

    #Pure Bending with Internal Pressure

    Consider a thin-walled pressure vessel with mean radius r and thickness t.
    The pressure vessel is subjected to a bending moment about the x-axis.

    Inspecting an element on the wall of the pressure vessel, the element is subjected to hoop stress as well as tensile/compressive stress due to bending.

    Along the longitudinal z-axis,

    σz​=σ2​±σbending​

    💡
    σz​=2tpr​±πr2tM​

    Along the y-axis,

    σy​=σ1​

    💡
    σy​=tpr​

    Note that the normal stresses are principal stresses if there is no shear stress.

    #Twisting with Internal Pressure

    Consider a thin-walled pressure vessel with mean radius r and thickness t.

    Inspecting an element on the wall, the element is subjected to stresses due to the pressure as well
    as shear stresses due to the twisting.

    💡
    τzy​=2A0​tT​

    Shear stress for a thin-walled closed section.

    The normal stresses on the element are the hoop stress and longitudinal stress of a pressure vessel.

    💡
    σz​=2tpr​
    💡
    σy​=tpr​

    Note that since there is shear stress on the element,
    the normal stresses are not the principal stresses.

    #Combined Loading with Internal Pressure

    Consider a thin-walled pressure vessel with mean radius r and thickness t.

    Loads applied to pressure vessel:

    • Bending moments Mx​ and My​
    • Shear forces Sx​ and Sy​
    • Torque Tz​

    Recall the bending stress equation,

    σz​=Ixx​Iyy​−Ixy​2Mx​(Iyy​y−Ixy​x)​+Ixx​Iyy​−Ixy​2My​(Ixx​x−Ixy​y)​

    σz​=Ixx​Mx​y​+Iyy​My​x​

    σz​=Ixx​Mx​r​+Iyy​My​r​

    Thus, the longitudinal stress is then,

    💡
    σz​=2tpr​±πr2tMx​​±πr2tMy​​

    Recall the shear flow equation for a thin-walled section.

    qs​−q0​=−Ixx​Sy​​∫0s​yt ds−Iyy​Sx​​∫0s​xt ds

    Since q=τt, shear stress due to the shear forces is,

    💡
    τzy​=tqs​​

    Note that shear flow due to shear force varies around the circumference.

    Additionally, the shear stress due to the torque is,

    💡
    τzy​=2A0​tT​

    Note that shear flow due to torque is constant around the circumference.

    #Part (c)

    Normal stresses acting on the element are,

    σz​=159+100p MPa

    σy​=200p MPa

    Shear stress acting on the element is,

    τzy​=2A0​tT​=2π(1)2(0.005)1×106​

    τzy​=31.8 MPa

    Principal stresses of the element are,

    σmax/min​=2σz​+σy​​±(2σz​−σy​​)2+τzy​2​

    A=79.5+150p MPa

    B=(2159+100p−200p​)2+31.82

    B=(79.5−50p)2+31.82 MPa

    Using the Von Mises criterion,

    (A+B​)2+(A−B​)2−(A+B​)(A−B​)<σy​2

    (A2+2AB​+B)+(A2−2AB​+B)−(A2−B)<σy​2

    A2+3B<σy​2
