    Torsion of Thin-Walled Structures

    #Torsion of Circular Shafts

    Assumptions for a solid, circular shaft:

    • Plane section remains plane before and after the deformation
    • Deformations are small — small angle approximation is valid
    • Section will only twist and does not bend (twist center)
    • Shear strains γ vary linearly from the central axis
    • Within elastic limit, Hooke’s law applies
    • Material is homogeneous

    tanγ=dzr dθ​

    γ=dzr dθ​

    Note that r dθ is the arc length.

    Since τ=Gγ where G is shear modulus,

    τ=Gγ

    τ=Grdzdθ​

    dT=r dF

    dT=r(τ dA)

    T=∫r(τ dA)

    T=∫r(Grdzdθ​) dA

    T=Gdzdθ​∫r2 dA

    T=GJdzdθ​

    Note that ∫r2 dA is the polar moment of inertia.

    Comparing the two equations,

    💡
    rτ​=JT​=Gdzdθ​

    The polar moment of inertia for solid, hollow, and thin-walled shafts are,

    Jsolid​=2πr4​=32πd4​

    Jhollow​=2π​(ro​4−ri​4)

    Jthin​=2πr0​3t

    where r0​ represents the mean radius (measured from center to centerline of thickness)

    Thus, the shear stress for a thin-walled shaft is,

    💡
    τ=2πr0​3tTrm​​=2A0​tT​

    where A0​ represents the mean area enclosed within
    the boundary of the centerline of the shaft’s thickness

    #Torsion Theory

    #Shear Flow

    Consider a hollow cylinder of non-circular section subjected to torsion.
    Assume the thickness of the wall is constant throughout the section.

    We now detach AB from the wall, which is bounded by
    two transverse planes at a distance Δx from each other.

    Since AB is in equilibrium, sum of forces in the
    longitudinal x direction must be equal to zero.

    FA​−FB​=0

    The only forces involved are the shearing forces FA​ and FB​.

    FA​=τA​(tA​Δx)

    FB​=τB​(tB​Δx)

    Substituting the above into the equilibrium equation,

    τA​(tA​Δx)=τB​(tB​Δx)

    τA​tA​=τB​tB​

    💡
    q=τt=const

    Shear flow in the wall of a hollow shaft expressed in N/m.

    #Single-Cell Closed Section

    A closed section refers to a structural member with a closed or continuous perimeter or boundary around
    its cross-sectional shape.

    Consider a closed section beam (tube) subjected to a
    pure torque. The shear force acting on an element ds
    of the tube’s wall is q ds.

    Note that the shear stress τ may vary around the cross-section, since the wall thickness t is now a function of s.

    Thus, the total torque on the tube wall is,

    T=∮r(q ds)

    T=2A0​q

    Substituting the above into the shear stress equation for a thin-walled tube,

    💡
    τ=tq​=2A0​tT​

    Note that the sign of the area A0​ is dependent on the sign of r, which is associated
    with the sign convention for torque. If the integral is carried out in an anticlockwise
    direction,     r is positive, and vice versa.

    The angle of twist per unit length ϕ=dzdθ​ for a thin-walled tube is,

    💡
    ϕ=4A0​2GT​∮tds​
    💡
    ϕ=2A0​G1​∮tq​ ds

    Note that the angle of twist per unit length is expressed in rad/m.

    Since JT​=Gϕ, the polar moment of inertia can also be expressed as,

    💡
    J=∮tds​4A0​2​

    #Example

    Enclosed area of the strut is,

    A0​=πr0​2+l×w

    A0​=π(0.025)2+0.05×0.05

    A0​=0.0044635 m2

    Using the shear stress equation,

    τ=2A0​tT​

    Tmax​=2A0​t⋅τmax​

    Tmax​=2(0.0044635)(0.002)(28×106)

    Tmax​=500 Nm

    Note that the section with the minimum wall thickness will experience
    the highest shear stress and thus, the value of     t must be a minimum.

    ∮tds​=themi​πd​+tsquare​2l​

    ∮tds​=0.003π(0.05)​+0.0022(0.05)​

    ∮tds​=102.36

    Note that the integral is the sum of the perimeter of the member
    divided by the thickness for that section of the member.

    Angle of twist per unit length is,

    ϕ′=4A0​2GT​∮tds​

    ϕmax′​=4(0.0044635)2(27×109)500​⋅102.36

    ϕmax′​=0.023786 rad/m

    Since the strut is 2 meters long,

    ϕmax​=0.023786(2)

    ϕmax​=0.047572 rad

    ϕmax​=2.7257°

    #Multi-Cell Closed Section

    For a multi-cell closed section, the torque acting through the twist center can be replaced with a torque for each individual cell.

    Consider a two-cell thin-walled closed section. At point A, the shear stresses must be in equilibrium along the length of the member.

    q1​L−q2​L−qw​L=0

    qw​=q1​−q2​=q12​

    where subscript w represents the partition wall

    Taking summation of moments about point O,

    T=∫OBCA​ rq1​ ds+∫OADB​ rq2​ ds−∫OAB​ rqw​ ds

    T=2(A1​+Aw​)q1​+2(A2​−Aw​)q2​−2Aw​qw​

    T=2A1​q1​+2A2​q2​

    Thus, for an n-cell closed section,

    💡
    T=2∑Ai​qi​

    Saint-Venant’s theory states that a cross-section does not distort in its own plane. Thus, each cell
    has the same angle of twist per unit length.

    💡
    ϕ=2Ai​G1​(∮tq​ ds)i​

    #Example

    Cell 1

    2Gϕ=A1​1​∮tq​ ds

    2Gϕ=40(80)1​[440+80+40​q1​+335​q12​+345​q13​]

    Cell 2

    2Gϕ=A2​1​∮tq​ ds

    2Gϕ=40(35)1​[(535​+440​)q2​+335​q21​+440​q23​]

    Cell 3

    2Gϕ=A3​1​∮tq​ ds

    2Gϕ=40(45)1​[(440​+545​)q3​+340​q32​+345​q31​]

    Solving the simultaneous equations,

    q1​=165Gϕ

    q2​=168.3Gϕ

    q3​=175.76Gϕ

    Subsituting into the torque equation,

    T=2∑Ai​qi​

    T=2(A1​q1​+A2​q2​+A3​q3​)

    2×106=2(70000)ϕ[40(80)(165)+40(35)(168.3)+40(45)(175.76)]

    ϕ=13.2×10−6 rad/mm

    #Open Section

    An approximate solution for a thin-walled open section can be obtained by representing the torsion of the section as a thin strip with a rectangular cross-section, where t≪b. Shear flow q flows in a closed loop within the section.

    Consider the strip to be made up of a series of thin-walled concentric tubes which all twist by the same amount.

    Enclosed area of the tube is,

    A0​=bt=2bh

    Shear stress of the tube is,

    τ=2A0​tT​=4bh dhdT​

    Angle of twist per unit length is,

    ϕ=4A0​2GT​∮tds​

    ϕ=4(2bh)2GdT​⋅dh2b​

    ϕ=8bh2G dhdT​

    dT=8ϕbh2G dh

    Substituting dT into τ,

    💡
    τ=2Ghϕ

    The applied torque can be determined
    by integrating the shear stress equation.

    ∫dT=∫4bhτ dh

    T=∫0t/2​4bh(2Ghϕ) dh

    T=8bGϕ[31​h3]0t/2​

    T=Gϕ(31​bt3)

    💡
    T=GϕJR​

    where JR​ is the polar moment of inertia of an open section

    Rearranging the equation, the angle of twist per unit length is,

    💡
    ϕ=GJR​T​

    Rearranging the equation and substituting into τ,

    τ=2Gh(GJR​T​)

    τ=JR​2Th​

    Maximum shear stress occurs at h=2t​ and h=−2t​.

    💡
    τmax​=±JR​Tt​

    #Hybrid Section

    For a hybrid section having both closed and open sections, in general, the torsional
    stiffness GJ of the closed section is dominant. The torsional stiffness of the open
    section is usually ignored.

    The combined polar moment of inertia of the hybrid section is,

    💡
    JC​=∮tds​4A0​2​+31​∑bi​ti​3

    The first term represents the polar moment of the closed section.
    The second term represents the polar moment of the open section/s.

    For the closed section,

    ϕ=2A0​G1​∮tq​ ds

    ϕ=2A0​Gq​(J4A0​2​)

    💡
    ϕ=GJ2A0​q​

    Note that J=∮tds​4A0​2​.

    For the open section,

    τmax​=±JR​Tt​

    τmax​=±JTt​

    💡
    τmax​=±GJGTt​

    #Warping of Sections

    Warping is an axially directed displacement, which do not
    vary in the axial direction if there are no axial constraints.

    The shear strain of the element is,

    γ=α+λ

    γ=dsdw​+dzr dθ​

    dsdw​=γ−dzr dθ​

    dsdw​=Gτ​−rϕ

    Integrating the above equation,

    ∫dw=∫Gτ​−rϕ ds

    ws​−w0​=G1​∫0s​τ ds−ϕ∫0s​r ds

    💡
    ws​−w0​=G1​∫0s​τ ds−2ϕAR​

    where AR​ is the sectorial area taken about the twist center O
    and     w0​ is a rigid-body axial translation

    #Open Section

    For an open section with an applied torque through its twist center, the resultant shear stress across the thickness is zero.

    The relative warping displacement is,

    💡
    ws​−w0​=−2ϕAR​

    • An open section will not warp if its sectorial area diagram is zero (e.g. members with rectangular plates that emanate from a single axis)
    • Warping is zero at axis of symmetry
    • Twist center lie on axis of symmetry

    #Closed Section

    For a closed section with an applied torque, the shear stress is replaced with the shear flow over
    its wall thickness.

    The relative warping displacement is,

    💡
    ws​−w0​=G1​∫0s​tq​ ds−2ϕAR​

    Substituting the angle of twist per unit length,

    ws​−w0​=G1​∫0s​tq​ ds−2AR​(2A0​G1​∮tq​ ds)

    💡
    ws​−w0​=G1​∫0s​tq​ ds−A0​GAR​​∮tq​ ds

    If a closed section does not warp, then ws​−w0​=0.

    G1​∫0s​tq​ ds=A0​GAR​​∮tq​ ds

    G1​∫0s​tq​ ds=A0​1​(21​∫0s​r ds)(∮q⋅Gtds​)

    Let δ=∮Gtds​.

    G1​∫0s​tq​ ds=A0​1​(21​∫0s​r ds)(δ∫0s​q)

    G1​∫0s​tq​ ds=2A0​δ​∫0s​rq ds

    💡
    rGt=δ2A0​​

    If the above equation is a constant, then warping of the section is zero.
