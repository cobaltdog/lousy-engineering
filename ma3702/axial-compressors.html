    Axial Compressors

    #Multi-Stage Axial Compressor

    Compressor stage:

    • Each compressor stage consists of a rotor blade and a stator blade
    • Each stage provides a small pressure rise to prevent flow separation
    • Rotor blade rotates with the drum, stator blade is fixed to the casing
    • Inlet guide vanes are not part of the first stage and are considered separately

    Pressure and velocity distribution:

    • Axial velocity is maintained relatively constant
    • Pressure is built up across the compressor stages
    • Constant velocity achieved by annulus geometry and diffusion action of stator blades

    #Generalized Turbomachine

    Notations for a turbomachine:

    • τAA​ torque about the A-A axis
    • ω angular velocity
    • Cθ​ tangential velocity
    • r radius of flow streamline

    Angular momentum of a fluid about axis of rotation,

    💡

    Angular Momentum=m˙rCθ​

    Euler’s equation for compressors,

    W˙=τω

    W˙=m˙(r2​Cθ,2​−r1​Cθ,1​)ω

    W˙=m˙(U2​Cθ,2​−U1​Cθ,1​)

    Euler’s equation for turbines,

    W˙=τω

    W˙=m˙(r1​Uθ,1​−r2​Uθ,2​)ω

    W˙=m˙(U1​Cθ,1​−U2​Cθ,2​)

    Recall that torque is defined as change in angular momentum.
    Blade tip speed of the rotor blades is computed by     U=rω.

    Total enthalpy change per unit mass,

    m˙W˙​=h0,2​−h0,1​=U2​Cθ,2​−U1​Cθ,1​ (for compressors)

    m˙W˙​=h0,1​−h0,2​=U1​Cθ,1​−U2​Cθ,2​ (for turbines)

    #Stage Analysis

    Let U be the absolute velocity of the flow entering the compressor.
    Let α be the absolute flow angle with respect to the horizontal axis.

    Let W be the relative velocity of the flow with respect to the rotor.
    Let β be the relative flow angle with respect to the horizontal axis.

    Approximations:

    • Radius of rotor is constant such that U=rω is constant
    • Axial velocity of the flow Cz​ is constant throughout
    • Absolute velocities C1​=C3​ in a normal stage
    • Absolute flow angles α1​=α3​

    Observations:

    • Rotor causes flow to turn to direction β2​ at velocity W2​
    • Stator deflect flow such that it exits at α3​ and velocity C3​
    • Relative velocity is diffused across the rotor (decrease in magnitude)
      Pressure of the flow rises as a result of this diffusion across the rotor.
    • Absolute velocity increases across the rotor due to increase in tangential velocity
      Work transfer to the flow by the rotor as it imparts angular momentum to the flow.

    #Total Enthalpy Change

    Total enthalpy change across a compressor stage,

    m˙W˙​=h0,2​−h0,1​

    m˙W˙​=U2​Cθ,2​−U1​Cθ,1​

    m˙W˙​=U(Cθ,2​−Cθ,1​)

    m˙W˙​=UCz​(tanα2​−tanα1​)

    💡

    Δh0stage​​=cp​ΔT0stage​​=UCz​(tanα2​−tanα1​)

    1st form of work done equation for a single stage.

    From the velocity diagram,

    Cθ2​=U−wθ,2​

    Cθ2​=U−Cz​tanβ2​

    Alternative expression for total enthalpy change,

    m˙W˙​=U(Cθ,2​−Cθ,1​)

    m˙W˙​=U[(U−Cz​tanβ2​)−(Cz​tanα1​)]

    💡

    Δh0stage​​=cp​ΔT0stage​​=U(U−Cz​tanβ2​−Cz​tanα1​)

    2nd form of work done equation for a single stage.

    Alternative expression for total enthalpy change,

    m˙W˙​=U(Cθ,2​−Cθ,1​)

    m˙W˙​=U[(U−wθ,2​)−(U−wθ,1​)]

    m˙W˙​=U(wθ,1​−wθ,2​)

    💡

    Δh0stage​​=cp​ΔT0stage​​=UCz​(tanβ1​−tanβ2​)

    3rd form of work done equation for a single stage.

    #Stage Pressure Rise

    Pressure ratio across a compressor,

    P2​P3​​=[1+ηc​(T2​T3​​−1)]γ−1γ​

    P2​P3​​=[1+ηc​(T2​T3​−T2​​)]γ−1γ​

    P2​P3​​=(1+ηc​T2​ΔT​)γ−1γ​

    Flow velocity is negligible in a compressor,

    P0,2​P0,3​​=(1+ηc​T0,2​ΔT0​​)γ−1γ​

    Pressure and temperature quantities can be
    replaced with stagnation (total) quantities.

    Similarly, total pressure ratio across a compressor stage,

    💡

    rstage​=P0,1​P0,3​​=(1+ηst​T0,1​ΔT0stage​​​)γ−1γ​=(1+ηst​cp​T0,1​UΔCθ​​)γ−1γ​

    where ηst​ is the compressor stage isentropic efficiency

    Factors affecting stage pressure rise:

    • Increasing blade velocity U
    • Increasing axial velocity Cz​
    • Higher fluid deflection β1​−β2​
    • Higher change in tangential velocity Cθ​

    #Degree of Reaction

    Degree of reaction is defined as the ratio of rotor to overall static enthalpy change.

    💡

    R=Δhstage​Δhrotor​​=Δhrotor​+Δhstator​Δhrotor​​

    For R=0.5 (50% reaction), static pressure rise across the compressor stage
    is split equally between the rotor and stator. 50% of the pressure rise occurs
    across the rotor, and 50% occurs across the stator.

    Assuming isentropic and incompressible flow,

    T ds=dh−v dP

    dh=T ds+v dP

    dh=T ds+ρdP​

    dh=ρdP​

    Δh=ρΔP​

    Static enthalpy change can be
    liken to static pressure change.

    Degree of reaction for an isentropic and incompressible flow,

    💡

    R=ΔPstage​ΔProtor​​

    In the absolute frame, total enthalpy change of the rotor is the same as
    that in the stage.

    h0,2​−h0,1​=h0,3​−h0,1​

    LHS is across the rotor.
    RHS is across the stage.

    In the rotor, total enthalpy change in the rotor’s frame of reference is constant as the flow is steady in that frame.

    h0,1​=h0,2​

    h1​+2w1​2​=h2​+2w2​2​

    h2​−h1​=21​(w1​2−w2​2)

    In a normal stage, absolute velocities C1​=C3​.

    h0,2​−h0,1​=h0,3​−h0,1​

    U(Cθ,2​−Cθ,1​)=(h3​+2C3​2​)−(h1​+2C1​2​)

    U(Cθ,2​−Cθ,1​)=h3​−h1​

    Degree of reaction is expressed as,

    💡

    R=Δhstage​Δhrotor​​=h3​−h1​h2​−h1​​=2U(Cθ,2​−Cθ,1​)w1​2−w2​2​

    If axial velocity Cz​ is constant,

    w1​2−w2​2=(Cz,1​2+wθ,1​2)−(Cz,2​2+wθ,2​2)

    w1​2−w2​2=wθ,1​2−wθ,2​2

    w1​2−w2​2=(wθ,1​+wθ,2​)(wθ,1​−wθ,2​)

    If blade velocity of the rotor U is constant,

    wθ,1​−wθ,2​=(U−Cθ,1​)−(U−Cθ,2​)

    wθ,1​−wθ,2​=Cθ,2​−Cθ,1​

    Substituting into the degree of reaction equation,

    R=2U(Cθ,2​−Cθ,1​)w1​2−w2​2​

    R=2U(wθ,1​−wθ,2​)(wθ,1​+wθ,2​)(wθ,1​−wθ,2​)​

    💡

    R=2Uwθ,1​+wθ,2​​

    #Alternative Expressions

    Let ϕ=UCz​​ be known as the flow coefficient.

    A measure of compressor stage flow rate.

    From the velocity diagram,

    wθ,1​=Cz,1​tanβ1​

    wθ,2​=Cz,2​tanβ2​

    R=2U1​(wθ,1​+wθ,2​)

    R=2UCz​​(tanβ1​+tanβ2​)

    💡

    R=21​ϕ(tanβ1​+tanβ2​)

    Degree of reaction as a function of relative flow angles across the rotor.

    From the velocity diagram,

    U=Cθ​+wθ​

    wθ,1​+wθ,2​=(U1​−Cθ,1​)+(U2​−Cθ,2​)

    wθ,1​+wθ,2​=2U−(Cθ,1​+Cθ,2​)

    R=2U1​[2U−(Cθ,1​+Cθ,2​)]

    R=1−2U1​(Cθ,1​+Cθ,2​)

    R=1−2UCz​​(Cz​Cθ,1​​+Cz​Cθ,2​​)

    💡

    R=1−21​ϕ(tanα1​+tanα2​)

    Degree of reaction as a function of absolute flow angles across the rotor.

    From the velocity diagram,

    U=Cθ​+wθ​

    wθ,1​+wθ,2​=(U−Cθ,1​)+wθ,2​

    R=2U1​[(U−Cθ,1​)+wθ,2​]

    R=21​(1−UCθ,1​​+Uwθ,2​​)

    R=21​[1−UCz​​(Cz​Cθ,1​​−Cz​wθ,2​​)]

    💡

    R=21​[1−ϕ(tanα1​−tanβ2​)]

    Degree of reaction as a function of absolute inflow angle and relative outflow angle.

    #Compressor Stall

    Compressor stall:

    • Flow over the blades or on the wall separates
    • Caused by high incidence angle (positive stall) or low incidence stall (negative stall)
    • Few blades may experience flow separation or a large number of them simultaneously
    • Rotating stall — the stall propagates around the annulus (from one blade to another)

    Flow separation can occur due to:

    • Rotor RPM different from design value
    • Mass flow rate less than design value
    • Inlet flow distortions

    Positive incidence → high AoA (too large)
    Negative incidence → low AoA (too small)

    #Blade Passage Stall

    Blade passage stall:

    • Blade passage is similar to that of a diffuser
    • Cross-section area increases in the flow direction
    • Flow separation occurs on passage walls if cross-section area increases too much

    Wall stall:

    • Diffuser-like stall can result in a wall stall
    • Flow separation occurs on the channel walls instead of on the blade
    • Channel walls include compressor casing, compressor hub, etc

    #Rotating Stall

    Consider a row of axial compressor blades
    operating at a high angle of attack such
    that the blades are close to stalling.

    • Non-uniformity in the inlet flow
    • Local AoA of one blade increases
    • Flow separation occurs on suction side
    • Large flow blockage in that blade channel

    • Blockage causes a diversion of inlet flow
    • Flow diverted to blade above and below
    • Blade above will have increased AoA
    • Blade below will have decreased AoA
    • Blade above is already on the verge
      of stall and it will now tend to stall

    • Stall propagates along the blade row
    • Chain reaction of blades stalling
    • Manifests as a rotating stall
