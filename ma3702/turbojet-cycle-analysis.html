    Turbojet Cycle Analysis

    #Engine Stations

    StationProcess
    a→1Velocity of the air relative to the engine is the flight velocity
    1→2Diffuser — decrease air velocity before entering compressor
    2→3Compressor
    3→4Combustor
    4→5Turbine — exhaust gases expand through the turbine to obtain power

    #Turbojet Cycle

    Stagnation (total) temperature is achieved when a flow is isentropically decelerated
    to zero velocity, with no heat transfer and work. The equation can be derived from
    the first law of thermodynamics.

    q−w=Δh+21​Δ(U2)+Δz

    0=(h2​+21​U2​2)−(h1​+21​U1​2)

    0=(cp​T2​+21​U2​2)−(cp​T1​+21​U1​2)

    0=(T2​+2cp​U2​2​)−(T1​+2cp​U1​2​)

    (T2​+2cp​U2​2​)=(T1​+2cp​U1​2​)

    T0,2​=T0,1​

    Stagnation temperature is therefore defined as,

    💡

    T0​=T+2cp​U2​

    where T is the static temperature, and the 2nd term is kinetic energy

    #Inlet/Diffuser (a→2)

    Assumptions:

    • a→1 is an isentropic process (properties at 1≈a)
    • 1→2 is an adiabatic process and zero velocity at 2
    • Flow decelerates from freestream a through inlet to 2

    Stagnation temperature at station a,

    T0,a​=Ta​+2cpa​​Ua​2​

    Stagnation temperature at station 2,

    T0,2​=T2​+2cpa​​U2​2​

    T0,2​=T2​+2cpa​​0​

    T0,2​=T2​

    Based on stagnation concept (energy conservation),

    T0,2​=T0,a​

    T2​=Ta​+2cpa​​Ua​2​

    💡

    Ta​T2​​=1+2cpa​​Ta​Ua​2​

    Inlet isentropic efficiency,

    ηi​=h2​−ha​h2s​−ha​​

    ηi​=T2​−Ta​T2s​−Ta​​

    ηi​=T2​/Ta​−1T2s​/Ta​−1​

    ηi​(Ta​T2​​−1)=Ta​T2s​​−1

    Ta​T2s​​=1+ηi​(Ta​T2​​−1)

    💡

    Ta​T2s​​=1+ηi​2cpa​​Ta​Ua​2​=1+ηi​2γ−1​Ma​2

    From isentropic relations of an ideal gas,

    Pa​P2​​=Pa​P2s​​=(Ta​T2s​​)γ−1γ​

    💡

    Pa​P2​​=(1+ηi​2cpa​​Ta​Ua​2​)γ−1γ​=(1+ηi​2γ−1​Ma​2)γ−1γ​

    #Compressor (2→3)

    Assumptions:

    • Flow velocity is small where T0​=T
    • Ideal process is an isentropic process
    • Real process is an irreversible process due to heat loss

    Compressor isentropic efficiency,

    ηc​=h3​−h2​h3s​−h2​​

    ηc​=T3​−T2​T3s​−T2​​

    ηc​=T3​/T2​−1T3s​/T2​−1​

    ηc​(T2​T3​​−1)=T2​T3s​​−1

    💡

    T2​T3s​​=1+ηc​(T2​T3​​−1)

    From isentropic relations of an ideal gas,

    P2​P3​​=P2​P3s​​=(T2​T3s​​)γ−1γ​

    💡

    P2​P3​​=[1+ηc​(T2​T3​​−1)]γ−1γ​

    Rearranging the efficiency equation,

    ηc​(T2​T3​​−1)=T2​T3s​​−1

    T2​T3​​=1+ηc​1​(T2​T3s​​−1)

    💡

    T2​T3​​=1+ηc​1​[(P2​P3​​)γγ−1​−1]

    #Polytropic Compression Process

    Compression process can be described as a polytropic process PVn=C.

    P2​P3​​=(T2​T3​​)n−1n​

    ηc,poly​=n−1n​⋅γγ−1​

    #Compressor Work

    Actual mechanical work of compressor,

    💡

    W˙m,c​=ηm,c​m˙a​cpa​​(T3​−T2​)​

    where ηm,c​ is the mechanical efficiency and is ≤1

    #Combustor (3→4)

    Assumptions:

    • Flow velocity is small where T0​=T
    • Mass conservation applies
    • Ideal process is an isobaric process
    • Real process has pressure drop (usually small and neglected)

    Heat addition in combustor,

    Q˙​−W˙=ΔH˙

    Q˙​in​=H˙4​−H˙3​

    💡

    Qin​=(m˙a​+m˙f​)cpg​​T4​−m˙a​cpa​​T3​

    In an ideal process, all the chemical energy of fuel is extracted and converted into heat.

    Q˙​fuel​=m˙f​QR​ where QR​ is the fuel heating value (caloric value)

    Combustion efficiency,

    💡

    ηb​=Q˙​fuel​ΔH˙​=m˙f​QR​(m˙a​+m˙f​)cpg​​T4​−m˙a​cpa​​T3​​

    Factors affecting efficiency:

    • Heat losses from combustor
    • Flow losses (friction due to fluid viscosity)
    • Incomplete combustion (not all the fuel is burned)

    #Turbine (4→5)

    Assumptions:

    • Flow velocity is small where T0​=T
    • Ideal process is an isentropic process
    • Real process is an irreversible process due to heat loss

    Turbine isentropic efficiency,

    ηt​=h4​−h5s​h4​−h5​​

    ηt​=T4​−T5s​T4​−T5​​

    ηt​=1−T5s​/T4​1−T5​/T4​​

    ηt​(1−T4​T5s​​)=1−T4​T5​​

    💡

    T4​T5s​​=1−ηt​1​(1−T4​T5​​)

    From isentropic relations of an ideal gas,

    P4​P5​​=P4​P5s​​=(T4​T5s​​)γ−1γ​

    💡

    P4​P5​​=[1−ηt​1​(1−T4​T5​​)]γ−1γ​

    Rearranging the efficiency equation,

    ηt​(1−T4​T5s​​)=1−T4​T5​​

    T4​T5​​=1−ηt​(1−T4​T5s​​)

    💡

    T4​T5​​=1−ηt​[1−(P4​P5​​)γγ−1​]

    #Polytropic Expansion Process

    Expansion process can be described as a polytropic process PVn=C.

    P4​P5​​=(T4​T5​​)n−1n​

    ηt,poly​=nn−1​⋅γ−1γ​

    #Compressor-Turbine Work

    Compressor is driven by the turbine. In an ideal cycle, the work input to the compressor
    is equal to the work output of the turbine. However, due to mechanical efficiencies, they
    are not the same.

    Actual mechanical work of turbine,

    💡

    W˙m,t​=[(m˙a​+m˙f​)cpg​​(T4​−T5​)]ηm,t​

    where ηm,t​ is the mechanical efficiency and is ≤1

    Mechanical work input to compressor is equal to mechanical work output of turbine.

    W˙m,c​=W˙m,t​

    ηm,c​m˙a​cpa​​(T3​−T2​)​=[(m˙a​+m˙f​)cpg​​(T4​−T5​)]ηm,t​

    Accounting for transmission efficiency, the above equation becomes,

    💡

    ηm,c​m˙a​cpa​​(T3​−T2​)​=ηtransmission​[(m˙a​+m˙f​)cpg​​(T4​−T5​)]ηm,t​

    #Nozzle (5→7)

    Assumptions:

    • Negligible inlet velocity U5​≈0
    • Expansion process with no work done
    • Final pressure P7​ is the exhaust pressure Pe​

    Nozzle isentropic efficiency,

    ηn​=h5​−h7s​h5​−h7​​

    ηn​=T5​−T7s​T5​−T7​​

    ηn​=1−T7s​/T5​1−T7​/T5​​

    ηn​(1−T5​T7s​​)=1−T5​T7​​

    💡

    T5​T7s​​=1−ηn​1​(1−T5​T7​​)

    From isentropic relations of an ideal gas,

    P5​P7​​=P5​P7s​​=(T5​T7s​​)γ−1γ​

    💡

    P5​P7​​=[1−ηn​1​(1−T5​T7​​)]γ−1γ​

    Rearranging the efficiency equation,

    ηn​(1−T5​T7s​​)=1−T5​T7​​

    T5​T7​​=1−ηn​(1−T5​T7s​​)

    💡

    T5​T7​​=1−ηn​[1−(P5​P7​​)γγ−1​]

    #Nozzle Exit Velocity

    Based on stagnation concept (energy conservation),

    T0,5​=T0,7​

    T5​+2cpg​​U5​2​=T7​+2cpg​​U7​2​

    T5​=T7​+2cpg​​U7​2​

    Nozzle exit velocity,

    U7​=2cpg​​(T5​−T7​)​

    U7​=2cpg​​ηn​T5​[1−(P5​P7​​)γγ−1​]​

    💡

    U7​=2ηn​γ−1γ​Rg​T5​[1−(P5​P7​​)γγ−1​]​

    Rewriting the nozzle exit velocity in terms of Mach number,

    💡

    M7​=γ−12​(T7​T5​​−1)​

    #Choked and Unchoked

    Based on stagnation concept,

    T5​=T7​+2cpg​​U7​2​

    T5​=T7​(1+2γ−1​M7​2)

    T5​T7​​=1+2γ−1​M7​21​

    Pressure ratio across the nozzle,

    💡

    P5​P7​​=[1−ηn​1​(1−1+2γ−1​M7​21​)]γ−1γ​

    If nozzle exit velocity M7​=1, the nozzle exit is choked (sonic).

    💡

    P5​Pcr​​=(1−ηn​1​γ+1γ−1​)γ−1γ​

    If nozzle exit velocity M7​=1 and nozzle efficiency ηn​=1,

    💡

    P5​Pcr​​​ηn​=1​=(γ+12​)γ−1γ​

    where Pcr​ is the critical pressure at which nozzle becomes choked

    For a subsonic nozzle, to determine if nozzle is choked or unchoked:

    • Calculate critical pressure Pcr​
    • Compare Pcr​ with ambient pressure Pa​
    • If Pcr​>Pa​ nozzle is choked and nozzle exit pressure P7​=Pcr​
    • If Pcr​<Pa​ nozzle is unchoked and nozzle exit pressure P7​=Pa​
