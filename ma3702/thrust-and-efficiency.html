    Thrust and Efficiency

    #Thrust Equation

    General form of the Reynolds transport theorem for a fixed, nondeforming control volume,

    dtdBsys​​=∂t∂​∫CV​ ρb dV+∫CS​ ρb(u⋅n) dA

    Valid for a stationary CV with a fixed volume.

    From Newton’s second law, F=ma=dtd​(mu). Let B=mu and b=u.

    dtd​(mu)=dtd​∫CV​ ρu dV+∮CS​ ρu(u⋅n) dA

    ∑F=dtd​∫CV​ ρu dV+∮CS​ ρu(u⋅n) dA

    where V is the volume of the mass

    Consider a thrust-producing device flying at velocity u and in ambient pressure Pa​.

    Control volume around device:

    • Sufficiently far upstream such that flow is undisturbed and uniform
    • At point 1, assume velocity is negligible and pressure is atmospheric
    • At point 2, flow is nearly one-dimensional and momentum can be measured

    Assumptions (control volume):

    • Inflow surface far from the engine such that flow properties are uniform
    • Outflow, excluding exhaust, flow has the same properties as freestream
    • Side surfaces are far from the engine such that u and p unchanged

    Assumptions (momentum equation):

    • Flow is steady such that dtd​ terms are zero
    • Flow is spatially uniform at each cross section
    • Only interested in the forces in the axial direction

    Momentum equation derived from Reynolds transport theorem,

    ∑F=dtd​∫CV​ ρu dV+∮CS​ ρu(u⋅n) dA

    Simplifying with the assumptions,

    💡
    ∑Fx​=∮CS​ ρux​(u⋅n) dA

    Sum of axial forces (LHS) is equal to the momentum flux (RHS),
    the rate of transfer of momentum across the control surfaces.

    #Summation of Momentum Fluxes

    Sign of u⋅n:

    • Negative if flow enters the control volume
    • Positive if flow exits the control volume

    Mass flux is related to momentum flux by,

    m˙=∫CS​ dm˙=∫CS​ ρ(u⋅n) dA

    For a steady-state flow, mass flux is zero.

    ∮CS​ ρ(u⋅n) dA=0

    Mass conservation.

    Mass flux through control volume,

    ∮CS​ ρ(u⋅n) dA=∫1​ ρ(u⋅n) dA+∫2​ ρ(u⋅n) dA+∫side​ ρ(u⋅n) dA

    0=[−m˙a​−ρu(A−Ai​)]+[m˙e​+ρu(A−Ae​)]−m˙f​+m˙side​

    0=−m˙a​+ρuAi​+m˙e​−ρuAe​−m˙f​+m˙side​

    where i represents inlet, and e represents exhaust

    Conservation of mass in the engine,

    m˙a​+m˙f​=m˙e​

    m˙e​−m˙a​−m˙f​=0

    Mass flux equation becomes,

    0=ρuAi​−ρuAe​+m˙side​

    💡
    m˙side​=ρu(Ae​−Ai​)

    If the sides of the control volume are sufficiently distant from the engine, it is assumed
    that the flow crossing the control surface in the     y-direction has a very small component. Therefore, the mass flow through the sides only has an undisturbed velocity component
    in the     x-direction.

    Momentum flux across the control volume,

    ∮CS​ ρux​(u⋅n) dA=−m˙a​ua​+ρuAi​u+m˙e​ue​−ρuAe​u+m˙side​u

    ∮CS​ ρux​(u⋅n) dA=−m˙a​ua​+ρuAi​u+m˙e​ue​−ρuAe​u+ρu(Ae​−Ai​)u

    💡
    ∮CS​ ρux​(u⋅n) dA=m˙e​ue​−m˙a​ua​

    Momentum of m˙f​ is neglected as we are only interested in the axial direction.

    #Summation of Axial Forces

    Now consider the forces acting on the control volume.

    Sum of axial forces acting on the control volume,

    ∑Fx​=T+(Pa​A)−(Pe​Ae​)−Pa​(A−Ae​)

    ∑Fx​=T−Pe​Ae​+Pa​Ae​

    ∑Fx​=T+(Pa​−Pe​)Ae​

    #Thrust Equation

    Momentum equation for the control volume,

    ∑Fx​=∮CS​ ρux​(u⋅n) dA

    T+(Pa​−Pe​)Ae​=m˙e​ue​−m˙a​ua​

    T+(Pa​−Pe​)Ae​=(m˙a​+m˙f​)ue​−m˙a​ua​

    T+(Pa​−Pe​)Ae​=m˙a​[(1+m˙a​m˙f​​)ue​−ua​]

    Let f=m˙a​m˙f​​ be defined as the fuel-air ratio.

    💡
    T=m˙a​[(1+f)ue​−ua​]+(Pe​−Pa​)Ae​

    1st term on the RHS represents the change in momentum flux (not a force).
    2nd term represents excess pressure in the nozzle, pressure force (a force).

    In most engine operating conditions, some approximations may apply.

    • Fuel is only a small percent of the airflow such that m˙f​≪m˙a​ and f≪1
    • Pressure thrust is small compared to change in momentum flux
    • Exhaust nozzle is designed such that Pe​≈Pa​

    Simplified thrust equation,

    💡
    T=m˙a​(ue​−ua​)

    #Engine Performance

    #Propulsion Efficiency

    The propulsion efficiency is a measure of the effectiveness of the propulsion device
    to convert the potentially useful kinetic energy to thrust power to propel the aircraft.

    The ratio of thrust power to the rate of production of propellant kinetic energy.
    The product of thrust and flight velocity T×ua​ is called the thrust power.

    For a single propellent stream,

    💡
    ηp​=ΔKEpropellent​Thrust Power​=21​m˙a​[(1+f)ue​2−ua​2]Tua​​

    Recall the simplified thrust equation,

    T=m˙a​(ue​−ua​)

    Propulsion efficiency is then,

    ηp​=21​m˙a​(ue​2−ua​2)m˙a​(ue​−ua​)ua​​

    ηp​=(ue​+ua​)(ue​−ua​)2(ue​−ua​)ua​​

    💡
    ηp​=ue​+ua​2ua​​=1+ue​/ua​2​

    The above equation is valid only for when f≪1 and pressure thrust is negligible.

    Extreme case 1, static condition, where ua​=0:

    • Based on thrust equation T=m˙a​ue​, thrust is maximum
    • Based on efficiency equation, ηp​=0, inefficient propulsion

    Extreme case 2, exhaust velocity equal to flight velocity, where ue​=ua​:

    • Based on thrust equation, T=0, no thrust produced
    • Based on efficiency equation, ηp​=1, perfect efficiency

    #Thermal Efficiency

    Thermal efficiency is a measure of the effectiveness of energy conversion from
    the fuel to potentially useful kinetic energy (mechanical work) for propulsion.

    The ratio of the rate of addition of kinetic energy to the propellent to the total
    energy consumption rate m˙f​QR​ where QR​ is the heat of reaction of the fuel.

    Thermal efficiency is defined as,

    💡
    ηth​=Q˙​fuel​ΔKEpropellent​​=m˙f​QR​21​m˙a​[(1+f)ue​2−ua​2]​

    Making use of the simplified thrust equation,

    ηth​=m˙f​QR​21​m˙a​(ue​2−ua​2)​

    💡
    ηth​=2fQR​ue​2−ua​2​

    The above equation is valid only for when f≪1 and pressure thrust is negligible.

    #Overall Efficiency

    Overall efficiency is defined as the ratio of the thrust power generated by the engine to
    the total energy consumption rate of the fuel. It is the product of the other 2 efficiencies.

    Overall efficiency is defined as,

    💡
    ηo​=Fuel PowerThrust Power​=m˙f​QR​Tua​​=ηp​×ηth​

    Making use of the simplified thrust equation,

    ηo​=ue​+ua​2ua​​×2fQR​ue​2−ua​2​

    ηo​=ue​+ua​2ua​​×2fQR​(ue​+ua​)(ue​−ua​)​

    💡
    ηo​=fQR​(ue​−ua​)ua​​

    The above equation is valid only for when f≪1 and pressure thrust is negligible.

    #Unused Kinetic Energy

    Mechanical work generated by the engine is converted into:

    • Usable thrust power
    • Unused kinetic energy that is dumped out

    ΔKE across engine=Thrust power+Unused KE

    21​m˙a​(ue​2−ua​2)=m˙a​(ue​−ua​)ua​+Unused KE

    Rearranging the terms,

    Unused KE=21​m˙a​(ue​2−ua​2)−m˙a​(ue​−ua​)ua​

    Unused KE=21​m˙a​[(ue​2−ua​2)−2ue​ua​+2ua​2]

    Unused KE=21​m˙a​(ue​2−2ue​ua​+ua​2)

    💡
    Unused KE=21​m˙a​(ue​−ua​)2

    The above equation is valid only for when f≪1 and pressure thrust is negligible.

    #Specific Thrust & Thrust Specific Fuel Consumption

    Specific thrust is a measure of how effective an engine
    is in producing thrust for a given amount of airflow rate.

    💡
    Ts​=m˙a​T​

    where T is in newtons, and m˙a​ is in kg/s

    Thrust specific fuel consumption is amount of fuel consumed per unit thrust produced.

    💡
    TSFC=Tm˙f​​=ηo​QR​ua​​

    where m˙f​ is in kg/hr, and T is in newtons, overall S.I. unit is kg/N⋅hr
