    Finite Elements (Part 2)

    #Constant Strain Triangular (CST) Element

    Element characteristics:

    • 2D element described by 3 nodes
    • 6 DOF per element (2 DOF per node)
    • Deformation only along the x and y axes
    • Nodes are numbered in anticlockwise direction

    Displacement field,

    u=N1​q1​+N2​q3​+N3​q5​ (displacement in the x-direction)

    v=N1​q2​+N2​q4​+N3​q6​ (displacement in the y-direction)

    Shape functions,

    N1​=ζ

    N2​=η

    N3​=1−ζ−η

    Note that N1​+N2​+N3​=1.

    Substituting the shape functions,

    u=ζq1​+ηq3​+(1−ζ−η)q5​

    u=(q1​−q5​)ζ+(q3​−q5​)η+q5​

    v=ζq2​+ηq4​+(1−ζ−η)q6​

    v=(q2​−q6​)ζ+(q4​−q6​)η+q6​

    Coordinate transformation,

    x=[N]{x}

    x=N1​x1​+N2​x2​+N3​x3​

    x=ζx1​+ηx2​+(1−ζ−η)x3​

    x=(x1​−x3​)ζ+(x2​−x3​)η+x3​

    x=x13​ζ+x23​η+x3​

    y=[N]{y}

    y=N1​y1​+N2​y2​+N3​y3​

    y=ζy1​+ηy2​+(1−ζ−η)y3​

    y=(y1​−y3​)ζ+(y2​−y3​)η+y3​

    y=y13​ζ+y23​η+y3​

    #Strain Matrix

    Strain-displacement relation,

    {ε}=⎩⎨⎧​εx​εy​γxy​​⎭⎬⎫​=⎩⎨⎧​∂u/∂x∂v/∂y∂u/∂y+∂v/∂x​⎭⎬⎫​=[B]{q}

    Solving for the partial derivatives gives an expression for B.

    Partial differentiation of displacement field,

    ∂ζ∂u​=∂x∂u​⋅∂ζ∂x​+∂y∂u​⋅∂ζ∂y​=q1​−q5​

    ∂η∂u​=∂x∂u​⋅∂η∂x​+∂y∂u​⋅∂η∂y​=q3​−q5​

    Note that u is a function of ζ and η.

    Partial differentiation in matrix form,

    {∂u/∂ζ∂u/∂η​}=[∂x/∂ζ∂x/∂η​∂y/∂ζ∂y/∂η​]{∂u/∂x∂u/∂y​}

    {∂u/∂x∂u/∂y​}=[∂x/∂ζ∂x/∂η​∂y/∂ζ∂y/∂η​]−1{∂u/∂ζ∂u/∂η​}

    {∂u/∂x∂u/∂y​}=[J]−1{q1​−q5​q3​−q5​​}

    {∂u/∂x∂u/∂y​}=det∣J∣1​[y23​−x23​​−y13​x13​​]{q1​−q5​q3​−q5​​}

    Jacobian matrix is defined as,

    💡

    [J​]=[∂ζ∂x​∂η∂x​​∂ζ∂y​∂η∂y​​]=[x13​x23​​y13​y23​​]

    Refer to the coordinate transformation equations.

    Determinant of the Jacobian matrix,

    💡

    det∣J∣=x13​y23​−x23​y13​

    Solving for the partial derivative,

    ∂x∂u​=det∣J∣1​[y23​(q1​−q5​)−y13​(q3​−q5​)]

    ∂x∂u​=det∣J∣1​[y23​q1​−y13​q3​−(y23​−y13​)q5​]

    ∂x∂u​=det∣J∣1​{y23​q1​−y13​q3​−[(y2​−y3​)−(y1​−y3​)]q5​}

    ∂x∂u​=det∣J∣1​[y23​q1​−y13​q3​−(y2​−y1​)q5​]

    ∂x∂u​=det∣J∣1​(y23​q1​−y13​q3​−y21​q5​)

    ∂x∂u​=det∣J∣1​(y23​q1​+y31​q3​+y12​q5​)

    The above equation corresponds to the 1st row of B.

    Substituting the partial derivatives into {ε}, we can obtain an expression for B.

    💡

    B=det∣J∣1​​y23​0x32​​0x32​y23​​y31​0x13​​0x13​y31​​y12​0x21​​0x21​y12​​​

    Since all elements in B are constants, the strain is constant.

    #Element Stiffness Matrix

    Assume plane stress conditions (only stresses in the x and y directions).

    {σ}=⎩⎨⎧​σx​σy​τxy​​⎭⎬⎫​=1−νE​⎩⎨⎧​1ν0​ν10​0021−ν​​⎭⎬⎫​⎩⎨⎧​εx​εy​γxy​​⎭⎬⎫​=[D][B]{q}

    Strain energy in a single element,

    Ue​=21​∫V​ εTDεd dV

    Ue​=21​∫A​ qTBTDBqte​ dA

    Ue​=21​qTkeq

    Element stiffness matrix,

    ke=∫A​ (BTDB)te​ dA

    💡

    ke=te​Ae​BTDB

    Note that element thickness te​ is assumed to be constant.

    #Nodal Force Vectors

    #Body Force f

    Nodal force vector due to body force,

    fe=∫e​ (NTf) dVe​

    fe=∫A​ (NTf)te​ dA

    fe=∫A​ [N]T{fx​fy​​}te​ dA

    💡

    fe=3te​Ae​​[fx​​fy​​fx​​fy​​fx​​fy​​]T

    #Surface Traction T

    Nodal force vector due to surface traction,

    fe=∫e​ (NTT) dVe​

    fe=∫L​ (NTT)te​ dl

    fe=∫L​ [N]T{Tx​Ty​​}te​ dl

    💡

    fe=6te​l12​​⎩⎨⎧​2Tx,1​+Tx,2​2Ty,1​+Ty,2​Tx,1​+2Tx,2​Ty,1​+2Ty,2​​⎭⎬⎫​

    where the subscript represents the respective forces at a local node,
    the term     Tx,1​ represents the force in the x-direction at local node 1

    #Isoparametric 4-Node Quadrilateral Element

    Elements with irregular shapes are mapped into a standard shape.

    • Mapped element is called the “natural” or “master” element
    • Mathematical operations are performed on the mapped element

    Displacement field,

    u=N1​q1​+N2​q3​+N3​q5​+N4​q7​

    v=N1​q2​+N2​q4​+N3​q6​+N4​q8​

    Coordinate transformation,

    x=N1​x1​+N2​x2​+N3​x3​+N4​x4​

    y=N1​y1​+N2​y2​+N3​y3​+N4​y4​

    Interpolating shape functions,

    N1​=41​(1−ζ)(1−η)

    N2​=41​(1+ζ)(1−η)

    N3​=41​(1+ζ)(1+η)

    N4​=41​(1−ζ)(1+η)

    #Element Stiffness Matrix

    Strain-displacement relation,

    {ε}=⎩⎨⎧​εx​εy​γxy​​⎭⎬⎫​=⎩⎨⎧​∂u/∂x∂v/∂y∂u/∂y+∂v/∂x​⎭⎬⎫​=[B]{q}

    Solving for the partial derivatives gives an expression for B.

    Partial differentiation of displacement field,

    ∂ζ∂f​=∂x∂f​⋅∂ζ∂x​+∂y∂f​⋅∂ζ∂y​

    ∂η∂f​=∂x∂f​⋅∂η∂x​+∂y∂f​⋅∂η∂y​

    Partial differentiation in matrix form,

    {∂f/∂ζ∂f/∂η​}=[∂x/∂ζ∂x/∂η​∂y/∂ζ∂y/∂η​]{∂f/∂x∂f/∂y​}

    {∂f/∂ζ∂f/∂η​}=[J]{∂f/∂x∂f/∂y​}

    {∂f/∂x∂f/∂y​}=[J]−1{∂f/∂ζ∂f/∂η​}

    {∂f/∂x∂f/∂y​}=det∣J∣1​[J22​−J21​​−J12​J11​​]{∂f/∂ζ∂f/∂η​}

    Note that det∣J∣>0 as a zero or negative value signifies invalid mapping.
    Solve by letting function     f represent u and v gives the partial derivatives.
    Substituting the partial derivatives into     {e} gives an expression for B.

    Jacobian matrix is defined as,

    💡

    [J]=[∂ζ∂x​∂η∂x​​∂ζ∂y​∂η∂y​​]=[J11​J21​​J12​J22​​]

    Determinant of the Jacobian matrix,

    💡

    det∣J∣=J11​J22​−J12​J21​

    Expressions for J,

    J11​=41​[−(1−η)x1​+(1−η)x2​+(1+η)x3​−(1+η)x4​]

    J12​=41​[−(1−η)y1​+(1−η)y2​+(1+η)y3​−(1+η)y4​]

    J21​=41​[−(1−ζ)x1​−(1+ζ)x2​+(1+ζ)x3​+(1−ζ)x4​]

    J22​=41​[−(1−ζ)y1​−(1+ζ)y2​+(1+ζ)y3​+(1−ζ)y4​]

    Element stiffness matrix,

    ke=∫e​ (BTDB)⋅te​ dxdy

    💡

    ke=te​∫−11​∫−11​ (BTDB)⋅det∣J∣ dζdη

    #Nodal Force Vectors

    Nodal force vector due to body force,

    💡

    fe=te​[∫−11​∫−11​ NT⋅det∣J∣ dζdη]{fx​fy​​}

    Nodal force vector due to edge loading,

    💡

    fe=∫edge​ (NTT)te​ dl
