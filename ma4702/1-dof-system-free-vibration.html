    1-DOF System (Free Vibration)

    #Vibration Modelling

    Degrees of freedom is the minimum number of independent coordinates
    required to determine the positions of all the particles/components in a
    system at any instant of time.

    Consider a car subjected to only vertical vibration.

    Elements of a vibration model:

    • Mass element with mass m
    • Spring element with spring constant k
    • Damping element with damping constant c

    #Spring Element

    Moment of inertia for beams,

    Irect​=12bh3​ (Rectangular section)

    Icyl​=64π(d2​4−d1​4)​ (Cylindrical section)

    Polar moment of inertia for shafts,

    IP​=2πr4​ (Solid shaft)

    IP​=32π(d2​4−d1​4)​ (Hollow shaft)

    Springs in parallel,

    💡

    keq​=k1​+k2​+⋯+kn​

    Spring elements are considered to be in parallel when they experience the same deformation.

    Springs in series,

    💡

    keq​1​=k1​1​+k2​1​+⋯+kn​1​

    #Cantilevered Beam

    Spring constant (stiffness) of the beam,

    💡

    k=δF​=L33EI​

    Expressed in N/m.

    #Simply Supported Beam

    Spring constant (stiffness) of the beam,

    💡

    k=L348EI​

    Expressed in N/m.

    Spring constant (stiffness) of the beam,

    💡

    k=a2(L−a)23EIL​

    Expressed in N/m.

    #Shaft

    Torsional stiffness of the shaft,

    💡

    kt​=θT​=LGIP​​

    Expressed in Nm/rad.

    #Cable

    Axial stiffness of the cable,

    💡

    ka​=δF​=LEA​

    Expressed in N/m.

    #Mass Element

    An object has a mass m and radius of gyration k.

    It can undergo two different types of vibrations.

    • Translational vibration
    • Rotational vibration

    In translational vibration, the object’s equivalent mass
    is simply the total mass of the object, where meq​=m.

    In rotational vibration, the object’s mass moment of inertia,

    J=mk2+ma2

    where k is the radius of gyration,
    and     a is the distance of mass center C from pivot point A

    #Principle of Equal Kinetic Energy

    For a coupled translational and rotational masses, they can be modeled as a 1-DOF
    system as the coordinates that describe the position of each mass are related by a
    common equation.

    For a rack-and-pinion mechanism,

    x=Rθ

    Equivalent translational mass,

    21​meq​x˙2=21​mx˙2+21​J0​θ˙2

    21​meq​x˙2=21​mx˙2+21​J0​R2x˙2​

    💡

    meq​=m+R2J0​​

    Equivalent mass moment of inertia,

    21​Jeq​θ˙2=21​mx˙2+21​J0​θ˙2

    21​Jeq​θ˙2=21​m(Rθ˙)2+21​J0​θ˙2

    💡

    Jeq​=J0​+mR2

    #Principle of Equal Potential Energy

    Besides equivalent mass, we also need to determine the equivalent stiffness.

    Let the displacement x1​=x such that θ=ax​.

    Displacement of spring 2,

    x=aθ     and     x2​=2bθ

    θ=ax​     and     θ=2bx2​​

    ax​=2bx2​​

    x2​=a2b​x

    Displacement of spring 3,

    x=aθ     and     x3​=bθ

    θ=ax​     and     θ=bx3​​

    ax​=bx3​​

    x3​=ab​x

    Equivalent stiffness,

    21​keq​x2=21​k1​x1​2+21​k2​x2​2+21​k3​x3​2

    21​keq​x2=21​k1​x2+21​k2​(a2b​x)2+21​k3​(ab​x)2

    21​keq​x2=21​k1​x2+21​k2​a24b2​x2+21​k3​a2b2​x2

    💡

    keq​=k1​+a24b2​k2​+a2b2​k3​

    #Undamped System

    Free vibration response of an undamped 1-DOF system can be described by,

    x(t)=Xsin(ωn​t+ϕ)

    where ωn​=2πfn​=T2π​

    Natural frequency of vibration of an undamped 1-DOF system,

    💡

    ωn​=mk​​

    Consider a 1-DOF system with mass m and spring stiffness k.
    Initial disturbance (conditions) are x(0)=x0​ and x˙(0)=x˙0​.

    Applying Newton’s 2nd law,

    F=ma

    −kx=mx¨

    0=mx¨+kx

    0=x¨+ωn​2x

    Solution to the linear homogeneous 2nd order O.D.E. is,

    x(t)=Acos(ωn​t)+Bsin(ωn​t)

    x˙(t)=−Aωn​sin(ωn​t)+Bωn​cos(ωn​t)

    Coefficients A and B are determined from initial conditions.

    Substituting x(0)=x0​ into the 1st equation,

    x0​=Acos(ωn​×0)+Bsin(ωn​×0)

    A=x0​

    Substituting x˙(0)=x˙0​ into the 2nd equation,

    x˙0​=−Aωn​sin(ωn​×0)+Bωn​cos(ωn​×0)

    x˙0​=Bωn​

    B=ωn​x˙0​​

    Free vibration response of an undamped 1-DOF system,

    💡

    x(t)=x0​cos(ωn​t)+ωn​x˙0​​sin(ωn​t)

    Free vibration response of an undamped 1-DOF system (alternative),

    💡

    x(t)=Xsin(ωn​t+ϕ)

    where X=A2+B2​ and ϕ=tan−1BA​+π,
    (add     π if denominator of arctan is less than zero)

    #Damped System

    If a system possesses damping, its free vibration due to initial disturbances
    will gradually die out with time and the system will eventually return to its
    static equilibrium state.

    Critical damping constant of a system,

    💡

    ccr​=2km​=2mωn​=ωn​2k​

    S.I. unit of newton-second per meter.

    Degree of damping of a system is defined in terms of the damping ratio.

    💡

    ζ=ccr​c​=2km​c​=2mωn​c​=2k/ωn​c​

    Consider a 1-DOF system with mass m, spring stiffness k, and damping constant c.

    Applying Newton’s 2nd law,

    ∑F=ma

    −kx−cx˙=mx¨

    mx¨+cx˙+kx=0

    General solution to the above equation is,

    x=est

    Differentiating the solution w.r.t. time t,

    x˙=sest

    x¨=s2est

    Substituting into Newton’s 2nd law equation,

    m(s2est)+c(sest)+k(est)=0

    est(ms2+cs+k)=0

    ms2+cs+k=0 (Characteristic equation)

    Roots of the characteristic equation are,

    s1,2​=2m−c±c2−4km​​

    s1,2​=−2mc​±(2mc​)2−mk​​

    Free vibration response of a damped 1-DOF system,

    x(t)=C1​es1​t+C2​es2​t

    💡

    x(t)=C1​e{−2mc​+(2mc​)2−mk​​ }t+C2​e{−2mc​−(2mc​)2−mk​​ }t

    Rewriting the power in terms of ccr​ and ζ,

    2mc​=ccr​c​⋅2mccr​​

    2mc​=ζ⋅2m2mωn​​

    2mc​=ζωn​

    Free vibration response of a damped 1-DOF system,

    x(t)=C1​e{−ζωn​+(ζωn​)2−ωn​2​ }t+C2​e{−ζωn​−(ζωn​)2−ωn​2​ }t

    💡

    x(t)=C1​e{−ζ+ζ2−1​ }ωn​t+C2​e{−ζ−ζ2−1​ }ωn​t

    #Underdamped

    For an underdamped system, where ζ<1 or c<2km​,

    x(t)=e−ζωn​t[Acos(ωn​t1−ζ2​)+Bsin(ωn​t1−ζ2​)]

    x˙(t)=−ζωn​e−ζωn​t[Acos(ωn​t1−ζ2​)+Bsin(ωn​t1−ζ2​)]            +e−ζωn​t[−Aωn​1−ζ2​sin(ωn​t1−ζ2​)            +Bωn​1−ζ2​cos(ωn​t1−ζ2​)]

    Coefficients A and B are determined from initial conditions.

    Assume initial conditions at t=0 are given as x(0)=x0​ and x˙(0)=x˙0​.

    Substituting x(0)=x0​,

    x0​=e0(Acos0+Bsin0)

    A=x0​

    Substituting x˙(0)=x˙0​,

    x˙0​=−ζωn​e0(Acos0+Bsin0)         +e0(−Aωn​1−ζ2​sin0+Bωn​1−ζ2​cos0)

    x˙0​=−ζωn​A+Bωn​1−ζ2​

    x˙0​=−ζωn​x0​+Bωn​1−ζ2​

    B=ωn​1−ζ2​x˙0​+ζωn​x0​​

    Damping frequency of the system is,

    💡

    ωd​=ωn​1−ζ2​

    Free vibration response of a 1-DOF system (underdamped),

    💡

    x(t)=e−ζωn​t[x0​cos(ωn​t1−ζ2​)+ωn​1−ζ2​x˙0​ζωn​x0​​sin(ωn​t1−ζ2​)]

    Rewriting the vibration response based on trigonometry,

    💡

    x(t)=Xe−ζωn​tsin(ωn​t1−ζ2​+ϕ)

    where X=A2+B2​ and ϕ=tan−1BA​

    #Logarithmic Decrement

    Logarithmic decrement is defined as,

    δ=lnx2​x1​​

    δ=lnXe−ζωn​(t+T)sin[ωn​(t+T)1−ζ2​+ϕ]Xe−ζωn​tsin(ωn​t1−ζ2​+ϕ)​

    δ=lne−ζωn​(t+T)e−ζωn​t​

    δ=ln(eζωn​T)

    δ=ζωn​T

    δ=ζωn​⋅ωn​1−ζ2​2π​

    💡

    δ=1−ζ2​2πζ​

    For most practical applications where ζ<0.1,

    💡

    δ≈2πζ

    Rearranging with damping ratio as the subject,

    💡

    ζ=4π2+δ2​δ​

    #Critically Damped

    For a critically damped system, ζ=1.

    c=ccr​=2km​

    Roots of the characteristic equation are,

    s1,2​=2m−c​=2m−2km​​=−ωn​

    Note that the imaginary part is zero.

    Free vibration response of a critically damped 1-DOF system,

    x(t)=(C1​+C2​t)e−ωn​t

    x˙(t)=C2​e−ωn​t−ωn​(C1​+C2​t)e−ωn​t

    Assume initial conditions at t=0 are given as x(0)=x0​ and x˙(0)=x˙0​.

    Substituting x(0)=x0​,

    x0​=(C1​+C2​⋅0)e−ωn​(0)

    C1​=x0​

    Substituting x˙(0)=x˙0​,

    x˙0​=C2​e−ωn​(0)−ωn​(x0​+C2​⋅0)e−ωn​(0)

    x˙0​=C2​−ωn​x0​

    C2​=x˙0​+ωn​x0​

    Free vibration response of a critically damped 1-DOF system,

    💡

    x(t)=[x0​+(x˙0​+ωn​x0​)t]e−ωn​t

    #Overdamped

    For an overdamped system, ζ>1.

    💡

    x(t)=C1​e{−ζ+ζ2−1​ }ωn​t+C2​e{−ζ−ζ2−1​ }ωn​t

    Assume initial conditions at t=0 are given as x(0)=x0​ and x˙(0)=x˙0​.

    Substituting x(0)=x0​,

    x0​=C1​+C2​

    Substituting x˙(0)=x˙0​,

    x˙0​=C1​ωn​(−ζ+ζ2−1​)+C2​ωn​(−ζ−ζ2−1​)

    x˙0​=C1​ωn​(−ζ+ζ2−1​)+(x0​−C1​)ωn​(−ζ−ζ2−1​)

    x˙0​=C1​ωn​(2ζ2−1​)+x0​ωn​(−ζ−ζ2−1​)

    C1​=2ωn​ζ2−1​x˙0​−x0​ωn​(−ζ−ζ2−1​)​

    C1​=2ωn​ζ2−1​x˙0​+x0​ωn​(ζ+ζ2−1​)​

    Substituting C1​ into x0​,

    C2​=x0​−2ωn​ζ2−1​x˙0​+x0​ωn​(ζ+ζ2−1​)​

    C2​=2ωn​ζ2−1​2x0​ωn​ζ2−1​−x˙0​−x0​ωn​ζ−x0​ωn​ζ2−1​​

    C2​=2ωn​ζ2−1​−x˙0​−x0​ωn​ζ+x0​ωn​ζ2−1​​

    C2​=2ωn​ζ2−1​−x˙0​−x0​ωn​(ζ−ζ2−1​)​
