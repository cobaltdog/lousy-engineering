    Finite Element Derivation

    #Total Potential Energy

    Consider an elastic body in equilibrium under
    a series of external loads P1​, P2​, …, Pn​.

    If we now impose on the elastic body a virtual state
    of deformation that produces virtual displacements dΔ1​, dΔ2​, …, dΔn​ in the directions of the loads.

    💡
    Virtual Work=r=1∑n​Pr​⋅dΔr​

    Virtual work causes a change in internal strain energy dU.

    dU=∫vol​ (σ⋅dε) dV

    Strain energy stored per unit volume is,

    U0​=∫ε​ σ dε

    From the law of conservation of energy, change in internal
    strain energy must be equal to external virtual work done.

    dU=∫vol​ (σ⋅dε) dV=r=1∑n​Pr​⋅dΔr​

    Since the loads remain constant during virtual displacement,

    dU−dr=1∑n​Pr​⋅Δr​=0

    d(U−r=1∑n​Pr​⋅Δr​)=0

    d(U+WP)=0

    dΠ=0

    Total potential energy is defined as,

    💡
    Π=U+WP

    Work potential is defined as,

    💡
    WP=−∑Pr​⋅dΔr​

    #Equivalent Spring System

    Consider a general spring element, where k is stiffness of the spring and q is displacement.

    Elastic force exerted by a spring is expressed as,

    f1​=k(q1​−q2​) (spring in compression)

    f2​=−k(q1​−q2​) (spring in tension)

    Force-displacement relation of an element is,

    {f1​f2​​}=[k−k​−kk​]{q1​q2​​}

    General expression for an element is,

    💡
    {f​}=[ke​]{q​}

    where {f} is the element nodal force vector,
    and     [ke] is the element stiffness matrix,
    and     {q} is the element nodal displacement vector

    #Two Springs Connected in Series

    The system can be broken down into two individual spring elements.

    • Forces and displacements for each element is represented in lowercase letters
    • Superscript of f and q represents which element the specific quantity belongs to

    Force-displacement relation of each element,

    {f1​(1)f2​(1)​}=[k1​−k1​​−k1​k1​​]{q1​(1)q2​(1)​} (spring element 1)

    {f1​(2)f2​(2)​}=[k2​−k2​​−k2​k2​​]{q1​(2)q2​(2)​} (spring element 2)

    In the global system, forces and displacements are represented in uppercase letters.

    • Elements sharing a common node have the same displacement at that node
    • Force at a node is shared among the elements

    Displacements at each node,

    Q1​=q1​(1)

    Q2​=q2​(1)=q1​(2)

    Q3​=q2​(2)

    Force at node 1,

    F1​=f1​(1)

    F1​=k1​q1​(1)−k1​q2​(1)

    F1​=k1​Q1​−k1​Q2​

    Force at node 3,

    F3​=f2​(2)

    F3​=−k2​q1​(2)+k2​q2​(2)

    F3​=−k2​Q2​+k2​Q3​

    Force at node 2,

    F2​=f2​(1)+f1​(2)

    F2​=−k1​q1​(1)+k1​q2​(1)+k2​q1​(2)−k2​q2​(2)

    F2​=−k1​Q1​+(k1​+k2​)Q2​−k2​Q3​

    Expressing the above force equations in matrix form,

    💡
    ⎩⎨⎧​F1​F2​F3​​⎭⎬⎫​=​k1​−k1​0​−k1​k1​+k2​−k2​​0−k2​k2​​​⎩⎨⎧​Q1​Q2​Q3​​⎭⎬⎫​

    #Derivation of FE Equations

    #Total Potential Energy Approach

    DisplacementStrainStress
    u=Nqε=Bqσ=DBq

    where q= nodal displacement vector,
    and     N= interpolation shape functions,
    and     B= strain matrix, D= elasticity matrix

    Total potential energy of a finite element model,

    Π=e∑​ (Ue​+WPe​)

    Π=e∑​ 21​∫e​ (σTε) dVe​−e∑​ ∫e​ (uTf) dVe​−e∑​ ∫e​ (uTT) dSe​−i∑​ Qi​⋅Pi​

    Strain energy in a single element,

    Ue​=21​∫e​ (σTε) dVe​

    Ue​=21​∫e​ (DBq)T(Bq) dVe​

    Ue​=21​∫e​ qT(DB)T(Bq) dVe​

    Ue​=21​∫e​ qTBTDTBq dVe​

    Ue​=21​qT∫e​ (BTDB dVe​)q

    Matrix operations,

    (Bq)T=qT⋅BT

    DT=D   →   Dij​=Dji​

    💡
    Ue​=21​qTkeq

    Element stiffness matrix,

    💡
    ke=∫e​ BTDB dVe​

    Work potential associated with body force f and surface traction T,

    qT⋅fe=∫e​ (uTf) dVe​+∫e​ (uTT) dSe​

    qT⋅fe=∫e​ (Nq)Tf dVe​+∫e​ (Nq)TT dSe​

    qT⋅fe=qT{∫e​ (NTf) dVe​+∫e​ (NTT) dSe​}

    From the above equation, the equivalent nodal force is,

    💡
    fe=∫e​ (NTf) dVe​+∫e​ (NTT) dSe​

    1st term represents the element nodal force vector for body force.
    2nd term represents the element nodal force vector for traction load.

    Total potential energy of the system (global),

    Π=e∑​ (21​qTkeq)−e∑​ (qT⋅fe)−i∑​ (Qi​⋅Pi​)

    💡
    Π=21​QTKQ−QTF

    Note that the terms in the equation are matrices.

    Differentiating with respect to Q,

    ∂Qi​∂Π​=KQ−F

    0=KQ−F

    💡
    KQ=F

    #Temperature Effects (Initial Strain)

    Initial strain due to temperature,

    ε0​=αΔT

    Expansion of the material due to heat causes
    the stress-strain curve to shift to the right.

    Stress of the material,

    σ=D(ε−ε0​)

    Strain energy per unit volume,

    U0​=21​σT(ε−ε0​)

    Strain energy of the system,

    U=21​∫e​ (ε−ε0​)TD(ε−ε0​) dVe​

    U=21​∫e​ εTDε−2εTDε0​+ε0​TDε0​ dVe​

    1st term gives the stiffness matrix.
    2nd term gives the temperature load.
    3rd term is a constant and has no effect.

    Integration of the 2nd term,

    ∫e​ εTDε0​ dVe​=∫e​ qT(BTDε0​) dVe​=qTfθ​e

    The equivalent nodal forces due to temperature is,

    💡
    fθ​e=∫e​ BTDε0​ dVe​

    Element nodal force vector for temperature load.

    #Steps in the Finite Element Method

    Steps in FEM:

    • E: element — evaluate element properties
    • A: assembly — form global stiffness matrix [K]
    • R: reduction — impose loads and boundary conditions
    • S: solution — solving {Q} from the reduced [F]=[K]{Q}

    Consider a 1-dimensional bar element, represented by two nodes.
    To perform numerical integration, coordinate transformation is required.

    Coordinate transformation equation,

    ζ=x2​−x1​2​(x−x1​)−1

    dxdζ​=x2​−x1​2​

    dxdζ​=le​2​

    dx=2le​​ dζ

    Rearranging the transformation equation, we obtain the shape functions.

    x=(21−ζ​)x1​+(21+ζ​)x2​=N1​x1​+N2​x2​

    where N1​ and N2​ are the shape functions

    Displacement field within the element is,

    u=(21−ζ​)q1​+(21+ζ​)q2​=N1​q1​+N2​q2​

    where q1​ and q2​ are the nodal displacement vectors

    Shape functions of the bar,

    N=[N1​​N2​​]=[21−ζ​​21+ζ​​]

    Strain is expressed as,

    ε=dxdu​

    Bq=dxd​[Nq​]

    Bq=dxdN​{q}

    Comparing the terms,

    B=dxdN​

    B=x2​−x1​1​[−1​1​]

    B=le​1​[−1​1​]

    Strain matrix for a 2-node bar element.

    Stress is expressed as,

    σ=Eε=EBq=DBq

    For a 1-dimensional bar element, there is no Poisson effect.
    Thus, the Poisson’s ratio is neglected such that     D=E.

    Element stiffness matrix is,

    ke=∫e​ BTDB dVe​

    ke​=(Ae​Ee​)∫e​ BTB dx

    ke=(Ae​Ee​)BTB∫e​ dx

    ke=(Ae​Ee​)⋅le​21​[−11​][−1​1​]⋅∫−1+1​ 2le​​ dζ

    💡
    ke=le​Ae​Ee​​[1−1​−11​]

    Nodal force vector due to body force f and surface traction T,

    fe=∫e​ (NTf) dVe​+∫e​ (NTT) dSe​

    fe=∫e​[N1​N2​​]fAe​ dx+∫e​[N1​N2​​]T dx

    fe=∫−1+1​[N1​N2​​]fAe​⋅2le​​ dζ+∫−1+1​[N1​N2​​]T⋅2le​​ dζ

    💡
    fe=2Ae​le​f​[11​]+2le​T​[11​]

    Assuming both f and T are constants.

    Nodal force vector due to temperature,

    fθ​e=∫e​ BTDε0​ dVe​

    fθ​e=∫e​ (BTEε0​)Ae​ dx

    fθ​e=Ae​EαΔT∫−1+1​ le​1​[−11​]2le​​ dζ

    💡
    fθ​e=Ae​EαΔT[−11​]
