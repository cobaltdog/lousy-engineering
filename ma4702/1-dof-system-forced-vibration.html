    #Harmonic Force Excitation

    #Undamped System

    Given a harmonic force F(t)=F0​cos(ωt).
    Initial conditions are x(0)=x0​ and x˙(0)=x˙0​.

    Applying Newton’s 2nd law,

    F(t)−kx=mx¨

    mx¨+kx=F(t)

    x¨+mk​x=mF(t)​

    x¨+ωn​2x=mF0​​cos(ωt)

    Non-homogeneous 2nd order ODE.

    Solution to the non-homogeneous 2nd order ODE consists of two parts,

    xh​(t)=Acos(ωn​t)+Bsin(ωn​t)

    xp​(t)=Xcos(ωt) or Xsin(ωt) same as the force F(t)

    Substituting xp​(t) into the equation of motion,

    x¨+ωn​2x=mF0​​cos(ωt)

    −ω2Xcos(ωt)+ωn​2Xcos(ωt)=mF0​​cos(ωt)

    −ω2X+ωn​2X=mF0​​

    X=m(ωn​2−ω2)F0​​

    X=k−mω2F0​​

    Note that the above equation applies for both types of excitation.

    Substituting X into the solution,

    x(t)=xh​(t)+xp​(t)

    x(t)=Acos(ωn​t)+Bsin(ωn​t)+k−mω2F0​​cos(ωt)

    x˙(t)=−Aωn​sin(ωn​t)+Bωn​cos(ωn​t)−k−mω2F0​ω​sin(ωt)

    Substituting x(0)=x0​,

    x0​=Acos0+Bsin0+k−mω2F0​​cos0

    x0​=A+k−mω2F0​​

    A=x0​−k−mω2F0​​

    Substituting x˙(0)=x˙0​,

    x˙0​=−Aωn​sin0+Bωn​cos0−k−mω2F0​ω​sin0

    B=ωn​x˙​

    Vibration response of an undamped 1-DOF system under force excitation,

    x(t)=Acos(ωn​t)+Bsin(ωn​t)+Xcos(ωt)

    💡

    x(t)=(x0​−k−mω2F0​​)cos(ωn​t)+ωn​x˙​sin(ωn​t)+k−mω2F0​​cos(ωt)

    Note that the above equation only applies to F(t)=F0​cos(ωt).
    Expressions for     A and B are different for F(t)=F0​sin(ωt).

    Vibration response of an undamped 1-DOF system under force excitation (alternative),

    💡

    x(t)=Xsin(ωn​t+ϕ)+k−mω2F0​​cos(ωt)

    where X=A2+B2​ and ϕ=tan−1BA​+π,
    (add     π if denominator of arctan is less than zero)

    #Damped System

    Given a harmonic force F(t)=F0​cos(ωt).
    Initial conditions are x(0)=x0​ and x˙(0)=x˙0​.

    Applying Newton’s 2nd law,

    F(t)−cx˙−kx=mx¨

    mx¨+cx˙+kx=F(t)

    x¨+mc​x˙+mk​x=mF(t)​

    x¨+2ζωn​x˙+ωn​2x=mF0​​cos(ωt)

    Note that mc​=ccr​c​⋅mccr​​=ζm2km​​=2ζωn​.

    If the system is undamped, the solution to the O.D.E. is,

    xh​(t)=e−ζωn​t[Acos(ωn​t1−ζ2​)+Bsin(ωn​t1−ζ2​)]

    xp​(t)=Xcos(ωt−ϕ) or Xsin(ωt−ϕ) same as the force F(t)

    Homogeneous solution is usually ignored as it tends to die out quickly.
    Thus, we are only interested in the particular solution as it is sustained.

    Substituting xp​(t) into the equation of motion,

    mF0​​cos(ωt)=−ω2Xcos(ωt−ϕ)                      +2ζωn​[−ωXsin(ωt−ϕ)]                      +ωn​2Xcos(ωt−ϕ)

    mF0​​cos(ωt)=(ωn​2−ω2)Xcos(ωt−ϕ)                      −2ζωωn​Xsin(ωt−ϕ)

    mF0​​cos(ωt)=(ωn​2−ω2)X[cos(ωt)cosϕ+sin(ωt)sinϕ]                      −2ζωωn​X[sin(ωt)cosϕ−cos(ωt)sinϕ]

    mF0​​cos(ωt)=cos(ωt)[(ωn​2−ω2)Xcosϕ+2ζωωn​Xsinϕ]                      +sin(ωt)[(ωn​2−ω2)Xsinϕ−2ζωωn​Xcosϕ]

    Comparing the coefficients,

    mF0​​=(ωn​2−ω2)Xcosϕ+2ζωωn​Xsinϕ

    0=(ωn​2−ω2)Xsinϕ−2ζωωn​Xcosϕ

    In matrix form,

    {F0​/m0​}=[(ωn​2−ω2)−2ζωωn​​2ζωωn​(ωn​2−ω2)​]{XcosϕXsinϕ​}

    Solving the above matrix using Cramer’s rule,

    💡

    X=mF0​​(ωn​2−ω2)2+4ζ2ω2ωn​2​1​

    💡

    ϕ=tan−1(ωn​2−ω22ζωωn​​)

    Note that the above equations apply to both F(t)=F0​cos(ωt)=F0​sin(ωt).

    #Frequency Ratio r and Magnification Factor M

    Frequency ratio is defined as,

    💡

    r=ωn​ω​

    Magnification factor is defined as,

    💡

    M=X0​X​

    Rewriting the vibration response for a damped system,

    X=mF0​​(ωn​2−ω2)2+4ζ2ω2ωn​2​1​

    X=mωn​2F0​​(1−ωn​2ω2​)2+ωn​24ζ2ω2​​1​

    💡

    X=kF0​​(1−r2)2+(2ζr)2​1​

    Rewriting the vibration response for a damped system,

    ϕ=tan−1(ωn​2−ω22ζωωn​​)

    ϕ=tan−1(1−ω2/ωn​22ζω/ωn​​)

    💡

    ϕ=tan−1(1−r22ζr​)

    #Harmonic Base Excitation

    Consider a damped system excited at the location of the support (base).
    Given a harmonic base excitation of y=Ysin(ωt) and y˙​=ωYcos(ωt).

    Applying Newton’s 2nd law,

    −c(x˙−y˙​)−k(x−y)=mx¨

    mx¨+c(x˙−y˙​)+k(x−y)=0

    mx¨+cx˙+kx=cy˙​+ky

    Substituting the expressions for y and y˙​,

    mx¨+cx˙+kx=cωYcos(ωt)+kYsin(ωt)

    Let the RHS be defined as the equivalent force.

    Feq​(t)=cωYcos(ωt)+kYsin(ωt)

    💡

    Feq​(t)=F0​sin(ωt+α)

    where F0​=Yk2+(cω)2​ and tanα=kcω​

    For a harmonic base excitation of y=Ycos(ωt),

    F0​=Yk2+(cω)2​

    tanα=−cωk​

    Rewriting the equation of motion,

    mx¨+cx˙+kx=F0​sin(ωt+α)

    Similar to the expression for harmonic
    force excitation of a damped system.

    Solution of the above equation is,

    x=Xsin(ωt+α−ϕ)

    Expression for the coefficient,

    X=kF0​​(1−r2)2+(2ζr)2​1​

    X=kYk2+(cω)2​​⋅(1−r2)2+(2ζr)2​1​

    X=Y1+(kcω​)2​⋅(1−r2)2+(2ζr)2​1​

    X=Y1+4mkc2​(k4m​)ω2​⋅(1−r2)2+(2ζr)2​1​

    X=Y1+ζ2(ωn​24​)ω2​⋅(1−r2)2+(2ζr)2​1​

    💡

    X=(1−r2)2+(2ζr)2​Y1+(2ζr)2​​

    💡

    ϕ=tan−1(1−r22ζr​)

    Displacement transmissibility is defined as the ratio of the amplitude X of the induced
    vibration of the mass (output) to the amplitude Y of the base excitation (input).

    💡

    Td​=YX​=(1−r2)2+(2ζr)2​1+(2ζr)2​​
