    MA4702 · AIRCRAFT STRUCTURES 2

    2-DOF System

    #Free Vibration

    #Natural Frequencies and Mode Shapes

    Consider a 2-DOF spring-mass system.
    Assume the displacements x1​ and x2​ are in the positive direction.
    Also assume that x1​>x2​ such that springs 1 and 2 are in compression.

    Applying Newton’s 2nd law on mass 1,

    −kx1​−k(x1​−x2​)=mx¨1​

    mx¨1​+kx1​+k(x1​−x2​)=0

    mx¨1​+2kx1​−kx2​=0

    Applying Newton’s 2nd law on mass 2,

    −kx2​+k(x1​−x2​)=mx¨2​

    mx¨2​+kx2​+k(x2​−x1​)=0

    mx¨2​+2kx2​−kx1​=0

    Expressing the equations of motion in matrix form,

    💡

    [m0​0m​]{x¨1​x¨2​​}+[2k−k​−k2k​]{x1​x2​​}={00​}

    [M​]{X¨​}+[K​]{X​}={0​}
    1st matrix represents the mass matrix.
    2nd matrix represents the stiffness matrix.

    If the system experiences an initial disturbance, it
    will vibrate sinusoidally with a certain frequency ω.

    Assume the free vibration due to the disturbance as,

    x1​=A1​sin(ωt)     →     x¨1​=−ω2A1​sin(ωt)

    x2​=A2​sin(ωt)     →     x¨2​=−ω2A2​sin(ωt)

    For a given problem, A1​, A2​, and ω are to be determined.

    Substituting into the equations of motion,

    −ω2[m0​0m​]{A1​A2​​}sin(ωt)+[2k−k​−k2k​]{A1​A2​​}sin(ωt)={00​}

    💡

    [2k−ω2m−k​−k2k−ω2m​]{A1​A2​​}={00​}

    #Natural Frequencies

    To determine ω, equate the determinant of the above matrix to zero.

    det​(2k−ω2m)−k​−k(2k−ω2m)​​=0

    (2k−ω2m)(2k−ω2m)−(−k)(−k)=0

    4k2−4kmω2+m2ω4−k2=0

    m2ω4−(4km)ω2+3k2=0

    ω4−4(mk​)ω2+3(mk​)2=0

    λ2−4(mk​)λ+3(mk​)2=0

    where λ=ω2

    Roots of the characteristic equation,

    λ1,2​=2a−b±b2−4ac​​

    λ1,2​=24k/m±16k2/m2−12k2/m2​​

    λ1,2​=24k/m±2k/m​

    λ1,2​=2(mk​)±mk​

    Roots of the characteristic equation,

    λ1​=mk​

    λ2​=m3k​

    Natural frequencies of the system (smaller of the two is the 1st natural frequency),

    💡

    ω1​=mk​​

    💡

    ω2​=m3k​​

    #Mode Shapes

    Each natural frequency has a mode shape that
    defines the relative motion of the two masses.

    From the equations of motion,

    (2k−ω2m)A1​−kA2​=0

    A2​A1​​=2k−ω2mk​

    1st mode shape where ω1​=mk​​,

    A2​A1​​(1)=2k−(k/m)mk​=1

    2nd mode shape where ω2​=m3k​​,

    A2​A1​​(2)=2k−(3k/m)mk​=−1

    Let the vibration amplitude A2​=1.
    Express the mode shape pairs in vector form.

    💡

    {ϕ​}1​={A1​A2​​}(1)={11​}

    💡

    {ϕ​}2​={A1​A2​​}(2)={−11​}

    #Free Vibration Response

    Vibration response for each mode shape,

    {x1​x2​​}1​=C1​{11​}sin(ω1​t+α1​)=C1​{ϕ11​ϕ21​​}sin(ω1​t+α1​)

    {x1​x2​​}2​=C2​{−11​}sin(ω2​t+α2​)=C2​{ϕ12​ϕ22​​}sin(ω2​t+α2​)

    Unknowns C1​, C2​, α1​, and α2​ are determined from given initial conditions.

    Using superposition principle, the total vibration response is,

    {x​}={x​}1​+{x​}2​

    {x1​x2​​}=C1​{ϕ11​ϕ21​​}sin(ω1​t+α1​)+C2​{ϕ12​ϕ22​​}sin(ω2​t+α2​)

    {x˙1​x˙2​​}=C1​ω1​{ϕ11​ϕ21​​}cos(ω1​t+α1​)+C2​ω2​{ϕ12​ϕ22​​}cos(ω2​t+α2​)

    #Example

    Assume k=m=1.
    Assume initial conditions at t=0 are, x1​=5, x2​=0, x˙1​=x˙2​=0.

    Natural frequencies,

    ω1​=mk​​=1 rad/s

    ω2​=m3k​​=3​ rad/s

    At t=0, the displacement matrix simplifies to,

    {50​}=C1​{11​}sinα1​+C2​{−11​}sinα2​

    At t=0, the velocity matrix simplifies to,

    {00​}=C1​{11​}cosα1​+3​C2​{−11​}cosα2​

    {00​}=[cosα1​cosα1​​−3​cosα2​3​cosα2​​]{C1​C2​​}

    Determinant of matrix,

    det​cosα1​cosα1​​−3​cosα2​3​cosα2​​​=0

    23​(cosα1​)(cosα2​)=0

    Since C1​=0 and C2​=0, then either cosα1​=0 or cosα2​=0.

    Assume cosα1​=0 and substitute into the velocities matrix.

    −3​C1​(cosα2​)=0

    3​C2​(cosα2​)=0

    cosα2​=0

    Solving for α1​ and α2​,

    α1​=α2​=2π​

    Substituting into the displacement matrix,

    {50​}=C1​{11​}sin2π​+C2​{−11​}sin2π​

    {50​}={C1​−C2​C1​+C2​​}

    C1​=2.5

    C2​=−2.5

    Free vibration response of the system,

    {x1​x2​​}=2.5{11​}sin(t+2π​)−2.5{−11​}sin(3​t+2π​)

    {x1​x2​​}=2.5{11​}cos(t)−2.5{−11​}cos(3​t)

    x1​(t)=2.5cos(t)+2.5cos(3​t)

    x2​(t)=2.5cos(t)−2.5cos(3​t)

    #Forced Vibration

    Consider a 2-DOF spring-mass system.
    Each mass is subjected to a forced vibration Fi​sin(ωt).
    Assume that x1​>x2​ such that springs 1 and 2 are in compression.

    Applying Newton’s 2nd law on mass 1,

    m1​x¨1​=F1​sinωt−k1​x1​−k2​(x1​−x2​)

    m1​x¨1​+k1​x1​+k2​(x1​−x2​)=F1​sin(ωt)

    Applying Newton’s 2nd law on mass 2,

    m2​x¨2​=F2​sinωt+k2​(x1​−x2​)−k3​x2​

    m2​x¨2​+k3​x2​−k2​(x1​−x2​)=F2​sin(ωt)

    Expressing the equations of motion in matrix form,

    💡

    [m1​0​0m2​​]{x¨1​x¨2​​}+[k1​+k2​−k2​​−k2​k2​+k3​​]{x1​x2​​}={F1​sin(ωt)F2​sin(ωt)​}

    [M​]{X¨​}+[K​]{X​}={0​}
    1st matrix represents the mass matrix.
    2nd matrix represents the stiffness matrix.

    In steady-state, the vibration response of each mass x1​(t) and x2​(t) will be the same as the forced vibration input, such that if the input is sinusoidal, the response will also be sinusoidal.

    In this case, since forced vibration is Fi​sin(ωt),

    x1​=X1​sin(ωt)   →   x¨1​=−ω2X1​sin(ωt)

    x2​=X2​sin(ωt)   →   x¨2​=−ω2X2​sin(ωt)

    whereby excitation frequency ω is known,
    and amplitudes     X1​ and X2​ are unknowns

    Substituting into the equations of motion,

    −ω2[m1​0​0m2​​]{X1​X2​​}+[k1​+k2​−k2​​−k2​k2​+k3​​]{X1​X2​​}={F1​F2​​}

    💡

    [k1​+k2​−m1​ω2−k2​​−k2​k2​+k3​−m2​ω2​]{X1​X2​​}={F1​F2​​}

    Note that if the excitation frequency ω is not equal to any of the system’s
    natural frequencies, the determinant of the matrix is not zero,     det∣A∣=0.

    Using Cramer’s rule,

    Δ=det​k1​+k2​−m1​ω2−k2​​−k2​k2​+k3​−m2​ω2​​

    Δ1​=det​F1​F2​​−k2​k2​+k3​−m2​ω2​​

    Δ2​=det​k1​+k2​−m1​ω2−k2​​F1​F2​​​

    Amplitudes of the vibration response of each mass are then,

    💡

    X1​=ΔΔ1​​

    💡

    X2​=ΔΔ2​​

    Note that if the excitation frequency ω is equal to the system’s natural frequencies, the determinant Δ=0 and the vibration amplitudes become infinite (for the case of zero
    damping). Thus, resonance occurs when     ω=ω1,2​.

    #Vibration Absorber

    A vibration absorber reduces the vibration
    of a machine operating at running speed ω.

    By designing the natural frequency of the absorber to be equal to the machine’s ω, the absorber will vibrate in place of the machine.

    For a properly tuned vibration absorber,

    ω=m2​k2​​​ (Natural frequency of absorber)

    X1​=0 (Displacement of machine)

    X2​=−k2​P​ (Displacement of absorber)

    Assume x2​>x1​ such that springs k1​ and k2​ are in tension.

    Applying Newton’s 2nd law on mass 1,

    Psin(ωt)+k2​(x2​−x1​)−k1​x1​=m1​x¨1​

    m1​x¨1​+(k1​+k2​)x1​−k2​x2​=Psin(ωt)

    Applying Newton’s 2nd law on mass 2,

    −k2​(x2​−x1​)=m2​x¨2​

    m2​x¨2​−k2​x1​+k2​x2​=0

    Expressing the equations of motion in matrix form,

    💡

    [m1​0​0m2​​]{x¨1​x¨2​​}+[k1​+k2​−k2​​−k2​k2​​]{x1​x2​​}={Psin(ωt)0​}

    In this case, since forced vibration is Psin(ωt),

    x1​=X1​sin(ωt)   →   x¨1​=−ω2X1​sin(ωt)

    x2​=X2​sin(ωt)   →   x¨2​=−ω2X2​sin(ωt)

    whereby excitation frequency ω is known,
    and amplitudes     X1​ and X2​ are unknowns

    Substituting into the equations of motion,

    💡

    [k1​+k2​−m1​ω2−k2​​−k2​k2​−m2​ω2​]{X1​X2​​}={P0​}

    Using Cramer’s rule,

    Δ1​=​P0​−k2​k2​−m2​ω2​​=P(k2​−m2​ω2)

    Δ2​=​k1​+k2​−m1​ω2−k2​​P0​​=Pk2​

    Amplitudes of the vibration response of each mass are then,

    💡

    X1​=ΔΔ1​​

    💡

    X2​=ΔΔ2​​

    For the machine vibration to be zero, equate X1​=0.

    P(k2​−m2​ω2)=0

    ω=m2​k2​​​

    Since P(k2​−m2​ω2)=0, displacement of the absorber simplifies to,

    X2​=(k1​+k2​−m1​ω2)(k2​−m2​ω2)−k2​2Pk2​​

    X2​=−k2​2Pk2​​

    X2​=−k2​P​
