    Fundamental Concepts

    #Definition of a Fluid

    A fluid is a substance that deforms continuously under the action of an applied shear stress.

    When common solids such as steel or other metals are acted on by a shearing stress, they will initially deform (usually a very small deformation), but they will not continuously deform (flow).

    It is not practical to study the behavior of individual molecules when trying to describe the behavior of fluids at rest or in motion. Instead, we characterize the behavior by considering
    the macroscopic value of the quantity in interest. Thus, velocity at a point in a fluid can be interpreted as the average velocity of the molecules in a small volume surrounding the point.

    #Properties of a Fluid

    #Density

    The density of a fluid, ρ, is defined as its mass per unit volume.

    The specific volume, v, is defined as the volume per unit mass.

    #Specific Weight

    Whereas density is mass per unit volume, specific weight is weight per unit volume.

    Specific gravity of a substance is the ratio of its density to the density of water at 4°C.

    #Viscosity

    Viscosity is the measure of the resistance the fluid has to an externally applied shear stress.

    Consider a fluid-filled space formed by two horizontal parallel plates. When a force P is applied to the upper plate, it will move continuously with a velocity U.

    Fluid in contact with the upper plate moves with plate velocity U, and at the bottom fixed plate, fluid velocity is zero.

    The observation that fluid “sticks” to the solid boundaries is known as the no-slip condition.

    The fluid between the two plates moves with velocity u=u(y). Thus, a velocity gradient dydu​ is formed.

    In a small time period dt, the imaginary line AB would rotate through an angle dβ.

    tan dβ≈dβ=bda​

    dβ=bU dt​

    dtdβ​=bU​

    γ˙​=bU​

    The rate of shearing strain γ˙​ can be defined as,

    💡
    γ˙​=bU​=dydu​

    Shearing stress τ is proportional to the rate of shearing strain γ˙​,

    💡
    τ=μdydu​

    The constant μ is called the absolute viscosity, dynamic viscosity or simply viscosity of a fluid. The dimensions of μ is N⋅s/m2. Fluids for which τ is linearly related to γ˙​ are known as Newtonian fluids.

    #Kinematic Viscosity

    Kinematic viscosity is the ratio of absolute viscosity to density.

    💡
    ν=ρμ​

    The dimensions of ν is m2/s.

    #Vapor Pressure

    If a container is closed with a small air space left above the surface, and this space evacuated to form a vacuum,
    a pressure will develop in the space as a result of the vapor that is formed by escaping molecules.

    When an equilibrium condition is reached such that the number of molecules leaving the surface is equal to the number entering, the vapor is said to be saturated.

    The pressure that the vapor exerts on the liquid surface is known as the vapor pressure.

    In flowing fluids, it is possible to develop very low pressure due to fluid motion, and if the pressure is lowered to the vapor pressure, boiling will occur. When vapor bubbles are formed in a flowing fluid, they are swept along into regions of higher pressure where they suddenly collapse with sufficient intensity to cause structural damage. The formation and subsequent collapse of vapor bubbles in a flowing fluid is known as cavitation.
