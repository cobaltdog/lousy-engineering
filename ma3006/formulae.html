    #Pressure Variation in a Fluid

    Newton’s second law:

    −∇p−γk=ρa

    Pressure Gradient of a Stationary Fluid

    dzdp​=−γ

    Pressure Gradient in Rigid-Body Motion

    dp=∂x∂p​dx+∂y∂p​dy+∂z∂p​dz

    ∂x∂p​=−ρax​

    ∂y∂p​=−ρay​

    ∂z∂p​=−ρ(g+az​)

    Pressure Gradient in Rigid-Body Rotation

    dp=∂r∂p​dr+∂θ∂p​dθ+∂z∂p​dz

    ∂r∂p​=ρrω2

    ∂θ∂p​=0

    ∂z∂p​=−ρ(g+az​)

    Height of fluid’s free surface along a constant pressure line

    drdz​=g+az​rω2​

    z=2(g+az​)r2ω2​+constant

    As r increases, the height of the fluid increases.

    Surfaces of constant pressure

    ∫dp=∫ρrω2 dr−∫ρ(g+az​) dz

    p=2ρr2ω2​−ρ(g+az​)z+constant

    As r increases, the pressure of the fluid increases.
    As     z increases, the pressure of the fluid decreases.
    Thus, pressure varies radially, as well as hydrostatically in the vertical direction.

    #Control Volume Analysis

    #Conservation of Mass

    Fixed Control Volume

    Reynolds transport theorem:

    0=∂t∂​∫CV​ ρ dV+∫CS​ ρ(V⋅n) dA

    Continuity equation:

    ∑m˙in​=∑m˙out​

    ∑Qin​=∑Qout​

    Moving Control Volume

    Reynolds transport theorem:

    0=∂t∂​∫CV​ ρ dV+∫CS​ ρWcosθ dA

    Relation between absolute and relative velocity:

    Vabs​=W+VCV​

    #Momentum Equation

    Linear Momentum

    Reynolds transport theorem,

    ∑F=∂t∂​∫CV​ Vρ dV+∫CS​ Vρ(V⋅n) dA

    Simplified equation:

    ∑F=∑(m˙V)out​−∑(m˙V)in​

    Assuming steady flow and uniform velocity across inlets and outlets.

    Angular Momentum

    Reynolds transport theorem:

    ∑T=∂t∂​∫CV​ (r×V)ρ dV+∫CS​ (r×V)ρ(V⋅n) dA

    Simplified equation:

    ∑T=∑[m˙(r×V)]out​−∑[m˙(r×V)]in​

    Assuming steady flow and uniform velocity across inlets and outlets.

    Energy equation in terms of height:

    hin​+(ρgP​+2gV2​+z)in​=hout​+(ρgP​+2gV2​+z)out​

    Power input to a pump:

    W˙pump​=m˙ghpump​=ρQghpump​

    #Dimensional Analysis

    #Buckingham Pi Theorem

    Step 1: List the variables involved in the problem — n variables.

    Step 2: Express each variable in terms of basic dimensions — m reference dimensions.

    Step 3: Determine the required number of Π-groups given by k=n−m.

    Step 4: Select m number of repeating variables.

    Step 5: Construct the Π-groups.

    Step 6: Compare coefficients and solve for the exponents.

    #Modeling and Similitude

    Dimensionless Numbers

    Reynolds number (most fluid problems)Re=Viscous ForceInertia Force​=μρVL​=νVL​
    Froude number (free surface flows)Fr=Gravitational ForceInertia Force​=gL​V​
    Euler number (flows with pressure differentials)Eu=Inertia ForcePressure Force​=ρV2p​
    Strouhal number (compressible flows)St=Convective Inertia ForceLocal Inertia Force​=VfL​
    Weber number (flows with surface tension)We=Surface Tension ForceInertia Force​=σρV2L​

    To achieve dynamic similitude, Reynolds number of the model and prototype must be
    the same. Then, force coefficients (e.g. drag force coefficient), will be the same as well.

    Net Positive Suction Head

    Net positive suction head available:

    NPSHA​=ρgPs​−Pv​​+2gVs​2​

    where Ps​ is pressure at pump suction side, and Pv​ is vapor pressure
    Note that the pressures used in the equation are absolute pressure.

    Alternative expression for NPSHA​:

    ρgPatm​​+2gV1​2​+z1​=ρgPs​​+2gVs​2​+zs​+hL​

    ρgPatm​​+0+z1​=ρgPs​​+2gVs​2​+zs​+hL​

    ρgPatm​​−ρgPv​​+z1​=ρgPs​​−ρgPv​​+2gVs​2​+zs​+hL​

    ρgPatm​​−ρgPv​​+z1​=NPSHA​+zs​+hL​

    NPSHA​=ρgPatm​−Pv​​+(z1​−zs​)−hL​

    For cavitation to not occur:

    NPSHA​≥NPSHR​

    Required NPSH is a characteristic of the pump and its operating conditions.
