    #Buckingham Pi Theorem

    Fluid Mechanics: Dimensional Analysis: Buckingham Pi TheoremExplanation and application of Buckingham Pi Theorem as a method in Dimensional Analysis Credits to PowerPoint School (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bYPb3Ih9SXk) for the ending part background animation.https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pBkfjNMW26Y

    If there are n variables in a dimensionally homogeneous equation, described by m
    reference (fundamental) dimensions, the variables can be grouped in k=n−m dimensionless groups, which are referred to as Π-terms or Π-groups.

    Consider the steady flow of an incompressible fluid through a long, smooth-walled,
    horizontal, circular pipe. You are then required to characterize the pressure drop
    per unit length ΔPL​, which is a function of pipe diameter D, fluid density ρ,
    fluid viscosity μ, and fluid velocity V.

    💡
    ΔPL​=f(D, ρ, μ, V)

    To determine the general relationship between the above variables, it will be a costly
    and time-consuming experimental process. Dimensional analysis offers a simpler
    approach by grouping the variables into dimensionless groups, Π-groups.

    Step 1 — List the variables involved in the problem:

    • ΔPL​, D, ρ, μ, V

    Number of dimensional variables n=5.

    Step 2 — Express each variable in terms of basic dimensions:

    • ΔPL​=mPa​=m3N​=m3kg(m/s2)​=m2s2kg​=L2T2M​
    • D=m=L
    • ρ=m3kg​=L3M​
    • μ=m2N⋅s​=m2kg(m/s2)s​=mskg​=LTM​
    • V=sm​=TL​

    Number of basic dimensions m=3.

    Step 3 — Determine the required number of Π-groups:

    • Number of Π-groups k=5−3=2

    Step 4 — Select m number of repeating variables:

    • ρ, D, V

    It is generally advantageous to choose variables that
    are related to mass, geometry, and kinematics.

    Step 5 — Construct the dimensionless groups:

    • Π1​=ρa1​⋅Db1​⋅Vc1​⋅ΔPL​
    • Π2​=ρa2​⋅Db2​⋅Vc2​⋅μ
    • Π1​=f(Π2​)

    Solving for the 1st group,

    Π1​=ρa1​⋅Db1​⋅Vc1​⋅ΔPL​

    M0L0T0=(ML−3)a1​(L)b1​(LT−1)c1​(ML−2T−2)

    M0L0T0=Ma1​+1⋅L−3a1​+b1​+c1​−2⋅T−c1​−2

    ∴ a1​=−1     b1​=1     c1​=−2

    Solving for the 2nd group,

    Π2​=ρa2​⋅Db2​⋅Vc2​⋅μ

    M0L0T0=(ML−3)a2​(L)b2​(LT−1)c2​(ML−1T−1)

    M0L0T0=Ma2​+1⋅L−3a2​+b2​+c2​−1⋅T−c2​−1

    ∴ a2​=−1     b2​=−1     c2​=−1

    Substituting into the dimensionless relation,

    Π1​=f(Π2​)

    ρ−1DV−2⋅ΔPL​=f(ρ−1D−1V−1⋅μ)

    💡
    ρV2D⋅ΔPL​​=f(ρVDμ​)=f(RE−1)

    The dimensionless pressure drop per unit length is only a function of Reynolds number.

    #Example

    #Part (a)

    Number of dimensional variables n=6:

    • ΔP=m2N​=m2kg(m/s2)​=m⋅s2kg​=LT2M​
    • μ=m2N⋅s​=m2kg(m/s2)s​=mskg​=LTM​
    • ρ=m3kg​=L3M​
    • Q=sm3​=TL3​
    • D=m=L
    • ω=s1​=T1​

    Number of Π-groups required,

    k=n−m=6−3=3

    Note that the number of basic dimensions m=3.

    Selected of repeating variables:

    • Density ρ
    • Diameter D
    • Angular velocity ω

    Note that the repeating variables have
    relations to mass, geometry, and velocity.

    Equations for Π-groups,

    Π1​=ρa1​⋅Db1​⋅ωc1​⋅ΔP

    Π2​=ρa2​⋅Db2​⋅ωc2​⋅μ

    Π3​=ρa3​⋅Db3​⋅ωc3​⋅Q

    Solving the system of linear equations,

    a1​=−1a2​=−1a3​=0​b1​=−2b2​=−2b3​=−3​c1​=−2c2​=−1c3​=−1​

    ∴ Π1​=CH​=ρω2D2ΔP​ (head coefficient)

    ∴ Π2​=Re−1=ρωD2μ​ (Reynolds number)

    ∴ Π3​=CQ​=ωD3Q​ (flow coefficient)

    Relationship among Π-groups,

    ρω2D2ΔP​=f(ρωD2μ​, ωD3Q​)

    #Part (b)

    Recall that power gained by a fluid in a pump is,

    W˙fluid​=ρghpump​⋅Q=ΔP⋅Q

    Using the Π-groups from Part (a),

    Π=Π1​⋅Π3​

    Π=ρω2D2ΔP​⋅ωD3Q​

    Π=CP​=ρω3D5W˙fluid​​ (power coefficient)

    #Modeling and Similitude

    A model is a representation of a physical system that may be used to predict the behavior of the system is some desired respect. The physical system for which the predictions are to be made is called the prototype.

    The basic requirement in modeling is to achieve similarity between “the model and its test conditions” and “the prototype and its operating conditions”. Variables without a subscript
    shall refer to the prototype while those with the subscript m shall refer to the model.

    Types of similitude studies:

    • Geometric similarity — similar shape
    • Dynamic similarity — ratio of forces are the same
    • Kinematic similarity — velocity and acceleration ratios are the same
