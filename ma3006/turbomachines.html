    Turbomachines

    #Types of Turbomachines

    Turbomachines are mechanical devices that either extract energy from a fluid (turbine)
    or add energy to a fluid (pump) as a result of dynamic interactions between the device
    and the fluid.

    Turbomachines are classified into:

    • Axial-flow (can handle larger volume flow rates)
    • Radial-flow (can generate higher pressures)
    • Mixed-flow

    For the same size (diameter) and running speed (RPM), a centrifugal pump will produce a
    higher pressure head than a axial-flow pump. However, the axial-flow pump will deliver
    a greater flow rate.

    #Centrifugal Pump

    Losses in the pump:

    • Hydraulic — losses at impeller entrance and the volute (reduce pressure head)
    • Mechanical — friction losses between rotating shroud and the casing
    • Leakage — small proportion of fluid passing through the impeller does not discharge from the pump but instead flow back through the clearances between the rotating
      and stationary parts of the pump, to the inlet of the impeller

    Recall that the pressure head developed across the pump is,

    hp​=ρgP2​−P1​​+2gV2​2−V1​2​+z2​−z1​

    Power gained by the fluid is then,

    Pfluid​=ρQghp​

    Overall efficiency of a pump is defined as,

    💡
    ηpump​=Pshaft​Pfluid​​=Pshaft​ρQghp​​

    #Similarity Laws

    Flow coefficient is defined as,

    💡
    CQ​=ωD3Q​

    Head rise coefficient is defined as,

    💡
    CH​=ω2D2ghp​​

    Power coefficient is defined as,

    💡
    CP​=ρω3D5Pshaft​​

    From dimensional analysis, for incompressible flow at high Reynolds number, both
    head rise and power coefficient are dependent on the flow coefficient of a pump.

    Similarity laws provide a means of obtaining pump characteristics of another geometrically similar pump or allow for the prediction of the performance of the same pump operating
    at another speed.

    #Special Pump Scaling Laws

    Two special cases of pump similitude:

    • Case 1 — change in operating speed of the same pump (no change in diameter)
    • Case 2 — change in diameter of the pump (no change in operating speed)

    If there is only a change in operating speed,

    💡
    ω1​Q1​​=ω2​Q2​​
    💡
    ω1​2H1​​=ω2​2H2​​
    💡
    ω1​3P1​​=ω2​3P2​​

    If there is only a change in pump diameter,

    💡
    D1​3Q1​​=D2​3Q2​​
    💡
    D1​2H1​​=D2​2H2​​
    💡
    D1​5P1​​=D2​5P2​​

    For small pumps, the effects of viscosity and surface roughness becomes significant.
    Thus, as pump size decreases, these factors significantly influence pump efficiency.

    💡
    1−η1​1−η2​​=(D2​D1​​)1/5

    In general, similarity laws will not be very accurate if tests on a model pump with water
    are used to predict the performance of a prototype pump with a highly viscous fluid,
    such as oil, due to the much smaller Reynolds number associated with the oil flow.

    #Specific Speed

    Specific speed of a pump is defined as,

    💡
    Ns​=(ghp​)3/4ωQ​​=CH​3/4CQ​1/2​

    Note that specific speed is defined at flow coefficient corresponding to peak efficiency.

    #Pump and System Match

    #System Demand Curve

    Head loss in a system is the sum of losses through all flow elements in the system.

    hL​=f⋅Dl​⋅2gV2​+KL​2gV2​

    hL​=2gV2​(Dfl​+KL​)

    hL​=2g1​(AQ​)2(Dfl​+KL​)

    💡
    hL​=BQ2

    Applying the energy equation,

    ρgP1​​+2gV1​2​+z1​+hp​=ρgP2​​+2gV2​2​+z2​+hL​

    hp​=z2​−z1​+hL​

    hp​=z2​−z1​+2gA2Q2​(Dfl​+∑KL​)

    💡
    E=z2​−z1​+2gA2Q2​(Dfl​+∑KL​)

    The above equation represents the system demand curve.

    #Pumps in Series

    Total head produced by pumps in series is,

    💡
    Htot​=H1​+H2​+⋯+Hn​

    Flow rates through pumps in series are equal.

    💡
    Q=Q1​=Q2​=Qn​

    #Pumps in Parallel

    Head produced by pumps in parallel are equal.

    💡
    H=H1​=H2​=Hn​

    Total flow rate through pumps in parallel is,

    💡
    Qtot​=Q1​+Q2​+⋯+Qn​

    #Net Positive Suction Head

    Net positive suction head is the difference between the total head
    on the pump suction side (inlet), and liquid vapor pressure head.

    Net positive suction head available represents the head that occurs for a system.

    💡
    NPSHA​=ρgPs​−Pv​​+2gVs​2​

    where Ps​ is pressure at pump suction side, and Pv​ is vapor pressure
    Note that the pressures used in the equation are absolute pressure.

    Applying the energy equation from suction surface to pump suction side,

    ρgPatm​​+2gV1​2​+z1​=ρgPs​​+2gVs​2​+zs​+hL​

    ρgPatm​​+0+z1​=ρgPs​​+2gVs​2​+zs​+hL​

    ρgPatm​​−ρgPv​​+z1​=ρgPs​​−ρgPv​​+2gVs​2​+zs​+hL​

    ρgPatm​​−ρgPv​​+z1​=NPSHA​+zs​+hL​

    💡
    NPSHA​=ρgPatm​−Pv​​+(z1​−zs​)−hL​

    Using the pump suction level as the datum, the equation simplifies to,

    💡
    NPSHA​=ρgPatm​−Pv​​+z1​−hL​

    Required NPSH must be maintained so that cavitation will not occur.

    💡
    NPSHA​≥NPSHR​

    Required NPSH is a characteristic of the pump and its operating conditions.
