    External Forced Convection

    #Flow over Flat Plates

    Fluid temperature in the boundary layer varies from Ts​ at the surface to about T∞​ at the
    outer edge of the boundary. Fluid properties also vary with temperature, and thus also
    varies with position across the boundary layer.

    To account for the variation, fluid properties are evaluated at the film temperature.

    💡
    Tfilm​=2Ts​+T∞​​

    Fluid properties are assumed to remain constant during the entire flow.

    If the freestream pressure is not atmospheric pressure, value of ν needs to be corrected.

    💡
    ν=P∞​Patm​​⋅ν1atm​

    Correction for kinematic viscosity is only required for gases.

    Consider the parallel flow of a fluid over a flat plate
    of length L. The fluid has a uniform velocity V and temperature T∞​.

    Flow in the velocity boundary layer start out as
    laminar, but if the plate is sufficiently long, the
    flow becomes turbulent at a distance xcr​.

    Note that xcr​ is the distance from the leading edge at which the Reynolds number reaches its critical value
    for flow transition.

    For flow over a flat plate, critical Reynolds number is,

    💡
    Recr​=μρVxcr​​=5×105

    #Friction Coefficient

    Boundary layer thickness δ and local friction coefficient for a flat plate are,

    δ=Rex​​4.91x​ and Cf,x​=Rex​​0.664​ (Laminar flow)

    δ=Rex​1/50.38x​ and Cf,x​=Rex​1/50.059​ (Turbulent flow)

    Average friction coefficient over the entire plate can be obtained by substituting
    the above equations into Cˉf​=L1​∫0L​ Cf,x​ dx and performing the integration.

    For laminar flow,

    💡
    Cˉf​=ReL​​1.33​

    where ReL​<5×105

    For turbulent flow,

    💡
    Cˉf​=ReL​1/50.074​

    where 5×105≤ReL​≤107

    In some cases, a flat plate is sufficiently long for the flow to become turbulent, but not
    long enough to disregard the laminar flow region. Thus, the integration is performed
    in two parts, from 0≤x≤xcr​ and xcr​<x≤L.

    Average coefficient over the entire plate is then,

    Cˉf​=L1​(∫0xcr​​ Cf,x  laminar​ dx+∫xcr​L​ Cf,x  turbulent​ dx)

    💡
    Cˉf​=ReL​1/50.074​−ReL​1742​

    #Heat Transfer Coefficient

    Local Nusselt numbers for a flat plate are,

    Nux​=0.332Rex​1/2Pr1/3 (Laminar flow)

    Rex​<5×105

    Pr>0.6

    Nux​=0.0296Rex​0.8Pr1/3 (Turbulent flow)

    5×105≤Rex​≤107

    0.6≤Pr≤60

    Average Nusselt number over the entire plate can be obtained by substituting
    the above equations into hˉ=L1​∫0L​ hx​ dx and performing the integration.

    For laminar flow,

    💡
    Nuˉ=0.664ReL​1/2Pr1/3

    where ReL​<5×105 and Pr>0.6

    For turbulent flow,

    💡
    Nuˉ=0.037ReL​0.8Pr1/3

    where 5×105≤ReL​≤107 and 0.6≤Pr≤60

    Similarly, if there is both laminar and turbulent regions, the average Nusselt number is,

    💡
    Nuˉ=(0.037ReL​0.8−871)Pr1/3

    where 5×105≤ReL​≤107 and 0.6≤Pr≤60

    For liquid metals with high thermal conductivities, local Nusselt number is,

    💡
    Nux​=0.565(Rex​Pr)1/2=0.565Pex​1/2

    where Pex​≥100 and Pr≤0.05, and Pex​ is the dimensionless Peclet number

    #Flow across Cylinders and Spheres

    The characteristic length for a circular cylinder or sphere is taken to be
    the external diameter D. The critical Reynolds number for flow across
    a circular cylinder or sphere is Recr​≈2×105.

    Average Nusselt number for flow across cylinders is,

    💡
    Nuˉcyl​=khˉD​=0.3+[1+(0.4/Pr)2/3]1/40.62Re1/2Pr1/3​[1+(282000Re​)5/8]4/5

    Alternatively, the average Nusselt number for a cylinder is,

    💡
    Nuˉcyl​=khˉD​=C⋅ReD​mPr1/3

    where C and m are an experimentally determined constants

    Average Nusselt number for flow over a sphere is,

    💡
    Nuˉsph​=khˉD​=2+[0.4Re1/2+0.06Re2/3]Pr0.4(μs​μ∞​​)1/4

    which is valid for 3.5≤Re≤8×104,
    and     0.7≤Pr≤380,
    and     1.0≤μμ∞​​≤3.2

    Fluid properties are evaluated at the freestream temperature T∞​,
    except for μs​, which is evaluated at the surface temperature Ts​.

    For small Reynolds number, where Re≤0.5, and CD​=Re24​,

    💡
    Nuˉsph​=2

    The equation corresponds to heat transfer by conduction
    from a sphere to a stationary, infinite medium.
