    Transient Heat Conduction

    #Lumped System Analysis

    A transient heat conduction problem can be simplified if the temperature of a solid
    is spatially uniform during the process (no temperature gradients within the solid).

    Consider a body of arbitrary shape initially at a uniform temperature Ti​.
    At time t=0, the body is placed into a medium at temperature T∞​.
    Heat transfer takes place between the body and its environment.

    Assume T∞​>Ti​.

    Heat is transferred from the environment to the body.

    Energy balance of the solid is,

    E˙st​=Q˙​in​−Q˙​out​+E˙gen​

    E˙st​=Q˙​conv​−0+0

    mcdtdT​=hAs​(T∞​−T)

    ρVcdtdT​=−hAs​(T−T∞​)

    Integrating the above equation,

    ρVc⋅dtd(T−T∞​)​=−hAs​(T−T∞​)

    ∫θi​θ​T−T∞​d(T−T∞​)​=−ρVchAs​​∫0t​dt

    lnTi​−T∞​T−T∞​​=−ρVchAs​​t

    lnθi​θ​=−ρVchAs​​t

    lnθi​θ​=−bt

    where b=ρVchAs​​ is a positive quantity expressed in s−1
    and the reciprocal of     b is the thermal time constant

    Rearranging the equation,

    💡
    θi​θ​=Ti​−T∞​T(t)−T∞​​=e−bt

    Note that as t→∞, temperature of the system approaches the environment temperature such
    that the numerator becomes zero.

    #Total Heat Transfer Q

    Total heat transfer from the body to the environment is,

    Q=∫0t​ hAs​(T−T∞​) dt

    Q=∫0t​ hAs​θ dt

    Q=∫0t​ hAs​⋅θi​e−bt dt

    Q=hAs​θi​[−be−bt​]0t​

    Q=hAs​θi​(b1−e−bt​)

    💡
    Q=ρVc(1−e−bt)

    Total heat transfer when the body loses heat to the environment (e.g. quenching).

    Total heat transfer from the environment to the body is,

    Q=∫0t​ hAs​(T∞​−T) dt

    Q=∫0t​ −hAs​(T−T∞​) dt

    Q=−∫0t​ hAs​θ dt

    Q=−∫0t​ hAs​⋅θi​e−bt dt

    💡
    Q=−ρVc(1−e−bt)

    Total heat transfer when the body gains heat from the environment (e.g. heating).

    #Biot Number Bi

    The Biot number determines whether the lumped system analysis can be applied.

    Energy balance at the surface,

    Q˙​cond​=Q˙​conv​

    kALTs1​−Ts2​​=hA(Ts2​−T∞​)

    Ts2​−T∞​Ts1​−Ts2​​=kA/LhA​

    Ts2​−T∞​Ts1​−Ts2​​=1/hAL/kA​

    Ts2​−T∞​Ts1​−Ts2​​=Rconv​Rcond​​

    Biot number is defined as,

    💡
    Bi=Rconv​Rcond​​=khL​

    Note that the Biot number is for simple geometries in which heat transfer
    is 1-dimensional, such as a large plane wall, a long cylinder, and a sphere.

    Alternatively, Biot number can be expressed as the ratio of the rate of heat transfers.

    💡
    Bi=Q˙​cond​Q˙​conv​​=kALΔT​hAΔT​=khL​

    Note that the lumped system analysis is valid if Bi<0.1.

    For arbitrarily-shaped bodies, L is replaced with the characteristic length Lc​.

    💡
    Lc​=As​V​

    Note that for a finite cylinder, surface area of the end faces are included in As​.

    Relationship between θ and θi​ can be expressed in terms of the Biot number.

    lnθi​θ​=−ρVchAs​​t

    lnθi​θ​=−(khLc​​)(Lc​2αt​)

    lnθi​θ​=−Bi⋅Fo

    💡
    θi​θ​=Ti​−T∞​T(t)−T∞​​=e−Bi⋅Fo

    where Fo is the Fourier number

    #Example

    Assumptions:

    • Thermocouple junction is spherical in shape
    • Thermal properties of the junction are constant
    • Convection heat transfer coefficient is constant
    • Radiation effects are negligible

    Characteristic length of junction,

    Lc​=As​V​=4πr2(4/3)πr3​=3r​=30.5×10−3​=1.67×10−4 m

    Biot number of junction,

    Bi=khLc​​=35210(1.67×10−4)​=0.001

    Since 0.001<0.1, the lumped system analysis is valid.

    For thermocouple to read 99% of the initial temperature difference,

    Ti​−T∞​T(t)−T∞​​=1001​=0.01

    For example, when Ti​=0°C and T∞​=100°C, a thermocouple is
    considered to have read 99% of the applied temperature difference
    when its reading indicates     T(t)=99°C.

    Evaluating the exponent b,

    b=ρVchAs​​=ρLc​ch​=8500(1.67×10−4)(320)210​=0.462 s−1

    Time constant is the reciprocal of b,

    τ=b1​=2.1645 s

    Time required by thermocouple,

    θi​θ​=Ti​−T∞​T(t)−T∞​​=e−bt

    0.01=e−0.462t

    t=9.9679 s

    We must wait at least 10s for the temperature of the thermocouple junction
    to approach within 99% of the initial junction-gas temperature difference.
