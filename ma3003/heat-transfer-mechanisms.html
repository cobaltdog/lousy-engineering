    Heat Transfer Mechanisms

    #Heat Transfer

    Thermodynamic analysis:

    • Deals with equilibrium states and changes from one state to another
    • Determine the amount of heat transfer for a system undergoing a process
    • Amount of heat that must be transferred to realize a specified change of state

    Heat transfer analysis:

    • Determine rate of heat transfer (how long a process will take)
    • Basic requirement is the presence of a temperature difference
    • Rate of heat transfer is dependent on the magnitude of temperature gradient

    Objectives of heat transfer:

    • Thermal insulation — minimize rate of heat transfer Q˙​ (thermoflask)
    • Heat transfer augmentation — minimize temperature gradient ΔT

      Maximum rate of heat transfer with lowest temperature gradient

    • Temperature control of a component

    Description of heat transfer:

    • No net movement of material, only a diffusion of energy
    • Can occur in solids, liquids, and gases
    • 3-dimensional in nature

    #First Law of Thermodynamics

    Energy balance for any system undergoing a process (for a finite time interval) is,

    💡
    ΔEsys​=Ein​−Eout​=ΔU+ΔKE+ΔPE

    where ΔEsys​=ΔU for a stationary system

    Energy balance for any system expressed in rate form is,

    💡
    dtdEsys​​=E˙in​−E˙out​

    Note that energy transfer can take place via heat, work, or mass flow.

    Heat balance equation for any system is then,

    💡
    E˙stored​=Q˙​in​−Q˙​out​+E˙gen​

    where E˙gen​ is heat generated from the conversion
    of other types of energies (e.g. electrical → heat)

    Heat balance equation across a control surface is,

    💡
    E˙in​=E˙out​ or Q˙​1​=Q˙​2​+Q˙​3​

    #Conduction

    Conduction is the transfer of energy from more energetic particles of a substance
    to the adjacent less energetic ones as a result of interactions between the particles.

    In solids, conduction is due to the combination of vibrations of molecules in a lattice
    and the energy transport by free electrons. In stationary fluids, it is due to collisions
    and diffusion of the molecules during their random motion.

    Fourier’s law of heat conduction is given by,

    💡
    Q˙​cond​=kA⋅ΔxT1​−T2​​=−kAdxdT​

    where k is the thermal conductivity of a material (W/m⋅K)

    Heat flux due to conduction is,

    💡
    q˙​cond​=AQ˙​​=kdxdT​

    where A is the area normal to the direction of heat transfer

    #Convection

    Convection is the mode of energy transfer between a solid surface and adjacent fluid
    that is in motion. The faster the fluid motion, the greater the convection heat transfer.

    Types of convection:

    • Forced convection — fluid is forced to flow over a surface by external means (fan)
    • Natural convection — fluid motion is due to buoyancy forces induced by density change
    • Mixed convection — magnitudes of forced and natural convection are relatively equal

    Heat transfer processes that involve phase change of a fluid is also considered
    to be convection as fluid motion is induced, such as the rise of vapor bubbles
    during boiling or the fall of liquid droplets during condensation.

    Energy is transferred through:

    • Random molecular motion — conduction of heat from surface to adjacent fluid molecules
    • Bulk fluid motion — formation of convection currents due to changes in fluid density

    Convection is the diffusion of energy due to random molecular motion plus
    energy transfer due to bulk motion of the fluid, also referred to as advection.

    Newton’s law of cooling is given by,

    💡
    Q˙​conv​=hAs​(Ts​−T∞​)

    where h is the convection heat transfer coefficient (W/m2⋅K)
    and     As​ is the surface area in contact with the fluid

    Heat flux due to convection is,

    💡
    q˙​conv​=h(Ts​−T∞​)

    Convection heat transfer coefficient is dependent on:

    • Position along the surface
    • Surface geometry of the surface
    • Type of fluid flow (laminar vs turbulent)
    • Thermo-physical properties of the fluid
    • Temperature difference between surface and fluid
    • Mode of convection (forced vs natural vs mixed)

    As​=π(0.003)(2)=0.01885 m2

    Q˙​=hAs​(Ts​−T∞​)

    h=0.01885(152−15)90​

    h=34.9 W/m2⋅K

    #Radiation

    Radiation is the energy emitted by matter in the form of electromagnetic waves as
    a result of the changes in the electronic configurations of the atoms or molecules.

    Heat transfer by radiation:

    • Does not require the presence of a medium
    • Fastest heat transfer method (occurs at the speed of light in a vacuum)

    Maximum rate of radiation that can be emitted by an ideal radiator (blackbody) is given by,

    💡
    Q˙​emit,max​=σAs​Ts​4

    where σ=5.67×10−8 W/m2⋅K4

    Rate of radiation that can by emitted by a real surface is,

    💡
    Q˙​emit​=εσAs​Ts​4

    where ε is emissivity of the surface, ranging from 0≤ε≤1, with ε=1 for a blackbody

    Maximum flux emitted by a blackbody is,

    💡
    Eb​=q˙​emit,max​=σTs​4

    Flux emitted by a real surface is,

    💡
    E=q˙​emit​=εσTs​4

    Another important property is absorptivity α, which
    is the fraction of incident radiation that is absorbed
    by the surface.

    💡
    Q˙​absorb​=αQ˙​incident​

    where α ranges from 0≤α≤1,
    with     α=1 for a blackbody

    Net radiation heat transfer is the difference between the rates of radiation emitted by the surface and the
    radiation absorbed by it.

    If the rate of radiation absorption is greater than the rate of radiation emission, the surface is said to be gaining energy by radiation and vice versa.

    For a surface that is completely enclosed by a much larger (or black) surface, net radiation heat transfer between the two surfaces can be simplified.

    Q˙​rad​=Q˙​emit​−Q˙​absorb​

    Q˙​rad​=εσAs​Ts​4−ασAs​Tsurr​4

    💡
    Q˙​rad​=εσAs​(Ts​4−Tsurr​4)

    Surrounding surfaces are treated as blackbodies, where Q˙​incident​=Q˙​emit,max​.
    Also, the surface of interest is treated as a gray surface, where     ε=α.

    #Linearized Radiation Heat Transfer Coefficient

    Factorizing the above equation,

    Q˙​rad​=εσAs​(Ts​2+Tsurr​2)(Ts​2−Tsurr​2)

    Q˙​rad​=εσAs​(Ts​2+Tsurr​2)(Ts​+Tsurr​)(Ts​−Tsurr​)

    If the magnitude of Ts​−Tsurr​≪Ts​, then Tm​=2Ts​+Tsurr​​ and Ts​2+Tsurr​2=2Tm​2.

    The temperature difference between the surface and surroundings is extremely small.
    Thus, the equation can be simplified by taking the average of the two temperatures.

    Rewriting the equation,

    Q˙​rad​=εσAs​(2Tm​2)(2Tm​)(Ts​−Tsurr​)

    Q˙​rad​=εσAs​⋅4Tm​3(Ts​−Tsurr​)

    Q˙​rad​=hrad​As​(Ts​−Tsurr​)

    Comparing with the equation for heat transfer by convection,

    💡
    hrad​=εσ4Tm​3

    Note that the convection equation is Q˙​conv​=hAs​(Ts​−T∞​).

    At a room temperature of 298 K,

    💡
    hrad​=6ε

    Radiation heat transfer coefficient at room temperature is 6 times the surface’s emissivity.
