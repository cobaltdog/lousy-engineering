    Radiation Heat Transfer

    #Radiation Mechanism

    Radiation heat transfer:

    • Does not require the presence of a material medium to take place
    • Energy transfer is fastest (at the speed of light in a vacuum)
    • Energy by occurs by electromagnetic waves
    • Suffers no attenuation in a vacuum

    Electromagnetic waves are characterized by,

    💡
    λ=vc​

    where c is the propagation speed of a wave in that medium,
    and     λ is the wavelength, and v is the frequency of the wave

    Propagation speed of a wave in a medium is,

    💡
    c=nc0​​

    where speed of light in a vacuum c0​=2.9979×108 m/s
    and     n is the refraction index of that medium

    Intensity of radiation varies with the wavelength and direction of emission from the surface.
    For opaque solids, radiation is a surface phenomenon since the radiation emitted by the
    interior regions can never reach the surface, and incident radiation is usually absorbed.

    #Blackbody Radiation

    A blackbody is a perfect emitter and absorber of radiation.
    A blackbody emits radiation uniformly in all directions per
    unit area, normal to the direction of emission.

    Spectral blackbody emissive power is defined as the amount of radiation emitted by
    a blackbody at a thermodynamic temperature T per unit time, per unit surface area,
    and per unit wavelength about the wavelength λ.

    Spectral blackbody emissive power is known as Planck’s law.

    💡
    Ebλ​(λ,T)=λ5[exp(C2​/λT)−1]C1​​

    where C1​=2πhc0​2=3.74177×108 W⋅μm4/m2
    and     C2​=hc0​/k=1.43878×104 μm⋅K
    and     h=6.625×10−34 J⋅s
    and     k=1.3805×10−23 J/K

    As temperature increases, peak emissive power shifts towards shorter wavelengths.

    Wavelength at which the peak occurs
    for a specified temperature is given by,

    💡
    (λT)max power​=2897.8 μm⋅K

    The total blackbody emissive power Eb​ is the integral of the spectral
    blackbody emissive power Ebλ​ over the entire wavelength spectrum.

    💡
    Eb​(T)=∫0∞​ Ebλ​(λ,T) dλ=σT4

    #Radiative Properties

    #Emissivity

    Emissivity of a surface is defined as the ratio of the radiation emitted by the surface at
    a given temperature to the radiation emitted by a blackbody at the same temperature.

    💡
    ε(T)=Eb​(T)E(T)​

    Emissivity of a real surface is not constant. It varies with the temperature of the surface,
    and wavelength and direction of the emitted radiation. In practice, it is more convenient
    to work with radiation properties averaged over all directions (hemispherical properties).

    Spectral hemispherical emissivity is expressed as,

    💡
    ελ​(λ,T)=Ebλ​(λ, T)Eλ​(λ, T)​

    where Eλ​ is the spectral emissive power of a real surface

    Rearranging the above equation, we get the spectral emissive power of a real surface.

    Eλ​(λ,T)=ελ​(λ,T)⋅Ebλ​(λ,T)

    Integrating the above gives the total emissive power of the real surface.

    E(T)=∫0∞​ ελ​(λ,T)⋅Ebλ​(λ,T) dλ

    Total hemispherical emissivity is then,

    💡
    ε(T)=Eb​(T)E(T)​=σT4∫0∞​ ελ​(λ,T)⋅Ebλ​(λ,T) dλ​

    Diffuse surface → independent of direction θ

    Gray surface → independent of wavelength λ

    Gray, diffuse surface → independent of θ and λ

    Emissivity of a gray, diffuse surface is simply the
    total hemispherical emissivity of that surface.

    #Absorptivity, Reflectivity, and Transmissivity

    Radiation flux incident on a surface is called irradiation and is denoted by G.

    Absorptivity (fraction of irradiation absorbed).

    💡
    α=IncidentAbsorbed​=GGabs​​

    Reflectivity (fraction of irradiation reflected).

    💡
    ρ=IncidentReflected​=GGref​​

    Transmissivity (fraction of irradiation transmitted).

    💡
    τ=IncidentTransmit​=GGtr​​

    From the 1st law of thermodynamics,

    G=Gabs​+Gref​+Gtr​

    1=GGabs​​+GGref​​+GGtr​​

    1=α+ρ+τ

    For idealized blackbodies, which are perfect absorbers, α=1.
    For opaque surfaces, with negligible transmission, α+ρ=1.
    For gases, which do not reflect irradiation, α+τ=1.

    #Kirchhoff’s Law

    Consider a small body at temperature T
    enclosed in a large isothermal enclosure
    at the same temperature.

    Radiation absorbed by the small body is,

    Gabs​=αG=ασT4

    Radiation emitted by the small body is,

    Eemit​=εσT4

    If the small body is in thermal equilibrium with
    the enclosure, net rate of heat transfer to the
    body must be zero.

    Gabs​=Eemit​

    ασT4=εσT4

    α=ε

    Therefore, for any surface in the enclosure,

    💡
    ε(T)=α(T)

    Total hemispherical emissivity of a surface is equal to the total hemispherical
    absorptivity for radiation coming from a blackbody at the same temperature.

    #View Factor

    Radiation heat transfer between surfaces depends on the orientation of the surfaces relative to each other
    as and their radiation properties and temperatures.

    The view factor accounts for the effects of orientation on the radiation heat transfer between two surfaces.

    It is a geometric quantity, and is independent
    of the surface properties and temperature.

    View factor is denoted by Fi→j​ or simply Fij​.

    F12​ represents the fraction of radiation leaving surface 1 that strikes surface 2 directly.
    F21​ represents the fraction of radiation leaving surface 2 that strikes surface 1 directly.

    Note that radiation that strikes a surface does not need to be absorbed by that surface.
    Also, radiation that strikes a surface after being reflected by other surfaces is not
    considered in the evaluation of view factors.

    Value of the view factor ranges between 0<F<1.

    Plane surface: F1→1​=0

    Convex surface: F2→2​=0

    Concave surface: F3→3​=0

    When Fi→j​=0, it indicates that two surfaces do not have a direct view of each other. Thus, the radiation leaving surface i cannot strike surface j directly.

    When Fi→j​=1, surface j completely surrounds surface i, such that all radiation leaving surface i
    is intercepted by surface j.

    #Reciprocity Relation

    Reciprocity relation for view factors is expressed as,

    💡
    Ai​Fij​=Aj​Fji​

    Note that Fij​=Fji​ unless the areas of both surfaces are equal.

    #Summation Rule

    If a surface i is interacting with N surfaces,
    including itself, sum of view factors from
    surface i to all surfaces must be unity.

    Summation rule for an enclosure is,

    💡
    1=F11​+F12​+⋯+F1n​

    where N is the number of surfaces

    #Example

    F11​=0 (no radiation leaving surface 1 strikes itself)

    F12​=1 (all radiation leaving surface 1 strikes 2)

    Applying summation rule to surface 2,

    1=F21​+F22​

    Applying the reciprocity rule,

    A1​F12​=A2​F21​

    F21​=A2​A1​​F12​

    F21​=4πr2​24πr1​2​F12​

    F21​=(r2​r1​​)2

    Radiation leaving surface 2 that strikes itself,

    F22​=1−F21​

    F22​=1−(r2​r1​​)2

    #Superposition Rule

    View factor from surface i to surface j is equal to the sum of the view factors from i to all parts of
    the surface j.

    💡
    Fij​=Fi→j1​+Fi→j2​+⋯+Fi→jn​

    Note that Fji​=Fj1→i​+Fj2→i​+⋯+Fjn→i​.

    #Example 1

    Applying the superposition rule,

    F2→(1,3)​=F2→1​+F2→3​

    WL2​​=0.50.4​=0.8

    Substituting into the equation,

    0.26=F21​+0.19

    F21​=0.07

    A2​F21​=A1​F12​

    F12​=A1​A2​​F21​

    F12​=0.5(0.2)0.5(0.3)​⋅0.07

    F12​=0.105

    If surface 1 is brought closer to surface 2, then F12​ will increase.

    #Example 2

    Surface area of 3,

    A3​=A2​+Aring​=A(2,ring)​

    View factor from surface 1 to surface 3,

    F13​=F12​+F1ring​

    Superposition rule.

    From view factor chart,

    r1​L​=0.10.1​=1

    #Symmetry Rule

    Two (or more) surfaces that posses symmetry about a third surface will have identical view factors from that surface.

    💡
    Fij​=Fik​

    #Example

    The four surfaces are symmetric about the base surface.

    Since surface 1 is a flat surface (plane),

    F11​=0

    Applying the symmetry rule,

    F12​=F13​=F14​=F15​

    Applying the summation rule,

    ∑Fij​=F11​+F12​+F13​+F14​+F15​

    1=F11​+F12​+F13​+F14​+F15​

    View factors of the side surfaces,

    1=F12​+F13​+F14​+F15​

    0.25=F12​=F13​=F14​=F15​

    #Diffuse Gray Surface

    To simplify the analysis, it is common to assume:

    • Surfaces are diffuse
    • Temperatures are uniform
    • Bodies are gray and opaque

    #Radiosity

    Total radiation leaving a surface per unit time and
    per unit area is the radiosity and is denoted by J.

    Note that J is expressed in W/m2.

    For a surface that is gray and opaque,

    J=Eemit​+Gref​

    J=εEb​+ρG

    J=εEb​+(1−ε)G

    For a gray, opaque surface, τ=0.

    1=α+ρ+τ

    ρ=1−α

    ρ=1−ε

    Rearranging the above equation,

    G=1−εJ−εEb​​

    Net energy leaving the surface is then,

    AQ˙​​=J−G

    AQ˙​​=J−1−εJ−εEb​​

    AQ˙​​=1−εJ(1−ε)−(J−εEb​)​

    💡
    AQ˙​​=1−ε(Eb​−J)ε​

    #Radiation Exchange to/from a Surface

    Net rate of radiation heat transfer from a surface is,

    Q˙​=A(J−G)

    Q˙​=A(J−1−εJ−εEb​​)

    Q˙​=1−εεA​(Eb​−J)

    Q˙​=(1−ε)/εAEb​−J​

    If Eb​>J, it is heat loss from the surface.
    If     Eb​<J, it is heat gain by the surface.

    Surface resistance to radiation heat transfer is then,

    💡
    Ri​=ϵA1−ε​

    For a blackbody, where ε=1 and α=1,

    J=Eb​

    R=0

    #Radiation Exchange between 2 Surfaces

    Consider two diffuse, gray, and opaque surface maintained at uniform temperatures. Net rate
    of heat transfer from surface i to surface j is,

    Q˙​i→j​=Ji​Ai​Fi→j​−Jj​Aj​Fj→i​

    1st term represents radiation leaving
    entire surface     i that strikes surface j.

    2nd term represents radiation leaving
    entire surface     j that strikes surface i.

    Applying the reciprocity relation,

    Aj​Fj→i​=Ai​Fi→j​

    Substituting into the rate of heat transfer,

    Q˙​i→j​=Ji​Ai​Fi→j​−Jj​Aj​Fj→i​

    Q˙​i→j​=Ji​Ai​Fi→j​−Jj​Ai​Fi→j​

    Q˙​i→j​=Ai​Fi→j​(Ji​−Jj​)

    Q˙​i→j​=1/(Ai​Fi→j​)Ji​−Jj​​

    Space resistance to radiation heat transfer is then,

    💡
    Ri→j​=Ai​Fi→j​1​=Aj​Fj→i​1​

    #Radiation Exchange between 2 Enclosures

    Consider two enclosures consisting of two opaque surfaces at temperatures T1​ and T2​.

    Net rate of heat transfer from surface 1 to 2 is,

    💡
    Q˙​12​=R1​+R2​+R3​Eb1​−Eb2​​

    where R1​ is surface 1 resistance,
    and     R3​ is surface 2 resistance,
    and     R2​ is space resistance

    Substituting the resistances,

    Q˙​12​=ε1​A1​1−ε1​​+A1​F12​1​+ε2​A2​1−ε2​​Eb1​−Eb2​​

    Q˙​12​=ε1​A1​1−ε1​​+A1​F12​1​+ε2​A2​1−ε2​​σ(T1​4−T2​4)​

    #Radiation Exchange between 3 Enclosures

    Consider three enclosures of opaque surfaces at temperatures T1​, T2​, and T3​.

    Resistance network consists of:

    • Surface resistance of each surface
    • Space resistance between the surfaces
    • Sum of heat transfer at each node is zero

    Summation equation for node J1​.

    💡
    R1​Eb1​−J1​​+R12​J2​−J1​​+R13​J3​−J1​​=0

    Summation equation for node J2​.

    💡
    R2​Eb2​−J2​​+R12​J1​−J2​​+R13​J3​−J2​​=0

    Summation equation for node J3​.

    💡
    R3​Eb3​−J3​​+R13​J1​−J3​​+R23​J2​−J3​​=0

    #Radiation Shielding

    Radiation heat transfer between two surfaces can be greatly reduced by inserting a
    thin, high-reflectivity sheet of material (radiation shields) between the two surfaces.

    Role of radiation shields:

    • Reduce the rate of heat transfer by increasing the resistance
    • Lower the emissivity of the shield, the higher the resistance

    Consider a radiation shield placed between two parallel plates.

    💡
    Q˙​12, one shield​=ε1​A1​1−ε1​​+A1​F13​1​+ε3,1​A3​1−ε3,1​​+ε3,2​A3​1−ε3,2​​+A3​F32​1​+ε2​A2​1−ε2​​Eb1​−Eb2​​

    For infinite parallel plates, where A1​=A2​=A3​, and F13​=F32​=1,

    Q˙​12, one shield​=ε1​1−ε1​​+1+ε3,1​1−ε3,1​​+ε3,2​1−ε3,2​​+1+ε2​1−ε2​​A(Eb1​−Eb2​)​

    Q˙​12, one shield​=(ε1​1​+ε2​1​−1)+(ε3,1​1​+ε3,2​1​−1)A(Eb1​−Eb2​)​

    Q˙​12, one shield​=(ε1​1​+ε2​1​−1)+(ε3,1​1​+ε3,2​1​−1)Aσ(T1​4−T2​4)​

    Therefore, for N number of radiation shields,

    💡
    Q˙​12, N shields​=(ε1​1​+ε2​1​−1)+(ε3,1​1​+ε3,2​1​−1)+ … +(εN,1​1​+εN,2​1​−1)Aσ(T1​4−T2​4)​

    If the emissivities of all surfaces are equal, the equation reduces to,

    Q˙​12, N shields​=(N+1)(ε1​+ε1​−1)Aσ(T1​4−T2​4)​

    💡
    Q˙​12, N shields​=N+11​ Q˙​12, no shields​
