    Heat Exchangers

    #Types of Heat Exchangers

    Types of heat exchanger designs:

    • Double-pipe heat exchanger
    • Crossflow heat exchanger
    • Shell-and-tube heat exchanger

    #Double-Pipe Exchanger

    Description of heat exchanger:

    • Consists of two concentric pipes of different diameters
    • Possible flow arrangements — parallel flow and counter flow
    • One fluid flows through smaller pipe, while the other flows in the annular space

    Parallel flow arrangement:

    • Fluids flow in the same direction
    • ΔT is initially large but decays
    • Tcold,out​<Thot,out​

    Counter flow arrangement:

    • Fluids flow in opposite directions
    • ΔT is nearly constant
    • Tcold,out​ can exceed Thot,out​

    #Crossflow Exchanger

    Description of heat exchanger:

    • Two fluids usually move perpendicular to each other (crossflow)
    • Further categorized into unmixed and mixed flows

    A crossflow heat exchanger is unmixed when there are fins which force cold fluid to flow through the spacing between fins and prevent it from flowing in the transverse direction,
    or parallel to the tubes.

    #Shell-and-Tube Exchanger

    Description of heat exchanger:

    • Most common type used in industrial applications
    • Contain a large number of tubes packed in a shell
    • Baffles force fluid in the shell to flow across the tubes to enhance heat transfer

    #Overall Heat Transfer Coefficient

    In a typical heat exchanger, heat is transferred from
    the hot fluid to the wall by convection, through the
    wall by conduction, and from the wall to the cold
    fluid again by convection.

    Note that outer surface of the outer pipe is assumed
    to be perfectly insulated and thus no heat transfer
    occurs from the outer surface.

    For a double-pipe heat exchanger,

    Rwall​=Rcyl​=2πkLln(Do​/Di​)​

    where k is thermal conductivity of the wall,
    and     L is length of the tube

    Inner and outer surface areas of the wall are,

    Ai​=πDi​L

    Ao​=πDo​L

    Recall that the overall heat transfer coefficient is expressed as,

    Q˙​=Rtot​ΔT​=UAs​ΔT=Ui​Ai​ΔT=Uo​Ao​ΔT

    💡
    Rtot​=UAs​1​=Ui​Ai​1​=Uo​Ao​1​

    Note that the different overall heat transfer coefficients are not equal.

    Total thermal resistance is,

    Rtot​=Rconv,i​+Rwall​+Rconv,o​

    💡
    UAs​1​=hi​Ai​1​+2πkLln(Do​/Di​)​+ho​Ao​1​

    Note that overall coefficient is dominated by the smaller convection coefficient,
    which results in a higher convection resistance and thus, impedes heat transfer.
    This arises when one of the fluids is a gas and the other is a liquid.

    If thickness of the wall is negligible, then As​≈Ai​≈Ao​.

    💡
    U1​=hi​1​+ho​1​

    #Fouling Factor

    Fouling of heat exchangers:

    • Deteriorates performance due to accumulation of deposits on surfaces
    • Layer of deposits contribute to additional resistance to heat transfer
    • Most common type of fouling is precipitation of solid deposits

    Net effect of accumulation of deposits is represented by the fouling factor.
    For a double-pipe heat exchanger, overall heat transfer coefficient becomes,

    💡
    UAs​1​=hi​Ai​1​+Ai​Rf,i​​+2πkLln(Do​/Di​)​+Ao​Rf,o​​+ho​Ao​1​

    where Rf,i​ and Rf,o​ are the fouling factors of the inner and outer surfaces

    #Log Mean Temperature Difference

    Consider a parallel-flow double-pipe heat exchanger. Assume the outer surface of the exchanger to be well insulated such that any heat transfer occurs between the two fluids.

    Energy balance of each fluid is,

    dQ˙​=m˙h​cp,h​(Th,out​−Th,in​)=−m˙h​cp,h​dTh​

    dQ˙​=m˙c​cp,c​(Tc,out​−Tc,in​)=m˙c​cp,c​dTc​

    Rate of heat loss from the hot fluid is equal
    to the rate of heat gain by the cold fluid.

    Rearranging the above equations,

    dTh​=−m˙h​cp,h​dQ˙​​

    dTc​=m˙c​cp,c​dQ˙​​

    Taking their difference, we get,

    dTh​−dTc​=−dQ˙​(m˙h​cp,h​1​+m˙c​cp,c​1​)

    dTh​−dTc​=−U dAs​(Th​−Tc​)(m˙h​cp,h​1​+m˙c​cp,c​1​)

    Th​−Tc​dTh​−dTc​​=−U dAs​(m˙h​cp,h​1​+m˙c​cp,c​1​)

    Performing integration on the above,

    lnTh,in​−Tc,in​Th,out​−Tc,out​​=−UAs​(m˙h​cp,h​1​+m˙c​cp,c​1​)

    lnTh,in​−Tc,in​Th,out​−Tc,out​​=−UAs​(−Q˙​Th,out​−Th,in​​+Q˙​Tc,out​−Tc,in​​)

    lnTh,in​−Tc,in​Th,out​−Tc,out​​=UAs​[Q˙​(Th,out​−Tc,out​)−(Th,in​−Tc,in​)​]

    lnΔT1​ΔT2​​=UAs​(Q˙​ΔT2​−ΔT1​​)

    Q˙​=UAs​ln(ΔT2​/ΔT1​)ΔT2​−ΔT1​​

    💡
    Q˙​=UAs​ΔTlm​

    Parallel-flow double-pipe: ΔT2​=Th,out​−Tc,out​ and ΔT1​=Th,in​−Tc,in​
    Counter-flow double-pipe:     ΔT2​=Th,out​−Tc,in​ and ΔT1​=Th,in​−Tc,out​
    Note that the above equation is only valid for double-pipe heat exchangers.

    #Correction Factor

    Log mean temperature difference ΔTlm​ is limited to double-pipe heat exchangers only.

    For crossflow and shell-and-tube heat exchangers,

    💡
    ΔTlm​=F⋅ΔTlm,CF​

    where F is the correction factor, and ΔTlm,CF​ is LMTD of a counter-flow double-pipe
    (counter-flow is the more efficient arrangement for a double-pipe heat exchanger)

    Special case 1, where temperature of one fluid is constant (condensation/boiling).

    💡
    F=1

    Special case 2, where both fluids have the same
    heat capacity in counter-flow double-pipe
    heat exchangers.

    💡
    F=1

    C=m˙h​cp,h​=m˙c​cp,c​

    −m˙h​cp,h​ΔTh​=m˙c​cp,c​ΔTc​

    Th,in​−Th,out​=Tc,out​−Tc,in​

    Th,in​−Tc,out​=Th,out​−Tc,in​

    ΔT1​=ΔT2​=ΔTlm​

    Temperature ratio,

    💡
    P=T1​−t1​t2​−t1​​

    Temperature ratio,

    💡
    R=t2​−t1​T1​−T2​​

    Note that subscripts 1 and 2 represent the inlet and outlet respectively, and
    uppercase     T and lowercase t represents shell- and tube-side temperatures.

    #Effectiveness - Number of Transfer Units

    This method is based on the heat
    transfer effectiveness parameter.

    💡
    ε=Q˙​max​Q˙​actual​​

    Actual heat transfer rate is,

    Q˙​=m˙h​cp,h​(Th,in​−Th,out​)=Ch​ΔTh​

    Q˙​=m˙c​cp,c​(Tc,out​−Tc,in​)=Cc​ΔTc​

    Maximum temperature difference in a heat exchanger is the difference between inlet temperatures of the
    hot and cold fluids.

    ΔTmax​=Th,in​−Tc,in​

    Typically, the fluid with the smaller heat capacity rate will experience a larger
    temperature change. Maximum possible heat transfer rate in a exchanger is,

    💡
    Q˙​max​=Cmin​(Th,in​−Tc,in​)

    where Cmin​ is the smaller of Ch​ and Cc​

    Once effectiveness ε is known,

    Q˙​=εQ˙​max​=εCmin​(Th,in​−Tc,in​)

    Energy balance equation for hot fluid,

    Ch​(Th,in​−Th,out​)=εCmin​(Th,in​−Tc,in​)

    (Th,in​−Th,out​)=Ch​εCmin​(Th,in​−Tc,in​)​

    Energy balance equation for cold fluid,

    Cc​(Tc,out​−Tc,in​)=εCmin​(Th,in​−Tc,in​)

    (Tc,out​−Tc,in​)=Cc​εCmin​(Th,in​−Tc,in​)​

    Summing both energy balance equations,

    (Th,in​−Th,out​)+(Tc,out​−Tc,in​)=εCmin​(Th,in​−Tc,in​)(Cc​1​+Ch​1​)

    (Th,in​−Tc,in​)−(Th,out​−Tc,out​)=εCmin​(Th,in​−Tc,in​)(Cc​1​+Ch​1​)

    1−Th,in​−Tc,in​Th,out​−Tc,out​​=εCmin​(Cc​1​+Ch​1​)

    Th,in​−Tc,in​Th,out​−Tc,out​​=1−εCmin​(Cc​1​+Ch​1​)

    Using the LMTD analysis of a parallel-flow double-pipe heat exchanger,

    lnTh,in​−Tc,in​Th,out​−Tc,out​​=−UAs​(m˙h​cp,h​1​+m˙c​cp,c​1​)

    ln[1−εCmin​(Cc​1​+Ch​1​)]=−UAs​(Cc​1​+Ch​1​)

    Rearranging the above equation, the effectiveness of a parallel-flow exchanger is,

    💡
    εpara​=Cmin​(Cc​1​+Ch​1​)1−exp[−UAs​(Cc​1​+Ch​1​)]​

    Expressing the above in terms of number of transfer units,

    εpara​=Cmin​(Cc​1​+Ch​1​)1−exp[−UAs​(Cc​1​+Ch​1​)]​

    εpara​=1+Cmax​Cmin​​1−exp[−Cmin​UAs​​(1+Cmax​Cmin​​)]​

    💡
    εpara​=1+c1−exp[−NTU(1+c)]​

    where NTU=Cmin​UAs​​ and heat capacity ratio c=Cmax​Cmin​​

    Special case 1, where temperature of one fluid remains constant (condensation/boiling).
    For a given NTU, effectiveness becomes a maximum when heat capacity ratio c→0
    as Cmax​→∞, which occurs during a phase-change process.

    💡
    εmax​=1−e−NTU

    The above equation applies to any heat exchanger as long as there is a phase-change.
