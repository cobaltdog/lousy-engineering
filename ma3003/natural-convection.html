    Natural Convection

    #Convection Mechanism

    In natural convection, fluid motion occurs as a result of buoyancy effects.
    Warmer air adjacent to a hot surface rises, and is replaced with cooler air,
    creating natural convection currents.

    Net force acting on a body is,

    Fnet​=W−Fbuoyancy​

    Fnet​=ρbody​Vbody​g−ρfluid​Vbody​g

    Fnet​=(ρbody​−ρfluid​)Vbody​g

    To express the net buoyancy force in terms of a temperature difference, we
    need to express the density difference in terms of a temperature difference.

    Volume expansion coefficient is defined as,

    💡
    β=V1​(∂T∂V​)P​=−ρ1​(∂T∂ρ​)P​

    Variation of the density of a fluid with temperature at constant pressure.

    For an ideal gas, the coefficient is,

    💡
    β ideal gas​=Tfilm​1​

    where temperature Tfilm​ is in Kelvins

    Expressing density difference in terms of temperature difference,

    β≈−ρ1​ΔTΔρ​

    β=−ρ1​T∞​−Ts​ρ∞​−ρs​​

    💡
    ρ∞​−ρs​=ρβ(Ts​−T∞​)

    where the subscript ∞ represents flow away from the surface

    #Momentum Equation

    Consider a vertical hot flat plate immersed in a fluid. Assume the natural convection flow to be steady, laminar, and 2-dimensional.

    Velocity components in the boundary layer are,

    u=u(x,y)

    v=v(x,y)

    Acceleration in the x-direction is,

    ax​=dtdu​

    ax​=∂x∂u​dtdx​+∂y∂u​dtdy​

    ax​=u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​

    Using Newton’s 2nd law,

    Fx​=dm⋅ax​

    Fx​=ρ(dxdy⋅1)(u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​)

    Summation of forces on the differential element.

    Fx​=∑P+∑τ−W

    Fx​=(∂y∂τ​dy)(dx⋅1)−(∂x∂P​dx)(dy⋅1)           −ρg(dxdy⋅1)

    Fx​=(dxdy⋅1)(∂y∂τ​−∂x∂P​−ρg)

    Fx​=(dxdy⋅1)(μ∂y2∂2u​−∂x∂P​−ρg)

    Note that surface shear stress τ=μ∂y∂u​.

    Momentum equation in the x-direction is then,

    ρ(u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​)=μ∂y2∂2u​−∂x∂P​−ρg

    ρ(u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​)=μ∂y2∂2u​−(−ρ∞​g)−ρg

    ρ(u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​)=μ∂y2∂2u​+(ρ∞​−ρ)g

    ρ(u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​)=μ∂y2∂2u​+ρβ(T−T∞​)g

    💡
    u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​=ν∂y2∂2u​+gβ(Ts​−T∞​)

    Note that for a vertical flat plate, the variation of pressure
    along the     x-direction is simply the pressure of the fluid.

    For a vertical isothermal plate, the governing equations are,

    Continuity equation: ∂x∂u​+∂y∂v​=0

    Momentum equation: u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​=ν∂y2∂2u​+gβ(Ts​−T∞​)

    Energy equation: u∂x∂T​+v∂y∂T​=α∂y2∂2T​

    Boundary conditions for the equations are,

    At y=0,

    At x=0,

    As y→∞,

    u(x,0)=0 and v(x,0)=0,

    u(0,y)=0,

    u(x,∞)→0

    T(x,0)=Ts​

    T(0,y)=T∞​

    T(x,∞)→T∞​

    #Grashof Number

    Nondimensionalizing the momentum equation in the x-direction,

    u∗∂x∗∂u∗​+v∗∂y∗∂u∗​=[ν2gβ(Ts​−T∞​)Lc​3​]ReL​2T∗​+ReL​1​∂y∗2∂2u∗​

    Grashof number is defined as,

    💡
    GrL​=ν2gβ(Ts​−T∞​)Lc​3​

    Represents the ratio of buoyancy force
    to the viscous force acting on the fluid.

    If ReL​2GrL​​≫1, natural convection effects dominate.

    If ReL​2GrL​​≪1, forced convection effects dominate.

    If ReL​2GrL​​=1, both effects must be considered.

    #Rayleigh Number

    Nusselt number for natural convection is,

    💡
    Nu=C(GrL​Pr)n=C(RaL​)n

    where C and n depend on geometry
    of the surface and the flow regime

    Rayleigh number is defined as,

    💡
    RaL​=ν2gβ(Ts​−T∞​)Lc​3​Pr
    💡
    RaL​=ναgβ(Ts​−T∞​)Lc​3​

    Represents the ratio of buoyancy forces and the product of momentum and thermal diffusivities.

    #Natural Convection over Surfaces

    #Vertical Plate

    For a vertical plate, its characteristic length is the plate height L.

    Nusselt numbers for laminar and turbulent flow are,

    Nu lami​=0.59RaL​1/4 where 104<RaL​<109

    Nu turb​=0.1RaL​1/3 where 109<RaL​<1013

    Average Nusselt number for laminar flow is,

    💡
    NuˉL​=0.68+[1+(0.492/Pr)9/16]4/90.67RaL​1/4​

    where 104<RaL​<109

    Average Nusselt number for the entire range of Rayleigh number is,

    💡
    NuˉL​={0.825+[1+(0.492/Pr)9/16]8/270.387RaL​1/6​}2

    ν=1.896×10−5 m2/s

    Pr=0.7202

    #Horizontal Plate

    Characteristic length of a horizontal plate is,

    💡
    Lc​=PerimeterHeat Transfer Area​=PAs​​

    Nusselt numbers for the upper surface of a hot
    plate, or the lower surface of a cold plate are,

    NuˉL​=0.54RaL​1/4 where 104<RaL​<107

    NuˉL​=0.15RaL​1/5 where 107<RaL​<1011

    These cases are characterized by warm air rising from
    a hot surface, or cold air sinking from a cold surface.

    Nusselt numbers for the lower surface of a hot
    plate, or the upper surface of a cold plate are,

    NuˉL​=0.27RaL​1/4 where 105<RaL​<1011

    This case is characterized by warm air rising around a
    hot surface, or cold air sinking around a cold surface.

    #Vertical Cylinder

    For a vertical cylinder, its outer surface is treated as a vertical plate when diameter of the cylinder is large enough that curvature effects are negligible.

    💡
    D≥GrL​1/435L​

    where L is the height of the cylinder

    #Horizontal Cylinder

    Boundary layer over a hot horizontal cylinder starts to develop at the bottom, increasing in thickness along
    the circumference and forming a rising plume.

    Average Nusselt number of the flow is,

    💡
    NuˉD​={0.6+[1+(0.559/Pr)9/16]8/270.387RaD​1/6​}2

    where RaD​≤1012

    #Example

    Film temperature,

    Tfilm​=270+20​=45°C

    Volume expansion coefficient,

    β=Tfilm​1​=45+2731​=3181​

    From Table A15, at T=45°C,

    k=0.02699 W/m⋅K

    ν=1.75×10−5 m2/s

    Pr=0.7241

    Rayleigh number of pipe,

    RaD​=[ν2gβ(Ts​−T∞​)Lc​3​]Pr

    RaD​=(1.75×10−5)29.81(1/318)(70−20)(0.08)3​(0.7241)

    RaD​=1.867×106

    Nusselt number for natural convection,

    NuˉD​={0.6+[1+(0.559/Pr)9/16]8/270.387RaD​1/6​}2

    NuˉD​={0.6+[1(0.559/0.7241)9/16]8/270.387(1.867×106)1/6​}2

    NuˉD​=17.39

    Natural convection coefficient,

    NuˉD​=kf​hˉD​

    hˉ=0.0817.39(0.02699)​

    hˉ=5.867 W/m2⋅K

    Heat loss by natural convection,

    Q˙​conv​=hˉAs​(Ts​−T∞​)

    Q˙​conv​=hˉ(πDL)(Ts​−T∞​)

    Q˙​conv​=5.867π(0.08)(6)(70−20)

    Q˙​conv​=442 W

    Heat loss by radiation, assuming an emissivity of 1,

    Q˙​rad​=εσAs​(Ts​4−T∞​4)

    Q˙​rad​=(5.67×10−8)π(0.08)(6)(3434−2934)

    Q˙​rad​=553 W

    Assuming the outer surface of the pipe is black and the inner surfaces of the walls
    of the room to be at room temperature, the radiation heat transfer is larger than
    that of natural convection.
