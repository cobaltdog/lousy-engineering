    Internal Forced Convection

    #Average Velocity and Temperature

    Fluid velocity in a pipe changes from zero at the surface, due to no-slip condition, to
    a maximum at the tube center. Thus, it is convenient to work with an average velocity.

    From conservation of mass,

    m˙=ρVavg​Ac​

    m˙=∫Ac​​ ρ⋅u(r) dAc​

    where Ac​ is the cross-sectional area of the pipe

    Average velocity for incompressible flow in
    a circular pipe of radius R is expressed as,

    Vavg​=ρAc​∫Ac​​ ρ⋅u(r) dAc​​

    Vavg​=ρπR2∫0R​ ρ⋅u(r)⋅2πr dr​

    💡
    Vavg​=R22​∫0R​ r⋅u(r) dr

    where u(r) is the velocity profile of the flow

    When a fluid is heated or cooled as it flow through a pipe, temperature of the fluid
    at any cross section changes from Ts​ at the surface of the wall to some maximum
    or minimum at the pipe center.

    From conservation of energy,

    E˙fluid​=m˙cp​Tm​

    E˙fluid​=∫m˙​ cp​⋅T(r) dm˙

    E˙fluid​=∫Ac​​ ρcp​⋅T(r)u(r) dAc​

    Mean temperature of a fluid with constant density and specific heat flowing in a circular pipe of radius R is,

    Tm​=m˙cp​∫m˙​ cp​⋅T(r) dm˙​

    Tm​=ρVavg​(πR2)cp​∫0R​ ρcp​⋅T(r)u(r)⋅2πr dr​

    💡
    Tm​=Vavg​R22​∫0R​ r⋅T(r)u(r) dr

    where T(r) is the temperature profile of the flow

    Fluid properties in internal flow are evaluated at the bulk mean fluid temperature.

    💡
    Tm,bulk​=2Tinlet​+Texit​​

    #Entrance Region

    Consider a fluid entering a circular pipe at a uniform velocity. Due to viscous effects, the
    velocity profile of the fluid only fully develops after the hydrodynamic entry length Lh​.

    Entry region for laminar flow,

    💡
    Dx​=0.05ReD​

    where ReD​<2300

    Entry region for turbulent flow,

    💡
    Dx​>10

    where ReD​>2300

    Now consider a fluid at a uniform temperature entering a circular pipe whose surface is maintained at a lower temperature than the fluid. A thermal boundary layer develops
    along the tube and is only fully developed after the thermal entry length Lt​.

    The thermally fully developed region is described by the dimensionless temperature profile,

    ∂x∂​[Ts​(x)−Tm​(x)Ts​(x)−T(r,x)​]=0

    In a thermally fully developed region, dimensionless temperature profile is independent of x.

    💡
    ∂r∂​(Ts​−Tm​Ts​−T​)​r=R​=Ts​−Tm​−(∂T/∂r)​r=R​​=f(x)

    Note that the temperature profile can be different at different cross sections in the
    developed region, but the dimensionless temperature profile remains unchanged.

    Surface heat flux is expressed as,

    💡
    q˙​s​=−kf​∂y∂T​​y=0​=kf​∂r∂T​​r=R​

    From Newton’s law of cooling,

    q˙​s​=h(Ts​−Tm​)

    kf​∂r∂T​​r=R​=h(Ts​−Tm​)

    💡
    kf​h​=Ts​−Tm​(∂T/∂r)​r=R​​=f(x)

    In a thermally fully developed region, the local convection coefficient is independent of x, and
    remains constant in this region.

    Thermal entry length for laminar flow,

    💡
    Lt,laminar​=0.05ReD​PrD

    where ReD​<2300

    Thermal entry length for turbulent flow,

    💡
    Lt,turbulent​>10D

    where ReD​>2300

    #Hydraulic Diameter

    For flow in a circular pipe,
    Reynolds number is defined as,

    💡
    ReD​=μρVavg​D​=μρD​(ρπD2/4m˙​)=μπD4m˙​

    For flow through non-circular pipes,
    diameter is replaced with hydraulic diameter.

    💡
    Dh​=p4Ac​​

    where p is the wetted diameter of the pipe

    Consider concentric annulus of inner diameter Di​, and outer diameter Do​. The hydraulic diameter of
    the annulus is,

    Dh​=p4Ac​​

    Dh​=π(Do​+Di​)4π(Do​2−Di​2)/4​

    Dh​=Do​−Di​

    #General Thermal Analysis

    If work input is zero, from the 1st law of thermodynamics, the heat transfer is,

    Q˙​=m˙cp​(Tm,e​−Tm,i​)

    Surface heat flux is expressed as,

    q˙​s​=hx​(Ts​−Tm​)

    During heating or cooling of the fluid, mean fluid temperature Tm​ must change.
    Thus, when     q˙​s​ and hx​ are constants, the surface temperature Ts​ must change.
    When     Ts​ and hx​ are constants, the rate of heat transfer q˙​s​ must change.
    Hence, for any given case, either     q˙​s​=const or Ts​=const.

    #Constant Surface Heat Flux

    If rate of heat transfer is constant,

    Q˙​=q˙​s​As​=m˙cp​(Tm,e​−Tm,i​)

    Mean fluid temperature at the pipe exit is then,

    💡
    Tm,e​=Tm,i​+m˙cp​q˙​s​As​​

    Note that Tm,e​ increases linearly in the flow direction
    as surface area increases linearly in the flow direction.

    From Newton’s law of cooling,

    q˙​s​=h(Ts​−Tm​)

    💡
    Ts​=Tm​+hq˙​s​​

    Note that Ts​ increases linearly in the flow direction since q˙​s​ and h are constants.

    Performing energy balance on an element,

    dQ˙​=q˙​s​(p dx)=m˙cp​ dTm​

    dxdTm​​=m˙cp​q˙​s​p​=const

    where p is the perimeter of the pipe

    Differentiating the Ts​ equation with respect to x,

    dxdTs​​=const

    Since the dimensionless temperature profile is also a constant,

    ∂r∂​(Ts​−Tm​Ts​−T​)=0

    Ts​−Tm​1​(∂x∂Ts​​−∂x∂T​)=0

    ∂x∂T​=dxdTs​​

    Combining the above equations,

    💡
    ∂x∂T​=dxdTs​​=dxdTm​​=m˙cp​q˙​s​p​=m˙cp​h(Ts​−Tm​)πD​=const

    In a fully developed flow in a pipe subjected to constant surface heat flux,
    the temperature gradient is independent of     x, and thus the shape of the
    temperature profile does not change along the pipe.

    Integrating the above equation,

    dxdTm​​=m˙cp​q˙​s​p​

    ∫dTm​=∫m˙cp​q˙​s​p​ dx

    💡
    Tm​(x)=Tm,i​+m˙cp​q˙​s​p​x

    At x=0, the mean fluid temperature is Tm,i​.

    Since dxdTs​​=dxdTm​​, for fully developed region,
    both the surface and mean temperature
    distributions are parallel to each other.

    L=17.736 m

    #Part (b)

    Assuming constant surface heat flux,

    q˙​s​=As​Q˙​conv​​=As​E˙gen​​

    q˙​s​=πDi​Le˙gen​π(ro​2−ri​2)L​

    #Constant Surface Temperature

    Consider the heating of a fluid in a pipe of constant cross section whose inner surface is maintained at a constant temperature Ts​. Mean temperature Tm​ of the fluid increases in the flow direction due to heat transfer.

    Performing energy balance on an element,

    dQ˙​=m˙cp​ dTm​=h(Ts​−Tm​) dAs​

    Increase in energy of the fluid is equal to the
    heat transferred from the surface to the fluid.

    Rearranging the equation,

    m˙cp​[−d(Ts​−Tm​)]=h(Ts​−Tm​)(p dx)

    Ts​−Tm​d(Ts​−Tm​)​=−m˙cp​hp​ dx

    Integrating from x=0 to x=L,

    ∫Tm,i​Tm,e​​Ts​−Tm​d(Ts​−Tm​)​=∫0x​−m˙cp​hp​ dx

    ∫Tm,i​Tm,e​​Ts​−Tm​d(Ts​−Tm​)​=−m˙cp​p​[x1​∫0x​ h dx]

    💡
    lnTs​−Tm,i​Ts​−Tm,e​​=−m˙cp​hˉAs​​

    Log mean temperature difference is defined as,

    Q˙​=m˙cp​(Tm,e​−Tm,i​)

    Q˙​=m˙cp​[(Ts​−Tm,i​)−(Ts​−Tm,e​)]

    Q˙​=−ln[(Ts​−Tm,e​)/(Ts​−Tm,i​)]hˉAs​​⋅[(Ts​−Tm,i​)−(Ts​−Tm,e​)]

    Q˙​=ln[(Ts​−Tm,e​)/(Ts​−Tm,i​)]hˉAs​[(Ts​−Tm,e​)−(Ts​−Tm,i​)]​

    Q˙​=ln(ΔTm,e​/ΔTm,i​)hˉAs​(ΔTm,e​−ΔTm,i​)​

    Comparing the above equation with Newton’s law of cooling, Q˙​=hAs​ΔTlm​,

    💡
    ΔTlm​=ln(ΔTm,e​/ΔTm,i​)ΔTm,e​−ΔTm,i​​

    Log mean temperature is the average of the temperature
    difference between the wall and fluid over the pipe length.

    Number of transfer units (NTU) is defined as,

    💡
    NTU=m˙cp​hˉAs​​=m˙cp​hˉpx​

    A measure of the effectiveness of heat transfer.

    For an NTU>5, the exit temperature of the fluid becomes almost equal to the surface temperature. Thus, the limit is reached for heat transfer, where heat transfer does not increase no matter how much we extend the length of the pipe.

    #Example

    Log mean temperature,

    ΔTlm​=ln(ΔTe​/ΔTi​)ΔTe​−ΔTi​​

    ΔTlm​=ln[(Ts​−Tm,e​)/(Ts​−Tm,i​)](Ts​−Tm,e​)−(Ts​−Tm,i​)​

    ΔTlm​=ln100−15100−57​(100−57)−(100−15)​

    ΔTlm​=61.633°C

    From the 1st law of thermodynamics,

    Q˙​conv​=m˙cp​(Tm,e​−Tm,i​)

    hˉAs​ΔTlm​=m˙cp​(Tm,e​−Tm,i​)

    hˉ(πDL)ΔTlm​=m˙cp​(Tm,e​−Tm,i​)

    hˉ=π(0.05)(6)(61.633)0.25(4178)(57−15)​

    hˉ=755.22 W/m2⋅K

    #Turbulent Flow in Pipes

    For fully developed turbulent flow in smooth pipes,

    💡
    NuD​=0.023ReD​0.8Prn

    where ReD​≥10000, and 0.7≤Pr≤160, and DL​≥10

    For heating, where Ts​>Tm​, the exponent n=0.4.
    For cooling, where Ts​<Tm​, the exponent n=0.3.
