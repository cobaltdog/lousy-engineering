    Entropy

    #Clausius Inequality

    The Clausius inequality is expressed as,

    💡
    ∮TdQ​≤0

    The symbol ∮ indicates the integration is to be performed over the entire cycle. Any heat
    transfer to or from a system can be considered to consist of differential amounts of heat
    transfer. The cyclic integral of     TdQ​ is the sum of these differential amounts of heat transfer.

    The cyclic integral can be expressed as,

    ∮TdQ​=∫TH​dQH​​−∫TL​dQL​​

    ∮TdQ​=TH​QH​​−TL​QL​​

    For a reversible cycle, the integral becomes,

    💡
    ∮TdQ​rev​=0

    Recall that for a reversible cycle, (QL​QH​​)rev​=TL​TH​​. Rearranging the terms, we get TH​QH​​=TL​QL​​.

    For an irreversible cycle, thermal efficiency is lower than that of a reversible cycle.

    ηth,irrev​<ηth,rev​

    (1−QH​QL​​)irrev​<(1−QH​QL​​)rev​

    Since heat input QH​ of both cycles are the same, QL,irrev​>QL,rev​.

    (TH​QH​​−TL​QL​​)irrev​<(TH​QH​​−TL​QL​​)rev​

    (TH​QH​​−TL​QL​​)irrev​<0

    💡
    ∮TdQ​irrev​<0

    #Derivation of Entropy

    Consider the volume occupied by a gas in a piston-cylinder device undergoing a cycle. When the piston returns to its initial position at the end of a cycle, the volume of gas also returns to its initial value.

    Thus, the net change in volume during a cycle is zero.
    That is ∮dV=ΔVsystem​=0. Therefore, a quantity
    whose cyclic integral is zero depends on the state only
    and not the process path.

    The property entropy is defined as,

    💡
    dS=TdQ​rev​

    Entropy change of a system during a process is then,

    💡
    ΔS=∫12​TdQ​rev​=S2​−S1​

    For isothermal heat transfer processes,

    ΔS=∫12​TdQ​rev​=T0​1​∫12​ dQrev​

    💡
    ΔS=T0​Q2​−Q1​​=T0​Q​

    where T0​ is the constant temperature of the system and Q is the net heat transfer.

    #Increase of Entropy Principle

    Consider a cycle made up of two processes: process 1-2, which is arbitrary, and process 2-1, which is reversible.

    ∮TdQ​≤0

    ∫12​TdQ​+∫21​TdQ​rev​≤0

    ∫12​TdQ​+S1​−S2​≤0

    S2​−S1​≥∫12​TdQ​

    💡
    dS≥TdQ​

    For an irreversible process, the entropy change is greater than the integral of TdQ​
    evaluated for that process. For a reversible process, the quantities become equal.

    For an irreversible process, since entropy change is greater than ∫12​TdQ​, which
    represents entropy transfer with heat, it can be said that some entropy is
    generated during an irreversible process which is denoted as Sgen​.

    💡
    ΔSsys​=S2​−S1​=∫12​TdQ​+Sgen​

    The entropy generation Sgen​ is always a positive quantity or zero. The more
    irreversible a process, the larger the entropy generated during that process.

    For an isolated (adiabatic) system, heat transfer Q is zero, thus the equation becomes,

    💡
    ΔSisolated​=Sgen​ ⟶ ΔSisolated​≥0

    The entropy of an isolated system during a process always increases or, in the case
    of a reversible process, remains constant. This is the increase of entropy principle.

    A system and its surroundings constitute an isolated system since both can be enclosed by a sufficiently large arbitrary boundary.

    The entropy change of this isolated system during
    a process is the sum of the entropy changes of the system and its surroundings.

    💡
    Sgen​=ΔSsys​+ΔSsurr​≥0

    The increase of entropy principle does not imply
    that the entropy of a system cannot decrease.
    The entropy change of a system can be negative during a process but entropy generation cannot.

    #Example

    The reservoirs undergo an internally reversible, isothermal process, ΔSres​=TQ​.

    ΔSsource​=800−2000​=−2.5 kJ/K

    ΔSsink A​=5002000​=4.0 kJ/K

    ΔSsink B​=7502000​=2.7 kJ/K

    Treating the source and sink as a system,

    ΔSsys​=∫12​TdQ​+Sgen​

    ΔSsys​=Sgen​

    Sgen​=ΔSsource​+ΔSsink​

    Q=0 as there is no loss of heat from the system.

    Sgen(A)​=−2.5+4.0=1.5 kJ/K

    Sgen(B)​=−2.5+2.7=0.2 kJ/K

    Since process B has a lower entropy generation, it is more reversible.

    #Some Remarks about Entropy

    1. Processes can occur in a certain direction only. A process must proceed in the direction that complies with the increase in entropy principle Sgen​≥0. A process that violates this principle is impossible.
    2. Entropy is a non-conserved property. Entropy is conserved during idealized reversible processes only and increases for all real irreversible processes.
    3. Performance of engineering systems is degraded by the presence of irreversibilities, and entropy generation is a measure of the magnitudes of the irreversibilities present during that process.

    #Property Diagrams

    A process during which the entropy remains constant is called an isentropic process. The property diagrams commonly used in second-law analysis are the T-s and h-s diagrams.

    Rearranging the entropy equation,

    dQrev​=T dS

    dqrev​=T ds

    qrev​=∫12​ T ds

    The total heat transfer during an internally
    reversible process corresponds to the area
    under the process curve on a     T-s diagram.

    An isentropic process on a T-s diagram appears
    as a vertical line segment since an isentropic
    process involves no heat transfer.

    #T ds Relations

    Energy balance for a closed stationary system can be expressed for an internally reversible process as,

    dQrev​−dWnet​=dU

    T dS−P dV=dU

    T ds−P dv=du

    💡
    T ds=du+P dv

    Eliminating du using h=u+Pv,

    dh=du+P dv+v dP

    dh=T ds+v dP

    💡
    T ds=dh−v dP

    #Entropy Change of Solids and Liquids

    Solids and liquids can be approximated as incompressible substances since their specific volumes remain nearly constant during a process. Thus, P dv=0 for liquids and solids.

    T ds=du=cavg​ dT

    ds=Tdu​=cavg​ TdT​

    Δs=cavg​∫12​T1​ dT

    💡
    Δs=cavg​ lnT1​T2​​

    where cavg​ is the average specific heat of the substance over the given temperature interval

    #Entropy Change of Ideal Gases

    Substituting du=cv​ dT and P=vRs​T​ into the first relation,

    T ds=du+P dv

    T ds=cv​ dT+Rs​T vdv​

    ds=cv​ TdT​+Rs​ vdv​

    Δs=cv,avg​∫12​T1​ dT+Rs​∫12​v1​ dv

    💡
    Δs=cv,avg​ lnT1​T2​​+Rs​ lnv1​v2​​

    Substituting dh=cp​ dT and v=PRs​T​ into the second relation,

    T ds=dh−v dP

    T ds=cp​ dT−Rs​T PdP​

    ds=cp​ TdT​−Rs​ PdP​

    Δs=cp,avg​∫12​T1​ dT−Rs​∫12​P1​ dP

    💡
    Δs=cp,avg​ lnT1​T2​​−Rs​ lnP1​P2​​

    For ideal gases whose specific heats vary almost linearly in the temperature range of interest,
    the error is minimized by using specific heat values evaluated at the average temperature as
    long as the temperature range is not greater than a few hundred degrees.

    #Isentropic Processes of Ideal Gases

    Isentropic relations can be obtained by equating the above equations to zero.

    lnT1​T2​​=−cv,avg​R​ lnv1​v2​​

    lnT1​T2​​=ln(v2​v1​​)cv,avg​R​

    💡
    T1​T2​​=(v2​v1​​)k−1

    lnT1​T2​​=cp,avg​R​ lnP1​P2​​

    lnT1​T2​​=ln(P1​P2​​)cp,avg​R​

    💡
    T1​T2​​=(P1​P2​​)kk−1​

    Recall that R=cp​−cv​ and k=cv​cp​​, thus cv​R​=k−1.

    Equating both equations above,

    (P1​P2​​)kk−1​=(v2​v1​​)k−1

    P1​P2​​=(v2​v1​​)k

    💡
    Pvk=Const

    The above is the equation of an isentropic process for an ideal gas.

    #Reversible Steady Flow Work

    Work done during a process depends on the path followed as well as the properties at the end states.

    Energy balance for a steady-flow device undergoing an internally reversible process can be expressed as,

    dqrev​−dwrev​=dh+d(KE)+d(PE)

    T ds−dwrev​=dh+d(KE)+d(PE)

    dh−v dP−dwrev​=dh+d(KE)+d(PE)

    dwrev​=−v dP−d(KE)−d(PE)

    💡
    wrev​=−∫12​v dP−ΔKE−ΔPE

    When kinetic and potential energies are negligible,

    💡
    wrev​=−∫12​v dP

    The negative sign indicates work input to steady-flow devices such as compressors and pumps.

    When working fluid is incompressible, the specific volume v is a constant,

    💡
    wrev​=−v(P2​−P1​)−ΔKE−ΔPE

    For a steady-flow device that does no work, the equation becomes,

    💡
    v(P2​−P1​)+21​(V2​2−V1​2)+g(z2​−z1​)=0

    The above equation is the Bernoulli equation and is only applicable to incompressible fluids that involve no irreversibilities such as friction or shock waves.

    #Minimizing Compressor Work

    When changes in kinetic and potential energies are negligible, the compressor work is given by wrev​=−∫12​v dP. Thus, keeping the specific volume of the gas as small as possible during the compression process reduces the compressor work.

    Work input for isentropic process: Pvk=Const,

    wcomp,in​=k−1kRs​(T2​−T1​)​=k−1kRs​T1​​[(P1​P2​​)kk−1​−1]

    Work input for polytropic process: Pvn=Const,

    wcomp,in​=n−1nRs​(T2​−T1​)​=n−1nRs​T1​​[(P1​P2​​)nn−1​−1]

    Work input for isothermal process: Pv=Const,

    wcomp,in​=Rs​T lnP1​P2​​

    #Multistage Compression with Intercooling

    Compression process with intercooling:

    • Gas is compressed from P1​ to Px​
    • Cooled at constant temperature to T1​
    • Compressed again to final pressure P2​

    wcomp,in​=wcomp,1​+wcomp,2​

    wcomp,in​=n−1nRs​T1​​[(P1​Px​​)nn−1​−1]                  +n−1nRs​T1​​[(Px​P2​​)nn−1​−1]

    Differentiating the above with respect to Px​,

    💡
    Px​=P1​⋅P2​​ → P1​Px​​=Px​P2​​

    To minimize the compressor work during a two-stage compression, the pressure ratio across each stage must be the same.

    #Isentropic Efficiencies of Steady-Flow Devices

    The more closely an actual process approximates the idealized isentropic process, the better
    the device performs. Isentropic, or adiabatic efficiency, is a measure of the deviation of actual processes from the corresponding idealized ones.

    #Isentropic Efficiency of Turbines

    Isentropic efficiency of a turbine is,

    💡
    ηtur​=IsentropicActual​=ws​wa​​

    If kinetic and potential energies are negligible,

    💡
    ηtur​=h1​−h2s​h1​−h2a​​

    #Example

    At P1​=3 MPa and T1​=400°C,

    h1​=3231.7 kJ/kg

    s1​=6.9235 kJ/kg⋅K

    At P2a​=50 kPa and T2a​=100°C,

    h2a​=2682.4 kJ/kg

    At P2s​=50 kPa and s2s​=s1​,

    hf​=340.54 kJ/kg

    hfg​=2304.7 kJ/kg

    sf​=1.0912 kJ/kg⋅K

    sfg​=6.5019 kJ/kg⋅K

    s2s​=sf​+x⋅sfg​

    x2s​=sfg​s2s​−sf​​

    x2s​=6.50196.9235−1.0912​

    x2s​=0.897

    h2s​=hf​+x2s​⋅hfg​

    h2s​=340.54+0.897(2304.7)

    h2s​=2407.9 kJ/kg

    ηtur​=h1​−h2s​h1​−h2a​​

    ηtur​=3231.7−2407.93231.7−2682.4​=0.667

    Q˙​−W˙=m˙Δh

    −2×103=m˙(h2a​−h1​)

    Isentropic efficiency of a compressor is,

    💡
    ηcomp​=ActualIsentropic​=wa​ws​​

    ws​ is a smaller quantity than wa​. This prevents ηcomp​ from becoming greater than 100%.

    If kinetic and potential energies are negligible,

    💡
    ηcomp​=h2a​−h1​h2s​−h1​​

    #Isentropic Efficiency of Nozzles

    Isentropic efficiency of a nozzle is,

    💡
    ηnoz​=Isentropic KE at ExitActual KE at Exit​=V2s​2V2a​2​

    Nozzles involve no work interactions.

    💡
    ηnoz​=h1​−h2s​h1​−h2a​​

    #Example

    Assuming the average temperature of the air to be 850K.
    The average values of cp​=1.11 kJ/kg⋅K and k=1.349.

    T1​T2s​​=(P1​P2s​​)kk−1​

    T2s​=950(200110​)1.3491.349−1​=814 K

    h1​−h2s​=21​(V2s​2−V1​2)

    V2s​=2(h1​−h2s​)​

    V2s​=2cp​(T1​−T2s​)​

    V2s​=2(1.11)(950−814)​

    V2s​=549 m/s

    ηnoz​=V2s​2V2a​2​

    V2a​=ηnoz​⋅V2s​2​

    V2a​=0.92(549)2​

    V2a​=527 m/s

    ηnoz​=h1​−h2s​h1​−h2a​​=cp​(T1​−T2s​)cp​(T1​−T2a​)​

    comes at the expense of kinetic energy.

    #Entropy Balance

    The second law states that entropy can be created but cannot be destroyed. Thus, entropy change of a system during a process is equal to the net entropy transfer through the system boundary plus the entropy generated within the system.

    💡
    ΔSsys​=S2​−S1​=Sin​−Sout​+Sgen​

    For a reversible process, Sgen​=0 and the entropy change is equal to the entropy transfer.

    Entropy can be transferred to or from a system by two mechanisms — heat transfer and mass flow. Entropy transfer represents the entropy gained or lost by a system during a process. No entropy is transferred by work.

    #Entropy Transfer through Heat Transfer

    Heat transfer to a system increases entropy of that system and vice versa.

    The ratio of heat transfer Q at a location to the absolute temperature T is the entropy transfer.

    💡
    Sheat​=TQ​

    When T is not a constant, the entropy transfer can be determined by integration/summation.

    💡
    Sheat​=∫12​TdQ​

    Recall that a system and its surroundings can be treated as an isolated (adiabatic) system. Thus, total entropy change is equal to the sum of the entropy changes of its parts where ΔSsys​=m(s2​−s1​) and ΔSsurr​=Tsurr​Qsurr​​ if temperature is constant.

    For a closed system, the entropy balance equation becomes,

    💡
    ΔSsys​=S2​−S1​=TQ​+Sgen​

    Entropy change of a closed system during a process is equal to the sum of the net entropy transferred through the system boundary by heat transfer and the entropy generated within the system boundaries.

    #Entropy Transfer through Mass Flow

    Mass contains entropy as well as energy.

    Entropy transfer into or out of a system
    is proportional to the mass flow rate.

    💡
    Smass​=m⋅s

    Taking the positive direction of heat transfer to be into the system,

    💡
    ΔSCV​=S2​−S1​=∑TQ​+∑mi​si​−∑me​se​+Sgen​

    For a steady-flow process, there is no change in entropy in the control volume.

    💡
    Sgen​=∑me​se​−∑mi​si​−∑TQ​

    #Example 1

    Rate of Entropy Generation in the Wall

    dtdSsys​​=S˙in​−S˙out​+S˙gen​

    0=Tin​Q˙​in​​−Tout​Q˙​out​​+S˙gen​

    S˙gen​=5+2731035​−20+2731035​

    S˙gen​=0.191 W/K

    Rate of Total Entropy Generation

    Extend the system to include the regions on both sides of the wall that experience a temperature change.

    S˙gen,tot​=0+2731035​−30+2731035​

    S˙gen,tot​=0.341 W/K
