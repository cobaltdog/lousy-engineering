    Properties of Pure Substances

    #Pure Substance

    A pure substance is one that has a fixed chemical composition throughout — homogeneous.
    A mixture of two or more phases of a pure substance is still a pure substance as long as the chemical composition of all phases is the same.

    A mixture of liquid air and gaseous air is not a pure substance. The different components in
    air condense at different temperatures at a specified pressure, thus the mixture is no longer chemically homogeneous.

    #Phases of a Pure Substance

    The three principal phases are solid, liquid and gas.

    #Phase-Change Processes

    #Compressed Liquid and Saturated Liquid

    Consider a piston-cylinder device containing liquid at 20°C and 1 atm pressure.

    Description of state 1:

    • Water exists in the liquid phase — compressed liquid
    • As temperature rises, liquid water expands slightly
    • Specific volume increases and piston moves up

    Pressure in the cylinder remains constant at 1 atm during this process since it depends on the outside barometric pressure and the weight of the piston, both of which are constant.

    💡
    A liquid that is not about to vaporize is a compressed liquid.

    Description of state 2:

    • Temperature rises until it reaches 100°C
    • Any heat addition will cause some of the liquid to vaporize
    • A phase-change process from liquid to vapor is about to occur

    💡
    A liquid that is about to vaporize is a saturated liquid.

    #Saturated Vapor and Superheated Vapor

    Once boiling starts, the temperature stops rising until the liquid is completely vaporized. The temperature will remain constant during the process if the pressure is held constant.

    Description of state 3:

    • Large increase in volume and steady decline in liquid
    • Midway about the vaporization line, the cylinder contains equal amounts of liquid and vapor

    Description of state 4:

    • Vaporization process continues until the last drop of liquid is vaporized as heat is continually transferred into the system
    • Cylinder is filled with vapor on the borderline of liquid phase
    • Any heat loss from the vapor will result in condensation
    💡
    A vapor that is about to condense is a saturated vapor.

    Between states 2 and 4, the substance exists as a saturated liquid-vapor mixture since the liquid and vapor phases coexist in equilibrium.

    Description of state 5:

    • Phase-change process is completed — vapor phase
    • Any heat addition increases temperature and specific volume
    • Any heat loss does not result in condensation of the vapor as long as the temperature remains above 100°C (P=1 atm)
    💡
    A vapor that is not about to condense is a superheated vapor.

    #Saturation Temperature and Saturation Pressure

    At a given pressure, phase change occurs at the saturation temperature Tsat​.

    At a given temperature, phase change occurs at the saturation pressure Psat​.

    The amount of heat absorbed or released during a phase change is called the latent heat.

    During a phase change, pressure and temperature are dependent properties.

    A plot of Psat​ versus Tsat​ is called a liquid-vapor saturation curve.

    #Property Diagrams

    #T-v Diagram

    At higher pressures:

    • Specific volume of saturated liquid is larger
    • Specific volume of saturated vapor is smaller
    • Saturation line shrinks until it becomes a point — critical point

      Temperature, pressure and specific volume of a substance at the critical point are
      called critical temperature       Tcr​, critical pressure Pcr​ and critical specific volume vcr​.

    Description of critical point:

    • Saturated liquid and saturated vapor states are identical
    • No distinct phase-change processes at pressures above Pcr​
    • Above the critical temperature — superheated vapor
    • Below the critical temperature — compressed liquid

    Description of Tv diagram:

    • Saturated liquid line connects all the saturated liquid states
    • Saturated vapor line connects all the saturated vapor states
    • Both lines meet at the critical point and form a dome
    • Left of saturated liquid line — compressed liquid region
    • Right of saturated vapor line — superheated vapor region
    • Below the dome — saturated liquid-vapor mixture region

      The saturated liquid-vapor mixture region is also known as the wet region.

    #P-v Diagram

    Consider a piston-cylinder device that contains liquid water at 150°C and 1 MPa. Water at this state exists as a compressed liquid. Weights on top of the piston are removed one
    by one such that pressure inside decreases gradually. Water is allowed to exchange heat with the surroundings so its temperature remains constant.

    Description of process:

    • Decrease in pressure results in an increase in specific volume
    • Saturation pressure is reached for corresponding temperature
    • Water starts to boil — liquid-vapor region
    • Pressure and temperature remain constant in this region
    • Last drop of liquid is vaporized — superheated vapor region
    • Further decrease in pressure results in further increase in v

    The general shape of the Pv diagram of a pure substance is similar to the Tv diagram.

    #Extending the Diagrams to Include Solid Phase

    Both the Tv and Pv diagrams can be extended to include the solid phase.

    Under some conditions, all three phases of a pure substance coexist in equilibrium. On a Tv and Pv diagram, these triple-phase states form a triple line. On a PT diagram, the triple line appears as a point — triple point.

    A substance can pass from the solid to the vapor phase by either melting first into a liquid and subsequently evaporates, or it evaporates directly without melting first. Passing from the solid phase directly into the vapor phase is known as sublimation.

    #P-T Diagram

    Description of diagram:

    • Also known as the phase diagram
    • Vaporization line ends at the critical point since no distinction can
      be made between the liquid and vapor phases above the critical point

    #P-v-T Surface

    The state of a simple compressible substance is fixed by any two independent, intensive properties. Once the two appropriate substances are fixed, all other properties become dependent properties.

    A PT diagram is a projection of the surface on the P-T plane.

    A Tv diagram is bird’s-eye view of this surface.

    #Enthalpy — A Combination Property

    Enthalpy is a property associated with the second law of thermodynamics.

    💡
    H=U+PVˉ
    💡
    h=u+Pv

    #Saturated Liquid and Vapor States

    The subscript f is used to denote properties of a saturated liquid.
    The subscript g is used to denote properties of a saturated vapor.
    The subscript fg denotes the difference between values of the same property.

    The quantity hfg​ is the enthalpy of vaporization. It represents the amount of energy needed to vaporize a unit mass of saturated liquid at a given temperature or pressure.

    Quality is the ratio of the mass of vapor to the total mass of the mixture.

    💡
    x=mtotal​mvapor​​

    Vˉt​=Vˉf​+Vˉg​

    #Superheated Vapor

    For a given temperature, if P<Psat​, substance is in the superheated vapor phase.

    For a given pressure, if T>Tsat​, substance is in the superheated vapor phase.

    Compared to saturated vapor, superheated vapor is characterized by:

    • Lower pressures (P<Psat​ at a given T)
    • Higher temperatures (T>Tsat​ at a given P)
    • Higher specific volumes (v>vg​ at a given P or T)
    • Higher internal energies (u>ug​ at a given P or T)
    • Higher enthalpies (h>hg​ at a given P or T)

    #Example 1

    v1​=2.29 ft3/lbm

    vg@250​=1.8440 ft3/lbm

    Since v1​>vg​, it is superheated vapor.

    T1​=550 °F

    P2​=Psat@100​

    Psat@100​=0.95052 psia

    When a substance undergoes a process in a closed rigid tank, the specific volume
    remains constant, and the process appears as a vertical line in the     Pv diagram.

    #Compressed Liquid

    For a given temperature, if P>Psat​, substance is in the compressed liquid phase.

    For a given pressure, if T<Tsat​, substance is in the subcooled region.

    In general, a compressed liquid is characterized by:

    • Higher pressures (P>Psat​ at a given T)
    • Lower temperatures (T<Tsat​ at a given P)
    • Lower specific volumes (v<vf​ at a given P or T)
    • Lower internal energies (u<uf​ at a given P or T)
    • Lower enthalpies (h<hf​ at a given P or T)

    In the absence of compressed liquid data, a general approximation is to treat compressed liquid as saturated liquid at the given temperature. This is because the compressed liquid properties depend on temperature much more strongly than they do on pressure.

    usat. liq.​=334.97 kJ/kg

    Error=333.82334.97−333.82​

    Error=0.34%

    #Ideal-Gas Equation of State

    Any equation that relates the pressure, temperature and specific volume of a substance is called an equation of state. The simplest and best-known equation of state for substances in the gas phase is the ideal-gas equation.

    PVˉ=nRT

    PVˉ=Mm​RT

    💡
    PVˉ=mRspecific​T

    PmVˉ​=MR​T

    Pv=MR​T

    💡
    Pv=Rspecific​T

    The gas constant Rspecific​ is different for each gas, unlike the universal gas constant R.

    #Compressibility Factor

    Gases deviate from the ideal-gas behavior significantly at states near the saturation region
    and the critical point. This deviation can be accounted for with the compressibility factor Z.

    💡
    Pv=ZRspecific​T

    Z=Rspecific​TPv​

    Z=videal​vactual​​

    Gases behave the same at temperatures and pressures normalized with respect to their
    critical temperatures and pressures. The Z factor for all gases is approximately the same
    at the same reduced pressure and temperature.

    A gas is an ideal gas if Pr​<0.01 or TR​>2.

    💡
    PR​=Pcr​P​
    💡
    TR​=Tcr​T​
    💡
    vR​=(RTcr​)/Pcr​vactual​​

    PR​ is called the reduced pressure and TR​ is called the reduced temperature.

    Pcr​=4.059 MPa

    Tcr​=374.2 K

    TR​=374.250+273.15​=0.864
