    General Energy Analysis

    #Energy Transfer by Heat

    Energy can cross the boundary of a closed system in the form of heat and work.

    The amount of heat transferred during a process between two states is denoted by Q.

    Heat is defined as the form of energy that is transferred between two systems by virtue of a temperature difference.

    A process in which there is no heat transfer is called an adiabatic process. The energy content and temperature
    of the system can still be changed by other means.

    #Energy Transfer by Work

    Work is an energy interaction between a system and its surroundings.

    The work done per unit time, also known as power, is denoted by W˙.

    Sign convention for heat and work:

    • Heat transfer into a system is positive
    • Heat transfer out of a system is negative
    • Work done by a system is positive
    • Work done on a system is negative

    #Mechanical Forms of Work

    In mechanics, work done by a constant force F on a body displaced a distance s is,

    💡
    W=F⋅s=Fs cosθ

    #Shaft Work

    Work done of a shaft is proportional to the torque applied and the number of revolutions of the shaft.

    T=Fr

    s=(2πr)n

    Wshaft​=2πnT

    💡
    W˙shaft​=2πn˙T

    n˙ represents the number of revolutions per unit time.

    #Example

    W˙shaft​=2π(604000​)(200)=83.8 kW

    #Spring Work

    For linear elastic springs, the displacement is proportional to the force applied.

    💡
    F=kx
    💡
    Wspring​=21​k(xf​2−xi​2)

    F=kx

    (P2​−P1​)A=kAV2​−V1​​

    P2​=P1​+kA2V2​−V1​​

    W=∫12​ P dV

    W=∫12​ P1​+kA2V2​−V1​​ dV

    W=ΔVP1​(V2​−V1​)​​+Wspring​2A2k(V2​−V1​)2​​​

    Factoring and simplifying the above equation,

    W=(V2​−V1​)[P1​+2A2k(V2​−V1​)​]

    W=(V2​−V1​)[P1​+2P2​−P1​​]

    W=2P2​+P1​​(V2​−V1​)

    Work done by expansion of gas in a piston-cylinder device against a spring is,

    💡
    W=2P2​+P1​​(V2​−V1​)=Pavg​ΔV

    Total work done is due to a change in volume during the expansion and the work done against the spring as the gas expands and compresses the spring.

    #Elastic Solid Bar

    Under the action of a force, solids contract or elongate. When the force is lifted, they return to their original
    length, as long as the force is within the elastic range.

    σ=AF​

    Welastic​=∫12​ F dx

    💡
    Welastic​=∫12​ σA dx

    #Stretching of a Liquid Film

    Consider a liquid film such as a soap film suspended on a wire frame. It will take some force to stretch this film by the movable portion of the frame. This force is called the surface tension σs​ (N/m).

    💡
    Wsurface​=∫12​ σs​ dA
    where dA=2b dx

    The factor 2 is due to the fact that the film has two surfaces in contact with air.

    #Work Done to Raise or to Accelerate a Body

    The work transfer needed to raise a body is equal to the change in the potential energy of the body, and the work transfer needed to accelerate a body is equal to the change in the kinetic energy of the body.

    💡
    Wgrav​=mg(z2​−z1​)
    💡
    WK​=21​m(V2​2−V1​2)

    #Non-Mechanical Forms of Work

    Non-mechanical forms of work can be treated in a similar manner as mechanical work by identifying a generalized force F acting in the direction of a generalized displacement x.

    #Electrical Work

    The generalized force is the voltage V and the generalized displacement is the electrical charge.

    When N coulombs of electrical charge move through
    a potential difference V, the electrical work done is,

    W=VN

    💡
    W˙electrical​=VI

    #First Law of Thermodynamics

    The first law of thermodynamics states that energy can be neither created nor destroyed during a process; it can only change forms. Therefore, every bit of energy should be accounted for.

    For all adiabatic processes, the net work done is the same regardless of the nature of the closed system and the details of the process.

    The change in total energy of a system during a process is equal to the net energy transfer into
    or from the system.

    Energy change is the difference between the initial state and final state of the system.

    💡
    ΔEsys​=Ein​−Eout​=Ef​−Ei​

    #Energy Change of a System

    The change in total energy can be expressed in terms of the different forms of energy.

    💡
    ΔEsys​=ΔU+ΔKE+ΔPE
    💡
    ΔEsys​=(U2​−U1​)+21​m(V2​2−V1​2)+mg(z2​−z1​)

    For stationary systems, changes in kinetic and potential energies are zero, and
    the total energy change is due to a change in internal energy,     ΔE=ΔU.

    For a closed system undergoing a cycle, the initial and final states are identical.

    💡
    Ein​=Eout​Ef​=Ei​

    #Mechanisms of Energy Transfer

    Energy can be transferred to and from a system in the form of heat, work and mass flow.

    For a closed system or a system with a fixed mass, energy is transferred via heat and work.

    💡
    ΔEsys​=(Qin​−Qout​)+(Win​−Wout​)+(Emin​​−Emout​​)
    💡
    ΔEsys​=Q−W+ΔEm​

    Heat transferred to the system is positive, thus Q=Qin​−Qout​.
    Work done by the system is positive, thus W=Wout​−Win​.

    Energy balance for any system undergoing any process can be expressed in rate form.

    💡
    dtdEsys​​=E˙in​−E˙out​

    Energy balance can also be expressed on a per unit mass basis.

    💡
    Δesys​=ein​−eout​

    Description of heat transfer Q:

    • Heat transfer into a system increases the internal energy of the system
    • Heat transfer from a system decreases it since energy is transferred out
    • Heat comes from the energy of the molecules in the system
    • Caused by a temperature difference

    Description of work transfer W:

    • Work transfer into a system increases the energy of the system
    • Work transfer from a system decreases it since energy is transferred out

    Description of mass flow m:

    • Mass carries energy with it
    • Mass entering a system increases the energy of the system
    • Mass leaving a system decreases it as the departing mass takes out energy with it

    #Mechanisms of Heat Transfer

    #Conduction

    Conduction is the transfer of energy from the more energetic particles of a substance to the adjacent less energetic ones as a result of interactions between the particles.

    Fourier’s law of heat conduction is given by,

    💡
    Q˙​cond​=−kAdxdT​

    k is the thermal conductivity of the material, which is a measure of the ability of a material to conduct heat.

    Heat is conducted in the direction of decreasing temperature — negative temperature gradient.

    #Convection

    Convection is the mode of energy transfer between a solid surface and the adjacent fluid that is in motion.

    Newton’s law of cooling is given by,

    💡
    Q˙​conv​=hA(Tsurface​−Tfluid​)

    h is the convection heat transfer coefficient and is dependent on surface geometry, nature of fluid motion, properties of the fluid, and the bulk fluid velocity.

    Forced convection occurs if the fluid is forced to flow in a tube or over a surface by external means.

    Natural convection occurs if the fluid motion is due to buoyancy forces induced by density differences due to variation of temperature in the fluid.

    Heat transfer processes that involve change of phase of a fluid are also considered to be convection since fluid motion is induced — rise of vapor bubbles during boiling.

    #Radiation

    Radiation is the energy emitted by matter in the form of electromagnetic waves.
    The transfer of energy by radiation does not require the presence of a medium.

    The Stefan-Boltzmann law is given by,

    💡
    Q˙​emit,max​=σATsurface​4

    The above equation is the maximum rate of radiation that can be emitted from a surface.

    The Stefan-Boltzmann constant is,

    💡
    σ=5.67×10−8 W/m2⋅K4

    The idealized surface that emits radiation at this maximum rate is called a blackbody, and the radiation emitted by a blackbody is called blackbody radiation. The radiation emitted
    by all real surfaces is less than the radiation emitted by a blackbody.

    💡
    Q˙​emit​=εσATs​4

    ε is the emissivity of the surface. The emissivity, whose value is in the range 0≤ε≤1,
    is a measure of how closely a surface approximates a blackbody for which     ε=1.

    In the special case of a relatively small surface of emissivity ε and surface area A
    at absolute temperature Ts​ that is completely enclosed by a much larger surface at
    absolute temperature Tsurr​ separated by a gas that does not intervene with radiation.

    Net rate of radiation heat transfer is given by,

    💡
    Q˙​rad​=εσA(Ts​4−Tsurr​4)

    In this special case, the emissivity and the surface area of the surrounding surface do not have any effect on the net radiation heat transfer.
