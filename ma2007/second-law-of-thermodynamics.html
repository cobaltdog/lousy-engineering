    Second Law of Thermodynamics

    Processes occur in a certain direction and energy has quality as well as quantity.

    #Heat Engines

    Work can be converted to heat directly and completely, but converting heat to work requires the use of special devices known as heat engines.

    Description of heat engines:

    • Receive heat from a high-temperature source
    • Convert part of this heat into work
    • Reject remaining waste heat to a low-temperature sink
    • Operate on a cycle

    Performance of a heat engine is measured by its thermal efficiency ηth​.

    💡
    ηth​=Qin​Wnet​​=Qin​Qin​−Qout​​=1−Qin​Qout​​

    Cyclic devices operate between a high-temperature
    reservoir at temperature TH​ and a low-temperature reservoir at temperature TL​.

    💡
    ηth​=1−QH​QL​​

    QH​ and QL​ are defined as the magnitude of heat transfer between the heat engine and the different reservoirs.

    #Refrigerators

    The objective of a refrigerator is to maintain
    the refrigerated space at a low temperature
    by removing heat QL​ from it.

    Coefficient of performance of a refrigerator is,

    💡
    COPR​=Wnet​QL​​=QH​−QL​QL​​=QL​QH​​−11​

    The amount of heat removed from the refrigerated
    space can be greater than the amount of work input.

    #Heat Pumps

    The objective of a heat pump is to maintain a heated
    space at a high temperature by absorbing heat from
    a low-temperature source, and supplying it to a high-temperature source.

    Coefficient of performance of a heat pump is,

    💡
    COPHP​=Wnet​QH​​=QH​−QL​QH​​=1−QH​QL​​1​

    Comparing the two coefficients of performance,

    COPHP​=QH​−QL​QL​​+1

    COPHP​=QH​−QL​QL​+(QH​−QL​)​

    COPHP​=QH​−QL​QH​​

    💡
    COPHP​=COPR​+1

    #Second Law of Thermodynamics

    #Kelvin-Planck Statement

    A heat engine must exchange heat with a low-temperature sink as well as a high-temperature source to keep operating. That is, no heat engine can convert all the heat it receives into
    useful work. This limitation applies to both idealized and actual heat engines.

    To put it simply, no heat engine can have a thermal efficiency of 100%.

    #Clausius Statement

    It is impossible to construct a device that operates in a cycle and produces no effect other than the transfer of heat from a low-temperature reservoir to a high-temperature reservoir.

    In addition to the transfer of heat from a colder body to a warmer one, there must be the consumption of some energy in the form of work. Thus, a refrigerator cannot operate
    unless it’s fed by an external power source.

    #Carnot Cycle and Carnot Principles

    The Carnot cycle composes of four reversible processes — two isothermal and two adiabatic.

    Isothermal expansion (Process 1 → 2):

    • Gas expands and does work
    • Heat is transferred from reservoir to gas
    • Gas temperature is kept constant at TH​

    QH​ is the total heat transferred to the gas.

    Adiabatic expansion (Process 2 → 3):

    • Gas continues to expand and do work
    • Gas temperature drops from TH​ to TL​

    Isothermal compression (Process 3 → 4):

    • Work is done on the gas to compress it
    • Heat is transferred from gas to sink
    • Gas temperature is kept constant at TL​

    QL​ is the total heat rejected from the gas.

    Adiabatic compression (Process 4 → 1):

    • Gas is further compressed adiabatically
    • Gas temperature rises from TL​ to TH​

    P-V diagram of the Carnot cycle:

    • Area under curve 1-2-3 is work done by the gas during expansion part of the cycle
    • Area under curve 3-4-1 is work done on the gas during compression part of the cycle
    • Area enclosed by path is net work done

    P-V diagram of the reversed Carnot cycle:

    • QL​ absorbed from low-temperature sink
    • QH​ rejected to high-temperature reservoir
    • Work input Wnet​ is required to accomplish it

    #Carnot Principles

    The Carnot principles are:

    1. The efficiency of an irreversible heat engine is always less than that of a reversible one operating between the same reservoirs.
    2. The efficiencies of all reversible heat engines operating between the same reservoirs are the same.

    No reversible heat engine can be more efficient than another reversible heat engine, regardless of how the cycle is completed or the working fluid used.

    #Thermodynamic Temperature Scale

    Efficiency of a reversible heat engine is independent of the working fluid, the way the
    cycle is executed and the type of reversible engine used. Since energy reservoirs are characterized by their temperatures, the thermal efficiency of reversible heat engines
    is a function of the reservoir temperatures only.

    ηth​=g(TH​,TL​)

    QL​QH​​=f(TH​,TL​)

    Amount of heat rejected by engines B and C must be the same since heat input to engine C is the same as the combined engines A and B.

    Q3​Q1​​=Q2​Q1​​⋅Q3​Q2​​

    Q3​Q1​​=f(T1​,T2​)⋅f(T2​,T3​)

    Q3​Q1​​=ϕ(T2​)ϕ(T1​)​⋅ϕ(T3​)ϕ(T2​)​

    Q3​Q1​​=ϕ(T3​)ϕ(T1​)​

    💡
    (QL​QH​​)rev​=TL​TH​​

    #Carnot Heat Engine

    The thermal efficiency of any heat engine, reversible or irreversible is ηth​=1−QH​QL​​.

    For reversible heat engines, the heat transfer ratio can be replaced with the ratio of the absolute temperatures of the two reservoirs. Thus, the efficiency of any reversible heat engine becomes,

    💡
    ηth,rev​=1−TH​TL​​

    whereby TH​ and TL​ are absolute temperatures in K

    The above equation is the Carnot efficiency — the highest efficiency of a heat engine operating between two thermal energy reservoirs at temperatures TH​ and TL​. All irreversible heat engines operating between these same temperature limits have lower efficiencies.
