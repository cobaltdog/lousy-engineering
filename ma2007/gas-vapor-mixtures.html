    Gas-Vapor Mixtures

    The term vapor implies a gaseous state that is close to the saturation region of a substance.

    #Dry Air and Atmospheric Air

    Dry air refers to air that contains no water vapor. Atmospheric air refers to air that contains some
    water vapor (moisture).

    Between −10°C and 50°C, in air-conditioning applications, dry air can be treated as an ideal
    gas with a constant cp​ with negligible error.

    Enthalpy change of dry air can be obtained from,

    💡
    Δhdry air​=cp​ΔT

    At 50°C, the Psat​ of water is 12.3 kPa. At pressures below this, water vapor, including saturated vapor, can be treated as an ideal gas with negligible error.

    Atmopheric air can be treated as an ideal gas mixture.

    💡
    P=Pa​+Pv​

    Pa​ is the partial pressure of dry air
    Pv​ is the partial pressure of water vapor

    Since water vapor is an ideal gas, the enthalpy of water vapor in air can be taken to be equal to the enthalpy of saturated vapor at the same temperature.

    💡
    hv​(T)≈hg​(T)

    The partial pressure of water vapor is usually referred to as vapor pressure — the pressure
    water vapor would exert if it existed alone at the temperature and volume of atmospheric air.

    #Specific and Relative Humidity of Air

    The absolute or specific humidity of air is,

    ω=ma​mv​​

    ω=Pa​V/Ra​TPv​V/Rv​T​

    ω=Pa​Rv​Pv​Ra​​

    ω=Pa​Pv​​(0.46150.287​)

    💡
    ω=0.622Pa​Pv​​=P−Pv​0.622Pv​​

    where P is the total pressure of the mixture

    By definition, dry air contains no water vapor, and thus its specific humidity is zero. As more moisture is added, the specific humidity will keep increasing until the air can hold no more moisture. At this point, the air is said to be saturated with moisture. Any moisture introduced
    to saturated air will condense.

    If the air is saturated with moisture,

    💡
    ωsat​=P−Pg​0.622Pg​​

    where Pg​ is the saturation pressure at the given temperature

    Relative humidity is the ratio of the amount of
    moisture the air holds mv​ to the maximum amount
    of moisture the air can hold at that temperature mg​.

    💡
    ϕ=mg​mv​​=Pg​Pv​​

    Substituting Pv​=ϕPg​ into specific humidity,

    💡
    ω=P−ϕPg​0.622ϕPg​​

    The total enthalpy of atmospheric air is the sum of the enthalpies of dry air and water vapor.

    💡
    H=Ha​+Hv​=ma​ha​+mv​hv​
    💡
    h=ha​+ωhv​≈ha​+ωhg​

    Note that h is the enthalpy of moist air per unit mass of dry air, kg H2​O/kg dry air.

    #Dew-Point Temperature

    Dew-point temperature Tdp​ is defined as the temperature at which condensation begins
    when the air is cooled at constant pressure. At this point, air is saturated, and its relative humidity is 100%.

    Tdp​ is the saturation temperature of water corresponding to the vapor pressure Pv​.

    💡
    Tdp​=Tsat@Pv​​

    As the air cools at constant pressure, vapor in the
    air undergoes an isobaric process until it reaches the saturated vapor line (state 2). As temperature drops below     Tdp​, vapor condenses out and the amount of vapor decreases, which results in a decrease in Pv​.

    #Adiabatic Saturation and
    Wet-Bulb Temperature

    A way to determine the absolute or relative humidity is through an adiabatic saturation process. The system consists of a long insulated channel that contains a pool of water.

    Description of system:

    • Steady stream of unsaturated air that has
      a specific humidity of ω1​ passes through
    • Some water evaporates and mixes with the
      airstream and increases its moisture content
    • Temperature of airstream decreases as energy from airstream is used to vaporize the water
    • Airstream exits as saturated air at T2​, which is the adiabatic saturation temperature, if the channel is long enough

    If water is supplied at the rate of evaporation at T2​, the adiabatic saturation process can be analyzed as
    a steady-flow process.

    Using the conservation of mass for open systems,

    m˙a1​​=m˙a2​​=m˙a​ (Mass flow rate of dry air remains constant)

    m˙v1​​+m˙f​=m˙v2​​ (Mass flow rate of vapor increases due to evaporation of water)

    Using the specific humidity equation,

    m˙a​ω1​+m˙f​=m˙a​ω2​

    m˙f​=m˙a​(ω2​−ω1​)

    Note that ω=ma​mv​​.

    Using the first law of thermodynamics,

    Q˙​−W˙=m˙Δh

    m˙a​ha1​+m˙v​hv1​+m˙f​hf2​=m˙a​ha2​+m˙v​hv2​

    m˙a​(ha1​+ω1​hv1​)+m˙a​(ω2​−ω1​)hf2​=m˙a​(ha2​+ω2​hv2​)

    ha1​+ω1​hg1​+hf2​(ω2​−ω1​)=ha2​+ω2​hg2​

    ha2​−ha1​+ω2​(hg2​−hf2​)=ω1​(hg1​−hf2​)

    💡
    ω1​=hg1​−hf2​cp​(T2​−T1​)+ω2​(hg2​−hf2​)​

    where hg1​, hg2​, and hf2​ are the enthalpy values (hg​ or hf​) at the given temperature

    Since the air exits with a relative humidity ϕ=1,

    💡
    ω2​=P2​−Pg2​0.622Pg2​​

    where Pg2​ is the saturation temperature at T2​

    The adiabatic saturation process requires a long channel or spray mechanism to achieve saturation conditions at the exit.

    A more practical approach is to use a thermometer whose bulb is covered with a cotton wick saturated with water and blow air over the wick. Temperature measured in this manner is called wet-bulb temperature Twb​.

    #Psychrometric Chart

    Description of chart:

    • Horizontal axis represent dry-bulb temperature
    • Vertical axis represent specific humidity
    • Curve represents 100% relative humidity

    Other relative humidity curves have the same
    general shape. For saturated air, dry-bulb, wet-
    bulb, and dew-point temperatures are identical.

    #Air-Conditioning Processes

    Maintaining a living space or an industrial facility at
    the desired temperature and humidity requires air-conditioning processes.

    Most air-conditioning processes can be treated
    as steady-flow processes. Neglecting kinetic and
    potential energy changes, the energy balance is,

    💡
    Q˙​in​+W˙in​+m˙h=Q˙​out​+W˙out​+m˙h

    Work input usually consists of fan work, which is
    small relative to the other terms in the equation.

    #Simple Heating and Cooling

    The amount of moisture in the air remains constant during this process. Thus, specific humidity ω also remains constant.

    Relative humidity ϕ decreases during a heating process due to increased moisture capacity of
    the air at higher temperatures, and vice versa.

    Since no humidification/dehumidification occurs,

    m˙a1​​=m˙a2​​=m˙a​ (for dry air)

    ω1​=ω2​ (for water vapor)

    Neglecting any fan work that may be present,

    💡
    Q˙​=m˙a​(h2​−h1​)

    The enthalpies are per unit mass of dry air.

    #Heating with Humidification

    Heated air can be humidified by passing it first through a heating section and then through a humidifying section.

    If steam is used for humidifying, it will result in additional heating, T3​>T2​. If a water spray is
    used, part of the latent heat of vaporization
    comes from the air, resulting in cooling of
    the heated airstream, then T3​<T2​.

    When a water spray is used, air should be heated to a higher temperature in the heating section to make up for the cooling effect during the humidification.

    #Example

    Properties of air at room temperature:

    Ra​=0.287 kJ/kg⋅K

    cp​=1.005 kJ/kg⋅K

    #Part (a)

    Mass balance for heating section:

    m˙a1​​=m˙a2​​=m˙a​

    ω1​=ω2​

    Energy balance for heating section:

    Q˙​−W˙=m˙a​Δh

    Q˙​in​=m˙a​(h2​−h1​)

    ϕ1​=Pg1​​Pv1​​​ where Pg1​​=Psat@T1​​=1.2281 kPa

    Pv1​​=0.3(1.2281)=0.368 kPa

    P1​=Pa1​​+Pv1​​

    Pa1​​=100−0.368

    Pa1​​=99.632 kPa

    Pa1​​va1​​=Ra​T1​

    va1​​=99.6320.287(10+273)​

    va1​​=0.815 m3/kg (dry air)

    m˙a​=va1​​V˙a1​​​=0.81545​=55.2 kg/min (dry air)

    ω1​=P1​−Pv1​​0.622Pv1​​​

    ω1​=99.6320.622(0.368)​=0.0023 kg H2​O/kg dry air

    h1​=ha1​​+ω1​hv1​​=cp​T1​+ω1​hg1​​=cp​T1​+ω1​hsat@T1​​

    h1​=1.005(10+273)+0.0023(2519.2)=15.8 kJ/kg dry air

    h2​=ha2​​+ω2​hv2​​=cp​T2​+ω2​hg2​​=cp​T2​+ω2​hsat@T2​​

    h2​=1.005(22+273)+0.0023(2541.0)=28.0 kJ/kg dry air

    Q˙​in​=55.2(28.0−15.8)=673 kJ/min

    #Part (b)

    Mass balance for water in humidifying section:

    m˙v2​​+m˙w​=m˙v3​​

    m˙a2​​ω2​+m˙w​=m˙a3​​ω3​

    m˙w​=m˙a​(ω3​−ω2​)

    Mass flow rate of dry air remains constant throughout.

    ω3​=P3​−Pv3​​0.622Pv3​​​=P3​−ϕ3​Pg3​​0.622ϕ3​Pg3​​​

    ω3​=100−0.6(3.1698)0.622(0.6)(3.1698)​=0.01206 kg H2​O/kg dry air

    m˙w​=55.2(0.01206−0.0023)=0.539 kg/min

    #Cooling with Dehumidification

    When relative humidity reaches undesirably high levels, it may be necessary to remove some moisture from the air. This requires cooling the air below its dew-point temperature.

    Instead of heating coils, hot, moist air is passed through cooling coils that reduces
    its temperature, and thus increasing relative humidity at constant specific humidity.
    If the cooling section is sufficiently long, air reaches its dew point.

    #Example

    Mass balance for dry air:

    m˙a1​​=m˙a2​​=m˙a​

    Mass balance for water vapor:

    m˙v1​​=m˙v2​​+m˙w​

    m˙a1​​ω1​=m˙a2​​ω2​+m˙w​

    m˙w​=m˙a​(ω1​−ω2​)

    Energy balance:

    m˙a1​​h1​=Q˙​out​+m˙a2​​h2​+m˙w​hw​

    Q˙​out​=m˙a​(h1​−h2​)−m˙w​hw​

    Q˙​out​=m˙a​(h1​−h2​)−m˙w​hf​

    From psychrometric chart,

    h1​=85.4 kJ/kg dry air

    ω1​=0.0216 kg H2​O/kg dry air

    v1​=0.889 m3/kg dry air

    h2​=39.3 kJ/kg dry air

    ω2​=0.01 kg H2​O/kg dry air

    m˙a​=v1​V˙​=0.88910​=11.25 kg/min

    m˙w​=11.25(0.0216−0.01)=0.131 kg/min

    Q˙​out​=11.25(85.4−39.3)−0.131(58.8)

    Q˙​out​=511 kJ/min

    #Evaporative Cooling

    As water evaporates, the latent heat of vaporization is absorbed from the water body and the surrounding air. Thus, both the water and the air are cooled during this process.

    Hot, dry air is sprayed with liquid water, where part of the water evaporates by absorbing heat from the airstream. Temperature of the airstream decreases and its humidity increases.

    In the limiting case, the air leaves as saturated air (100% relative humidity). This is the lowest temperature that can be achieved.

    The process follows a line of constant wet-bulb temperature on the psychrometric chart.

    💡
    Twb​≈constant
    💡
    h≈constant

    The constant wet-bulb temperature lines almost coincide with the constant enthalpy lines, thus the enthalpy can be assumed to be constant as well.

    #Adiabatic Mixing of Airstreams

    Many air-conditioning applications require the mixing of two airstreams, such as hospitals which require conditioned air to be mixed with a certain fraction of fresh outside air.

    Mass balance for dry air and water vapor:

    m˙a1​​+m˙a2​​=m˙a3​​

    m˙a1​​ω1​+m˙a2​​ω2​=m˙a3​​ω3​

    Energy balance:
    m˙a1​​h1​+m˙a2​​h2​=m˙a3​​h3​

    Eliminating m˙a3​​ from the above equations,

    💡
    m˙a2​​m˙a1​​​=ω3​−ω1​ω2​−ω3​​=h3​−h1​h2​−h3​​

    The ratio of the distance 2-3 and 3-1 is equal
    to the ratio of mass flow rates     m˙a1​​ and m˙a2​​.

    When two airstreams at two different states are mixed adiabatically, the state of the mixture lies
    on the straight line connecting states 1 and 2 on
    the psychrometric chart.

    #Example

    From the psychrometric table,

    h1​=39.4 kJ/kg dry air

    ω1​=0.010 kg H2​O/kg dry air

    v1​=0.826 m3/kg dry air

    h2​=79.0 kJ/kg dry air

    ω2​=0.0182 kg H2​O/kg dry air

    v2​=0.889 m3 kg/dry air

    m˙a1​​=v1​V˙1​​=0.82650​=60.5 kg/min

    m˙a2​​=v2​V˙2​​=0.88920​=22.5 kg/min

    m˙a3​​=60.5+22.5=83 kg/min

    m˙a2​​m˙a1​​​=ω3​−ω1​ω2​−ω3​​=h3​−h1​h2​−h3​​

    22.560.5​=ω3​−0.0100.0182−ω3​​=h3​−0.01079.0−h3​​

    ω3​=0.0122 kg H2​O/kg dry air

    h3​=50.1 kJ/kg dry air

    From the psychrometric chart,

    ϕ3​=89%

    T3​=19.0°C

    v3​=0.844 m3/kg dry air

    V˙3​=m˙a3​​v3​

    V˙3​=83(0.844)=70.1 m3/min

    #Wet Cooling Towers

    A wet cooling tower is a semi-enclosed evaporative cooler. Air is drawn into the tower from the bottom and leaves through the top.

    Warm water is sprayed into the airstream, and
    a small fraction of it evaporates and cools the remaining water. The temperature and moisture
    content of the air increase during this process.

    Makeup water must be added to replace the water
    lost by evaporation and air draft. When the air circulation is provided by a fan, it is classified
    as a forced-draft cooling tower.

    #Example

    Mass balance for dry air:

    m˙a1​​=m˙a2​​=m˙a​

    Mass balance for water:

    m˙3​+m˙a1​​ω1​=m˙4​+m˙a2​​ω2​

    m˙3​−m˙4​=m˙a​(ω2​−ω1​)

    m˙makeup​=m˙a​(ω2​−ω1​)

    Energy balance:

    ∑m˙i​hi​=∑m˙e​he​

    m˙3​h3​+m˙a1​​h1​=m˙4​h4​+m˙a2​​h2​

    m˙a​(h2​−h1​)=m˙3​h3​−m˙4​h4​

    m˙a​(h2​−h1​)=m˙3​h3​−(m˙3​−m˙makeup​)h4​

    m˙a​(h2​−h1​)=m˙3​(h3​−h4​)+m˙a​(ω2​−ω1​)h4​

    m˙a​=(h2​−h1​)−(ω2​−ω1​)h4​m˙3​(h3​−h4​)​

    From the psychrometric chart,

    h1​=42.2 kJ/kg dry air

    ω1​=0.0087 kg H2​O/kg dry air

    v1​=0.842 m3/kg dry air

    h2​=100.0 kJ/kg dry air

    ω2​=0.0273 kg H2​O/kg dry air

    From Table A4,

    h3​=146.64 kJ/kg at 35°C

    h4​=92.28 kJ/kg at 22°C

    m˙a​=(100.0−42.2)−92.28(0.0273−0.0087)100(146.64−92.28)​=96.9 kg/s

    V˙1​=m˙a​v1​=96.9(0.842)=81.6 m3/s

    m˙makeup​=96.9(0.0273−0.0087)=1.80 kg/s
