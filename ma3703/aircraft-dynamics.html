    Aircraft Dynamics

    #Eigenvalue Method

    The linearized equations of motion are expressed in the form x˙=Ax+Bu.
    For dynamic stability analysis, the control inputs are taken as zero, u=0.

    The state-space form of longitudinal motion simplifies to,

    x˙=Ax

    Characteristic equation is given by the eigenvalue method.

    A1​λ4+B1​λ3+C1​λ2+D1​λ+E1​=0

    Solutions to the characteristic equation,

    λ1,2​=σspo​±jωspo​ (Short period oscillations)

    λ3,4​=σphu​±jωphu​ (Long period oscillations)

    #Longitudinal Dynamics

    Axial force equation for longitudinal motion,

    Δu˙=Xu​Δu+Xw​Δw+Xδe​​Δδe​+XδT​​ΔδT​−g(cosθ0​)Δθ

    Normal force equation for longitudinal motion,

    (1−Zw˙​)Δw˙=Zu​Δu+Zw​Δw+(Zq​+u0​)Δq                           +Zδe​​Δδe​+ZδT​​ΔδT​−g(sinθ0​)Δθ

    Pitching moment equation for longitudinal motion,

    Δq˙​=Mu​Δu+Mw​Δw+Mw˙​Δw˙+Mq​Δq+Mδe​​Δδe​+MδT​​ΔδT​

    #Long Period Approximation to EoM

    Phugoid (long period) oscillations:

    • Aircraft pitches up and climb, then pitches up and descends
    • Slows down as it goes “uphill” and speeds up as it goes “downhill”
    • Altitude and flight speed fluctuate sinusoidally around equilibrium point
    • Oscillation is lightly damped and aircraft angle of attack is nearly constant

    Assumptions:

    • Pitching moment equation Δq˙​=…  is neglected
    • Change in angle of attack Δα≈0 such that normal velocity Δw≈0
    • Normal force derivative Zq​≈0 such that initial aircraft velocity u0​≫Zq​
    • Aircraft is initially in level flight where θ0​=0 (small angle approximation)

    Axial force equation simplifies to,

    Δu˙=Xu​Δu+Xw​Δw+Xδe​​Δδe​+XδT​​ΔδT​−g(cosθ0​)Δθ

    Δu˙=Xu​Δu−Xw​(0)+0+0−g(1)Δθ

    💡

    Δu˙=Xu​Δu−gΔθ

    Normal force equation simplifies to,

    (1−Zw˙​)Δw˙=Zu​Δu+Zw​Δw+(Zq​+u0​)Δq                           +Zδe​​Δδe​+ZδT​​ΔδT​−g(sinθ0​)Δθ

    (1−Zw˙​)(0)=Zu​Δu+Zw​(0)+(0+u0​)Δq+0+0−g(0)Δθ

    0=Zu​Δu+u0​Δq

    0=Zu​Δu+u0​Δθ˙

    💡

    Δθ˙=−u0​Zu​​Δu

    Pitch rate Δq=Δθ˙.

    Expressing the equations as a matrix,

    💡

    {Δu˙Δθ˙​}=[Xu​−u0​Zu​​​−g0​]{ΔuΔθ​}

    Characteristic equation of the matrix,

    0=det​A−λI​​

    0=det​Xu​−λ−u0​Zu​​​−g−λ​​

    0=(Xu​−λ)(−λ)−u0​Zu​g​

    0=λ2−Xu​λ−u0​Zu​g​

    0=s2+(2ζωn​)s+ωn​2

    Natural frequency of oscillation,

    💡

    ωn,phu​=−u0​Zu​g​​

    Damping ratio of oscillation,

    💡

    ζphu​=−2ωnphu​​Xu​​

    Longitudinal stability derivatives,

    Xu​=−mu0​qS​(2CD​+CDu​​)=−mu0​qS​(2CD​)

    Zu​=−mu0​qS​(2CL​+CLu​​)=−mu0​qS​(2CL​)

    Note that CDu​​ and CLu​​ are negligible for low speed flights.

    Rewriting the stability derivatives,

    Xu​=−mu0​mg/CL​​(2CD​)=−u0​2g​LD​

    Zu​=−mu0​mg/CL​​(2CL​)=−u0​2g​

    For initial level flight condition,

    W=L

    mg=qSCL​

    qS=CL​mg​

    Natural frequency of oscillation,

    💡

    ωn,phu​=2​u0​g​

    Damping ratio of oscillation,

    💡

    ζphu​=2​1​L/D1​

    #Short Period Approximation to EoM

    Short period oscillations:

    • Rapid pitching up and down of aircraft about its CG
    • Oscillation is heavily damped due to aircraft’s high inertia
    • Resulting oscillation is an angle of attack variation with no change in speed

    Assumptions:

    • Axial force equation Δu˙=…  is neglected
    • Change in aircraft velocity Δu≈0
    • Zq​≪u0​ and Zw˙​≪1
    • Aircraft is initially in level flight where θ0​=0 (small angle approximation)

    Normal force equation simplifies to,

    (1−Zw˙​)Δw˙=Zu​Δu+Zw​Δw+(Zq​+u0​)Δq                           +Zδe​​Δδe​+ZδT​​ΔδT​−g(sinθ0​)Δθ

    (1−0)Δw˙=Zu​(0)+Zw​Δw+(0+u0​)Δq+0+0−g(0)Δθ

    💡

    Δw˙=Zw​Δw+u0​Δq

    Pitching moment equation simplifies to,

    Δq˙​=Mu​Δu+Mw​Δw+Mw˙​Δw˙+Mq​Δq+Mδe​​Δδe​+MδT​​ΔδT​

    Δq˙​=Mu​(0)+Mw​Δw+Mw˙​Δw˙+Mq​Δq+0+0

    💡

    Δq˙​=Mw​Δw+Mw˙​Δw˙+Mq​Δq

    Change in aircraft angle of attack,

    Δα≈u0​Δw​

    Δw=u0​Δα

    Δw˙=u0​Δα˙

    Substituting into normal force equation,

    💡

    Δα˙=u0​Zα​​Δα+Δq

    Substituting into pitching moment equation,

    Δq˙​=Mw​u0​Δα+Mw˙​u0​Δα˙+Mq​Δq

    Δq˙​=Mα​Δα+Mα˙​Δα˙+Mq​Δq

    Δq˙​=Mα​Δα+Mα˙​(u0​Zα​​Δα+Δq)+Mq​Δq

    💡

    Δq˙​=(Mα​+Mα˙​u0​Zα​​)Δα+(Mα˙​+Mq​)Δq

    Expressing the equations as a matrix,

    💡

    {Δα˙Δq˙​​}=[u0​Zα​​Mα​+Mα˙​u0​Zα​​​1Mα˙​+Mq​​]{ΔαΔq​}

    Characteristic equation of the matrix,

    0=det​A−λI​​

    0=det​u0​Zα​​−λMα​+Mα˙​u0​Zα​​​1Mα˙​+Mq​−λ​​

    0=(Mα˙​+Mq​−λ)(u0​Zα​​−λ)−(Mα​+Mα˙​u0​Zα​​)

    0=λ2−(Mα˙​+Mq​+u0​Zα​​)λ+(Mq​u0​Zα​​−Mα​)

    0=s2+(2ζωn​)s+ωn​2

    Natural frequency of oscillation,

    💡

    ωn,spo​=Mq​u0​Zα​​−Mα​​

    Damping ratio of oscillation,

    💡

    ζspo​=2ωnspo​​−(Mα˙​+Mq​+Zα​/u0​)​

    #1-DOF Approximation to Pure Pitching Motion

    Simplifications and substitutions:

    • Change in aircraft velocity Δu=0
    • Δα=Δθ=u0​Δw​
    • Δα˙=Δθ˙=Δq

    Assume that the aircraft’s CG is constrained to move along a straight line at
    a constant flight speed and is free to pitch about the yb​-axis as a rigid body.

    From Newton’s 2nd law,

    MCG​=Iyy​α=Iyy​θ¨

    ΔMCG​=Iyy​Δθ¨

    Using 1st-order Taylor series expansion,

    ΔM=ΔM(Δα, Δα˙, Δq, Δδe​)

    ΔM=∂α∂M​Δα+∂α˙∂M​Δα˙+∂q∂M​Δq+∂δe​∂M​Δδe​

    ΔM=∂α∂M​Δα+(∂α˙∂M​+∂q∂M​)Δα˙+∂δe​∂M​Δδe​

    Substituting into the equation of motion,

    ∂α∂M​Δα+(∂α˙∂M​+∂q∂M​)Δα˙+∂δe​∂M​Δδe​=Iyy​Δθ¨

    Iyy​1​∂α∂M​Δα+Iyy​1​(∂α˙∂M​+∂q∂M​)Δα˙+Iyy​1​∂δe​∂M​Δδe​=Δθ¨

    Mα​Δα+(Mα˙​+Mq​)Δα˙+Mδe​​Δδe​=Δα¨

    💡

    Δα¨−(Mα˙​+Mq​)Δα˙−Mα​Δα=Mδe​​Δδe​

    In dynamic stability analysis, the control input (elevator) is taken as zero.

    💡

    Δα¨−(Mα˙​+Mq​)Δα˙−Mα​Δα=0

    Characteristic equation,

    0=λ2−(Mα˙​+Mq​)λ−Mα​

    0=s2+(2ζωn​)s+ωn​2

    Natural frequency of motion,

    💡

    ωn,pitch​=−Mα​​

    Damping ratio of motion,

    💡

    ζpitch​=2ωnpitch​​−(Mα˙​+Mq​)​

    #Pitching Response to Step Elevator Input

    Response of a 2nd-order system to a step input is given by,

    x(t)=kF0​​[1−1−ζ2​1​e−ζωn​tsin(ωd​t+ϕ)]

    Response to step elevator input is then expressed as,

    Δα(t)=Δαtrim​[1−1−ζ2​1​e−ζωn​tsin(ωd​t+ϕ)]

    where desired change in trim angle of attack Δαtrim​=−Mα​Mδe​​​Δδe​

    #Directional-Lateral Dynamics

    Side force equation for lateral-directional motion,

    Δv˙=Yv​Δv+Yp​Δp+(Yr​−u0​)Δr+Yδr​​Δδr​+g(cosθ0​)Δϕ

    Rolling moment equation for lateral-directional motion,

    Δp˙​−Ixx​Ixz​​Δr˙=Lv​Δv+Lp​Δp+Lr​Δr+Lδa​​Δδa​+Lδr​​Δδr​

    Yawing moment equation for lateral-directional motion,

    Δr˙−Izz​Ixz​​Δp˙​=Nv​Δv+Np​Δp+Nr​Δr+Nδa​​Δδa​+Nδr​​Δδr​

    #Spiral Mode Approximation to EoM

    Assumptions:

    • Negligible roll rate Δp≈0
    • Negligible moments of inertia Ixx​Ixz​​≈0 and Izz​Ixz​​≈0

    Rolling moment equation simplifies to,

    Δp˙​−Ixx​Ixz​​Δr˙=Lv​Δv+Lp​Δp+Lr​Δr+Lδa​​Δδa​+Lδr​​Δδr​

    0−(0)Δr˙=Lv​Δv+Lp​(0)+Lr​Δr+0+0

    0=Lv​Δv+Lr​Δr

    💡

    0=Lβ​Δβ+Lr​Δr

    Note that sideslip angle is given by Δβ=u0​Δv​.

    Rearranging the rolling moment equation,

    Δβ=−Lβ​Lr​​Δr

    Yawing moment equation simplifies to,

    Δr˙−Izz​Ixz​​Δp˙​=Nv​Δv+Np​Δp+Nr​Δr+Nδa​​Δδa​+Nδr​​Δδr​

    Δr˙−(0)Δp˙​=Nv​Δv+Np​(0)+Nr​Δr+0+0

    Δr˙=Nv​Δv+Nr​Δr

    💡

    Δr˙=Nβ​Δβ+Nr​Δr

    Note that sideslip angle is given by Δβ=u0​Δv​.

    Substituting the expression for Δβ,

    Δr˙=Nβ​(−Lβ​Lr​​Δr)+Nr​Δr

    Δr˙=(Nr​−Lβ​Lr​​Nβ​)Δr

    Δr˙=(Lβ​Lβ​Nr​−Lr​Nβ​​)Δr

    0=Δr˙+Lβ​Lr​Nβ​−Lβ​Nr​​Δr

    Equation is a 1st-order homogeneous ODE.

    Characteristic equation of the ODE,

    0=λ+Lβ​Lr​Nβ​−Lβ​Nr​​

    λspiral​=−Lβ​Lr​Nβ​−Lβ​Nr​​

    Note that Lβ​ is negative.

    For a stable spiral mode,

    💡

    λspiral​=−Lβ​Lr​Nβ​−Lβ​Nr​​<0

    Time constant of spiral mode,

    💡

    τ=−λspiral​1​

    #Dutch Roll Approximation to EoM

    Assumptions:

    • Negligible rolling motion such that Δp=0 and Δϕ=0
    • Negligible moments of inertia Ixx​Ixz​​≈0 and Izz​Ixz​​≈0

    Side force equation simplifies to,

    Δv˙=Yv​Δv+Yp​Δp+(Yr​−u0​)Δr+Yδr​​Δδr​+g(cosθ0​)Δϕ

    Δv˙=Yv​Δv+Yp​(0)+(Yr​−u0​)Δr+0+g(cosθ0​)(0)

    💡

    Δv˙=Yv​Δv+(Yr​−u0​)Δr

    Yawing moment equation simplifies to,

    Δr˙−Izz​Ixz​​Δp˙​=Nv​Δv+Np​Δp+Nr​Δr+Nδa​​Δδa​+Nδr​​Δδr​

    Δr˙−(0)(0)=Nv​Δv+Np​(0)+Nr​Δr+0+0

    💡

    Δr˙=Nv​Δv+Nr​Δr

    Rewriting in terms of the sideslip angle,

    Δβ˙​=u0​Yβ​​Δβ−(1−u0​Yr​​)Δr

    Δr˙=Nβ​Δβ+Nr​Δr

    Expressing the equations as a matrix,

    💡

    {Δβ˙​Δr˙​}=[u0​Yβ​​Nβ​​u0​Yr​​−1Nr​​]{ΔβΔr​}

    Characteristic equation of the matrix,

    0=det​A−λI​​

    0=det​u0​Yβ​​−λNβ​​u0​Yr​​−1Nr​−λ​​

    0=(u0​Yβ​​−λ)(Nr​−λ)−Nβ​(u0​Yr​​−1)

    0=λ2−(u0​Yβ​​+Nr​)λ+(u0​Yβ​Nr​−Yr​Nβ​​+Nβ​)

    0=λ2−u0​Yβ​+u0​Nr​​λ+u0​Yβ​Nr​−Yr​Nβ​+u0​Nβ​​

    0=s2+(2ζωn​)s+ωn​2

    Natural frequency of Dutch roll,

    💡

    ωn,DR​=u0​Yβ​Nr​−Yr​Nβ​+u0​Nβ​​​

    Damping ratio of Dutch roll,

    💡

    ζDR​=−2ωn,DR​u0​Yβ​+u0​Nr​​

    #1-DOF Approximation to Pure Rolling Motion

    Approximation to rolling motion is expressed as,

    M=Iα=Iθ¨

    ΔL=Ixx​dtdΔp​

    ∂p∂L​Δp+∂δa​∂L​Δδa​=Ixx​dtdΔp​

    where rolling moment L is a function of
    roll rate     Δp and aileron deflection Δδa​

    Rearranging the above equation,

    dtdΔp​=Ixx​1​∂p∂L​Δp+Ixx​1​∂δa​∂L​Δδa​

    dtdΔp​=Lp​Δp+Lδa​​Δδa​

    dtdΔp​−Lp​Δp=Lδa​​Δδa​

    Equation is a 1st-order ODE.

    Characteristic equation of the ODE,

    λ−Lp​=0

    λroll​=Lp​

    Time constant of motion,

    τ=−Lp​1​

    Solution to the 1st-order ODE,

    💡

    Δp(t)=−Lp​Lδa​​​Δδa​(1−e−t/τ)

    Steady-state solution for roll rate as t→∞,

    💡

    Δpss​=−Lp​Lδa​​​Δδa​

    #1-DOF Approximation to Pure Yawing Motion

    Approximation to yawing motion is expressed as,

    M=Iα=Iθ¨

    N=Izz​dt2d2Δψ​

    ∂β∂N​Δβ+∂r∂N​Δr+∂δr​∂N​Δδr​=Izz​dt2d2Δψ​

    where yawing moment N is a function of
    sideslip angle     Δβ, yaw rate Δr and rudder deflection Δδr​

    Substituting the following relations,

    Δψ=−Δβ

    Δr=dtdΔψ​=−dtdΔβ​

    Equation for yawing motion becomes,

    dt2d2Δψ​=Izz​1​∂β∂N​Δβ+Izz​1​∂r∂N​Δr+Izz​1​∂δr​∂N​Δδr​

    dt2d2Δψ​=Nβ​Δβ+Nr​Δr+Nδr​​Δδr

    −dt2d2Δβ​=Nβ​Δβ−Nr​dtdΔβ​+Nδr​​Δδr​

    dt2d2Δβ​−Nr​dtdΔβ​+Nβ​Δβ=−Nδr​​Δδr​

    Equation is a 2st-order homogeneous ODE.

    Characteristic equation of the ODE,

    0=λ2−Nr​λ+Nβ​

    Natural frequency of motion,

    💡

    ωn,yaw​=Nβ​​

    Damping ratio of motion,

    💡

    ζyaw​=−2ωn,yaw​Nr​​

    Solution to the 2nd-order ODE,

    💡

    Δβ(t)=Δβtrim​[1−1−ζ2​e−ζωn​t​sin(ωd​t+ϕ)]

    where tanϕ=ζωn​ωd​​=ζ1−ζ2​​

    Desired change in trim sideslip angle,

    💡

    Δβtrim​=−Nβ​Nδr​​​Δδr​

    #Yaw Damper

    A yaw damper serves to damp out the rolling and yawing oscillations in Dutch roll.

    #With Yaw Rate Feedback

    • Corrective rudder deflection is proportional to yaw rate
    • Yaw rate gyro provides feedback on the yaw rate
    • Actuator applies the control force on the rudder
    💡

    (Δδr​)feedback​=kΔψ˙​

    Equation for yawing motion is expressed as,

    dt2d2Δβ​−Nr​dtdΔβ​+Nβ​Δβ=−Nδr​​Δδr​

    dt2d2Δψ​−Nr​dtdΔψ​+Nβ​Δψ=Nδr​​Δδr​

    Equation without yaw rate feedback.

    With yaw rate feedback,

    dt2d2Δψ​−Nr​dtdΔψ​+Nβ​Δψ=Nδr​​Δδr​+Nδr​​(Δδr​)feedback​

    dt2d2Δψ​−Nr​dtdΔψ​+Nβ​Δψ=Nδr​​Δδr​+Nδr​​(kdtdΔψ​)

    dt2d2Δψ​−(Nr​+kNδr​​)dtdΔψ​+Nβ​Δψ=Nδr​​Δδr​

    Equation is a 2st-order homogeneous ODE.

    Characteristic equation of the ODE,

    0=λ2−(Nr​+kNδr​​)λ+Nβ​

    Damping ratio of motion (with yaw damper),

    💡

    ζyaw damper​=−2ωn,yaw​Nr​+kNδr​​​

    The desired damping ratio can be obtained by a proper choice of k.

    #With Yaw Rate and Yaw Angle Feedbacks

    • Modify both natural frequency and damping ratio of the motion
    • Apply an additional feedback that is proportional to yaw angle
    💡

    (Δδr​)feedback​=k1​Δψ˙​+k2​Δψ

    With yaw rate and yaw feedback,

    dt2d2Δψ​−Nr​dtdΔψ​+Nβ​Δψ=Nδr​​Δδr​+Nδr​​(Δδr​)feedback​

    dt2d2Δψ​−Nr​dtdΔψ​+Nβ​Δψ=Nδr​​Δδr​+Nδr​​(k1​dtdΔψ​)+Nδr​​(k2​Δψ)

    dt2d2Δψ​−(Nr​+k1​Nδr​​)dtdΔψ​+(Nβ​−k2​Nδr​​)Δψ=Nδr​​Δδr​

    Characteristic equation of the ODE,

    0=λ2−(Nr​+k1​Nδr​​)λ+(Nβ​−k2​Nδr​​)

    Natural frequency of motion,

    💡

    ωn,yaw damper​=Nβ​−k2​Nδr​​​

    Damping ratio of motion,

    💡

    ζyaw damper​=−2ωn,yaw damper​Nr​+k1​Nδr​​​
