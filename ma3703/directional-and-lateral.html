    Directional & Lateral

    #Directional Static Stability

    Sideslip angle β is defined as,

    💡

    β≈uv​

    Assuming small angles <15°.

    • Arise due to sideways gust of wind or
      due to momentary rudder deflection
    • Increases drag considerably due to
      increase in frontal area of fuselage

    Positive β → starboard side of the aircraft.
    Negative     β → port side of the aircraft.

    Coefficient of yawing moment,

    💡

    Cn​=21​ρV2SbN​

    where N is the yawing moment

    Criterion for directional static stability,

    💡

    dβdCn​​=Cnβ​>0

    A positive yawing moment is required
    to decrease a positive sideslip angle     β.

    #Wing Contribution

    Contribution of wing to directional stability is small if the wing has no dihedral
    and does not have a significant sweep. Thus, its contribution can be ignored.

    For a sweptback wing (assume positive β):

    • Velocity normal to wing is higher on starboard side than port side
    • Starboard wing produces higher lift and drag than port side
    • Difference in drag creates a positive yawing moment
    • Positive yawing moment is stabilizing (decreases β)
    • Sweptback wing increases directional stability
    • Also gives rise to negative rolling moment

    For a dihedral wing (assume positive β):

    • Starboard wing has a positive normal component of sideslip velocity
    • Port side wing has a negative normal component of sideslip velocity
    • Starboard wing experiences an increase in angle of attack α
    • Port side wing experiences a decrease in angle of attack α
    • Starboard wing will generate more lift and thus more drag
    • Difference in drag creates a positive yawing moment
    • Wing dihedral increases directional stability
    • Also gives rise to negative rolling moment

    Effect of location of wing (assume positive β):

    • High wing results in starboard wing experiencing a higher angle of attack
    • Higher angle of attack in turn produces higher lift and higher drag
    • Difference in drag creates a positive yawing moment
    • High wing increases directional stability

    A low wing decreases directional stability as the starboard wing will experience a lower
    angle of attack, and thus produces lower lift and lower drag than the port side wing.
    This results in a negative yawing moment.

    #Wing-Fuselage Contribution

    Expression for wing-fuselage contribution,

    💡

    Cnβwf​​=−kn​kRI​SbSfs​lf​​

    where Sfs​ is the projected side area, and lf​ is the fuselage length

    Wing-fuselage contribution is destabilizing.

    • Empirical factors kn​ and kRI​ are always positive
    • Other terms in the equation are also always positive
    • Therefore Cnβwf​​ is always negative (recall the criterion)

    #Vertical Tail Contribution

    Local angle of attack of the fin,

    💡

    αvt​=β+σ

    where σ is the sidewash angle caused
    by the trailing vortices of the wing

    Due to sideslip, fin produces a lift force Lvt​
    that generates a restoring yawing moment.

    Resolving Lvt​ into a side force Yvt​,

    Yvt​=−Lvt​cos(β+σ)

    Yvt​=−Lvt​ (small angle approximation)

    Yvt​=−qvt​Svt​CLvt​​

    Yvt​=−qvt​Svt​(CLαvt​​​αvt​)

    Yvt​=−qvt​Svt​CLαvt​​​(β+σ)

    where qvt​=21​ρVvt​2 and Svt​ is fin area

    Yawing moment produced by the fin,

    💡

    Nvt​=−lvt​Yvt​=lvt​qvt​Svt​CLαvt​​​(β+σ)

    where lvt​ is the distance of the fin ac from the aircraft CG

    Nondimensionalizing the above equation,

    Cnvt​​=qw​SbNvt​​

    Cnvt​​=qw​qvt​​⋅Sblvt​Svt​​⋅CLαvt​​​(β+σ)

    💡

    Cnvt​​=ηvt​Vvt​CLαvt​​​(β+σ)

    where ηvt​ is the vertical tail efficiency, and Vvt​ is the vertical tail volume ratio

    Differentiating the above equation,

    💡

    Cnβvt​​=dβdCnvt​​​=Vvt​ηvt​CLαvt​​​(1+dβdσ​)

    where dβdσ​ is the sidewash factor

    Note that all the terms in the equation are positive.
    Thus, a vertical tail generates a stabilizing moment.

    #Total Directional Static Stability

    Total aircraft directional static stability is,

    💡

    Cnβ​=Cnβwf​​+Cnβvt​​

    Ignoring wing-alone contribution and power effects.

    #Lateral Static Stability

    Lateral static stability:

    • Aircraft’s rolling moment tendency in response to bank angle ϕ disturbance
    • Lateral static stable aircraft will generate a restoring rolling moment
    • During banking, the lateral force imbalance results in a sideslip
    • Sideslip generates a restoring rolling moment

    A positive bank angle ϕ results in a positive sideslip angle β.
    Resulting rolling moment generated is a negative moment.

    Criterion for lateral static stability,

    💡

    dβdCl​​=Clβ​<0

    To correct for a positive sideslip β, a
    negative rolling moment is required.

    #Wing Contribution

    For a sweptback wing (assume positive ϕ):

    • Aircraft rolls to the right and begins to sideslip to the right
    • Starboard wing has a larger normal velocity component than port wing
    • Difference in lift generates a restoring rolling moment (negative)
    • Sweptback wing increases lateral static stability
    • Swept-forward wing decreases lateral static stability

    For a dihedral wing (assume positive ϕ):

    • Going down wing experiences an increase in AoA and produces more lift
    • Going up wing experiences a decrease in AoA and produces less lift
    • Difference in lift generates a restoring rolling moment (negative)
    • Dihedral wing increases lateral static stability
    • Anhedral wing decreases lateral static stability

    Wing-fuselage relative positions (assume positive ϕ):

    • High wing results in starboard wing experiencing a higher angle of attack
    • Higher angle of attack in turn produces higher lift and higher drag
    • Difference in drag creates a positive yawing moment
    • High wing increases directional stability

    #Vertical Tail Contribution

    During a sideslip, the vertical tail generates a side force, resulting in a rolling moment.

    Rolling moment generated by vertical tail,

    Lvt​=−zvt​Yvt​

    Lvt​=−lvt​zvt​​⋅lvt​Yvt​

    Lvt​=−lvt​zvt​​Nvt​

    Recall that Nvt​ is yawing moment of the fin.

    In non-dimensional form,

    💡

    Clβvt​​=−lvt​zvt​​Cnβvt​​

    Lateral-directional coupling.

    Since Cnβvt​​>0:

    • Top-side fin is laterally stabilizing (generates a negative rolling moment)
    • Bottom-side fin is laterally destabilizing (generates a positive rolling moment)

    #Total Lateral Static Stability

    Total aircraft lateral static stability is,

    💡

    Clβ​=Clβw​​+Clβwf​​+Clβvt​​

    Note that lateral static stability cannot be accurately determined using analytical
    prediction and is instead determined using empirical data or wind tunnel testing.

    #Directional Control

    Convention for rudder control:

    • Positive δr​ (deflect to the left)
    • Positive δr​ generates a positive yawing force ΔYvt​
    • Positive δr​ generates a negative yawing moment ΔN

    Rudder must provide sufficient control authority for:

    • Adverse yaw
    • Asymmetric power
    • Crosswind takeoff/landing
    • Spin recovery

    #Adverse Yaw

    Adverse yaw is described as a yawing moment in the opposite direction of turn.

    During a level turn:

    • Assume aircraft is turning to the left
    • Outer wing travels at a higher velocity as V=rω
    • Outer wing generates more lift and thus, more drag
    • Drag difference results in a yaw to the right (opposite to turn direction)

    At the point of aileron deflection:

    • Assume aircraft is rolling to the right
    • Port aileron deflected downwards and starboard upwards
    • Port aileron generates more lift (for the roll) and thus, more drag
    • Drag difference results in a yaw to the left (opposite to roll direction)

    During a roll:

    • Assume aircraft is rolling to the right
    • Port wing (going up) experience an increase in AoA
    • Starboard wing (going down) experience a decrease in AoA
    • Lift vector of port wing is tilted backwards with an aft lift component
    • Lift vector of starboard wing is tilted forwards with a forward lift component
    • Aft and forward lift components results in a yaw to the left (opposite to turn direction)

    #Crosswind Takeoff/Landing

    For a directional static stable aircraft:

    • Aircraft is landing in crosswind condition
    • Aircraft tends to yaw towards the crosswind direction
    • Rudder is used to counter the yawing moment due to crosswind
    • Aircraft maintains alignment with the runway

    #Rudder Effectiveness and Control Power

    Change in yawing moment due to rudder deflection δr​,

    ΔN=−lvt​ΔYvt​

    ΔN=−lvt​(qvt​Svt​ΔCLvt​​)

    ΔN=−lvt​qvt​Svt​(dδr​dCLvt​​​δr​)

    Nondimensionalizing the above equation,

    ΔCn​=qw​SbΔN​

    ΔCn​=−qw​qvt​​⋅Sblvt​Svt​​(dδr​dCLvt​​​δr​)

    💡

    ΔCn​=−ηvt​Vvt​dδr​dCLvt​​​δr​

    A positive δr​ results in a negative N.

    Rudder effectiveness/control power is,

    💡

    Cnδr​​​=−ηvt​Vvt​dδr​dCLvt​​​

    A more negative value indicates the rudder is more effective.

    Lift slope of vertical tail w.r.t. rudder deflection,

    dδr​dCLvt​​​=dαvt​dCLvt​​​⋅dδr​dαvt​​=CLαvt​​​τ

    where τ is rudder flap effectiveness

    #Lateral Control

    Convention for aileron control:

    • Positive δa​ (starboard aileron deflect down)
    • Positive δa​ generates a negative rolling moment ΔL

    Spoilers can also be used for lateral control.

    • Reduces the lift on one wing
    • Difference in lift generates a rolling moment

    #Aileron Effectiveness and Control Power

    Assume the flow over the wing is only altered at the aileron.

    ΔL=y⋅ΔLift(y)

    ΔL=y(qSΔCLw​​)

    ΔL=y[q⋅c(y)dy⋅ΔCLw​​]

    Change in wing lift coefficient can be rewritten as,

    ΔCLw​​=dδa​dCLw​​​δa​=dαdCLw​​​⋅dδa​dα​δa​=CLαw​​​τδa​

    where τ is the aileron effectiveness

    Rolling moment due to aileron deflection,

    ΔL=y[q⋅c(y)dy⋅CLαw​​​τδa​]

    L=2qCLαw​​​τδa​∫y1​y2​​ y⋅c(y) dy

    💡

    Cl​=Sb2CLαw​​​τδa​​∫y1​y2​​y⋅c(y) dy

    Aileron control power (rate of change of rolling moment with aileron deflection),

    💡

    Clδa​​​=Sb2CLαw​​​τ​∫y1​y2​​y⋅c(y) dy

    A larger value indicates the ailerons are more effective.
