    Directional-Lateral Coupling

    #Coupling of Controls

    Rudder deflection induces a rolling moment:

    • Aerodynamic center of rudder is offset from the aircraft longitudinal axis
    • Side force generated by rudder during sideslip induces a rolling moment
    • Coefficient of rolling moment Cl​=Clβ​β+Clδr​​​δr​+Clδa​​​δa​

    Aileron deflection induces a yawing moment:

    • Ailerons result in a difference in lift and thus a difference in drag
    • Going down wing produces more lift and thus more drag
    • Drag difference during rolling induces a yawing moment
    • Coefficient of yawing moment Cn​=Cnβ​β+Cnδa​​​δa​+Cnδr​​​δr​

    Deflection of δr​ and δa​ will also generate a side force.

    💡

    CY​=CYβ​β+CYδr​​​δr​+CYδa​​​δa​

    Coefficient of side force is assumed to be constant.

    #Trim in Coupled Flights

    To trim an aircraft in a crosswind landing,

    0=Clβ​β+Clδr​​​δr​+Clδa​​​δa​

    0=Cnβ​β+Cnδr​​​δr​+Cnδa​​​δa​

    0=CYβ​β+CYδr​​​δr​+CYδa​​​δa​+21​ρV∞​2SWsinϕ​

    where Wsinϕ is the lateral weight component due to banking

    To trim an aircraft with one engine failure,

    0=Clβ​β+Clδr​​​δr​+Clδa​​​δa​+21​ρV∞​2SbLE​​

    0=Cnβ​β+Cnδr​​​δr​+Cnδa​​​δa​+21​ρV∞​2SbNE​​

    0=CYβ​β+CYδr​​​δr​+CYδa​​​δa​+21​ρV∞​2SWsinϕ​

    where LE​ and NE​ are the rolling and
    yawing moments due to engine failure

    As there are 4 unknowns and only 3 equations, the value for one parameter
    will be chosen so as to obtain the solution for the remaining 3 parameters.

    #Crosswind Landing

    Consider an aircraft approaching a runway with a flight speed V∞​.

    • Aircraft will drift to the left due to crosswind v
    • Drift correction required to maintain course

    Types of drift correction:

    • Crabbed — flying into relative wind with sideslip angle β=0°
    • Sideslip — flying at constant sideslip angle β0​ with bank angle ϕ0​

    #Crabbed Approach

    Crabbed approach:

    • Zero sideslip angle maintained by flying into relative wind
    • Additional thrust is applied to achieve higher speed V
    • Sideways component of V is equal to crosswind speed
    • Runway component of V is equal to desired V∞​

    Trim equations simplify to,

    0=Clδa​​​δa​+Clδr​​​δr​

    0=Cnδr​​​δr​+Cnδa​​​δa​

    0=CYδr​​​δr​+CYδa​​​δa​+21​ρV∞​2SWsinϕ​

    Solving the simultaneous equations. the solution is,

    💡

    δa​=0          δr​=0          ϕ=0

    #Sideslip Approach

    Sideslip approach:

    • Aircraft heading aligned with runway centerline
    • Maintain a sideslip angle β0​=tan−1V∞​v​
    • Apply positive δr​ (rudder deflect to the left)
    • Rudder deflection counters tendency to yaw
    • Apply negative δa​ (starboard aileron deflect up)
    • Aileron deflection counters induced rolling moment
    • Maximum bank angle limited to 5° to avoid wing-strike

    Banking is necessary to generate a weight component to counter the unbalanced side force produced by the
    sideslip as     δr​ only may not be sufficient.

    Solving the trim equations:

    • Substitute β=β0​ and solve for the unknowns
    • Calculated ϕ<5° to avoid wing-strike with runway
    • If ϕ>5°, let ϕ=5° and then solve for β, δr​ and δa​
    • If δr​ or δa​ are outside of deflection limits, set δr​ or δa​ and solve

    #One-Engine Failure

    One-engine failure:

    • Asymmetric thrust results in a yawing moment
    • Rudder deflection required to correct the yaw
    • Aileron required to correct induced rolling

    Right engine failure:

    • Asymmetric thrust causes yaw to the right
    • Apply positive δr​ to counter the yawing
    • Side force of rudder induces roll to the right
    • Apply positive δa​ to counter the rolling

    #Flight/Landing

    Types of scenarios:

    • Aircraft heading to be maintained and no crosswind, then β=0
    • Wings-level flight is desired (no banking), then ϕ=0
    • Calculated δr​ and δa​ must be within deflection limits
    • For landing approach, then calculated ϕ<5°

    #Ground Run

    Assume the yawing and rolling moments can be trimmed by suitable δr​ and δa​.

    • Bank angle ϕ=0 as the aircraft is on the ground
    • Sideslip angle β is applicable if there is crosswind
    • Side force generated by rudder is unbalanced as the aircraft is unable to roll
    • Ground friction is the only force that is able to counter the rudder side force

    Trim equations simplify to,

    0=Clβ​β+Clδa​​​δa​+Clδr​​​δr​+21​ρV∞​2SbLE​​

    0=Cnβ​β+Cnδr​​​δr​+Cnδa​​​δa​+21​ρV∞​2SbNE​​

    21​ρV∞​2SY​=CYβ​β+CYδr​​​δr​+CYδa​​​δa​

    where Y is the unbalanced side force of the rudder

    Solving the simultaneous equations, unbalanced side force Y can be determined.
    If Y is less than ground friction fr=μ(W−L), then the aircraft will not skid.
    If Y is more than ground friction fr=μ(W−L), then the aircraft will skid.

    #Aircraft Spin

    Spin:

    • Special category of stall
    • Autorotation about the vertical axis
    • Occurs if aircraft develops sufficient yaw at the stall point

    #Spin Initiation

    Consider an aircraft near the stalling point at wings-level condition.
    Assume the aircraft rolls to the right sufficiently such that the right wing stalls.

    Spin initiation:

    • Right wing (falling wing) operates in the stall regime
    • Falling wing produces more drag and less lift
    • Left wing (rising wing) operates as per normal
    • Rising wing produces less drag and more lift
    • Difference in drag results in a yawing moment
    • Difference in lift results in a rolling moment
    • Overall lift is also reduced due to stalled wing
    • Altitude of aircraft begins to drop due to reduced lift
    • Combination of yaw and roll, and altitude drop, creates a spiral motion
    • As aircraft drops in altitude, AoA increases for both wings due to downward speed
    • Aircraft continues spinning and its altitude drops faster

    Recovery from a spin:

    • Reduce angle of attack (pushing the yoke forwards)
    • Apply rudder opposite to spin direction
