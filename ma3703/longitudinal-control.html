    Longitudinal Control

    #Elevator Effectiveness and Control Power

    Change in aircraft’s lift, due to elevator deflection,
    is equal to change in lift acting on the horizontal tail.

    ΔL=ΔLt​

    qSΔCL​=qt​St​ΔCLt​​

    where dynamic pressure q=21​ρV2

    Rearranging the above equation,

    ΔCL​=ηH​SSt​​ΔCLt​​

    ΔCL​=ηH​SSt​​dδe​dCLt​​​δe​

    dδe​dCL​​δe​=ηH​SSt​​dδe​dCLt​​​δe​

    CLδe​​​δe​=ηH​SSt​​dδe​dCLt​​​δe​

    Comparing both sides of the equation,

    💡

    CLδe​​​=ηH​SSt​​dδe​dCLt​​​=ηH​SSt​​ae​

    where ae​ is elevator effectiveness

    Elevator effectiveness is dependent on the size of the elevator.

    💡

    ae​=dδe​dCLt​​​=dαt​dCLt​​​dδe​dαt​​=dαt​dCLt​​​τ

    where τ is flap effectiveness

    #Elevator Control Power

    Change in an aircraft’s pitching moment due to δe​ is equal to
    change in pitching moment contributed by the horizontal tail.

    ΔM=ΔMt​≈−lt​ΔLt​

    qScˉΔCm​=−qt​St​lt​ΔCLt​​

    Rearranging the above equation,

    ΔCm​=−ηH​VH​ΔCLt​​

    ΔCm​=−ηH​VH​ae​δe​

    dδe​dCm​​δe​=−ηH​VH​ae​δe​

    Cmδe​​​δe​=−ηH​VH​ae​δe​

    Comparing both sides of the equation,

    💡

    Cmδe​​​=dδe​dCm​​=−ηH​VH​ae​

    where Cmδe​​​ is the elevator control power

    Interpretation of equation:

    • Determines how effective the elevator is in generating pitching moment
    • Control power is affected by horizontal tail volume and elevator size
    • Elevator size is related to flap effectiveness τ, which is related to ae​

    #Aircraft Pitching Control

    Total pitching moment about the aircraft CG with elevator deflection,

    Cm​=Cm0​​+Cmα​​α+Cmδe​​​δe​

    Cm​=CmL=0​​+dCL​dCm​​CL​+Cmδe​​​δe​

    • Elevator deflection does not change the longitudinal static stability
    • Elevator deflection changes the longitudinal trim point where Cm​=0

    #Elevator Angle to Trim

    In trim condition, total aircraft pitching moment is zero.

    0=Cm0​​+Cmα​​α+Cmδe​​​δe​

    0=Cm0​​+Cmα​​αtrim​+Cmδe​​​δetrim​​

    💡

    δetrim​​=−Cmδe​​​Cm0​​+Cmα​​αtrim​​

    In trim condition, total aircraft pitching moment is zero.

    0=CmL=0​​+dCL​dCm​​CL​+Cmδe​​​δe​

    0=CmL=0​​+dCL​dCm​​CLtrim​​+Cmδe​​​δetrim​​

    💡

    δetrim​​=−Cmδe​​​CmL=0​​​−Cmδe​​​dCm​/dCL​​CLtrim​​

    The above equation can be expressed as a straight line equation.

    💡

    δetrim​​=δetrim0​​​+dCLtrim​​dδetrim​​​CLtrim​​

    where dCLtrim​​dδetrim​​​=−Cmδe​​​dCm​/dCL​​=Cmδe​​​SMstick fixed​​

    Gradient of the elevator trim curve depends on the location of the CG.

    Note that static margin is defined as the distance between the neutral point and the CG.

    dCLtrim​​dδetrim​​​=0     →     cˉxNP​​=cˉxCG​​

    #Determination of Neutral Point through Flight Testing

    Since dCLtrim​​dδetrim​​​=0 when the neutral point coincides with the CG, for a given CG, it
    is possible to determine the neutral point of an aircraft by flying the aircraft at three
    different flight speeds and recording the δetrim​​ and CLtrim​​ required to maintain level
    flight at each speed.

    #Forward Most Location of CG

    Forward most location is limited by the maximum upward elevator deflection.
    This location is at minimum δetrim​​ required to maintain level flight at CLmax​​.

    Note that the upward deflection of the elevator is denoted by a negative δe​.

    Forward most location when the aircraft is trimmed at CLmax​​ and elevator deflection
    is at its minimum,

    💡

    cˉxCGf​​​=cˉxNP​​−(δemin​​+Cmδe​​​CmL=0​​​)CLmax​​Cmδe​​​​

    Alternatively, using the total aircraft pitching moment equation,

    0=CmL=0​​+dCL​dCm​​CLtrim​​+Cmδe​​​δetrim​​

    0=CmL=0​​+dCL​dCm​​CLmax​​+Cmδe​​​δemin​​

    Solving for dCL​dCm​​, which is also equal to static margin,

    −dCL​dCm​​=cˉxNP​​−cˉxCGf​​​

    cˉxCGf​​​=cˉxNP​​+dCL​dCm​​

    #Aft Most Location of CG

    Aft most location is limited by the minimum static margin required.

    0.05=cˉxNP​​−cˉxCGa​​​

    cˉxCGa​​​=cˉxNP​​−0.05

    Static margin is usually 0.05.

    Range of CG locations,

    💡

    ΔxˉCG​=cˉxCGa​​​−cˉxCGf​​​

    Permissible operating region on a δe​ vs CL​ graph:

    • Region enclosed between the forward most and aft most location of CG
    • Also limited by the maximum and minimum CL​ achievable in level flight

    Minimum lift coefficient,

    CLmin​​=21​ρVmax​2SW​

    Maximum lift coefficient,

    CLmax​​=21​ρVstall​2SW​

    #Ground Effect

    Ground effect:

    • Ground reduces the downwash of the wing
    • Tail experiences a smaller downwash angle ε
    • Tail becomes more effective, increase in lift slope CLαt​​​ of the tail
    • Neutral point of the aircraft shifts backwards (as observed from its equation)
    • Static margin increases which results in a steeper Cm​ slope (higher static stability)

    The moment generated by the tail becomes larger and thus, a upward deflection of the elevator is required to counteract this increase in moment. Demand for the elevator to
    trim at CLmax​​ becomes severe during takeoff and landing.

    Consider an aircraft flying at forward most CG location.
    This location is determined by CLmax​​ achievable at δemin​​.

    Maximum lift coefficient at minimum elevator deflection.

    • Ground effect increases static margin
    • Slope of δe​ vs CL​ graph becomes steeper
    • Maximum CL​ achievable at δemin​​ is reduced
    • Not desirable as high CL​ is required for takeoff and landing
    • Forward most CG location xCGf​​ deliberately shifted backwards
      to ensure that CLmax​​ can be achieved at δemin​​ with ground effect
