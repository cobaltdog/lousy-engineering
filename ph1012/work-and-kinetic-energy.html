    Work and Kinetic Energy

    In physics, work has a more precise definition. Total work done on an object by all forces that
    act on it equals the change in its kinetic energy — a quantity related to the object’s speed.

    #Work Done by a Constant Force

    Work is done by exerting a force on a body while that body undergoes displacement. While the body moves, a constant force F acts on it in the same direction as the displacement s.

    💡
    W=F⋅s
    💡
    W=Fscosθ

    #Positive, Negative or Zero Work

    When the force has no component in the direction of motion, work done is zero.

    Similarly, when the force does not result in any displacement of the object, work done is zero.

    When F is in the same direction as s, work done is positive.

    When F is in the opposite direction as s, work done is negative.

    #Example 1

    WP​=FP​​xcos37°=3194 J

    Wfr​=Ffr​​xcos180°=−2000 J

    Wmass​=mgxcos90°=0J

    Wnet​=3194−2000=1194 J

    #Example 2

    ∑Fx​=5000cos36.9°−3500=500 N

    Wtot​=Fx​⋅s=500(20)=10000 J

    #Work Done by a Varying Force

    When F is not constant in magnitude and direction, the work done on an object can be approximated by taking the sum of Δx intervals.

    💡
    W=∫xi​xf​​Fx​ dx

    #Work Done by a Spring

    If the spring is either stretched or compressed a small distance from its equilibrium position, it exerts a force on the block.

    💡
    Fs​=−kx

    k is a measure of the stiffness of the spring.

    The force exerted by a spring is always directed opposite the displacement from equilibrium.

    Ws​=∫xi​xf​​−kx dx

    💡
    Ws​=21​kxi​2−21​kxf​2

    Work done by a spring.

    The applied force Fapp​ is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the spring force Fs​.

    💡
    Wapp​=21​kxf​2−21​kxi​2

    Work done on a spring.

    #Work-Energy Theorem

    The total work done on a body by external forces is also related to changes in its speed.

    v2​2=v1​2+2ax​s

    ax​=2sv2​2−v1​2​

    max​=m 2sv2​2−v1​2​

    F=m 2sv2​2−v1​2​

    Fs=21​mv2​2−21​mv1​2

    W=21​mv2​2−21​mv1​2

    💡
    K=21​mv2
    💡
    Wtot​=ΔK

    The work-energy theorem tells us only about changes in speed, not velocity.

    #Meaning of Kinetic Energy

    To accelerate an object of mass m from rest up to a speed, the total work done on it must equal the change in kinetic energy from zero to K=21​mv2.

    Since F and s are the same for both iceboats, assuming a frictionless surface, the total work done on each iceboat is the same. Thus, based on the work-energy theorem, both iceboats
    will have the same kinetic energy at the finish line as both started from rest.

    #Power

    Power is the time rate at which work is done.

    💡
    Pavg​=ΔtΔW​
    💡
    P=dtdW​
    💡
    P=F⋅v
