    Fluid Mechanics

    A fluid is any substance that can flow; we use the term for both liquids and gases.

    #Density

    Density is defined as mass per unit volume.

    A homogeneous material has the same density throughout.

    💡
    ρ=Vm​

    #Pressure

    When a fluid is at rest, it exerts a force perpendicular to any surface in contact with it.

    💡
    P=AF​

    While the fluid as a whole is at rest, the molecules that make up the fluid are in motion; the force exerted by the fluid is due to molecules colliding with their surroundings.

    #Pascal’s Law

    Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished to every portion of the fluid and the walls of the containing vessel.

    💡
    P=P0​+ρgh
    💡
    Pabs​=Patm​+Pgauge​

    If the absolute pressure is less than atmospheric, partial vacuum, the gauge pressure is negative.

    #Example

    PA​=PB​

    #Buoyancy

    When a body is completely or partially immersed in a fluid, that fluid exerts an upward force on the body equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the body.

    💡
    Fb​=ρfluid​Vdisplaced​g

    #Fluid Flow

    An ideal fluid is a fluid that is incompressible and has no internal friction (viscosity). Internal friction causes shear stresses when two adjacent layers of fluid move relative to each other.

    The path of an individual particle in a moving fluid is called a flow line. Flows lines passing through an edge of an imaginary element
    of area form a tube called a flow tube.

    Laminar flow occurs when adjacent layers of fluid slide smoothly past each other and the
    flow is steady. At sufficiently high flow rates, or when boundary surfaces cause abrupt
    changes in velocity, the flow becomes irregular and chaotic — turbulent flow.

    #Continuity Equation

    The mass of a fluid does not change as it flows.

    V1​=A1​v1​ dt

    V2​=A2​v2​ dt

    ρA1​v1​ dt=ρA2​v2​ dt

    Volume flow rate is given by,

    💡
    dtdV​=Av

    Mass flow rate is given by,

    💡
    m˙=ρAv

    #Bernoulli’s Equation

    In a small time interval t, the fluid at a moves to b and the fluid at c moves to d. The volume of fluid V passing any cross section during t is the same — V=A1​s1​=A2​s2​.

    Work done on fluid element during t.

    W=p1​A1​s1​−p2​A2​s2​

    W=(p1​−p2​)V

    Change in kinetic energy during t.

    ΔK=21​ρV(v2​2−v1​2)

    Change in potential energy during dt.

    ΔU=ρVg(y2​−y1​)

    Using the work-energy theorem W=ΔK+ΔU.

    (p1​−p2​)V=21​ρV(v2​2−v1​2)+ρVg(y2​−y1​)

    p1​−p2​=21​ρ(v2​2−v1​2)+ρg(y2​−y1​)

    💡
    p1​+21​ρv1​2+ρgy1​=p2​+21​ρv2​2+ρgy2​

    Bernoulli’s equation is not applicable to regions where there is turbulent flow (unsteady flow).

    #Viscosity and Turbulence

    Viscosity is internal friction in a fluid. Viscous forces oppose the motion of one portion of a fluid relative to another.

    The greater the viscosity, the greater the tendency for the fluid to flow in sheets and thus, the more likely the flow is to be laminar.
