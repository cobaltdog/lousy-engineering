    Magnetic Fields

    #Magnetic Forces and Fields

    Like electric field, magnetic field is a vector with the symbol B. At any position, the direction of B is defined as the direction in which the north pole of a compass needle tends to point.

    #Magnetic Forces on Moving Charges

    Characteristics of a magnetic field:

    • A charged particle at rest experiences no magnetic force
    • A charged particle moving parallel to the magnetic field experiences no magnetic force
    • Magnetic force on a negative charge is in the direction opposite of a positive charge
    • Magnetic force acts in a direction perpendicular to both v and B when angle θ=0

    💡
    FB​=qv×B
    💡
    ∣FB​∣=∣q∣vBsinθ

    The SI unit of magnetic field is the Tesla.

    Difference between electric and magnetic forces:

    • Magnetic force is perpendicular to the field
    • Magnetic force only acts on a moving particle
    • Magnetic force does no work as the force acts perpendicular to the displacement of the particle

    The direction of the magnetic force on a moving charged particle can be determined using the right-hand-rule or Fleming’s left hand rule. If the charge is negative, the direction of the force is opposite to that given by the right-hand-rule.

    #Example

    FB​=qvBsinθ

    FB​=(1.6×10−19)(3×105)(2)sin30°

    FB​=4.8×10−14 N

    #Magnetic Forces on a Straight Wire

    Suppose a charge q is moving with drift velocity vd​. The magnetic force on the charge is F=qvd​×B.

    The total number of charges in the wire is the charge density n, multiplied with the volume of the wire segment Al.

    I=n∣q∣vd​A

    FB​=(qvd​×B)nAl

    💡
    FB​=Il×B
    💡
    ∣FB​∣=IlBsinθ

    in a Uniform Magnetic Field

    If B is directed perpendicular to and out of the page, it is represented with green dots and the field is labelled Bout​.

    If B is directed perpendicular into the page, it is represented with green crosses and is labelled Bin​.

    Consider a positively charged particle moving in a uniform magnetic field with the initial v perpendicular to the field.

    As the particle changes the direction of its velocity v in response to the magnetic force FB​, FB​ always remains perpendicular to the velocity, and the path is a circle.

    #Applications of Motion
    of Charged Particles

    #Velocity Selector

    In a beam of charged particles, not all particles move with the same speed. Many applications, however, require a beam in which all the particle speeds are the same.

    A charged particle with mass m, charge q, and velocity v enters a region where the E and B
    fields are perpendicular to v and to each other.

    Fe​=qE

    FB​=qvB

    Resolving the forces in the horizontal axis,

    qE=qvB

    💡
    v=BE​

    Only particles with velocities equal to v=BE​ can pass through without being deflected by the fields.

    #Magnetic Field Lines and Flux

    Any magnetic field can be represented by magnetic field lines. When adjacent lines are close together,
    the field magnitude is large, and vice versa.

    Magnetic field lines do not point in the direction of the force on a charge. The direction of the force depends
    on the particle’s velocity and the sign of its charge.

    #Gauss’s Law for Magnetism

    The total magnetic flux through a surface is the sum of the contributions from the individual elements.

    ΦB​=∫B⊥​ dA=∫Bcosθ dA

    💡
    ΦB​=∫B⋅dA
    💡
    ΦB​=BAcosθ

    The SI unit of magnetic flux is the weber Wb.

    As magnetic field lines always form a closed loop, there is no such thing as a magnetic monopole. Thus, the total magnetic flux through a closed surface is always zero.

    💡
    ∮B⋅dA=0

    #Example

    ΦB​=BAcosθ

    B=AcosθΦB​​

    #Force between Parallel Conductors

    Each conductor lies in the magnetic field set up by the other, so each experiences a force.

    The lower conductor produces magnetic field, which exerts a force on a length L of the upper conductor.

    #Field of a Circular Current Loop

    The direction of the magnetic field on the axis of a current-carrying loop is given by the right-hand-rule. Your right thumb points in the direction of the field.

    Suppose that instead of a single loop, we have a coil consisting of
    N loops, all with the same radius.

    The loops are closely spaced such that the plane of each loop is the same distance from a field point P.

    #Ampere’s Law

    Gauss’s law states that the magnetic flux through any closed surface is always zero, whether or not there are currents within the surface. Thus, Gauss’s law for magnetism cannot be used to determine the magnetic field produced by a particular current distribution.

    Magnetic flux through a surface is given by ∮B⋅dl.

    Consider the magnetic field produced by a straight conductor. The magnetic field lines form circles
    around the conductor and the magnitude of B
    is the same everywhere on the circular path.

    B=2πrμ0​I​

    Evaluating the dot product B⋅dl for a small length element dl and sum the products for all elements
    over the closed circular path.

    ∮B⋅dl=B∮dl=B(2πr)

    💡
    ∮B⋅dl=μ0​Iencl​

    #Field of a Solenoid

    A solenoid is a long wire wound in the form of a helix. A reasonably uniform magnetic field can be produced in the space surrounded by the turns of wire — interior of the solenoid.

    An ideal solenoid is where the turns are closely spaced and the length is much greater than the radius of the turns.

    ∮B⋅dl=Bl

    Bl=μ0​Iencl​

    The total current flowing through length l of the solenoid is equal to the current I through each
    turn multiplied by the number of turns N.

    Bl=μ0​NI

    B=lμ0​NI​

    💡
    B=μ0​nI

    n represents the number of turns per unit length.

    The magnetic field at the ends of the solenoid is approximately half that in the center.
