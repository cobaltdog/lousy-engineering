    #Momentum and Impulse

    The net force acting on an object equals the time rate of change of mv, the product of the object’s mass and velocity. The symbol, p​, is used to represent linear momentum.

    ∑F=ma=dtd​ mv=dtd​ p​

    💡
    p​=mv
    💡
    Fthrust​=v dtdm​

    #Impulse-Momentum Theorem

    The impulse of a net force is defined to be the product of the force and the time interval.

    💡
    J=∑F(tf​−ti​)

    ∑F=dtd​p​=tf​−ti​p​f​−p​i​​

    ∑F(tf​−ti​)=p​f​−p​i​

    💡
    J=p​f​−p​i​

    The change in momentum of an object during a time interval equals the impulse of the net force that acts on the object during that time interval.

    #Conservation of Momentum

    The concept of momentum is particularly important in situations in which we have two or more bodies that interact. Each body exerts a force on the other. According to Newton’s third law, the two forces are always equal in magnitude but opposite in direction.

    For any system, the forces that the bodies exert on each other are internal forces. The two forces are always equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

    FB on A​=dtd​p​A​

    FA on B​=dtd​p​B​

    FB on A​=−FA on B​

    FB on A​+FA on B​=0

    dtd​p​A​+dtd​p​B​=0

    dtd​P=0

    The time rate of change of P is zero. Hence the total momentum of the system is constant, even though the individual momenta can change.

    💡
    P=p​A​+p​B​+......=mA​vA​+mB​vB​+......=Constant

    #Example 1

    If the marksman exerts negligible horizontal forces on the rifle, then there is no horizontal force on the system during the firing, and the total horizontal momentum is conserved.

    PR​+PB​=0

    mR​vRx​+mB​vBx​=0

    vRx​=mR​−mB​vBx​​=−0.50 m/s

    p​R​=mR​vRx​=−1.5 kg⋅m/s

    p​B​=mB​vBx​=1.5 kg⋅m/s

    KR​=21​mR​vRx2​=0.375 J

    KB​=21​mB​vBx​=225 J

    The bullet and rifle have equal but opposite momentum. But the bullet travels a much greater distance than the rifle during the interaction, thus the force on the bullet does
    more work than the force on the rifle. Hence, the bullet has a higher kinetic energy.

    #Elastic and Inelastic Collisions

    If the forces between the bodies are much larger than any external forces, we can neglect the external forces entirely and treat the bodies as an isolated system. Momentum is conserved
    and the total momentum of the system is the same before and after the collision.

    #Elastic Collisions

    In an elastic collision, the total kinetic energy
    of the system is the same before and after.

    💡
    p​A1​+p​B1​=p​A2​+p​B2​
    💡
    KA1​+KB1​=KA2​+KB2​

    In a straight-line collision of two bodies, the relative velocities before
    and after the collision have the same magnitude but opposite sign.

    Using conservation of momentum,

    mA​vA1​+mB​vB1​=mA​vA2​+mB​vB2​

    mA​(vA1​−vA2​)=mB​(vB1​−vB2​)   −[1]

    Using conservation of kinetic energy,

    21​mA​vA1​2+21​mB​vB1​2=21​mA​vA2​2+21​mB​vB2​2

    mA​vA1​2+mB​vB1​2=mA​vA2​2+mB​vB2​2

    mA​(vA1​2−vA2​2)=mB​(vB1​2−vB2​2)

    mA​(vA1​−vA2​)(vA1​+vA2​)=mB​(vB1​−vB2​)(vB1​+vB2​)   −[2]

    [2]÷[1]

    vA1​+vA2​=vB1​+vB2​

    vA1​−vB1​=vB2​−vA2​

    The relationship between the initial and final velocities is then,

    💡
    vA1​−vB1​=−(vA2​−vB2​)

    #Inelastic Collisions

    In an inelastic collision, the total kinetic energy after colliding is less than before the collision.

    If the colliding bodies stick together and move as one body after the collision, it is a completely inelastic collision.

    💡
    mA​vA1​+mB​vB1​=(mA​+mB​)v2​

    #Rocket Propulsion

    Momentum considerations are particularly useful for analyzing a system in which
    the masses of parts of the system change with time. Thus, we can’t use Newton’s
    second law ∑F=ma.

    A rocket is propelled forward by rearward ejection of burned fuel that initially was
    in the rocket. The forward force on the rocket is reaction to the backward force on
    the ejected material. The total mass of the system is constant, but the mass of the
    rocket decreases as material is ejected.

    Suppose at some time t that the momentum of a rocket plus its fuel is (M+Δm)v,
    where v is the velocity of the rocket relative to earth. Over a short time interval Δt,
    the rocket ejects fuel of mass Δm. At the end of this interval, the rocket’s mass is M
    and its speed is v+Δv. If the fuel is ejected with a speed vex​ relative to the rocket,
    the velocity of the fuel relative to the earth is v−vex​.

    Using conservation of momentum Pi​=Pf​.

    Pi​=(M+Δm)v

    Pf​=M(v+Δv)−Δm(v−vex​)

    (M+Δm)v=M(v+Δv)+Δm(v−vex​)

    Mv+vΔm=Mv+MΔv+vΔm−vex​Δm

    MΔv=vex​Δm

    As Δt→0, we let Δv→dv and Δm→dm.

    M dv=vex​ dm=−vex​ dM

    The increase in exhaust mass dm corresponds to an equal decrease in the rocket mass, dm=−dM.

    Dividing the equation by M and integrate, taking the initial mass of the rocket
    and fuel to be Mi​, and the final mass of the rocket plus remaining fuel to be Mf​.

    ∫vi​vf​​dv=−vex​∫Mi​Mf​​MdM​

    💡
    vf​−vi​=vex​ lnMf​Mi​​

    Increase in rocket speed is proportional to the exhaust speed of the ejected gases.

    The thrust on the rocket is the force exerted on it by the ejected exhaust gases.

    💡
    Thrust=Mdtdv​=∣vex​dtdM​∣

    Thrust increases as the exhaust speed increases and as the burn rate increases.
