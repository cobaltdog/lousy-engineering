    Universal Gravitation

    Gravity acts in the same fundamental way between the Earth and your body, between
    the Sun and a planet, and between a planet and one of its moons. This law is universal.

    #Motion of Satellites

    Suppose we launch a projectile from point A in the direction AB, tangent to the earth’s
    surface. If the projectile is launched at a great enough speed, the curvature of the earth becomes significant — the earth curves away as the projectile falls.

    In trajectories 3 through 5, the projectile misses the Earth and becomes a satellite. If there
    is no retarding force, the projectile’s speed, when it returns to point A, is the same as its
    initial speed and it repeats its motion indefinitely.

    Trajectories 1 to 5 close on themselves and are called closed orbits (ellipses). Trajectory 4 is a circle, a special case of an ellipse. Trajectories 6 and 7 are open orbits, whereby the projectile never returns to its starting point but travels further away.

    #Circular Orbits

    Many artificial satellites have nearly circular orbits; the satellite is in uniform circular motion
    and its speed is constant. The only force acting on a satellite in a circular orbit is the earth’s gravitational attraction, which is directed towards the center of the Earth.
