    Temperature and Heat

    Heat refers to energy transfer caused by temperature differences.

    #Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics

    If C is initially in thermal equilibrium with both A and B, then A and B are also in thermal equilibrium with each other — zeroth law of thermodynamics.

    Two systems are in thermal equilibrium if and only if they have the same temperature.

    Conversion from Celsius scale to Kelvin scale.

    💡
    TK​=TC​+273.15

    #Thermal Expansion

    #Linear Expansion

    When the temperature of a solid object changes, the change in length ΔL is approximately proportional to ΔT.

    💡
    ΔL=αL0​ΔT

    L=L0​+ΔL=L0​+αL0​ΔT

    💡
    L=L0​(1+αΔT)

    #Example

    αbrass​=19×10−6/C°

    1.01L0​=L0​(1+αΔT)

    1.01=1+αΔT

    ΔT=α1.01−1​

    T2​=T1​+ΔT=551 C°

    #Volume Expansion

    Increasing temperature usually causes increases in volume for both solid and liquid. When the temperature of a solid object changes, the change in volume ΔV is approximately proportional to ΔT.

    💡
    ΔV=βV0​ΔT

    V=V0​+ΔV=V0​+βV0​ΔT

    💡
    V=V0​(1+βΔT)

    For solid materials, β≈3α.

    V=L.W.HV=L0​(1+αΔT)+W0​(1+αΔT)+H0​(1+αΔT)V=(L0​W0​H0​)(1+αΔT)3V=(L0​W0​H0​)[1+3(αΔT)+3(αΔT)2+(αΔT)3]V=V0​(1+3αΔT)V=V0​(1+βΔT)

    #Example 1

    Vgas​=V0​(1+βgas​ΔT)

    Vtank​=V0​(1+3αtank​ΔT)

    Voverflow​=V0​(1+βgas​ΔT)−V0​(1+3αtank​ΔT)

    Voverflow​=V0​ΔT(βgas​−3αtank​)

    Voverflow​=1.28 l

    #Example 2

    βwater​=210×10−6/C°

    m4C​=m94C​

    ρ4C​V4C​=ρ94C​V94C​

    #Thermal Expansion of Water

    Water, in the temperature range from 0°C to 4°C, decreases in volume with increasing temperature. In this range, its coefficient of volume expansion is negative.

    #Quantity of Heat

    #Specific Heat Capacity

    The heat capacity of a body is the heat required to produce a unit change in temperature.

    The quantity of heat Q required to increase the temperature of a mass m of a certain material from T1​ to T2​ is approximately proportional to the temperature change ΔT.

    💡
    Q=mcΔT

    The quantity of heat required depends on the nature of the material.

    #Molar Heat Capacity

    The total mass m of a material is equal to the molar mass M times the number of moles n.

    m=nM

    Q=nMcΔT

    💡
    Q=nCΔT

    #Latent Heat

    For any given pressure, a phase change takes place at a definite temperature, usually accompanied by absorption or emission of heat and a change in volume and density.

    The latent heat of fusion, or vaporization, is the heat required to cause a unit mass of a material to change states, without a change in temperature. This process is reversible.

    💡
    Q=±mL
