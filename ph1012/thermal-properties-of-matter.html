    Thermal Properties of Matter

    #Equations of State

    #Ideal-Gas Equation

    Behavior of gases:

    • Volume is proportional to number of moles
    • Volume varies inversely with absolute pressure
    • Pressure is proportional to absolute temperature

    💡
    R=8.314 J/mol⋅K
    💡
    PV=nRT

    #Example 1

    Tp​=Constant

    T1​p1​​=T2​p2​​

    p2​=T1​p1​T2​​

    p2​=10+273.15(200+101)(40+273.15)​=333 kPa (absolute)

    #Example 2

    From A to B, constant temperature.

    pa​Va​=pb​Vb​

    pa​=Va​pb​Vb​​

    pa​=10 atm

    pb​Vb​=nRTb​

    n=nTb​pb​Vb​​

    n=8.314(293)(2×1.01×105)(10×10−3)​=0.829 mol

    #Example 3

    Tp​=Constant

    T1​p1​​=T2​p2​​

    T2​=p1​p2​T1​​

    T2​=2(20+273.15)=586 K

    #Example 4

    p1​V1​=nRT1​

    p2​V2​=nRT2​

    p1​V1​p2​V2​​=T1​T2​​

    V1​V2​​=T1​p2​T2​p1​​

    V1​V2​​=1.40

    #Moles and Avogadro’s Number

    One mole is the amount of substance that contains as many elementary entities as there are atoms in 0.012kg of carbon-12. It is the same for all elements and compounds.

    The number of molecules in a mole is called Avogadro’s number, denoted by NA​.

    💡
    NA​=6.02214179×1023 molecules/mol

    The molar mass M of a compound is the mass of 1 mole, which is equal to the mass m of a single molecule multiplied by Avogadro’s number.

    💡
    M=NA​m

    The ideal gas law is often expressed in terms of the total number of molecules N.

    PV=nRT=NA​N​RT

    💡
    PV=NkB​T
    💡
    kB​=1.38×10−23 J/K

    kB​ is known as the Boltzmann’s constant.

    #Kinetic Theory of Gases

    Assumptions of kinetic-molecular model:

    • Number of molecules is large and average separation between them is large
    • Molecules obey Newton’s laws of motion, but as a whole they move randomly
    • Molecules interact only by short-range forces during elastic collisions
    • Molecules make elastic collisions with the walls of the container
    • Gas is a pure substance; all molecules are identical

    #Collisions and Gas Pressure

    Consider a container of volume V with N molecules of an ideal gas with mass m.

    As the molecules collides elastically with the wall, its velocity component perpendicular to the wall is reversed as the mass of the wall is far greater.

    Δpxi​=p2​−p1​

    Δpxi​=−mvxi​−mvxi​=−2mvxi​

    Fi,on molecule​Δt=Δpxi​=−2mvxi​

    For the molecule to make another collision with the same wall after the first collision, it must travel a distance of 2d in the x-direction.

    Δt=vxi​2d​

    Fi​ is the average force over the time interval for the molecule to move across the cube and back.

    Fi​=−Δt−2mvxi​​=−dmvxi​2​

    Fi,on wall​=−Fi​=dmvxi​2​

    The total force Ftot​ exerted by the gas on the wall is found by adding the average forces exerted by the individual molecules.

    Ftot​=i=1∑N​ dmvxi​2​=dm​i=1∑N​ vxi​2

    The average velocity of the molecules is the sum of the velocities divided by N.

    vx​2​=Ni=1∑N​ vxi​2​

    The total force of the molecules on the wall is given by,

    💡
    Ftot​=dm​Nvx​2​

    #Pressure and Molecular Kinetic Energies

    The Pythagorean theorem relates the velocity of the molecule to the velocity components.

    vi​2=vxi​2+vyi​2+vzi​2

    v2=vx​2​+vy​2​+vz​2​

    As the molecule motion is completely random, the average values are equal to each other.

    v2=3vx​2​

    💡
    Ftot​=31​Ndmv2​

    Using the above expression, we can find the total pressure exerted on the wall.

    Ptot​=AFtot​​=d2Ftot​​

    Ptot​=31​Nd3mv2​=31​(VN​)mv2

    💡
    Ptot​=32​(VN​)(21​mv2)

    The pressure of a gas is proportional to the number of molecules per unit volume and to the average translational kinetic energy of the molecules.

    #Molecular Interpretation of Temperature

    P=32​(VN​)(21​mv2)

    PV=32​N(21​mv2)

    PV=32​(21​Mv2)

    💡
    PV=32​Ktr​

    Equate the above equation to the ideal-gas equation and rearrange the terms.

    PV=nRT=32​Ktr​

    💡
    Ktr​=23​nRT=21​mv2

    The total translational kinetic energy of N molecules of gas is N times the average energy.

    💡
    Ktr(tot)​=21​Mv2=23​nRT=23​NkB​T

    The internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on the temperature.

    The square root of v2 is the root-mean-square (rms) speed of the molecules.

    💡
    vrms​=v2​=M3RT​​=m3kB​T​​

    When energy is added to a monatomic gas, a gas containing one atom per molecule, in a container of fixed volume, all the added energy goes into increasing the translational kinetic energy of the atoms.

    💡
    U=23​nRT=23​NkB​T → Monatomic gases
    💡
    U=25​nRT=25​NkB​T → Diatomic gases

    #Heat Capacities of Gases

    When we add heat to a substance, we are increasing its molecular energy. With the volume held constant, we are concerned with CV​.

    The molecular energy consists only of the translational kinetic energy Ktr​, which is directly proportional to the absolute temperature T.

    dKtr​=23​nR dT

    dQ=nCV​ dT

    💡
    CV​=23​R → Monatomic gases
    💡
    CV​=25​R → Diatomic gases
