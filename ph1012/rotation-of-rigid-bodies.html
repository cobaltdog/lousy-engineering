    Rotation of Rigid Bodies

    #Angular Velocity and Acceleration

    To describe rotational motion, the most natural way is to measure the angle θ in radians.

    💡
    s=rθ

    #Angular Velocity

    A reference line OP in a rotating body makes an angle θ1​ with the positive x-axis at time t1​. At a later time t2​, the angle has changed to θ2​.

    💡
    ωavg(z)​=tf​−ti​θf​−θi​​=ΔtΔθ​
    💡
    ωz​=dtdθ​

    The direction of ω is given by the right-hand-rule. If the rotation is about the z-axis, then ω has only a z-component. This component is positive if ω is along the positive z-axis.

    #Angular Acceleration

    When the angular velocity of an object changes, it has an angular acceleration.

    💡
    αavg(z)​=tf​−ti​ωf​−ωi​​=ΔtΔωz​​
    💡
    αz​=dtdωz​​

    In rotational motion, α points in the same direction as ω if the rotation is speeding up, and opposite if the rotation is slowing down.

    #Constant Angular Acceleration

    When angular acceleration is constant, the equations for angular velocity and angular position are identical to the equations in the Motion Along a Straight Line chapter.

    💡
    ωz​=ω0z​+αz​t
    💡
    θ=θ0​+ω0z​t+21​αz​t2
    💡
    ωz​2=ω0z​2+2αz​(θ−θ0​)

    #Example

    ω=ω0​+αt

    ω=27.5+(−10)(0.3)=24.5 rad/s

    θ=θ0​+ω0​t+21​αt2

    θ=27.5(0.3)+21​(−10)(0.3)2

    θ=7.8 rad=447°

    Angle=447°−360°=86.9°

    #Rotational Kinetic Energy

    A rotating rigid body consists of mass in motion, so it has kinetic energy. This kinetic energy can be expressed in terms of the body’s angular velocity and moment of inertia.

    💡
    KR​=21​Iω2

    #Moment of Inertia

    We can think of a body as being made up of a large number of particles, with masses m1​, m2​, ... at distances r1​, r2​, ... from the axis of rotation.

    The mass of ith particle is mi​ and its distance from the axis of rotation is ri​. When a body rotates about a fixed axis, the angular speed of a particle is ri​ω.

    21​mi​vi​2=21​mi​ri​2ω2

    Kr​=21​m1​r1​2ω2+21​m2​r2​2ω2+...

    Kr​=21​ω2(m1​r1​2+m2​r2​2+...)

    Kr​=21​(∑i​ mi​ri​2)ω2

    💡
    I=∑i​ mi​ri​2=∫R2 dm

    S.I. unit of moment of inertia is kg⋅m2.

    #Parallel Axis Theorem

    Consider two rotation axes, one which passes through the center of mass at the origin, and
    the other through a point P.

    The distance of the axis through P from the axis through the center of mass is d=a2+b2​.

    Icm​=∑i​ mi​(xi​2+yi​2)

    IP​=∑i​ mi​[(xi​−a)2+(yi​−b)2]

    IP​=∑i​ mi​(xi​2+yi​2)+∑i​ mi​(a2+b2)

    💡
    IP​=Icm​+Md2
