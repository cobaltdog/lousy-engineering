    Electric Fields

    #Electric Charge

    Charges of the same sign repel each other and charges with opposite signs attract one another. The algebraic sum of all the electric charges in any closed system is constant.

    #Coulomb’s Law

    💡
    Fe​=ke​r2∣q1​q2​∣​

    When q1​ and q2​ have the same signs, the forces are repulsive and vice versa.

    The direction of the forces the two charges exert on each other are always along the line joining them. The two forces obey Newton’s third law; they are always equal in magnitude and opposite in direction, even when the charges are not equal in magnitude.

    The constant ke​ is known as the Coulomb constant.

    #Electric Field

    The electric force on a charged body is exerted by the electric field created by other charged bodies.

    💡
    E=q0​F0​​

    The SI unit of electric field is newton per coulomb.

    If q0​ is positive, the force F0​ experienced by the charge is the same direction as E and vice versa.

    #Electric Field of a Point Charge

    To determine the direction of an electric field, consider a point charge q as a source charge. This charge creates an electric field at all points in space surrounding it. A test charge q0​ is placed at point P at a distance r from the source charge.

    💡
    E=ke​r2∣q∣​
    💡
    E=ke​r2q​ r^

    The unit vector is equal to the displacement vector r divided by the distance r.

    #Superposition of Electric Fields

    The total electric of a charge distribution is the vector sum of the fields due to each point charge in the charge distribution.

    💡
    F=F1​+F2​+F3​+......
    💡
    E=E1​+E2​+E3​+......

    Charge distributions are described by linear charge density λ, surface charge density σ,
    and volume charge density ρ.
