    Motion Along a Straight Line

    Kinematics is the part of mechanics that describe the motion of objects.

    #Displacement, Time and Velocity

    Displacement is the change in position of an object. It is a vector that points from the object’s initial position to the object’s final position. On the other hand, distance is the length of the path travelled by an object and it is a scalar.

    #Average Velocity

    The x-component of average velocity is the x-component of displacement divided by the time interval during which the displacement occurs. The average velocity can be either positive or negative.

    💡
    vavg(x)​=ΔtΔx​

    #Instantaneous Velocity

    The average velocity of an object during a time interval can’t tell us how fast, or in what direction, the object was moving at any given time during the interval. To do this, we need the instantaneous velocity of the object.

    💡
    vx​=dtdx​

    The displacement x tells us where an object is while vx​ tells us how it’s moving.

    #Example

    x=20+5t2

    x1​=20+5=25 m

    x2​=20+5(2)2=40 m

    Δx=x2​−x1​=40−25=15 m

    vavg(x)​=t2​−t1​x2​−x1​​

    vavg(x)​=2−140−25​=15 m/s

    vx​=dtdx​ (20+5t2)

    vx​=10t m/s

    #Displacement-Time Graph

    The velocity of an object can be found from the graph of its position as a function of time. The instantaneous velocity at any point is equal to the slope of the tangent to the curve at that point.

    #Average and Instantaneous Acceleration

    Acceleration describes the rate of change of velocity with time. Like velocity, it is a vector. Acceleration tells us how the speed and direction of motion are changing.

    #Average Acceleration

    The average acceleration is defined as the change in velocity divided by the time interval.

    💡
    aavg(x)​=ΔtΔvx​​

    #Instantaneous Acceleration

    The instantaneous acceleration is the limit of the average acceleration as the time interval approaches zero — in the language of calculus, the derivative of velocity with time.

    💡
    ax​=dtdvx​​

    #Example

    vx​=60+0.5t2

    v1x​=60+0.5=60.5 m/s

    v2x​=60+0.5(3)2=64.5 m/s

    Δvx​=v2x​−v1x​=64.5−60.5=4 m/s

    aavg(x)​=t2​−t1​v2x​−v1x​​

    aavg(x)​=3−164.5−60.5​=2.0 m/s2

    ax​=dtdvx​​ (60+0.5t2)

    ax​=t m/s2

    #Displacement/Velocity-Time Graph

    On a graph of velocity as a function of time, the instantaneous acceleration at any point is equal to the slope of the tangent to the curve at that point.

    The acceleration of an object can be determined from a graph of its position versus time.

    💡
    ax​=dtdvx​​=dtd​(dtdx​)=dt2d2x​

    The second derivative of any function is related to the concavity or curvature of the graph.

    #Free Falling Bodies

    An example of motion with (nearly) constant acceleration is a body falling under the influence of the earth’s gravitational attraction. All bodies within a particular location
    fall with the same downward acceleration, regardless of their size or weight.

    Assumptions in free fall:

    • Effects of the air can be neglected
    • Effects due to earth’s rotation can be neglected
    • Distance of the fall is small compared to the radius of the earth

    If the motion of an object is symmetrical, then tup​=tdown​.

    If the motion of an object is not symmetrical, to solve, break it down into various parts.
