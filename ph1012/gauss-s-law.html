    Gauss’s Law

    #Electric Flux

    #Flux of a Uniform Electric Field

    Consider an electric field that is uniform in magnitude and direction. The field lines penetrate a rectangular surface of area A, which is perpendicular to the field.

    💡
    ΦE​=EAcosθ

    The SI unit for electric flux is Nm2/C.

    #Example 1

    ΦE​=(2×103)[π(0.1)2cos30°]

    ΦE​=54.4 Nm2/C

    ΦE(90°)​=0

    ΦE(0°)​=(2×103)π(0.1)2

    ΦE(0°)​=62.8 Nm2/C

    #Example 2

    ΦE​=EA

    ΦE​=ke​r2∣q∣​(4πr2)

    ΦE​=4π(8.99×109)(3×10−6)

    ΦE​=3.39×105 Nm2/C

    The electric flux is independent of the size of the surface.

    #Flux of a Non-Uniform Electric Field

    Electric flux through a surface is evaluated as a surface integral.

    💡
    ΦE​=∫E⋅dA

    Electric flux through a closed surface (e.g. sphere) is evaluated as a closed integral.

    💡
    ΦE​=∮E⋅dA

    At element 1, the field lines are crossing the surface from the inside to the outside and θ<90°, hence the flux through this element is positive.

    At element 2, the field lines graze the surface and the flux is zero.

    At element 3, the field lines are crossing from the surface from outside to inside and 90°<θ<180°, hence the flux is negative as cosθ is negative.

    #Gauss’s Law

    #Point Charge inside a Closed Surface

    Consider a positive point charge q located at the center of a sphere of radius r. The field lines are directed radially outward and hence are perpendicular to the surface.

    E=ke​r2q​

    E=4πϵ0​1​r2q​

    ΦE​=∮E⋅dA

    ΦE​=4πϵ0​1​r2q​(4πr2)

    💡
    ΦE​=ϵ0​q​

    The flux is independent of the radius r of the sphere. It depends only on the charge q enclosed by the sphere.

    Consider several non-spherical surfaces surrounding a charge q. Surface S1​ is spherical while S2​ and S3​ are not.

    The number of field lines through S1​ is equal to the number of lines through the non-spherical surfaces. Therefore, the net flux through any closed surface is independent of the shape of that surface.

    Now consider a point charge located outside a closed surface of arbitrary shape. Any field lines entering the surface leaves the surface at another point.

    Therefore, the net flux through a closed surface that surrounds no charge is zero.

    💡
    ΦE​=ϵ0​qin​​

    #Application of Gauss’s Law

    Gauss’s law is valid for any distribution of charges and for any closed surfaces.

    When excess charge is placed on a solid conductor and is at rest, it resides entirely on the surface, not
    in the interior of the material.

    #Field of a Plane of Charge

    Consider a thin, flat infinite sheet with a uniform positive surface charge density of σ. The field of a uniformly charged plate is normal to the sheet.

    The flux through the cylindrical part of the Gaussian surface is zero as the field lines are parallel to the surface. The flux through the end faces is non-zero as the field lines are perpendicular to the surface.

    ΦE​=E(2A)

    ΦE​=ϵ0​q​=ϵ0​σA​

    E(2A)=ϵ0​σA​

    💡
    E=2ϵ0​σ​

    #Field of a Uniform Line Charge

    Consider an infinitely long, thin wire with a uniformly distributed electric charge. The charge per unit length is λ and the electric field of the wire is radially outwards.

    The flux through the flat ends of the Gaussian surface is zero as the field lines are parallel to the surfaces. On the cylindrical part of the surface, flux is non-zero.

    ΦE​=EA=E(2πrl)

    ΦE​=ϵ0​q​=ϵ0​λl​

    E(2πrl)=ϵ0​λl​

    💡
    E=2πϵ0​1​rλ​=2ke​rλ​

    #Field of a Uniformly Charged Sphere

    Consider a positive electric charge Q is distributed uniformly throughout the volume of an insulating sphere with radius R.

    To determine the magnitude of the electric field at a point P a distance r from the center of the sphere, we choose a concentric sphere with radius r as Gaussian surface.

    ρ=34​πR3Q​

    To find E inside of the sphere, we choose r<R.

    Venclosed​=34​πr3

    Qenclosed​=ρVenclosed​

    Qenclosed​=34​πR3Q​(34​πr3)=QR3r3​

    ΦE​=ε0​qin​​=ϵ0​Q​R3r3​

    ΦE​=EA=4πr2E

    4πr2E=ϵ0​Q​R3r3​

    💡
    E=4πϵ0​1​R3Qr​=ke​R3Qr​

    Electric field inside a uniformly charged sphere.

    To find E outside of the sphere, we choose r>R.

    This surface encloses the entire charged sphere, so Qenclosed​=Q.

    4πr2E=ϵ0​Q​

    💡
    E=4πϵ0​1​r2Q​=ke​r2Q​

    Electric field outside a uniformly charged sphere.

    #Field of Oppositely Charged Parallel Plates

    Consider two large parallel conducting plates are given charges of equal magnitude and opposite signs; the surface charge densities are +σ and −σ.

    Assuming that the plates are very large in comparison to the distance between them, the amount of charge on the outer surfaces is negligibly small. Hence, the fringing can be neglected except near the edges.

    The left end of surface S1​ is within the positive plate and since field is zero within the volume of any solid conductor under electrostatic conditions, there is no electric flux.

    ΦS1​​=EA

    ΦS1​​=ϵ0​q​=ϵ0​σA​

    EA=ϵ0​σA​

    💡
    E=ϵ0​σ​
