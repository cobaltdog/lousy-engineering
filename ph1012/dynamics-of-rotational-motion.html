    Dynamics of Rotational Motion

    #Torque

    The tendency of a force to rotate an object about some axis is measured by a quantity called torque τ.

    💡
    τ=r×F
    💡
    τ=rFsinθ

    Choosing the axis of rotation to be the z-axis, consider a particle with mass m1​ located at a distance r1​ from the axis.

    τ1z​=I1​αz​

    The above equation is valid only for rigid bodies.
    It does not apply to a rotating tank of water as different areas have different angular acceleration

    #Angular Momentum

    The angular momentum of a particle is denoted by L.

    💡
    L=r×p​=r×mv
    💡
    L=prsinθ=mvrsinθ

    When a net force acts on a particle, its velocity and momentum changes, so its angular momentum may also change.

    dtdL​=(dtd​ r×mv)+(r×dtd​ mv)

    dtdL​=(v×mv)+(r×ma)

    💡
    dtdL​=r×F=τ

    The rate of change of angular momentum of a particle equals the torque of the net force.

    #Angular Momentum of a Rigid Body

    Consider a rigid object rotating about the z-axis. Each particle of the object rotates in the xy-plane about the z-axis with an angular velocity ω.

    v=rω

    Lz​=pr=mvr

    #Conservation of Angular Momentum

    When the net external torque acting on a system is zero, the total angular momentum of the system is constant (conserved). The torques of the internal forces can transfer angular momentum from one body to another, but they can’t change the total angular momentum.

    #Rolling Motion of a Rigid Object

    If an object such as a cylinder rolls without slipping on the surface — pure rolling motion, a simple relationship exists between its rotational and translational motions.

    Consider a uniform cylinder of radius R rolling without slipping. As the cylinder rotates through an angle θ, its center of mass moves a linear distance s=Rθ.

    vcm​=dtds​=R dtdθ​=Rω

    acm​=dtdvcm​​=R dtdω​=Rα

    #Rolling without Slipping

    The contact point between the surface and the cylinder has a translational velocity of zero. Thus, at any instant, the cylinder is rotating about an “instantaneous axis” of rotation that passes through the point of contact P with the ground.

    We can interpret as though the object were pivoted at P and spun about an axis passing through P, where total kinetic energy can be expressed as rotational kinetic energy.

    KR​=21​IP​ω2

    KR​=21​(Icm​+MR2)ω2

    KR​=21​Icm​ω2+21​MR2ω2

    💡
    KR​=21​Icm​ω2+21​Mvcm​2

    The total kinetic energy of a rolling object is the sum of the rotational kinetic energy about the center of mass and the translational kinetic energy of the center of mass.

    #Rolling Friction

    When a perfectly rigid sphere rolls down a perfectly rigid incline, the line of action of the normal force passes through the center of the sphere. Thus, net torque is zero, and friction does no work.

    In a more realistic situation, the surface “piles up” in front of the sphere and the sphere rides in a shallow trench. The contact forces no longer act along a single point but over an area.

    As a result, the normal force now exerts a torque that opposes the rotation. There is also some sliding over the surface due to the deformation, causing mechanical energy to be lost.
