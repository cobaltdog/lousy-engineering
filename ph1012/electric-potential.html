    #Electric Potential Energy

    The work-energy theorem states that ΔK=Wtot​ during a displacement.

    Kf​−Ki​=−(Uelec,f​−Uelec,i​)

    💡
    Ki​+Uelec,i​=Kf​+Uelec,f​

    #Potential Energy of Two Point Charges

    Considering displacement along the radial line, the force on q0​ is given by Coulomb’s law.

    Fe​=4πϵ0​1​r2qq0​​

    F(r)=−drdU​

    ΔUelec​=−∫ri​rf​​4πϵ0​1​r2qq0​​ dr

    ΔUelec​=−4πϵ0​qq0​​[−r1​]ri​rf​​

    ΔUelec​=4πϵ0​qq0​​[rf​1​−ri​1​]

    💡
    Uelec​=4πϵ0​1​rqq0​​=ke​rqq0​​

    #Example

    Using conservation of energy,

    Ki​+Uelec,i​=Kf​+Uelec,f​

    21​mvi​2+ke​ri​qq0​​=21​mvf​2+ke​rf​qq0​​

    Ki​=21​(9.11×10−31)(3×106)2=4.0995×10−18 J

    Uelec,i​=(8.99×109)1×10−10(1.6×10−19)(3.2×10−19)​=4.6029×10−18 J

    Uelec,f​=(8.99×109)2×10−10(1.6×10−19)(3.2×10−19)​=2.3014×10−18 J

    21​mvf​2=(4.0995×10−18)+(4.6029×10−18)−(2.3014×10−18)

    vf​=3.75×106 m/s

    When the particle is very far away, Uelec,f​=0.

    21​mvf​2=(4.0995×10−18)+(4.6029×10−18)

    vf​=4.37×106 m/s

    When the moving particle is changed from a positron to an electron with charge −e, the force is attractive and the electron slows down as it moves away from α.

    Uelec,i​=(8.99×109)1×10−10(−1.6×10−19)(3.2×10−19)​=−4.6029×10−18 J

    Ki​+Uelec,i​=(4.0995−4.6029)×10−18=−0.5034×10−18 J

    The total mechanical energy would have to be positive for the electron to move infinitely far away from α. Thus, since the total mechanical energy is negative, the electron will reach a maximum separation of rd​, where its Kd​=0.

    Ki​+Uelec,i​=Uelec,d​

    21​mvi​2+ke​ri​qq0​​=ke​rd​qq0​​

    −5.034×10−18=(8.99×109)rd​(−1.6×10−19)(3.2×10−19)​

    rd​=9.1436×10−10 m

    Checking the speed of the electron at r=2×10−10.

    Ki​+Uelec,i​=Kr​+Uelec,r​

    Ki​=4.0995×10−18 J

    Uelec,i​=−4.6029×10−18 J

    Uelec,r​=−2.3014×10−18 J

    21​mvr​2=(4.0995×10−18)+(−4.6029×10−18)−(−2.3014×10−18)

    vr​=1.99×106 m/s

    We see that the electron’s speed at r=2×10−10 is slower than its initial speed, which confirms that the electron will slow down and eventually turn aruond.

    #Potential Energy in a Uniform Field

    The electric field exerts a downward force with magnitude F on a positive test charge q0​.

    As the charge moves down a distance d, the electric force is constant and independent of its location.

    Wa→b​=Fd=q0​Ed

    💡
    Uelec​=q0​Ey

    When the test charge moves from height ya​ to height yb​, the work done is −ΔUelec​.

    Wa→b​=−ΔUelec​

    Wa→b​=−(q0​Eyb​−q0​Eya​)=q0​E(ya​−yb​)

    If ya​>yb​, the field does positive work and potential energy decreases. If ya​<yb​, the field does negative work and potential energy increases.

    #Electric Potential (V)

    Electric potential is potential energy per unit charge.

    💡
    V=q0​Uelec​​

    The SI unit of potential is the volt V or joule per coulomb J/C.

    q0​Wa→b​​=q0​−ΔUelec​​=−(q0​Uelec,b​​−q0​Uelec,a​​)

    q0​Wa→b​​=−(Vb​−Va​)=Va​−Vb​

    Vab​=Va​−Vb​ is the potential of a with respect to b.

    Vab​ equals the work done by the electric force when a unit charge moves from a to b.

    💡
    q0​W​=Va​−Vb​

    #Electric Potential due to a Point Charge

    💡
    V=4πϵ0​1​rq​=ke​rq​
    💡
    V=4πϵ0​1​∑ri​qi​​=ke​∑ri​qi​​

    #Electric Potential from Electric Field

    In some problems in which the electric field is known, it is easier to determine V from E.

    The force F on a test charge is F=q0​E.

    W=∫ab​F⋅ds

    W=∫ab​ q0​E⋅ds

    q0​W​=∫ab​E⋅ds

    Va​−Vb​=∫ab​E⋅ds

    💡
    sVab​​=E
    💡
    1 Volt/meter=1 Newton/Coulomb

    If the line integral is positive, the electric field does positive work on a positive test charge as it moves from a to b. The electric potential energy decreases as the test charge moves, so the potential decreases as well. Thus, Vb​<Va​.

    #Electric Field from Electric Potential

    Va​−Vb​=∫ab​E⋅ds

    Vb​−Va​=−∫ab​E⋅ds

    💡
    E=−dsdV​

    #Equipotential Surfaces

    The potential at various points in an electric field can be represented by equipotential surfaces.

    Description of equipotential surface:

    • Field lines and equipotential surfaces are always perpendicular
    • Equipotential surfaces are close to each other when magnitude of E is large
